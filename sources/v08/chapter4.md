# Chapter 4: Encounter

I told the Principal it was “case closed,” but in truth, nothing’s over. There’s
still the possibility of undetected “Outer” users lying low, and the root
cause—who’s distributing the drug—remains unresolved. We’re still analyzing the
students’ combat data and searching for the so-called “merchant from the
city-state cluster.”

Plus, it’s unsettling that Eclara and Kiteros, who I personally suspect, weren’t
among those arrested.

Well, Eclara hasn’t even fought upperclassmen in “tournaments,” so my suspicion
of her is just a hunch—borderline accusation, really.

Still, something about them nags at me. That’s why I’m still digging into their
backgrounds. Finding a clue would be a bonus.

“Phew,” I sighed softly.

The sun was setting, painting the sky in gradients of red and purple. Gazing up
at the heavens, I let out a small breath.

After leaving the Principal’s office, I was walking home alone. I could’ve used
\[Phase Connection Gate\], but I chose to stroll to organize my thoughts.
Moments like this often spark inspiration.

Right now, no one—not even my retainers—is with me. I usually don’t bring anyone
to meetings with the Principal, but there’s another reason this time.

It’s the season for club tours and trial memberships. Everyone else is busy with
those.

How nostalgic. It was just a year ago that Laurel recruited me with a groveling
plea.

The Magic Piece Club, led by Caron, has grown into one of the academy’s two top
clubs, alongside the best. With members like us, that’s no surprise.

Over the past year, we’ve had plenty of hopefuls, but no new members since Dan
and the others. Our club operates on a strict referral system—only those
recommended by a member can join.

Why? Our strength is just too overwhelming. Leeches are inevitable, but Caron
wants to run the club seriously.

By the way, Skia, our newest ally, hasn’t joined. She’s a hardcore indoor type,
and a combat-focused club like Magic Piece doesn’t suit her.

I’m not about to force her into club activities, so we respect her wishes. Of
course, she still participates in Magic Piece during training.

Digression aside.

As I mentioned, our club is referral-only, but while we cap daily visitors,
tours are open to all. You never know when a serious talent might show up.

Being the club with Foranada members, our tours are packed daily, requiring
lotteries.

The tour is straightforward: first, they watch basic training, then a mock
battle, and finally, a Magic Piece match between members and visitors.

Most students pale at the training, roll their eyes during the mock battle, and
lose all confidence in the match. So far, zero applicants have joined. None have
caught Caron’s eye, either.

That said, a few might make it in. We’ve adjusted the difficulty to be barely
manageable for amateurs. Plus, I hear Tara’s definitely joining, so we’re not
worried about zero newbies.

That’s why I’m savoring this alone time.

As the head of an earl’s house, some might criticize this, but my case is
special. Clumsy guards would just slow me down, and thanks to my past-life
memories, I barely need attendants. I’m probably a nightmare for my retainers.

“Hm?” I muttered.

I’d been leisurely walking, gazing at the twilight sky, when I let out a sound.
My detection spell suddenly picked up something.

I snapped my gaze back to the ground.

Ten meters ahead stood a female student. Her knee-length blonde hair and golden
eyes, gleaming even in the dim light, were striking.

Her features were unnervingly perfect. Her youthful face and petite frame gave
an initial impression of “adorable,” but she was undeniably a beauty.

I tried to observe her cautiously, but she spoke first.

“Well, well,” she said, her tone self-absorbed. “Nie was so paranoid about you,
I got curious and came to see… Hmm, hmm.”

Her words weren’t directed at me—a soliloquy, more like self-questioning.

Someone told her about me, and she came to check me out? I had questions, but
they didn’t matter now. Three seconds of observation told me this was no time
for that.

—_BOOM_\!\!

In less than an instant, in my divine transformation state, I grabbed the blonde
girl’s head and slammed her into the ground. The compressed impact shook the
earth and rattled our eardrums.

My turn didn’t end there.

“\[Jail Chain\]\!” I declared, chanting for certainty.

About ten translucent mana chains appeared, binding the girl tightly in an
instant. I even added faux mithril wires for good measure.

She was restrained, unable to move a finger—

“Wow, that’s harsh, attacking out of nowhere,” she said, her voice light. “If it
wasn’t me, I’d be dead, you know?”

The next moment, she vanished from the chains and stood ten meters away,
unscathed, tossing out a casual quip.

I clicked my tongue inwardly. I’d tried binding her on a whim, but it was
pointless.

She continued, “You can _see_ me, huh? That’s surprising. My mana light blinds
even other Magic Administrators’ eyes. Are you _really_ human?”

Her cackling words confirmed my suspicion.

“What do you want with me, Gruewen Licht, Gold Magic Administrator?” I said, my
voice steady.

“Yup, you saw right through me,” she said, grinning. “How’d you know?”

This blonde girl was none other than the “Western Demon King”—the greatest
obstacle for me, no, for Caron.

I don’t know why she’s here, despite being sealed. But it’s definitely bad news
for me and Caron.

I’d love to crush her now, but I need more time to be fully prepared. So, I kept
talking.

“Distinguishing between a physical body and a mana body is easy enough,” I said.
“Plus, the curse you’re radiating is too sinister.”

First, Magic Administrators, having offered their entire being to the mana
world, are mana bodies. Unlike spirits, they’re visible, but I’m not so blind as
to miss the difference. Unlike Garuna, who materializes, Gruewen’s lack of a
physical form made it even easier to spot.

Second, the curse. As the “Western Demon King” who scatters curses across the
world, she was exuding one. It was darker than any curse I’d seen. Her surface
was all levity and smiles, but inside, she was a bottomless abyss.

Hearing my response, Gruewen shook her head. “No, no,” she said. “I’m asking why
you can see me.”

“Hm?” I said, frowning slightly.

Her phrasing confused me. Is she using some kind of perception-blocking magic? I
didn’t feel resistance to any spell, nor did I sense mana movement indicating
one.

…No, wait. She mentioned her “mana light blinding eyes.” Does her mana itself
have a perception-blocking effect?

Now that she mentions it, her mana does feel somewhat dazzling.

Seriously? Mana itself having an effect is a cheat I’ve never heard of. Garuna’s
intel didn’t mention this either.

Did she hide it from other Magic Administrators, or is it a side effect of her
seal? Either way, I need to rethink my strategy against her. It may not affect
me much, but if it works on others, she could run rampant. What a pain.

If I could deal with her now, I wouldn’t need to rethink anything… but we’ll
see.

For now, I’ll isolate her.

As I bantered, I deployed \[Alternate World: Baule de Tesoro\]. My mana
enveloped the world.

But she wasn’t going to let me cut off her escape so easily.

“What a hassle,” Gruewen said, her voice dripping with annoyance. “—\[Light: I
Dye Everything in Gold\]\!”

She activated a spell, and for a split second, a brilliant light flashed. The
next moment, my still-forming \[Alternate World: Baule de Tesoro\] was
nullified.

She wagged her finger, clicking her tongue. “No, no. I’m a Magic Administrator
specialized in light. When it comes to illusion and domination, no one rivals
me. A mere attribute mage like you can’t possibly stand against the pinnacle of
Magic Administrators.”

Her words were insufferably arrogant.

Infuriating, but she’s not wrong. I could tell I’d lose in barrier or spatial
magic against her.

This was something we’d confirmed in my mock battle with Garuna. Magic
Administrators have their specialties, and in those domains, they can outmatch
me. For Garuna, it was suppression and detection, right?

That said, the gap in our maximum output is huge, so I could brute-force a win.
In raw power, I can crush a Magic Administrator easily. The difference between
us is that stark.

So why not do it now?

As I’ve mentioned before, their immunity to non-native attributes is the
problem. It makes quick battles tricky. No matter how much I try to minimize
collateral damage, it’s never zero. The longer the fight drags, the more the
surroundings suffer. And as we just saw, isolating her with \[Alternate World:
Baule de Tesoro\] isn’t a simple task either.

In short, Gruewen Licht, the Gold Magic Administrator, excels at delaying
tactics. She might not realize it, but her skills and traits embody that
perfectly.

What a pain. She’s an utterly troublesome opponent. I’ve prepared methods to
pierce her immunity, but with her full capabilities unclear—especially after
this perception-blocking revelation—I hesitated to use my trump card. I’ve got
intel from Garuna, but that gap in information means I can’t be fully confident.

Even if I corner her, if she escapes with some trick, it’s a disaster. Types
like her go completely underground once they sense danger. With Caron’s fate on
the line, I can’t let her roam free. When I strike, it has to be certain.

This was an unexpected skirmish. The best move is to hold back and make her
reveal her hand. It might make her warier, but that’s a necessary cost.

After all that overthinking, it boils down to “fight with restraint to draw out
information.” From what Garuna said about Gruewen’s personality, she’s likely
underestimating me, a human.

Alright, decision made. Time to step it up. Based on that last exchange, I can
increase output without issue.

\[Compress Cube\], I thought, launching a silent sneak attack.

But the spell had no effect on Gruewen. I couldn’t trap her in the mana box—she
resisted instantly.

“I told you, domination spells won’t work,” she said, laughing with smug
confidence.

Got it. Any spell with even a hint of domination mechanics is useless. That
narrows my options. Annoying.

As I groaned inwardly, Gruewen chuckled. “You really want to fight me? I don’t
hate that recklessness. Fine. It’s been a while since I’ve fought, so I’ll play
along.”

With that, she snapped her fingers.

Instantly, five-centimeter-wide lasers fired from all directions, targeting me,
obviously.

One glance told me each was supreme-magic-tier. Half-baked defenses would be
pierced. She’s not called the Great Light Magic Administrator for nothing.

“\[Paper Moon\]\!” I shouted, reinforcing with a chant.

My mana formed countless sheets—resembling A4 copy paper—that moved on their
own, filling my field of vision. From the outside, it looked like a
two-meter-diameter sphere—a paper moon—had formed around me.

The lasers struck the sphere, tearing through several layers but failing to
reach me.

Seeing me block her first attack, Gruewen’s smile deepened. “Not bad. How about
this?”

She snapped her fingers again.

A glowing orb appeared right in front of me, pea-sized but bypassing \[Paper
Moon\]’s barrier entirely. No way I could underestimate it.

“Tch,” I clicked my tongue.

I opened a \[Phase Connection Gate\] at my feet and fell through, following
gravity.

The next instant, an explosion erupted, a devastating attack that dyed
everything in light. Thanks to \[Paper Moon\], the blast didn’t spread, but
without the barrier, this entire area would’ve been reduced to ash.

Having teleported to dodge, I charged at Gruewen, drawing a dagger reinforced
with \[Magic Envelopment\].

She just laughed, standing defenseless.

I struck without mercy, but the result was lackluster.

“Pointless,” she said. “Nothing but light reaches me.”

Her Magic Administrator trait made her impervious. As expected, without applying
penetration, no damage would land.

But hurting her now would make her overly cautious, complicating a decisive
blow.

After a few sword strikes, I landed a kick. She went flying and rolled but took
no damage, standing up with a smile.

“Told you, it’s pointless,” she said.

“You’re right, doesn’t seem like I can hurt you,” I admitted.

Continuing this restrained fight would never end. I’d finished analyzing—time
for her to exit.

I closed in again, grabbed her arm, and threw her over my shoulder like a judo
toss. Assuming she’d take no damage, she sailed through the air—straight toward
a \[Phase Connection Gate\] she hadn’t noticed.

“Huh?” she said, realizing too late.

By the time she reacted, the \[Phase Connection Gate\] was already closing.

“Hahaha, I see, I see,” she said, laughing gleefully. “That’s a clever move.
Alright, I’ll be good and head back this time.”

True to her word, she vanished without resistance, her laughter echoing.

The earlier chaos faded, replaced by silence. I hadn’t noticed due to Gruewen’s
light, but the sun had fully set, and night’s curtain had fallen.

“Phew,” I exhaled softly.

Job done, I waved my hand lightly, peeling away \[Concealment\] like wiping a
slate.

What appeared was my gleaming white left eye—the analytical Magic Eye \[White
Radiance\].

As you might’ve guessed, I used this Magic Eye to probe Gruewen’s data.
Analyzing a Magic Administrator was tough, but I bought enough time to expose
her hand.

Good thing she underestimated me. Though, applying \[Concealment\] to my whole
body was a total waste.

With careful observation, she could’ve noticed the \[Concealment\] and spotted
the Magic Eye.

But knowing her arrogant personality from Garuna’s intel, I banked on her
overconfidence, and it worked better than expected.

I was rattled by the sudden boss fight, but the results were solid. Now, I’ll
review the intel and work on countermeasures.

“…Time to head back,” I muttered.

Physically and magically, I’ve got plenty left, but the mental fatigue is real.
I need to get home and let Caron and the others heal me.

Closing my Magic Eye, I used \[Phase Connection Gate\] to return to my secondary
residence in the capital.

“Garuna, sorry to interrupt your meal, but I need your time. It’s urgent,” I
said.

On the night of my confrontation with Gruewen, I visited Garuna, the Blue Magic
Administrator. She was dining in the cafeteria with other retainers—Teria and
Maron—but this involved the “Western Demon King,” so I couldn’t afford to be
considerate.

“Fwa-fwa-fi-fwa\!” Garuna mumbled, her cheeks puffed like a chipmunk.

“Swallow before you answer\!” Shion snapped.

“That’s bad manners\!” Maron added.

As Garuna responded, the two at her table scolded her with exasperated looks.
From what she’d told me, the trio remained close even after the Magic
Administrator reveal.

Oops, no time to get all warm and fuzzy.

“Sorry for the wait\!” Garuna said, swallowing her food and dashing over to bow.

Acknowledging her, I said, “Follow me,” and started walking.

We headed to a deep underground laboratory, an isolated room with nothing
inside, accessible only via \[Phase Connection Gate\].

It wasn’t just me and Garuna. There was one other person waiting.

“Oh, Shion-senpai,” Garuna said.

As she noted, Shion was there.

I’d called her in advance. She knows some of Caron’s situation and oversees the
intelligence division, making her essential for this discussion.

“Sorry to keep you waiting,” I said.

“No, I’m sorry for making you, Zex-sama, come fetch me,” Shion replied.

“It’s fine,” I said. “Only I can access this place. Don’t worry about it.”

After a brief exchange, I pulled a table and chairs for three from \[Phase
Concealment\]. Sitting down, I gestured for them to do the same.

…Might as well serve tea. I’m pretty sure I stored some freshly brewed stuff in
\[Phase Concealment\].

After quickly setting up, I cut to the chase. With this group, small talk was
unnecessary.

“Around 6:00 p.m. today, I encountered the ‘Western Demon King’ on academy
grounds,” I said.

““Huh?”” Shion and Garuna gasped, freezing with wide eyes.

They seemed overwhelmed by my blunt delivery. My bad.

I waited for them to reboot.

Soon, Shion spoke. “My apologies. May I confirm?”

“Go ahead,” I said.

“By ‘Western Demon King,’ you mean _the_ Western Demon King, correct?” she
asked.

She was clearly rattled. If there were another “Western Demon King,” the world
would’ve ended by now.

I gave a wry smile. “The Demon King sealed in the west of the Holy Kingdom,
reinforced every century by a saintess chosen by God. That one.”

“Oh…” Shion sighed, slumping as if hoping it was a misunderstanding.

Garuna chimed in. “For real, it was Gruewen?”

“I’ve got a \[Mind Projection\] sketch for confirmation,” I said, pulling a
paper from my pocket.

It depicted the girl I’d met earlier.

Mind Projection is a mental magic that outputs brain images or memories. Artists
would kill for this spell.

Garuna studied the sketch, widened her eyes briefly, then sighed deeply. “She
looks a bit younger, but no doubt, it’s Grue-chan. Magic Administrators can
tweak their appearance easily.”

“Garuna,” Shion said sharply.

“Ah, sorry\!” Garuna squeaked.

“It’s fine,” I said. “That’s how shocking it was.”

After all, a malevolent, top-tier human threat had resurfaced.

Having shared that the “Western Demon King” was out, I detailed the encounter.

“…So, I focused on gathering intel and sent her packing,” I concluded.

“Wow, just…” Garuna trailed off.

“As expected of Zex-sama…” Shion said, her tone weary.

Huh? Why were they giving me exasperated looks?

“Something wrong?” I asked, tilting my head.

They let out dry laughs.

“Put yourself in our shoes, hearing you single-handedly repelled the ‘Western
Demon King,’ a being sealed by humanity led by a saintess,” Shion said.

“From what I heard, if Grue-chan didn’t have a trump card, you could’ve finished
her right there, right?” Garuna added. “I’m seriously doubting if you’re human,
Zex-sama. I mean, I kinda suspected when you pummeled me, but still.”

I got it—they were shocked by my strength. Fair enough, considering I chased off
a legendary Demon King.

But, “If it weren’t for that annoying immunity resistance, even you or Caron
could take her,” I said. “In terms of level, she’s barely 120.”

Gruewen’s raw power isn’t the issue. It’s her immunity to non-native attributes
and her ability to neutralize barriers that make her tricky.

They still looked exasperated.

“I hate to say it, but being Level Over already makes you a threat to humanity,”
Garuna said.

“You should realize it’s abnormal to counter color magic with attribute magic,”
Shion added. “That’s a problem even before immunity resistance.”

I get their point. Magic Administrators are humanity’s pinnacle, not something
humans typically defeat.

But I can’t just nod and agree. She might take Caron’s life—I have to crush her,
no matter how tough.

Their words made it clear: I’m the only one who can face Gruewen. Her color
magic and immunity wall are obstacles only I can break through right now.

Fighting her is a massive pain. And relying on the saintess to reseal her is now
pointless—she’s out despite the seal. I have no choice but to shift to
eliminating her.

As I sighed softly, Shion asked, “I forgot amid the shock, but why was the
‘Western Demon King’ out? Did she break the seal?”

Good question, one that should’ve come up first. She must’ve been floored.

Garuna shook her head. “No way, senpai. That seal’s a world-crafted formula. It
weakens every century, but no Magic Administrator bound by the world can break
it. If it was broken, the curse fallout would be everywhere.”

“Exactly,” I said. “If the seal were broken, the world wouldn’t be this calm. I
confirmed just now—the Demon King’s still sealed.”

“When did you…?” Shion asked.

“I’ve got \[Phase Connection Gate\],” I said.

I checked the western seal site—no breaches, no tampering.

“More to the point, she’s still sealed but out?” Shion asked.

“A soul fragment,” Garuna said.

As expected of a Magic Administrator, she’d already pieced it together.

Shion, lacking the knowledge, tilted her head. “What’s a soul fragment?”

“Literally, a split soul,” Garuna explained. “It’s like dividing someone’s
spirit, similar to a curse. It’s like separating pure and chaotic mana.”

“So the Demon King out there isn’t the main body,” Shion said.

“Probably,” Garuna said. “Which means Zex-sama’s call was right. Killing the
fragment would leave Grue-chan’s main body unharmed, and she’d share the memory,
becoming warier.”

Exactly. Garuna’s right—my judgment was spot-on. Trying to kill her then
would’ve backfired.

Garuna crossed her arms, grumbling. “But Grue-chan using soul fragments and
perception blocking? Back in the day, it was just brute-force light magic or
manipulating people’s darkness with illusions.”

“Maybe the seal made her rethink her approach,” I said. “Though, with that
personality, I doubt she’s reflected on anything fundamental.”

“Nope, impossible,” Garuna agreed.

Even a brief encounter made it clear: Gruewen’s self-centered to the core,
steeped in arrogance and greed. She doesn’t care about others and expects to be
served.

“Back to the point,” I said. “I called you two for intel sharing and to ask for
your help.”

“Tell us,” Shion said.

“I’ll do what I can,” Garuna added.

I gave a small smile at their reliable responses. “Thanks. Shion, I need you to
give orders to your subordinates. Two things. First, increase surveillance at
the seal site. I made a countermeasure for Gruewen’s perception
blocking—distribute these to the watchers.”

I handed her sunglasses-shaped magic tools, designed to disperse Gruewen’s mana
light based on her data. They’re rushed, not foolproof, so I warned against
overreliance and told her to carefully select who gets them, as mass production
is tricky.

The second request: “For Caron’s protection, always have either you or Garuna
with her, and at least two others.”

Gruewen might target Caron directly. Other retainers might fall short, but these
two can at least hold their own.

“Understood,” Shion said.

I turned to Garuna. “Garuna, I need you to help analyze Gruewen’s intel. We need
a Magic Administrator’s perspective.”

“Got it,” she said. “Starting now?”

“Yeah, I want to move fast,” I said. “Can I count on you?”

“Leave it to me\!” she said, thumping her chest confidently.

Good. That covers everything.

“Any questions?” I asked.

“Not at the moment,” Shion said.

“Same here,” Garuna added.

“Alright, we’re done here,” I said. “Get to your tasks.”

““Yes\!”” they replied.

Caron’s fate has entered a new phase. Whether I can turn this situation to our
advantage depends on my skill. Time to step up.

Several days passed since we started countermeasures against Gruewen. We’ve been
running tests, but results don’t come easy. For now, it’s been an uneventful
routine. Thankfully, Gruewen hasn’t shown up again.

“No change, huh,” I muttered.

I was in a forest at the western edge. Despite the lush greenery, a barren patch
of ground, about five hundred meters in radius, stood out unnaturally.

This was Gruewen’s seal site.

Some might imagine a grand ritual site for a Demon King’s seal, but in reality,
no one makes such obvious, destructible places. Preventing third-party sabotage
is critical.

Thus, the seal is entirely magical. Without spirit-level mana perception,
tampering’s impossible.

There’s a garrison hundreds of meters away, purely for monitoring the Demon
King, as this isn’t near any border.

But the knights there lack motivation. The cursed seal site has no nearby
villages—it’s the middle of nowhere. Centuries ago, it might’ve been tense, but
now, there’s no urgency. To them, this post is akin to exile, though it doesn’t
technically harm their careers.

Enough about the knights. The seal site’s my focus.

With \[Magic Vision\], I saw some age-related weakening, but no other issues.
The seal formula was active, and its contents were intact.

Since my encounter with Gruewen, I’ve checked daily, but nothing’s changed. No
run-ins with her soul fragment, just eerie peace.

Peace is good, don’t get me wrong. I’m working on Gruewen countermeasures, but I
can’t guarantee a clean kill without collateral damage. At best, I’d say a 95
percent win rate. That 5 percent could cause massive harm, which I want to avoid
for the future.

I know I’m being overly cautious. But with Caron’s life potentially at stake, I
have to be. I need to nail this on the first try.

Worst case, I’d ignore collateral damage to take her down, but that’s a last
resort. If Caron learned the truth, it’d break her heart.

By the way, I’ve shared the soul fragment encounter with all of Foranada. I
didn’t mention Caron’s fate—lacking proof—but everything else is too dangerous
to keep quiet.

I felt a bit guilty worrying Caron and the others with the news.

Anyway, the seal check’s done. Time to regroup with Shion and the others waiting
in the nearest town and head back to the capital.

My team didn’t come here. The seal site spews massive curses, so only I, with my
defenses, can linger. The garrison provides some oversight, too.

I passed through a \[Phase Connection Gate\] to a quiet alley in town. Getting
spotted mid-teleport would cause a stir.

For the record, I’m \[Concealed\]—plain brown hair, brown eyes, nondescript
features. Nothing to stand out.

Shion was waiting, also \[Concealed\], bowing politely. “Welcome back. How was
it?”

“No issues,” I said. “Same as always.”

“I see,” she said, visibly relieved.

No surprise there. To this continent’s people, the “Western Demon King” is a
legendary terror. No matter how strong you are, childhood fears linger. Only a
reincarnator like me, Garuna, or Caron—raised by me—wouldn’t flinch.

Though, Caron could stand to be a bit more scared. When she sparkled, “She’s no
match for Onii-sama\!” I had no choice but to live up to her faith. I can’t let
my adorable sister’s words be a lie.

“Status?” I asked.

“No abnormalities,” Shion said. “Surveillance teams across all areas report no
sightings of the target.”

“OK,” I said. “Notify all units: gather at designated points A, B, and C, and
await \[Phase Connection Gate\] activation.”

“Understood,” she said. “Repeating: ‘Gather at designated points A, B, and C,
and await \[Phase Connection Gate\] activation.’”

“Good,” I said.

“Please wait a moment,” she said.

Using her \[Magic Communicator\], Shion relayed the orders efficiently. It took
about three minutes.

“Sorry for the wait,” she said.

“Good work,” I said.

“My apologies—ah\!” she yelped, tripping mid-bow.

Her clumsiness was as reliable as ever.

I’d anticipated it and caught her swiftly. “Careful.”

“I-I-I’m so sorry\!” she stammered, her face turning beet red, trembling like a
boiled octopus, too weak to pull away.

From cool, beautiful secretary to adorable woman in a flash—that’s her charm.

“Calm down,” I said, knowing it’d make her redder but hugging her gently,
pressing her face to my chest like a lover.

“Au, au,” she mumbled, utterly overwhelmed, going limp.

After savoring her for a moment, I scooped her up in a princess carry. She was
nearly fainting, making it tricky, but with my training and \[Physical
Enhancement\], it was no issue.

_‘This is Surveillance Team Alpha. All units have reached designated
positions,’_ a subordinate reported via \[Telepathy\].

They’d followed Shion’s orders, so I remotely opened \[Phase Connection Gates\]
at three locations. Seconds later, they confirmed transfer completion, and I
closed the gates.

Just us left.

I was about to open a \[Phase Connection Gate\] to withdraw when—

“Haa,” I sighed, dispersing my gathered mana.

At the same time, Shion leapt from my arms.

“Zex-sama, step back,” she said, her earlier clumsiness gone, replaced by a
tense expression, stepping forward to shield me.

You probably guessed it—a third party had appeared. Classic alleyway trouble.

“Making out in a place like this? Showing off, huh?” a man said, his voice
dripping with cliché aggression.

His appearance was equally predictable—likely an adventurer. Rough clothes under
leather armor, a broadsword in one hand.

Standard-issue gear. Messy hair, decent muscle. Probably Rank E or D, just past
rookie, getting the hang of adventuring. Yup, \[Appraisal\] says Level
19—spot-on.

His drawn sword suggested a hotheaded personality. His glinting eyes hinted at a
slum upbringing.
