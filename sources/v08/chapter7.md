# Epilogue: Turning the Past into Fuel for Dreams

A week had passed since the incident orchestrated by Eclara came to a close.
During that time, interrogations led by Foranada were conducted on the
masterminds, but not many new facts came to light. At most, we uncovered some
research results related to the “Outer,” but the majority of the information was
already covered by what Eclara and Kiteros had revealed.

As for Gruewen, who was likely involved, no information surfaced at all. I
participated in the investigation myself, but it was like chasing shadows.

Most likely, this was due to the perception-inhibiting magic she emits. If I
could sense her directly, it might be different, but tracking her through people
or objects as intermediaries is difficult even for me. It’s a truly troublesome
ability.

By the way, the orbs used by the two to create the “Shadows” (Eija) and the
magical tools for \[Shadow Walk\] were all single-use consumables. We managed to
confiscate a few, but the items were steeped in curses and had to be destroyed
immediately. While it was theoretically possible to produce similar items, the
cost-effectiveness was abysmal, and there was a fatal flaw in their design, so
we gave up on it. Disposable teleportation tools would have been convenient, but
there was no helping it. The possibility of spontaneous explosions in their
design was something we couldn’t ignore.

After undergoing various investigations, Eclara and her group were executed just
moments ago. Since this incident was a request from Wiemlay—the Holy Royal
Family—it was carried out in accordance with the Holy Kingdom’s laws. Scattering
dangerous substances and plotting to overthrow the state were deeds that could
not be overlooked, no matter how one tried to justify them.

---

While I was sweating it out in the underground training grounds, a \[Telepathy\]
message from Wiemlay informed me that Eclara and her group’s execution had been
completed.

In fact, this punishment was carried out in secret. The reason being that there
might still be charmed individuals hiding among us. Publicly announcing them as
rebels could risk those individuals acting recklessly.

Well, the effect of \[Charm\] should have been severed the moment Orca destroyed
the Magic Eye, so all we can do now is wait for the brainwashing to gradually
fade.

What’s frightening about mental manipulation magic is that, unless undone by
similar magic, the effects don’t vanish instantly. For those who’ve been charmed
for years, it’s even worse—they might mistake it for their genuine emotions,
making it all the more troublesome.

Once again, I reminded myself to never use mental magic carelessly.

I don’t consider myself someone with particularly strong willpower. If there’s
an easy path, I’m confident I’d slide right toward it. If I loosened my
restraint on mental magic, I’d probably lower the threshold of “this much is
fine” bit by bit until I ended up using it every time I interacted with someone.
The end result would be a literal puppet regime.

A tyrant route that would make my family hate me? No thanks. If I went down that
path, would I even use mental magic to twist their values? …Yeah, I might
actually do that. I need to be really careful.

“Let’s take a break,” I said aloud.

To receive Wiemlay’s report, I’d deliberately trained without using
\[Timeless\]. Now that it was done, I wanted to make effective use of my time
going forward.

However, continuing training as is felt off, so I decided to catch my breath. I
grabbed the towel and water bottle I’d left in the corner of the training
grounds, addressing my thirst and wiping away the sweat.

Just as I was about to activate \[Timeless\] to resume training, my constantly
active detection spell picked up a signal. Orca was descending the stairs toward
the training grounds.

My use of the training grounds at this time should have been scheduled in
advance. That meant Orca likely had business with me.

Given the timing, it might be about Eclara’s execution.

With that in mind, I stopped the hand preparing to cast the spell. Then, I
carefully wiped the sweat I’d only roughly dealt with earlier.

As I finished, Orca appeared in the training grounds. Spotting me, he flashed a
smile and approached.

Lately, Orca’s attitude toward me felt softer than before. He’d always been
attached, but now it was almost… squishy, for lack of a better word.

Oh, I know the reason. Even now, waves of overflowing affection were radiating
from him.

I knew I had to respond properly, but it felt like an even higher hurdle than
dealing with Caron.

I felt guilty, but I hoped he’d forgive me until I could settle things with
Gruewen and make up my mind.

“Zex-nii, got a moment?” Orca called out as he closed the distance.

There was no reason to refuse. I nodded readily.

“About Eclara?” I asked.

“Not unrelated, but a bit different,” he replied.

Oh? I’d assumed it was about Eclara, but it seems I was off the mark.

Perhaps sensing my thoughts, Orca gave a wry smile.

“Eclara’s case is already over,” he said.

“Yeah… now that you mention it, what’s there to talk about?” I conceded.

It made sense. There was nothing left to discuss. She’d already been executed.
At most, it’d be reminiscing.

“So, what’s up?” I asked again.

Orca’s expression turned serious.

“It’s about the future,” he said.

At this, my mind immediately jumped to romantic notions, and I realized I’d been
poisoned by my surroundings.

Still, his demeanor made it clear this wasn’t some sweet, lovey-dovey topic.

“Hearing about Eclara and the others made me think a lot,” Orca continued. “What
was it they were so obsessed with achieving?”

I listened attentively to Orca’s words.

Honestly, I had zero interest in the motives of criminals, but it seemed my
childhood friend had been moved by something. He’d clearly thought it through
and come to his own conclusions.

I wasn’t so narrow-minded as to dismiss the earnest answer of my dear adopted
younger brother.

“They probably wanted ‘equality,’” Orca said.

“No discrimination, you mean?” I clarified.

“Yeah. They talked about destroying the noble society, but at the core, I think
it was resentment toward discrimination against non-attribute holders and
beastmen. That resentment got twisted under various pressures and ended up like
that,” he explained.

“I see,” I replied.

It wasn’t a perfect argument, but it was convincing enough. Wanting to do
something about the discrimination they faced—a natural feeling that, through
twists and turns, became distorted. It was a plausible scenario.

“And so,” Orca continued, “I want to take action to eliminate discrimination
against non-attribute holders and beastmen. I want to improve society so people
like Eclara don’t appear again.”

His eyes shone dazzlingly as he spoke with conviction. There was none of the
despair or grief from the original story—only hope, expectation, and
determination.

“Changing societal norms won’t be easy,” I warned.

“I know. It might not even be achievable in my generation. But I could lay the
foundation for a new era,” he countered.

“You can’t make a living just fighting for equality,” I pointed out.

“I’ll handle my work properly, don’t worry. I did say we’d be together forever,”
he replied with a grin.

“You might get criticized,” I pressed.

“It’s fine. I’ve got Zex-nii and everyone else with me,” he said confidently.

I threw questions to test his resolve, but he answered each one clearly. His
eyes told me he wasn’t making a half-hearted decision.

I shrugged. “Alright. I’ll support you fully.”

“Eh? You don’t have to go that far,” Orca said, eyes widening.

It seemed he’d only intended to declare his resolve. Heh, he underestimated me.

Chuckling, I said, “Don’t be silly. What kind of brother doesn’t support his
younger brother? Besides, I’m a non-attribute holder myself. I’ll gladly help.”

“Zex-nii… thank you\!” Orca exclaimed, his cheeks flushing as he beamed with
gratitude. He looked every bit like a lovestruck maiden. Well, despite being a
guy.

“…” I faltered, momentarily speechless at his overwhelming cuteness.

…Maybe my heart would fall sooner than I thought.

After that, we discussed plans for the future. For now, we’d focus on gathering
information until graduation, when we could start moving in earnest.

“Let’s do this\!” Orca declared, brimming with enthusiasm.

I couldn’t help but smile naturally at his energy.
