# Interludes

## Gruewen: Observing from the Sidelines

Timeline: In-between "Encounter"

---

In a secret room within the royal castle, concealed by my power—Gruewen’s—no one
can set foot here without my permission.

A loud voice echoed through the space.

“What are you doing?\!” the boy shouted.

The boy, with hibiscus-colored hair and eyes, was visibly furious.

“What’s with you?” I replied lazily, wincing at the ringing in my ears. “You
don’t have to yell; I can hear you just fine. Honestly, what did I even do?”

My attitude seemed to rub him the wrong way, as it only fueled his anger
further.

“All you’ve done is make things worse\!” he snapped. “I told you not to meet
that monster, didn’t I? And yet you went and fought him before the words even
settled\! What are you going to do about this?\!”

“Ugh, so noisy,” I muttered.

Even I, as formidable as I am, was taken aback by the boy’s shouting, his voice
breaking with intensity. I covered my ears with both hands, trying to block out
his irritating tirade. No matter how strong I am, I can’t use soundproofing
magic. Well, technically I can, but it turns into a barrier that blocks more
than just sound.

So, that’s what he’s mad about. His phrase “that monster” finally clued me in.

The so-called monster, Zex-shonen, is the older brother of my vessel candidate.
He’s been thwarting every plan my subordinates have set in motion, and he’s
someone this noisy boy is wary of.

To think I’d stay quiet after hearing about him is absurd. Everyone under me
knows I’m a bundle of curiosity.

…Oh, right. It’s only been half a year since I met this boy, hasn’t it? That
explains it.

“Sorry, I forgot,” I said casually.

“What—” he started to retort.

I gave the loudmouthed boy a flick on the forehead with my index finger.

For a brief moment, my fingertip glowed, and before he knew it, he fell silent.
His eyes glazed over, staring blankly into space.

I should’ve done this sooner. He’s been acting so familiar that I mistook him
for a long-time accomplice. My perception-infiltrating magic is convenient, but
it’s a hassle in moments like this. If I could properly remember my
relationships with others, I wouldn’t struggle so much. But, well, I’m barely
interested in anyone but myself.

“What should I do with this kid?” I mused aloud.

He was a sacrifice painstakingly crafted by my subordinates over time. He had
the potential to grow into a splendid king, but that was ruined by the
deep-seated hatred instilled in him—a pitiful existence.

I’d love to just toss him aside, but that feels wasteful. Hatred strong enough
to wish for a nation’s destruction isn’t easy to come by. Using him would make
revival easy, so I don’t want to discard him carelessly.

That said, I don’t want to keep such a loudmouth around forever. Hmm.

“Honestly, he’s got no use beyond being a sacrifice,” I muttered.

Thanks to his scheming, the continent’s curses have certainly increased, which
is a plus. The chaos caused by the research he abandoned is delightful.

But he’s out of moves now. Of the three research projects, one has been crushed,
and another is exposed. He can’t reclaim the research, and starting something
new is impossible with so many eyes watching.

In short, he’s checkmated. He can’t move forward, backward, left, right, up, or
down—just stand there. If he forces a move, he’ll be caught by the very monster
he fears.

“Yup, it’s time to part ways,” I concluded.

Thinking it over, staying with him is like boarding a sinking ship and going
down with it. I don’t have to stick with him forever. I can just use him when
the time comes.

Besides, if I want to scheme, I’d rather rely on _her_. She’s someone I recently
met—sharp as a tack. Her light is too weak to be a vessel, but as an accomplice,
there’s no one better.

No matter how I look at it, I should cut ties with this boy.

In that case, let’s get to work.

“\[Light, I am dazzling, leaving not a single shadow in my wake\],” I chanted,
flicking the still-dazed boy’s forehead again, casting a spell to erase his
perception of me. Instantly, he collapsed to the floor.

When he wakes, he won’t remember a thing about me.

“\[Light Return\],” I intoned.

With teleportation magic, I moved his body, and the cleanup was complete. Now,
_she_ is the only one who knows of me.

A giggle escaped my lips. “Heh heh heh.”

It’s a perfect job, if I do say so myself. No one but me could pull this off. As
expected of the greatest magic Administrator\!

I’d love to keep this momentum and sail toward revival, but—

“For now, I should lay low,” I admitted.

It’s not that I’m heeding the boy’s advice, but Zex-shonen’s movements are
concerning. He’s the only human who recognized me on his own.

I thought only Caroline, with her equal radiance, could bypass my perception
inhibition. Yet, he barged right into that territory. And, though I was caught
off guard, he managed to repel me once.

His type always has some hidden strategy. Or maybe, after our encounter, he’s
crafted a trump card.

I know from when I was sealed that people like him devise plans. So, for now,
it’s best to observe.

Being a background player doesn’t suit my radiant self, but I’m already sealed,
so what’s the difference? I’ll leave the tedious tasks to my subordinates and
wait patiently until the last moment. …Oh, maybe developing a new spell would be
fun.

Compared to the centuries I spent sealed, this is nothing. I doubt there’s even
a year left. I can endure that much.

“Just wait, world that denied me,” I murmured. “This time, I’ll deny you and dye
you in gold.”

## Tara: The Boy’s Heart

The final exams were over, with only the results announcement left. The students
waiting in the classroom were all buzzing with excitement. Naturally, I—Tara—was
among them.

Unlike my classmates, who were chatting noisily, I was alone.

Being a commoner yet the top student in my grade made my situation complicated.
Nobles were jealous, thinking, “How dare a commoner,” while commoners stayed
away, fearing that jealousy. As a result, I was stuck enjoying a lonely life.
Until recently, Eclara-san had been kind enough to talk to me, but she hasn’t
shown up at the academy lately.

I’m probably going to keep my top spot this term too. I felt confident about
that.

Given my situation, I wondered if I should’ve held back a bit. But in the end, I
gave it my all. I couldn’t face the people who raised me if I slacked off.

Thanks to Caron-chan and Zex-san teaching me, I am who I am today. I can’t
forget that debt.

—Yes, I learned magic from Zex-san too. My main teacher was Caron-chan, like my
older brothers, but I secretly learned from Zex-san as well.

What he taught me was mental magic. Apparently, he wanted a sample case for
teaching it to others.

Since it’s mental magic, he couldn’t just pick anyone—he needed someone
trustworthy with self-control.

Being chosen was both flattering and a lot of pressure. Knowing the details of
mental magic now, my stomach churns even more.

That said, due to my aptitude, I can only use a fraction of mental magic. Things
like calming agitation or slightly reading others’ emotions. Compared to the
depth of mental magic, I’ve only scratched the surface.

…Maybe that’s for the best, since mastering it all might burden me with
unnecessary trouble.

I mean, Zex-san, who uses emotion-reading magic constantly, is a monster. Even
for me, who can only use it a little, keeping it active makes me feel sick. It’s
like staring at a canvas splattered with countless colors of paint. He says it’s
just a matter of “getting used to it,” but that guy is way too extraordinary.

“Tara-san,” a voice called.

“Your Highness, is something up?” I replied, tilting my head.

It was Negro, the third prince, speaking to me out of the blue, with two student
guards behind him.

By the way, Negro-dono and I are fairly close. Being the top and second-ranked
students, we’ve had plenty of chances to talk. He asked me to treat him casually
at school, so I can speak somewhat informally without his guards complaining.

“Just curious about your thoughts before the results are announced,” Negro-dono
said, shrugging. “We’re rivals, after all.”

I could tell from his emotional flow that he meant it sincerely.

Being seen as a rival by a prince was flattering but also heavy. It’s
strange—Zex-san’s expectations feel easy to accept, but not Negro-dono’s.

Still, I knew from experience that denying the rival comment wouldn’t move the
conversation forward. Brushing it off, I responded, “I’m a bit nervous, but
mostly the same as always. I did everything I could.”

Some might find that arrogant, but it can’t be helped. After Foranada-style
training, humility isn’t an option. And to think that was the simplified
version—terrifying.

As expected, the two guards frowned in displeasure.

But Negro-dono just laughed brightly. “Haha, as expected of the top student\! I
envy that confidence.”

“It’s less confidence and more just facts,” I said. “With everyone around me
being so incredible, I can’t overestimate or underestimate myself.”

“Oh, Foranada,” Negro-dono said, mirroring my wry smile.

It’s almost comical how that single word creates a shared understanding. It’s
all because of Zex-san and the others’ deeds—hard to put into words.

I always knew they were amazing, but I never imagined they were world-class. As
an ordinary citizen, the scale is so grand I can only laugh.

Negro-dono’s expression grew more bitter as he continued, “I totally get the
‘everyone around me is incredible’ part. I’m in a similar spot.”

“Really?” I asked.

“Yup,” he replied. “Magic? That’s Wiemlay-nii-ue’s domain. Brains?
Arianorth-nee-ue’s got that covered. Combat? Gray-dono excels there. I’m just a
jack-of-all-trades, master of none.”

“I wouldn’t call you a master of none,” I said, faltering.

Honestly, Negro-dono’s abilities are well above average. He’s closer to an
all-rounder than a jack-of-all-trades. Every skill he has is above par.

Still, being called “mediocre” must stem from his siblings’ overwhelming
talents. In that sense, I understood his comment about similar circumstances.

But there was a key difference.

Without Zex-san and the others, I wouldn’t have my current strength. So, I’d
never harbor negative feelings like he does.

—I’ve sensed it since we met. Negro-dono always carries a smoldering resentment
in his heart, a murky, dark emotion.

With my limited ability to read emotions, I can’t see its true nature clearly.
But I know it’s negative. His public smiles are a facade, masking something
deeper.

I hope, someday, Negro-dono finds peace in his heart.

When he talks to me, he seems to genuinely enjoy the moment. It feels like an
honest emotion, untainted by pretense.

To me, his smiling face looks like that of an ordinary boy.

## Teria: What’s Lacking

I—Teria—and Maron dragged ourselves to the dining hall, utterly exhausted, and
collapsed into a seat with a heavy thud. Slumping face-first onto the table was
a breach of etiquette, but right now, I didn’t have the energy to move a single
finger.

“Good work, you two\!” Garuna called out cheerfully. “I brought dinner for ya\!”

Our friend Garuna, who approached our weary selves, held trays of food in both
hands. True to her words, she’d skillfully carried portions for all three of us.

Without waiting for a reply, she deftly set out the dishes. The fragrant aroma
rising from the food tickled my nose, stirring my empty stomach.

Faced with the exquisite cuisine provided by Foranada’s chefs, exhaustion was no
excuse. My body moved on its own, and before I knew it, my hands were clasped
together.

““Itadakimasu,”” Maron and I said in unison.

Apparently, Maron felt the same, her voice echoing mine from beside me.

“Here’s mine: Itadakimasu\!” Garuna chimed in, smiling as she watched us before
starting her own meal.

We ate in silence for a while, focusing on our chopsticks, until Garuna suddenly
spoke up.

“Man, that lecture was looong, huh?” she remarked.

Indeed. Maron and I had been enduring a lecture from Shion-senpai until just
moments ago.

The reason? During today’s assault mission in the Kashiyo Kingdom, we—supposed
to stay by Zex-sama’s side—were left behind.

Honestly, I wanted to argue that there was no way we could keep up with
Zex-sama, who vanished in an instant. But someone _did_ manage to follow him, so
that excuse didn’t hold. According to Shion-senpai, the difference in raw specs
was irrelevant, leaving us no room for justification.

“How did you keep up with Zex-sama, Garuna?” I asked, seizing the opportunity.

“Oh, I wanna know that too\!” Maron added eagerly, clearly sharing my curiosity.

Garuna tilted her head, looking puzzled. “Keep up? I just kept my eyes on
Zex-sama the whole time, that’s all.”

“That’s it?” I pressed.

Watching Zex-sama was something Maron and I had done too. After all, for this
strategy, we were assigned as his attendants in place of Shion-senpai.

I asked if there was another reason, but Garuna insisted there wasn’t.

“Hmm,” I murmured, pondering. Was the difference because she’s a Magic
Administrator? But Shion-senpai said that wasn’t it…

Lost in thought, my hand stilled over my meal, when Maron suddenly spoke up.

“Ohhh, I get it\!” she exclaimed.

“Have you figured out the reason, Maron?” I asked, a bit flustered.

“It’s simple\!” she replied breezily. “Garuna was only looking at Zex-sama,
right?”

“We were too… Wait, only?” I said, catching on to her phrasing.

“Exactly\!” Garuna confirmed, nodding enthusiastically.

Finally, it clicked for me.

“You mean you didn’t look at anything but Zex-sama?” I asked, incredulous.

“Yup\!” Garuna replied. “Otherwise, there’s no way I could keep up with that
kind of high-speed movement.”

“Wha—?\!” I let out a stunned yelp.

I couldn’t help it. We were also tasked with guarding, yet Garuna hadn’t been on
alert at all\!

When I pointed this out, she answered casually, “I left perimeter watch to
detection magic, obviously. Even without it, I’m confident I could guard just
fine.”

It was an irrefutable point. With our skills, staring at Zex-sama with our naked
eyes wouldn’t cause any issues.

No, wait—

“Isn’t it necessary to confirm with other methods?” I protested.

“If you’ve got the bandwidth, sure,” Garuna said. “But if you overdo it and get
left behind, that’s putting the cart before the horse, right?”

“Guh,” I muttered, unable to retort.

To think I’d be outdone by Garuna… Have I grown senile?

“I feel like I’m being totally mocked\!” Garuna whined.

“That’s what you get for your usual behavior,” Maron teased.

“That’s unfair\!” Garuna huffed.

She was lamenting, but I decided to let it slide. It’s just how she is.

More importantly, there was something critical to address.

“We need to get stronger,” I declared.

I finally understood the true meaning behind Zex-sama calling this a training
exercise. Compared to Shion-senpai, the scope of what we can do is far too
small. We need to train relentlessly to address our shortcomings.

As I quietly burned with determination, Garuna said with a hint of exasperation,
“It’s great to be motivated, but don’t let it mess with your regular duties,
okay?”

“Of course,” I replied firmly.

No need to point it out. Unlike Garuna, I’ll handle my work properly while
training.

“I’m being mocked again, aren’t I?” Garuna grumbled.

“You’re always so unserious,” Maron said, smirking.

“I’ve got nothing to say to that\!” Garuna admitted, deflated.

“Oh, come on,” I said. “I’m not saying you need to be as serious as me, but a
little more diligence wouldn’t hurt.”

“I’ll… try,” Garuna muttered.

Ugh, that’s the response of someone who won’t follow through.

Oh well. Kicking her into gear is part of my job.

“Heh,” I chuckled softly.

“What’s up?” Garuna asked.

“Something funny?” Maron added.

“No, it’s nothing,” I replied, waving it off.

Oops, I let a giggle slip.

A few years ago, I was just an ordinary commoner. Now, I’m in a position to prod
a Magic Administrator—one of the strongest beings—into action. I couldn’t help
but laugh at the absurdity, but I’d never say it aloud. Garuna would definitely
sulk.

Life is unpredictable, especially here in Foranada.

With that thought, I turned my attention back to the rest of my dinner.

## Minerva: The Path to Aim For

_Thud_.

In the training grounds beneath Foranada Castle, the sound of two things hitting
the ground echoed.

No need to ask what they were. One was me—Minerva—collapsing, and the other was
Caroline doing the same.

We’d just finished a mock battle.

“A draw again today,” Caroline said, her voice tinged with frustration.

“Indeed,” I agreed, equally bitter.

Our mana was nearly depleted, but the regret of not settling the score added to
the sourness in our voices.

That makes 959 wins, 959 losses, and 1,143 draws. For eight years, Caroline and
I have been competing in some form or another, but lately, the score hasn’t
budged. I desperately want to settle things with her, but our skills are too
evenly matched.

Facing Caroline, who’s top-tier in both offense and defense, my diverse
techniques are at a disadvantage. She can brute-force her way to victory, so the
best I can do is force a draw. They call her clumsy or a firepower fanatic, but
she’s a well-balanced mage. And she’s strong in close combat too—what a handful.

Not that I’ll let her win easily. If I can’t break through head-on, I’ll use
tricks. Today, I landed a brilliant counter to knock her out. Her firepower was
too much to fully deflect, though.

Honestly, her strength is ridiculous. How does she unleash magic with that kind
of power? It’s baffling.

—Enough complaining.

“Let’s do the post-match analysis,” I said.

“Got it,” Caroline replied.

Lying flat on our backs, we sat up almost simultaneously.

Ugh, why do we sync like that? It’s why Zex and the others tease us about being
too alike…

Still, complaining here would stall the conversation. I’m not foolish enough to
repeat mistakes I’ve learned from.

For the next hour or so, Caroline and I exchanged thoughts on our tactics.

“Look, stop being stubborn and hand it over,” I said, exasperated. “If your hair
gets damaged, Zex will hate you.”

“…Fine,” Caroline relented.

After washing off sweat in the training ground’s shower, I was drying Caroline’s
hair.

Why? Because her drying technique is sloppy. If she leaves it half-wet, her
beautiful golden locks will get damaged. Honestly, she’s so careless about these
things.

For the record, I use magic to style my hair perfectly, so there’s no slacking
on my part. Having mastery over five attributes is handy in times like this.
Others might call it a lavish use of magic, but whatever.

Using a towel, comb, and magic, I carefully dried Caroline’s hair.

“Why am I doing this?” I thought briefly, but seeing her contentedly narrow her
eyes made me think, “Oh well.” It’s strange how that happens. Is this the power
of the “Sunlight Saintess”? Some call her a “Demon King” these days, but her
essence is all about healing.

Caroline’s long, slightly wavy hair takes time to care for. Not wanting to spend
that time in silence, I struck up a conversation.

“What’s your plan after this?” I asked.

“Reading picture books at the orphanage,” she replied.

“Oh, right, you visit the orphanage,” I said, nodding.

I’d heard that Zex, Caroline, and Shion regularly stop by the orphanage.
Apparently, Caroline goes more often than the other two.

“You must have a lot of motivation to keep that up,” I remarked. “I couldn’t do
it.”

“Really?” she said. “The kids are adorable.”

“Honest kids are fine, but some are little troublemakers, aren’t they?”

It’s not that I hate kids, but rowdy ones aren’t my forte. If they were my own,
maybe I’d feel differently, but I can’t muster unconditional love for orphans.

In that sense, I respect Caroline. She acts with a truly saintly heart.

She gave a wry smile. “Haha, sure, there are some naughty ones. Boys around four
to six can be a handful. Some are precocious, too.”

“Precocious?” I asked.

“They try to touch my chest or butt,” she said.

“Oh…” I muttered.

Well, she _is_ unnecessarily endowed. That’s a sentiment I can’t relate to.

“My body belongs to Onii-sama, so I absolutely don’t let them touch it\!” she
declared.

“Don’t overreact to kids,” I cautioned.

“What do you take me for?” she huffed.

“A brocon,” I shot back.

“I can’t deny that,” she admitted, “but I wouldn’t raise a hand to kids. I just
dodge their attempts and scold them.”

“That’s fine, then,” I said.

Still, ever since she acknowledged her feelings, she’s been prone to going
overboard. She was even planning to sell a Zex photo collection the other day.

Let’s table that for now. Someone can check on it later.

“I heard you teach magic to some of the kids. Is that true?” I asked.

“Yes, it’s true,” she confirmed. “I teach the basics to those who want to
learn.”

“What’s it like?”

“What’s it like? Well, it’s lively,” she said. “They get so excited just from
successfully casting a basic fireball or stone ball. It’s refreshing and
rewarding.”

Her soft smile as she spoke showed she genuinely enjoyed interacting with the
kids.

A thought struck me.

“Are you aiming to be a teacher after graduation?” I asked.

She loves taking care of kids and seems to enjoy teaching. Being an orphanage or
elementary school teacher might be her calling. It was a spur-of-the-moment
idea, but it felt like a good fit.

Caroline tilted her head. “A teacher?”

“Not clicking?” I asked.

“No, I just never thought about it,” she said. “Honestly, I haven’t thought
about what to do after graduation at all.”

I found her stunned reaction a bit odd but reconsidered—most students don’t
think about their future until their third year.

“Well, you’re only halfway through your second year, but it doesn’t hurt to
think ahead,” I advised.

“Have you already decided your path, Minerva?” she asked.

I nodded. “Yes, I’m going into magic research. I’ve talked with Zex, and I’ll
likely work at the new research institute Foranada’s setting up.”

As the future head wife of Foranada, I technically don’t need a job.

But Zex suggested I pursue what I want. If it’s out of love, I can’t say no.
It’s a bit embarrassing, though.

Hearing my answer, Caroline gazed into space, deep in thought.

“I see…” she murmured. “Orca was saying recently that he’s decided on his dream
too. I suppose I should figure something out as well.”

“No need to rush, but having a dream or goal definitely gives life more
purpose,” I said.

“You’re right,” she agreed. “I’ll think it over carefully.”

“Good idea,” I said. “And… done drying.”

The conversation wrapped up just as I finished. Heh, nice work if I do say so
myself. Caroline’s blonde hair looked even more radiant now.

She ran her fingers through it, smiling softly. “Thank you. I could never do it
this well myself.”

“You’re welcome,” I replied. “It’s nice to be praised, but you really should
learn to do it yourself.”

“…I’ll try,” she said.

“That’s the ‘I won’t do it’ response, isn’t it?” I said, exasperated.

We both burst out laughing.

It felt so… youthful.

A moment I thought I’d never have, given my position, has enriched my life.

Together with my future husband, I want to protect this time dearly.

## Skia: Confidence

It happened right after the summer break began. I—Skia—was diligently hauling a
mountain of books from the Foranada library when it occurred.

“Got plans tomorrow?” Nina-san asked abruptly.

“N-no,” I stammered.

“Then, meet me at ten tomorrow morning in front of Foranada’s castle gate. We’re
going out to have fun,” she declared.

With that, Nina-san said what she wanted and left just as suddenly as she’d
appeared.

I was so stunned by the abruptness that it took me a full minute to snap out of
it.

“…Huh?” I muttered.

But it was too late. Nina-san was already gone.

In the end, I couldn’t catch up with her afterward, and since I really didn’t
have any plans, our outing for tomorrow was set in stone.

---

The next day, as promised, I headed to the castle gate and found Nina-san
already there. She wore a light cream-colored shirt paired with high-waisted
black tailored shorts—a look that exuded both freshness and vitality. Combined
with her tall frame, she looked incredibly cool.

_Wow, she’s really dressed up…_ I thought.

It’s a bit rude to admit, but I was a little surprised. My image of Nina-san was
always of someone fighting, so I hadn’t expected her to dress so stylishly.

That said, I’m in no position to judge. My outfit today was just a casually
chosen cut-and-sew top and a flare skirt. Wait, is it embarrassing to stand next
to her like this?

A wave of shame hit me over my appearance, but it was too late. The moment I
started panicking, Nina-san noticed me. Our eyes locked perfectly.

With no other choice, I approached her.

“S-sorry to keep you waiting,” I said nervously.

“No problem,” she replied calmly. “I just got here myself. Plus, you’re early.”

Reading her words alone, it felt like a lovers’ rendezvous. But with Nina-san’s
expressionless, matter-of-fact tone, it was anything but.

“Let’s go, then,” she said.

“Y-yes,” I agreed.

Following Nina-san’s lead, we headed into Foranada’s castle town. I wondered
what awaited me today. My stomach churned a bit.

The outing with Nina-san was more ordinary than I’d expected. We shopped in the
market, ate street food, and visited the capital’s landmarks. It was so typical
that I felt silly for bracing myself.

Well, along the way, Nina-san was constantly stopped by people. Considering her
beauty and fame as a titled adventurer, it made sense. Standing next to her,
with my messy hair, I felt a bit sorry for being so out of place.

But why did Nina-san invite _me_? She could’ve gone with someone less gloomy and
introverted. Someone like her best friend Caroline-san, her disciple Yurika-san…
or even gone on a date with Zex-sama.

My questions lingered unanswered, and in the end, I couldn’t help but ask during
our final stop—a meal at a diner Nina-san recommended.

She tilted her head, looking puzzled. “You get close to people you want to be
friends with. Isn’t that obvious?”

“F-friends? W-with me?” I stammered.

“Who else?” she replied bluntly.

I was floored by her unexpected words, and she continued in her usual
straightforward tone.

I understood what she meant, but I hadn’t expected such a direct reason.

I mean, I’m gloomy, tongue-tied, sloppy in appearance, and kind of a mess. Until
recently, I couldn’t even use magic—a major flaw. I don’t have much going for
me. Maybe my figure and looks are decent? But even that’s not something I can
boast about.

It’s only natural I wouldn’t expect someone to want to be friends with a failure
like me.

As I floundered, Nina-san spoke again. “I think you should have more confidence,
Skia. Wanting to be independent for your family is admirable, and keeping up
with Foranada’s grueling training is impressive. As for your magic skills, even
Caron and Minerva have praised you.”

“Wanting to be independent feels like a normal thing… Wait, those two praised
me?” I asked, shocked.

Caroline-sama, who drags me to the clinic every weekend for intense training,
and Minerva-sama, who relentlessly drills me in dark magic—I thought they saw me
as a hopeless student. Could it really be the opposite?

Noticing my reaction, Nina-san let out a small sigh. “Caron aside, Minerva’s
terrible at teaching. I’ll talk to her later.”

“N-no, you don’t have to go that far,” I protested.

“No, it’s necessary,” she insisted. “This kind of thing needs to be addressed,
or it’ll cause problems later. Minerva will keep teaching others, and not
praising her students is a fatal flaw. It needs fixing now.”

“That’s… true,” I conceded.

I thought it wasn’t worth mentioning, but she shut me down easily.

Nina-san was right. I only felt “this is tough, sorry for being so bad,” but
others might break under Minerva-sama’s strict, logical teaching style.

One thing still bothered me, though. “B-by the way, is it okay to leave
Caroline-sama out of this?”

Her training is just as strict, but Nina-san hadn’t mentioned her.

She gave a wry smile. “Caron takes on teaching roles elsewhere and gets good
reviews. It’s probably because light magic training is so strict.”

“What’s the connection?” I asked.

“Just a guess,” she said, “but light magic is mainly for healing. It’s a role
that handles others’ lives, so she can’t afford to compromise.”

“Oh,” I said, understanding dawning.

During light magic training, Caroline-sama always scolded, “You are the last
line of defense.” It meant light mages are the final hope for patients.

Thinking calmly, it made no sense for kind-hearted Caroline-sama to be
needlessly harsh. Minerva-sama is kind too, but she’s not honest about it.

As I smiled wryly to myself, Nina-san continued, “So, you should be confident,
Skia. You’re kind, and you have magical talent. We guarantee it.”

“Th-thank you,” I said.

Her gaze was so earnest that saying anything but thanks wasn’t an option.

Come to think of it, Zex-sama once said Nina-san is purer than anyone. I get it
now. Someone this genuinely straightforward is rare, especially considering her
past. It’s remarkable she stayed so untainted.

Nina-san went on, “You get along with everyone else, so I didn’t want to miss
this chance during summer break.”

“I-I get along with them?” I asked, tilting my head.

“You train with Caron and Minerva, chat often with Orca and Marina, work with
Shion and the servants, and talk a lot with Zex,” she said, counting off on her
fingers.

Now that she mentioned it, for someone as bad at socializing as me, I guess I am
getting along with them? But it’s mostly because they approach me first.

“W-with Orca-kyu…sama and Marina, it’s because they’re so outgoing,” I said.

Those two are so radiant, the complete opposite of me. They embody “light.”

Nina-san gave a cryptic smile. “Really? Well, that’s why I want to be friends
with you. We’ve got similar tastes, so I think it’ll work out. Here’s to more in
the future.”

“Y-yes, please take care of me,” I replied, startled by her casual words.

_Did she notice? No way. Calm down, Skia. It’s probably just a bluff_.

After that, we finished dinner uneventfully and headed home.

It was a nerve-wracking but meaningful day. I learned Nina-san has a playful
side.

But ugh, the grueling training starts again tomorrow.

**That alone felt so daunting.**

## Nina: The Reason to Keep Going

“I-I can’t do this anymore,” Yurika gasped, collapsing to the ground.

Sprawled out and whining was my friend and disciple, Yurika. Her adorable rabbit
ears and glossy dew-colored hair are her charm points, but they were covered in
dirt now, halving her usual appeal.

Why was she lying there, sweaty and battered? Because she’d been going through
my training. I had her register with the Adventurers’ Guild and take on a
request, but combining that with basic training was apparently too much.

Well, I expected this. I went through the same thing. If Yurika had been
unfazed, _my_ spirit would’ve broken.

I don’t like pushing harsh training, but Yurika was determined, so I steeled
myself and put her through the wringer. Despite the hardship, she’s holding
strong.

I couldn’t let her stay sprawled on the ground forever, so I called out,
“Yurika, get up. We’re going to report the request’s completion to the guild.”

“Just… a little break…” she pleaded.

“No way,” I said firmly. “The sun’s setting. It’s easier to get attacked by
magical beasts in the dark. Move it.”

I shook her shoulders, urging her to stand.

Tearing up, she grumbled, “You’re a demon\!”

“Hmph,” I huffed. “I’ll accept ‘ogre,’ but ‘demon’ is too far. This is still
easier than Zex’s training, you know.”

I can say with certainty that “demon” training refers to what _he_ puts people
through. Compared to that, I’m just an ogre.

As I recalled those old training days, my mind drifting, Yurika’s voice
trembled. “Is the earl _that_ harsh?”

“…Want to hear about it?” I asked.

“Uh, I’ll pass,” she said quickly.

Good call. In her exhausted state, just hearing about it might make her faint.

Thinking back, I’m amazed I survived Zex’s training. I know he tailored the
curriculum to push us to our limits, but it’s still overwhelming to reflect on.
It was _that_ brutal.

“Sorry for the wait,” Yurika said, finally managing to stand.

I smiled. “Let’s head back, then.”

“Yes\!” she replied enthusiastically.

I felt a bit bad for her excitement about returning, because more basic training
awaited her.

---

Having completed today’s quota, I brought Yurika to the tavern attached to the
Adventurers’ Guild for dinner.

I’d planned to go to my favorite diner, but training ran long, and we missed
their last order. So, as a compromise, we changed plans to the guild’s tavern.

It’s not as good as the diner, but the food here is decent enough, so I had no
complaints. Yurika looked disappointed, but she knew it was her fault and didn’t
voice any further discontent.

We lucked out with an open table for two and sat down, quickly calling over a
server. I ordered a balanced mix of meat and vegetables—extra portions, since
we’d just trained.

With time to kill until the food arrived, we started chatting. The tavern was
noisy, but not so much that we couldn’t talk.

“How was your first request?” I asked.

“Exhausting…” Yurika sighed, her long ears drooping, clearly showing her
fatigue.

Then she shot me a half-lidded glance. “Isn’t taking on a pack of Gray Wolves
for a first request a bit too tough? I thought you’d start with a single
target.”

I understood her point. A newbie charging into a pack of magical beasts sounds
insane.

But there was a reason. “They explained it already, didn’t they? You’re in the
top ten at the academy and completed dungeon training. That’s why you started at
Rank D. You passed the temporary practical exam too.”

That’s right. Yurika skipped Rank F and went straight to Rank D. Probably
because the guild doesn’t want to lose top academy performers. Adventurers are
usually a gathering of dropouts, after all.

Of course, it wasn’t just her grades. She faced a Rank B adventurer in a mock
battle and was deemed capable. They even said she could handle Rank B.

That’s why her first request was “Defeat a pack of Gray Wolves.” She ventured
deep into the forest and took on about sixty of them. I restricted her magic
use, so it was quite a struggle.

“If you’re limiting my magic, I’d have preferred fewer enemies,” she said,
teary-eyed.

“You came out unscathed,” I pointed out.

“That’s true, but…” she trailed off.

“No change in plan,” I said. “Keep it up.”

“Waaah\!” she wailed, collapsing onto the table in mock tears.

She’s probably just being dramatic, but she knows it.

Yurika’s pretty resilient. She’s sensitive, sure, but she’s got this odd grit.
She doesn’t flinch even in front of Zex, a noble head, which says a lot. If she
were truly timid, she wouldn’t have chosen to be an infighter. No matter how
talented, diving into an enemy’s range takes courage. Her natural toughness
shines through there.

Speaking of which—

“If you’ve got energy for jokes, maybe I should make it tougher?” I teased.

She’s still got some pep left, even after training. Maybe I went too easy?

She shot up, shouting, “Please, spare me\!”

Her desperate tone was so loud it silenced the noisy adventurers around us.

“Just kidding,” I said, raising my hands to calm her.

“That was a mean joke\!” she huffed, puffing out her cheeks.

“Sorry,” I apologized.

She really didn’t want extra training. I’d probably refuse just as hard in her
shoes. My bad.

Being in the mentor role makes me realize how amazing Zex is. I thought his
training was insane, but he knew our limits perfectly. His observational skills
are unreal.

“Here’s your order\!” the server announced.

“Let’s eat,” I said.

“Yes, I’m starving\!” Yurika exclaimed.

The food arrived at the perfect moment, so we paused the conversation.

Chatting while eating is fun, but I knew from experience that Yurika, exhausted
from training, wouldn’t have the energy. Sure enough, she started wolfing down
her food.

Watching her eat with such gusto, I turned my thoughts to a lingering question.

Why does Yurika push herself so hard?

She’s said it’s for her friends, but I’m skeptical that’s the whole story.

Having gone through similar training, I know it takes strong conviction to keep
going. I survived because I didn’t want to die—that’s my pride.

“For friends” isn’t a weak reason, but Yurika’s desperation feels too intense,
almost like my own life-or-death seriousness back then.

“Why do you try so hard?” I murmured under my breath.

If I asked, she’d probably just say, “To help my friends.” That’s all she said
last time.

Unconscious desperation. A kind of curse.

Zex seems to think she’s grappling with some issue. It’s probably tied to this.

I want to help her. I feel that strongly.

But for now, I’ll hold back. Zex warned that carelessly touching unconscious
issues could make things worse. I need to wait for the right moment.

Someday, I believe this kind-hearted friend will be saved. Until then, I’ll keep
training her.

## Shion: Mental Magic

It was during a routine mock battle that I—Shion—noticed it. While teamed up
with Marina-san against Zex-sama, I felt a faint sense of unease.

What was off? I sharpened my senses, trying to pinpoint it while staying focused
on the fight.

But all I could do was tilt my head in confusion.

Aside from that vague unease, nothing seemed out of the ordinary. As usual, my
invisibility was easily seen through, Marina-san and Esi-chan’s firepower was
deflected, my flurry of techniques was thoroughly crushed, and Marina and
Maimu-chan’s detection was forcefully overwhelmed.

In short, Marina-san and I were completely outmatched and toyed with by
Zex-sama. It was the same old scene.

It’s disrespectful to say, but Zex-sama fights with absurd movements. He uses my
magical barrages as stepping stones to dodge, and he creates decoys to counter
Marina-san’s detection. His methods make sense in theory, but they’re the kind
that make you think, _If you could pull that off, no one would ever struggle._

In the end, the mock battle only reaffirmed Zex-sama’s extraordinary nature, and
I couldn’t identify the source of my unease.

“Shion’s the second one, huh?” Zex-sama remarked.

Since pondering wouldn’t yield answers, I decided to ask Zex-sama directly after
training, and that was his response.

As I frowned in puzzlement, a barrier formed around us. I’d taken the precaution
of asking in Zex-sama’s private chambers, where prying ears and eyes were
unlikely, but it seemed that wasn’t enough. It must be highly sensitive
information. Or perhaps not _that_ sensitive, since he didn’t use \[Alternate
World: Baule de Tesoro\]?

While I speculated, Zex-sama likely finished scanning the surroundings.
“Alright,” he said, finally getting to the point.

“I think the unease you felt is due to a spell I keep active at all times,” he
explained.

“Like detection magic or \[Physical Enhancement\]?” I asked.

“Exactly,” he confirmed. “Besides those two, there’s another spell I keep active
constantly. Though I usually limit the range of the detection magic.”

His self-deprecating tone aside, what Zex-sama was doing was abnormal.

As someone trained in \[Physical Enhancement\], I understand it well. We trained
to maintain it constantly, but even then, the effect is weaker outside combat.
Even Nina-san, the most skilled in \[Physical Enhancement\], says she can only
manage half her combat boost—five times normal—at most in daily life. Yet
Zex-sama maintains a full tenfold boost _and_ uses detection magic
simultaneously.

And now, if his words are true, it seems he’s not just maintaining two spells
but several. His phrasing suggests it’s more than three. Just how much mana and
control does he have? Zex-sama’s depths are unfathomable.

I gave a dry smile and asked, “What kind of magic are you using?”

Since he brought it up, I doubted he’d be coy and refuse to answer.

As expected, Zex-sama answered without hesitation. “Simply put, it’s a weakening
debuff.”

“A weakening debuff?” I echoed.

His answer caught me off guard. In combat, I’m hyper-vigilant about status
ailments, so I’d naturally checked for weakening debuffs. The result goes
without saying.

Perhaps sensing my thoughts, Zex-sama shrugged. “Hard to believe, right?”

“I-I’m terribly sorry,” I stammered, bowing hastily.

Doubting my lord’s words was unforgivable.

I’d tried to hide my skepticism, but it was futile against Zex-sama. I realized
my mistake.

He waved a hand casually. “It’s fine, I don’t mind. In fact, that reaction is
helpful.”

“What do you mean?” I asked.

“I’m also using a weakening debuff that makes it hard to recognize weakening
debuffs,” he explained. “So, it’d be more troubling if you _did_ notice.”

“Huh? …Oh, forgive me\!” I said, bowing again as another slip escaped me.

But Zex-sama responded with leniency. “Raise your head, Shion. I really don’t
mind. Honestly, it’s a bit sad to be treated so formally when we’re alone.
You’re important to me, you know.”

“Z-Zex-sama,” I mumbled, unable to lift my head for a different reason now.

My face must be a mess—there’s no way I could show it to him.

But since he requested it, I had no choice. _Come on, Shion, control your
expression_\! I urged myself.

Somehow—I think—I managed to return to a normal expression and met Zex-sama’s
gaze. “L-let’s get back to the topic,” I said.

“Oh, right,” he replied, sounding a bit flustered.

“What kind of weakening debuff are you using, Zex-sama?” I asked, ignoring his
reaction and cutting to the core.

He raised both hands, counting off on his fingers. “Like I said, there’s the
‘makes weakening debuffs unrecognizable’ spell, one that slightly slows
perceived speed, one that slightly lowers morale, one that faintly delays
sensory transmission, one that marginally reduces calmness, one that subtly
dulls reasoning, one that causes a tiny numbness in limbs—”

“W-wait a moment, please\!” I interrupted, overwhelmed by the endless list of
effects.

Even for me, who’s known Zex-sama for years, this was too much.

“H-how many weakening debuffs are you using?” I asked, changing the question.

I was terrified that listening to every type would take until sunset.

Zex-sama grinned like a child who’d pulled off a prank. _Ugh, he knew what he
was doing._ I’m a bit hopeless for finding this childish side of him charming.

“About a hundred types, give or take,” he said.

“A hundred?\!” I exclaimed.

“But the effects are minimal,” he added. “Otherwise, the ‘unrecognizable debuff’
spell—\[Hishiki\]—wouldn’t work.”

“Even so, combined, that’s a significant effect, isn’t it?” I pressed.

“Depends on the person, but roughly a ten percent reduction,” he estimated.

“Ten percent…” I murmured.

In combat, where small differences can be fatal, a ten percent reduction is
massive. What’s scarier is that the target doesn’t even realize they’re
weakened.

“From the sound of it, is it mental magic?” I asked.

“Yup,” he confirmed. “All my weakening debuffs are mental magic.”

“You said you keep them active constantly, but how do you choose targets?” I
asked cautiously.

I doubted he’d say _everyone_ nearby, but I had to ask. His revelation was that
shocking.

He gave a wry smile. “Not indiscriminately, of course. The trigger is ‘hostility
toward me.’ That’s what activates the weakening debuffs.”

“Hostility?” I said. “Then they’d activate in mock battles too.”

“Exactly,” he nodded. “Killing intent would’ve been easier to detect, but that
wouldn’t work for enemies aiming to capture me. So, even though it’s trickier, I
used hostility as the trigger.”

As someone with no aptitude for mental magic, I couldn’t fully grasp how complex
using “hostility” as a trigger is, but I vaguely understood it’s harder than
“killing intent.”

It all made sense now. A ten percent reduction in strength would naturally cause
unease. Even with \[Hishiki\], a skilled fighter would notice.

—Or so I thought.

“By the way, I hit a hundred types of debuffs a year ago,” Zex-sama added
casually. “I’ve been using weakening debuffs since we started mock battles.”

With that, my pride was shredded to pieces.

**Zex-sama is just too extraordinary.**
