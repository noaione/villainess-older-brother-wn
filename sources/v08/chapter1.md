# Chapter 1: New System

_Scratch, scratch, scratch._ Two sets of faint, crisp sounds echoed through the
room.

The setting was a newly established private office within Foranada Castle’s
administrative wing. Unlike the main office, bustling with numerous clerks, this
small chamber was designed for a select few. With just three desks and stacks of
documents, the floor was nearly entirely claimed—a modest territory, to say the
least.

Two individuals were present, their pens dancing lightly across paper since
earlier.

The first was me, Zex Levito San Foranada. I inherited the earldom at the tender
age of ten and have served as its lord for roughly seven years. My white hair
and pale purple eyes betray my lack of aptitude for attribute magic—a
significant handicap. Yet I’ve overcome it to become, without exaggeration, the
strongest in the world.

The reason I’ve grown this powerful is to protect my beloved sister, Caroline.
In about two years, at the time of her academy graduation, she’s fated to die.

This world closely resembles the setting of a game I played in my previous life
in modern Japan. In that original story, my sister meets a tragic end in every
route. To prevent that at all costs, I’ve been honing myself relentlessly.

Of course, I haven’t neglected other measures. To ensure Caron doesn’t become a
villainess, I’ve been involved in her upbringing since childhood. I’ve also
trained those around me and bolstered our non-martial strengths.

Thanks to these efforts, Foranada now stands as the unrivaled powerhouse of the
Holy Kingdom.

That said, not everything runs smoothly. Perhaps because the original game
focused primarily on the protagonists’ first-person perspectives, unforeseen
events and mysteries crop up frequently. Worse, these tend to tie directly to my
goals. If I hadn’t undergone such grueling training, I might’ve been dead by
now.

But it doesn’t matter who or what stands in my way. My goal—to save my sister
from her doomed fate—remains unshaken.

Enough about me. Let’s introduce the other person in the room.

My adorable adoptive younger brother, Orca Falgata Ga San Foranada, so precious
I could stare at him forever without complaint. A fox-type beastman, he sports
reddish-brown short hair, matching beast ears, and a bushy tail.

At first glance, his delicate features might suggest a girl, but his male shirt
and slacks make it clear: he’s undeniably a boy. His petite frame and occasional
girlish gestures can be misleading, and he wears a skirt with his uniform, which
often confuses others. But make no mistake—he’s male.

Why do I emphasize this so much?

Because if I don’t, my perception starts to waver. I don’t have anything against
same-sex attraction, but it’s not my preference. More importantly, I don’t want
to harbor those feelings toward my brother. At least, not unless Orca himself
wishes it, like Caron might.

...That sounded suspiciously like a flag. Orca’s got a bit of a brother complex,
but I’d like to believe it won’t lead to _that_. It’ll be fine, right?

Suppressing a vague unease, I glanced at Orca while sorting through documents.

Seated at the adjacent desk, Orca focused intently, his yellow-green eyes
gleaming with determination as he worked diligently. His speed rivaled that of a
seasoned clerk, a testament to his years of effort.

He’d offered to help with administrative tasks around the time of the Nine
Ordinances, hadn’t he? He always had potential, but now he’s grown into a clerk
who could outshine veterans. Truly, a dependable brother.

“Something wrong, Zex-nii?” Orca asked.

I must’ve stared too long, as he noticed my gaze and tilted his head curiously.

Cute—no, wait.

“Nah, I was just thinking it’s about time for a break,” I said.

“Already? ...Oh, looks like we’ve been at it for about two hours,” he replied,
checking a stopwatch-like magical device on his desk and nodding in realization.

Thanks to my deliberately calm response, I managed to deflect smoothly.
Breathing an inward sigh of relief, I gave instructions to the figure waiting
behind us.

“Shion, please prepare some tea.”

“Understood,” came the reply.

A maid with pale blue-purple hair tied in a chignon stepped forward—Shion, my
secretary. With her cool demeanor intact, she bowed and began preparing tea with
practiced grace.

Orca piped up suddenly. “Whoa, that startled me. Shion-nee, you were here?”

It seemed he hadn’t sensed her presence. Shion’s stealth skills were impressive,
but failing to notice her for two hours suggested Orca needed more training. I’d
have to add that to his regimen.

“...I feel like I just dug my own grave,” Orca muttered, his expression sour.

No big deal, I thought, shifting my attention to Shion. I didn’t want to take my
eyes off her, and here’s why—

“Ah\!” she gasped.

“Here,” I said, moving swiftly.

Just as I’d feared, the issue arose. Shion had dropped a cup she was arranging.
Before it could shatter on the floor, I caught it with a quick motion.

Despite her mature appearance, Shion was quite the klutz. Her abilities were
top-notch, but she’d occasionally make careless mistakes. Thankfully, they were
usually minor enough for me to cover.

That clumsiness was part of her charm, though. I suppose it’s proof I’ve fallen
for her. Eight years ago, when she confessed, I didn’t feel anything special,
but she’s clearly won me over since.

Smiling wryly at my own change of heart, I placed the rescued cup back on its
saucer. Turning to Shion, who was bowing frantically and apologizing, “I’m so
sorry\!” I placed a hand on her lowered head.

“No harm done, so don’t worry. Just be more careful next time,” I said gently.

“Y-Yes,” she replied, her face flushing crimson.

Despite her age, that lingering innocence was part of her appeal.

As I continued patting her head, a complaint came from behind. “If you two wanna
flirt, I can step out, you know.”

“Sorry, sorry,” I said with a wry chuckle.

“F-Flirt—\?!” Shion froze in embarrassment, but I ignored it—standard reaction.
If I indulged her, Orca might stay sulky.

A few minutes later, after some minor chaos, Orca and I finally took a break.
Sipping the tea Shion poured, I felt the tension from paperwork ease from my
shoulders.

“By the way,” Orca said suddenly, “your magic never ceases to amaze me, Zex-nii,
but this one’s the most shocking yet.”

He spoke with a mix of awe and disbelief, scanning the room.

You might’ve guessed from his gesture, but I was currently maintaining a spell.
Its name: \[Timeless\]. Or, more precisely, an application of \[Alternate
World\]. I’d enveloped the room in \[Alternate World\], severing it from the
flow of time.

This spell was the culmination of my work on \[Time Stop\]. Within
\[Timeless\]’s domain, no matter what we did, less than a second would pass in
the real world once I dispelled it. In other words, the two hours we’d spent on
paperwork would equate to zero seconds. A workaholic’s dream spell.

Incidentally, this private office was built solely for using \[Timeless\]. I
couldn’t drag my entire staff into a black-hole work environment. Orca was here
by his own insistence.

“It’s incredible,” Orca said, his voice tinged with admiration. “Even if you
need to deploy \[Alternate World\], being able to secure infinite time is
insane. For paperwork, you could clear any backlog in an instant.”

“If you’ve got the mana for it,” I cautioned. “Defying the world’s laws is more
taxing than I expected. It drains mana like crazy. I doubt anyone but me could
sustain it.”

In just two hours, I’d burned through a tenth of my mana. That’s a tenth of a
mana pool far exceeding even Akatsuki-sensei, a former divine apostle. No
ordinary human could handle it. Even Caron or Orca, trained to near-human
limits—aptitude aside—might manage a split-second activation at best.

Orca let out a blatantly disappointed sound. “So, defying the world’s rules
comes with a fitting price, huh?”

“Wanna cheat? Get stronger,” I quipped.

“Fair point. If I’m desperate, I’ll just lean on you, Zex-nii,” he declared,
shamelessly planning to exploit his big brother.

I didn’t mind being relied on—it’s what brothers are for—but Orca’s knack for
efficiency was something else.

Then, unusually, Shion spoke up. “May I ask something?”

For a servant who prioritized her role, joining a conversation like this was
rare. It had to be serious.

Slightly tense, I nodded for her to continue.

Her expression stiffened as she asked, “Is it safe to stop time for so long?
We’ve spent about two and a half hours in this spell. Doesn’t that mean we’re
living two and a half hours longer than everyone else?”

“Oh, you’re worried about lifespan,” I said, grasping her concern.

Suppose I spent a century in \[Timeless\]. When I dispelled it, the outside
world would’ve aged mere seconds, but would I emerge as a frail old man? That
was her fear.

While I doubted we’d use \[Timeless\] for years at a time, the adage “many a
little makes a mickle” applied.

The predecessor spell, \[Time Stop\], had that flaw. But \[Timeless\] resolved
it.

“No need to worry,” I said softly, aiming to reassure her. “\[Timeless\] doesn’t
just halt time—it defy the world’s laws entirely, including those governing
lifespan.”

“So, while we’re in this space, we’re effectively immortal?” Orca ventured.

“Exactly,” I confirmed.

“Whoa,” Orca said, his face twisting in shock.

I understood his reaction, but he could’ve hidden it a bit. I worked hard on
this, you know.

Though I felt a touch of dissatisfaction, Shion’s visible relief made it
worthwhile. Good enough.

After finishing the remaining work, we disbanded.

Early April. On the second day of the new academic year, the academy was holding
its entrance ceremony.

In the Holy Kingdom, the academy is an educational institution attended by all
children aged fifteen to eighteen. Rare for a feudal nation, it operates on a
strict meritocracy. With over one hundred thousand students per year, it’s no
exaggeration to call it a city in its own right.

Typically, upperclassmen don’t attend the entrance ceremony. Only a handful,
like the student council president, participate. Yet here I was, seated in a
private room on the upper floor of the grand auditorium, reminiscent of an opera
house.

Why? Because I’m the head of an earldom.

The ceremony includes a tradition where influential nobles deliver
congratulatory speeches. Though I’m a student, Foranada’s current prominence led
to a request for me to give the address.

From the academy director’s tone, it seemed the decision sparked some heated
debate. I’m aware my position is complicated, so I had no complaints about that.

There was, however, another matter—not quite a problem, but something that
piqued my interest. In the room reserved for Foranada, I wasn’t alone.

“What’s that look for?” snapped the girl to my right, glaring with a pout.

Minerva Orleni Yu Kali Lorambel, my fiancée. A petite young woman with black
hair tied in twin tails, she was the eldest daughter of a ducal house and a
prodigy overflowing with intellect. She excelled in nearly every field,
particularly magic, where her knowledge rivaled that of her father, the
so-called Magic Maniac. Add her tireless work ethic, and she was practically
flawless.

That said, she wasn’t without weaknesses. She claimed to be “not great at
sports” and struggled with cooking.

Her most notable flaw, though, was her inability to be honest with those she
cared deeply for. A classic tsundere, she often spoke harshly to me. But my
mental magic let me read her emotions, so it didn’t bother me. Lately, when we
were alone, she’d been more open. Publicly, it’s called a flaw, but I personally
find it endearing.

In short, my fiancée is adorable.

As I chuckled wryly at Minerva’s prickly demeanor, the other person in the room
spoke up.

“If you’re jealous, Minerva, why not cling to Onii-sama yourself?” came a voice
tinged with playful mockery.

The speaker was a stunning woman with slightly wavy blonde hair tied in a
ponytail and crimson eyes, clutching my left arm. Her alluring figure evoked the
word _bountiful_, and she radiated joy. This was my beloved sister, Caroline
Flamer Ga Sari Foranada.

In the original game, Caron held the role of the “villainess,” but in this
reality, she’s carved her own path.

Her appearance is a clear example. The stereotypical drill curls have been
replaced with a ponytail, and her once-standard figure now rivals that of a
gravure idol. According to her, she “worked hard for Onii-sama’s sake.” I was
too scared to ask what that entailed.

Her personality diverges from the original too. Gone are the selfish and
tyrannical traits of a villainess. She’s grown into a kind girl who cherishes
others regardless of status. No one would dare call her a “villainess” now. If
they did, I’d make them regret it.

Her brother complex is a slight blemish, but I’ve accepted it. Her love for me
is so boundless it’s undeniable, leaving me no choice but to embrace it. I’ll
take responsibility for raising her.

Finally, her abilities. The most significant change is her mastery of light
magic, absent in the original. She wields it as naturally as her own limbs. Her
overall prowess far surpasses the game’s version, reaching what I’d call _Limit
Break Level._ Only those closest to her could pose a threat.

Yet I still can’t say she’s escaped her fated death.

I eliminated the demon who drove her down the path of evil in the original. I’ve
maneuvered to prevent the triggers for her downfall. That should offer some
reassurance, but the unease lingers.

Likely because too many ominous mysteries remain. The shadow of the Western
demon king—Gruewen, the Golden Magic Administrator—looms large. As long as it
persists, Caron’s peace feels out of reach.

Recently, I extracted some information from Leviathan, the Blue Magic
Administrator, who’d been hiding among us. According to her, Gruewen is an
arrogant and greedy woman. This is evident in her self-proclaimed title, “Great
Administrator of Light.” Yes, the term Septem mentioned was Gruewen’s own boast.
She equated gold with light, believing no one could surpass her in that domain.
Adding “Great” to her title reflected her conviction that she outshone all other
Administrators.

Her sealing, apparently, stemmed from “seducing the Eastern Demon King—the
Purple Magic Administrator—and embarking on world conquest.” Just hearing about
it was exhausting. Leviathan looked downright fed up recounting it, perhaps
recalling those days.

Given Grüen’s personality, she wouldn’t stay quietly sealed. The Saintess’s
appearance signals a weakening seal, a time when Grüen’s activity spikes. I
couldn’t afford to let my guard down.

My strategy remains unchanged, support the Saintess in resealing Grüen, as per
the original story. The seal weakens gradually over a century, so if the
Saintess fulfills her duty, the threat to Caron should dissipate.

I’ve prepared backup plans, but this is the priority for now. It’s the safest
path.

“Onii-sama?” Caron called.

“You went quiet all of a sudden. What’s wrong?” Minerva added.

“Huh? ...Oh, sorry. I was lost in thought,” I said, snapping back to reality.

That was careless. I’d let myself get absorbed in strategizing. Such thoughts
were better saved for another time. Neglecting the two I love was unacceptable.

For the record, it was just the three of us in the room. Servants waited
outside, but Orca and the others were left at home. There was no need for
everyone to tag along.

Apparently, there’d been a fierce battle over who’d accompany me, but I stayed
out of it. Being asked, “Who do you want?” would’ve complicated things.

To shift the mood, I spoke up. “By the way, I was surprised Tara took top rank.”

“Indeed,” Minerva nodded, impressed. “A commoner claiming first place might be a
historical first, no?”

Thanks to a prior opportunity, I’d seen the new students’ rankings, and to my
shock, my childhood friend Tara had claimed the top spot.

Tara’s a commoner girl from Foranada’s castle town, Dan’s younger sister. Back
in our childhood, she played with me, Caron, Orca, Dan, and Miria. Being a year
younger, I knew she’d enroll this year, but first place was a genuine surprise.
Even with Caron’s magical tutoring, Dan and Miria—also commoners—hadn’t made it
into A1.

Caron mused, “She was always a bright child, so the result makes sense. Unlike
our year, there don’t seem to be any standout prodigies among them.”

A fair point. Our year, filled with the game’s main characters, boasts the
nation’s highest potential. A slight edge in magical skill wouldn’t cut it. With
us hyper-enhanced students occupying the top five spots, the bar is sky-high.

Plus, Dan and Miria’s academics are... well, let’s just say lacking.

“Even so, Tara must’ve worked incredibly hard,” Minerva said. “She outscored the
Third Prince, after all. Some magical knowledge has been gradually disseminated
to the royal family and select others, hasn’t it?”

I nodded. “Things like stabilizing \[Body Enhancement\] and the importance of
non-\[Design\] processes—those have been circulated.”

Bit by bit, I’ve been sharing knowledge publicly. It carries some risks, but I
want the world’s baseline to improve. A stagnant world will eventually collapse.

It’s an investment in the future. I have no intention of revealing critical
knowledge, so as long as we keep advancing, our advantage won’t falter.

That said, Minerva was right—Tara’s effort was remarkable. In terms of shared
knowledge, she was only marginally ahead, if at all. The Third Prince had a
vastly superior training environment, so she must’ve overcome that gap through
sheer dedication.

“We’ll have to praise her heaps,” Caron said with a fond smile.

“Definitely,” I agreed.

Though reserved, Tara had a clingy side. She’d surely beam at our compliments.

As I calculated when to carve out time to meet her, I quietly observed the
entrance ceremony.

As an aside, when I took the stage, I caught Tara blinking in wide-eyed
surprise, a memory worth noting.

The day after the entrance ceremony, the new semester began for upperclassmen.
With no opening ceremony, classes started immediately.

There was some announcement to be made, so the first class of the day was,
unexpectedly, a practical one—Comprehensive Combat, a course teaching all-out,
no-holds-barred fighting techniques. Though a mandatory subject, it was a rather
intense choice for the opening lesson.

I knew the details of this announcement, so I had no qualms with the
curriculum’s structure. Why? Thanks to my knowledge of the original game and a
prior request from the academy director.

Not everyone was so informed. Except for one person, our classmates scattered
across the training field looked uniformly perplexed.

Minerva, standing to my right, turned to me. “You know something, don’t you?”

“What makes you think that?” I countered.

“Your face says it all,” she replied with a smug grin.

Answering a question with a question was a misstep, but she seemed pleased with
her deduction. She must’ve been watching me closely—practically admitting she’s
always observing me. Yet she seemed oblivious to the implications of her words.
Adorable, as always.

Our exchange caught the attention of two others, who jumped into the
conversation.

“Really?” chirped one.

“Has Onii-sama already done his research?” asked another.

The latter was, of course, my beloved sister Caron, standing beside Minerva.

The former was Marina Aroera Cruz, gripping my left hand tightly. With her deep
aqua hair tied in a single braid over her right shoulder and pale blue-purple
eyes, she was a striking figure. In the original game, she was one of the hero’s
romanceable heroines.

Befitting a heroine, her beauty was almost too refined for a commoner, and her
figure was second only to Caron’s in its curves.

After many twists and turns, she’d developed feelings for me, and I was inclined
to accept them in the future.

Having recently formed a contract with a spirit, her strength was now closing in
on Caron’s level. With a bit more training, she might even hold her own in a
fight.

I shrugged and addressed all three. “I got a work request, so I’ve heard the
gist of it.”

“Could it be the academy’s new magic tool purchase? I saw it in some documents,”
Orca said, turning from his spot in front of us.

I nodded. “That’s the one.”

Pleased with his correct guess, Orca’s cheeks flushed as he smiled bashfully...

Calm down, he’s your adoptive brother. Stay calm.

Maintaining my composure, I noticed others joining the discussion, drawn by the
topic.

“Magic tools for the academy... Magic Pieces?” ventured Nina Goshirane Harneus,
a wolf-type beastman. Her brown hair was woven into a single braid, and her
tall, striking figure marked her as one of my fiancées.

Yes, fiancées. She insists, _“If a fiancée is someone you promise to marry, I’m
your fiancée’s fiancée because I reserved my engagement.”_ It’s an eccentric
logic, but she’s content, so I let it slide.

In the original game, Nina was fated to die. But through my training, she
overcame that destiny.

In an anything-goes fight, she’s Foranada’s number two, a testament to her
extraordinary effort.

As an aside, among those who have feelings for me, Nina is by far the most
forward. Even now, she was hugging me from behind, pressing her ample chest
against me. We’re about to have a class—please don’t erode my self-control.

“If it’s about Magic Pieces, wouldn’t they announce that separately?” Minerva
mused.

“True. Since it’s tied to Comprehensive Combat, maybe it’s a barrier-related
tool?” Orca suggested.

“That’s plausible,” Caron agreed. “Perhaps a new version of the Damage
Conversion Ring?”

“Oh, right\! That ring gives a slight edge to mages, doesn’t it?” Marina added.

“Here we go,” Nina muttered.

“Nina, you overwhelmed me even with that ring,” Caron pointed out.

“How strong _are_ you, Nina?” Minerva exclaimed.

“Haha, none of us stand a chance against her now,” Orca laughed.

“I’m outmatched too,” Marina sighed.

The five continued their lively chatter, and I nodded along, watching with a
fond smile.

Not everyone joined the banter. Shion, prioritizing her role as a servant,
rarely spoke during academy activities.

The other was Skia Thornbull Yu Ga Tari Cheyni, a new addition this year. The
fourth daughter of a viscount’s branch family, she was set only to join Foranada
after graduation.

Her deep purple, voluminous hair—wild with curls—pale, almost sickly skin, dark
circles under her eyes, and slouched posture diminished her natural beauty.

Her most striking feature was her golden eyes. Due to a condition called
_Conflict Syndrome_, she couldn’t use magic for years, but my treatment enabled
her to become a skilled light mage.

During the recent stampede, she fought valiantly, achieving remarkable growth in
a short time. She’d even risen to A1 this year.

Perhaps that was her innate talent. When I occasionally trained with her, her
ability to absorb skills was astonishing. She’s likely a genius in her own
right.

Back to the topic.

Skia’s silence stemmed from her social anxiety. She struggled with group
interactions, often clamming up in crowds. She’d respond if addressed directly,
though.

As we continued our idle chatter, the class start time approached. The
instructor should appear soon.

Caron frowned. “Dan-san and Miria-chan are late, aren’t they?”

“I don’t see Yurika-san either,” Orca noted.

As they pointed out, three of our classmates hadn’t yet arrived at the training
field.

Dan and Miria were childhood friends of us Foranada siblings. Both were cheerful
and refreshingly unconcerned with status, making them great companions.

“Dan-kun and Miria-chan might’ve overslept,” Marina ventured.

That seemed plausible. Thanks to Caron’s tutoring, their magical skills ranked
high in our year, but their childish tendencies persisted. “We got too excited
for the new year and couldn’t sleep” sounds exactly like something they’d say.

The issue was Yurika.

A brilliant commoner who ranked in the top ten of our year, the rabbit-type
beastman was far too diligent to oversleep.

“Yurika’s fine,” Nina said abruptly.

My momentary confusion cleared when I recalled Nina had taken Yurika on as a
disciple. Perhaps a training challenge had delayed her.

“Keep it reasonable,” I told Nina.

As a scholarship student, Yurika couldn’t afford to let her grades slip, which
could jeopardize her national stipend. That was my intent, but Nina’s reaction
was dramatic.

Her eyes widened as if they might pop out. “_You’re saying that_?”

...Oops, I hadn’t been clear.

Hastily, I explained that falling from scholarship status would cut her funding.
That seemed to satisfy Nina.

Amid the flurry, the three in question appeared just before class began. The
instructor arrived right after, leaving no time to talk.

Curiously, two instructors stepped onto the training field.

One was the usual Comprehensive Combat teacher.

The other was a complete stranger—a man in his early thirties with gray hair and
gray-green eyes. His lean, muscular build suggested a close-combat adventurer,
perhaps? Racking my memory of the original game, I found no match.

As I eyed him warily, the regular instructor began introducing the newcomer.

“We’re holding this class now to explain a special initiative and to introduce
him. You’ve likely heard of him. This is Bruce Burgess Doega, the Rank A
adventurer known as ‘Wind Blade,’ formerly active in the Empire.”

What?

I furrowed my brow and glanced at Shion.

She seemed just as surprised, shaking her head in response to my look.

“Bruce, the Wind Blade” was a name any Foranada lord should know. As the
instructor said, he was a Rank A adventurer based in the Empire, rumored to have
retired due to a leg injury.

Why hadn’t I recognized him? I hadn’t studied Empire adventurer data thoroughly
enough, but the bigger issue was his hair color. It was supposed to be a pale
green, not gray. That key detail threw off my vague recollection of his face.

In my defense, who’d expect a retired Empire adventurer to show up in the Holy
Kingdom? The rigid class consciousness here would feel stifling to someone from
the meritocratic Empire.

Bruce gave a brief bow and spoke. “I’m Bruce, as introduced. For the next two
years, I’ll be teaching Comprehensive Combat. Feel free to call me Teacher
Bruce. Oh, and apologies in advance—I’m not great with formal speech. Please
overlook my rough manners. Nice to meet you.”

“Bruce-dono was scouted by Her Highness, Princess Arianorth,” the instructor
added.

“Exactly. Thanks to her, my injury’s fully healed. I can’t thank Her Highness
enough,” Bruce said.

“Everyone, show your gratitude for Her Highness’s generosity,” the instructor
urged.

As the class buzzed with excitement, offering words of thanks, Arianorth
responded with a faint smile and a nod.

One of the kingdom’s four light mages and the First Princess, she had golden
hair and pale blue eyes. Known as the “Ice-Witted Saintess” for her sharp
intellect and frequent use of ice magic, she was said to have a cold streak but
a genuine love for her people. In the original game, she was one of the hero’s
romanceable heroines.

In truth, her mental fortitude was extraordinary. She could manipulate her
emotions as deftly as her limbs, making snap decisions for the kingdom’s sake
with ultimate pragmatism. Given her cunning and political acumen, Arianorth
might be the most dangerous figure in the kingdom.

For now, we maintained a mutually beneficial relationship. I’d offloaded some
troublesome hero-related matters to her, after all.

Still, I was surprised she’d reached out to the Empire. It was likely a move
tied to the announcement to come, but I hadn’t anticipated it. Our intelligence
network abroad was evidently a step behind. Until Caron’s issue is resolved,
I’ve prioritized domestic matters, limiting our external reach. I’d have to
accept that limitation—resources aren’t infinite.

“Bruce-dono’s hiring aims to enrich the curriculum, but there’s another key
purpose tied to the announcement you’ve been waiting for,” the instructor said,
adding that the news would be shared with other classes in turn.

“Starting this year, we’re introducing the ‘Tournament System.’ It allows
students to challenge each other to mock battles freely, regardless of class or
year, with outcomes affecting your grades. More precisely, the rewards for
participating in the ‘Tournament System’ will directly influence your academic
standing.”

As expected, it came to this.

In the original game, the “Tournament System” was introduced in the second year,
spearheaded by Arianorth to push students to hone their skills. It’s a
quintessentially pragmatic initiative.

“We’ll distribute detailed rulebooks later, but there are two core regulations.
First, you may refuse a ‘tournament’ challenge, but excessive refusals will earn
a warning from faculty, so be mindful. Second, wearing the magical wristband
we’ll distribute is mandatory for participation. These wristbands ensure that
‘combat between wearers causes no physical harm or environmental damage.’
Violating this will result in severe penalties, so take it seriously. By the
way, Foranada crafted these wristbands, and their effects are guaranteed.”

Indeed, the wristbands central to the “Tournament System” were made by me and
Noma. Dissatisfied with the magic tools used in last year’s individual
tournaments, we’d been refining them. The new version consumed minimal mana and
used mental magic to gauge each user’s strength, setting an HP gauge
accordingly. The previous flaws were addressed.

The rules diverged from the original game, but that’s fine. Reducing injuries is
always a plus.

“Bruce-dono is one of the supervisors for the ‘Tournament System.’ If you need
assistance, turn to him or other designated faculty.”

“If it’s about rough stuff or tactics, come to me,” Bruce added.

The wristbands were then distributed.

Once everyone had equipped them, Bruce spoke again. “Now, let’s test these
wristbands with some mock battles in pairs... but that’s too ordinary and
boring.”

He turned his gaze toward us, grinning mischievously.

A terrible premonition hit me.

“Before coming to this academy, I heard an intriguing rumor. Apparently, a
student defeated a Rank A adventurer with a title in last year’s individual
tournament. As someone in the same position, I’m very curious. So, Caroline
Flamer Ga Sari Foranada-jou, how about a mock battle with me?”

The flag was raised and collected in record time.

I hadn’t expected him to call out Caron.

I glanced at her. She wore a troubled expression.

Understandably so. While Caron did defeat Nina, that victory hinged on favorable
conditions. Recently, I’d heard Nina won under the same setup, so it wasn’t
something Caron could boast about.

If she wasn’t keen, she could refuse.

I was about to tell her so when Nina interjected. “Caron, crush him.”

Her voice carried a hint of irritation.

She continued, “That guy’s convinced he’s stronger than me. It’s infuriating.”

I see. Being underestimated by someone likely in the level 40s range, even
generously, was unacceptable to her.

Normally, she wouldn’t ask someone to “crush” an opponent, but Caron was her
best friend—a trusted ally and a guaranteed victor—so she didn’t hold back.

Caron, for her part, seemed pleased to be relied upon. “I accept.”

With enthusiasm, she agreed to Nina’s request and approached Bruce.

Preparations for the “tournament” were swiftly completed. Those uninvolved,
including me, retreated to the spectator seats, leaving only the two combatants
on the field. A surrender or depleted HP gauge would trigger the wristband’s
robust barrier, eliminating the need for a referee. The system was designed to
track results meticulously.

Bruce gripped a single dagger in a reverse hold, assuming a combat stance. An
infighter blending dagger and martial arts, he stuck to his style.

Caron, meanwhile, raised her fists, clad in fingerless gloves. Unbelievably, she
was preparing to meet him in close-quarters combat.

“Whoa,” Orca gasped, stunned.

“She’s really going to crush him,” Minerva sighed.

Both had clearly grasped Caron’s intentions.

Caron was set on fulfilling Nina’s request to the letter—utterly demolishing her
opponent.

She could’ve used the hammer _Charul_, which I crafted, but she must’ve held
back. That weapon would’ve been overkill.

Bruce, likely aware Caron was a wide-area annihilation mage, initially looked
skeptical. But after observing her stance, he adjusted his expression, realizing
she was no amateur.

_Beep. Please commence combat._

A mechanical voice sounded from both combatants’ wristbands.

Simultaneously, they charged straight at each other.

Caron was overwhelmingly faster. Diving into Bruce’s range first, she maintained
her speed, unleashing a swift one-two punch. Enhanced by \[Body Enhancement\],
her fists sliced through the air, aiming for his chest and solar plexus.

But Bruce wasn’t a titled adventurer for nothing. Though startled by her speed,
he deftly twisted to dodge the first strike and countered with a low kick.

From there, a furious close-quarters exchange unfolded. At a mere meter apart,
they traded blows in a purely physical battle, devoid of attack magic.

“Caroline’s at it again,” Minerva said, exasperated, as fists and blades clashed
chaotically.

Caron had faced Minerva in more individual matches than anyone. From her tone,
it seemed Caron always relied on such brawler tactics. What kind of mage _is_
she?

To be fair, fire magic offers numerous power-enhancing buffs, and a frontline
fighter with light magic’s self-healing is formidable. Still, Caron’s specialty
is high-powered, wide-area annihilation magic. Her preference for the opposite
approach is downright bizarre. A glamorous beauty engaging in a slugfest creates
a jarring contrast.

“Teacher Bruce lives up to his title,” Orca noted. “He’s parrying Caron-chan’s
monstrous strength perfectly. Is he using wind magic to optimize his
deflections? Worthy of the ‘Wind Blade’ name.”

“Caron’s holding her own,” Marina added. “She’s blocking every slash from his
enhanced dagger with light magic barriers. Her magic control’s leaps and bounds
better than before.”

“Ever since Marina contracted a spirit, she’s become the weakest in magic
control among the mage group,” Minerva said. “Caron must’ve felt some pressure.
Though I wish she’d spare me from being her sparring partner every time.”

“Caron-chan and Minerva-chan are such good friends\!” Marina teased.

“We are _not_\!” Minerva shot back.

And so, Orca and the others watched the match with relaxed commentary.

Skia was trembling at the edge of the group, but I left her be. She was just
intimidated by the revelation that her direct superior was a “pummeling
saintess.”

Overall, Bruce had the edge in martial skill, while Caron dominated in magic and
tactics. The former was understandable—she wasn’t a professional fighter. The
latter was the result of the practical training I’d imposed. Especially this
year, our mock battles had significantly boosted her experience.

The slugfest soon shifted. Judging that pure physical combat wouldn’t settle it,
Bruce employed wind magic, scattering multiple daggers from his range. Five
blades hovered briefly in the air, propelled by wind magic, before hurtling
toward Caron, who was still trading blows.

That wasn’t all. Hidden among the daggers were multiple casts of the
intermediate wind spell \[Wind Edge\].

A cunning tactic. Bruce, capable of overpowering most foes head-on, was also a
cautious strategist, layering traps. Ruthless, you could say—an adventurer’s
no-holds-barred approach.

But he’d picked the wrong opponent. Against someone up to level 50, double or
triple traps might suffice, but Caron was a Limit Break Level existence. To face
her, he’d need at least a hundred ruthless strategies.

The deadly blades closed within centimeters of Caron. In the next instant, every
ranged attack, including \[Wind Edge\], was enveloped in spheres of light.
Countless glowing orbs floated in the air, sparkling in the sunlight, creating a
fantastical scene.

The intermediate light spell \[Sacred Sphere\]. It could seal enemy attacks or
small magical beasts, with the strength of deploying multiple at once.

Bruce, confident in his technique, was visibly shocked at how effortlessly she
countered. His eyes widened in disbelief.

He didn’t neglect the melee, but the match was effectively over.

After all, Bruce had broken the melee-only stalemate first. Caron no longer had
to restrain herself to fists.

As expected, she moved to finish it. Amid the chaotic close-quarters brawl, she
used small-scale barriers to lock Bruce’s right elbow, left elbow, left knee,
and right knee in sequence, completely immobilizing him.

She opted for barriers over binding magic due to her skill limitations. Precise
magic casting in a melee was tough for her, but defensive magic, honed since
childhood, was more manageable. Hence her choice.

Finally, with his neck bound by a barrier, Bruce was reduced to a scarecrow,
pinned in midair with no means to break free.

Faced with the overwhelming skill gap, he gave a wry smile and prepared to
concede. “I surr—”

But before he could finish—

_DADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADADA—_

It was like the sound of a drill at a construction site. A deafening roar echoed
across the field.

The source was Caron. Before Bruce could complete his surrender, she unleashed a
barrage of punches at his face. Her fists moved at such blistering speed that
they left countless afterimages.

Bruce took no damage—I can vouch for that as the wristband’s creator.

Still, the situation felt like it could leave psychological scars. Being
immobilized and subjected to a merciless facial onslaught would make most people
lose their nerve. Look, even our classmates are horrified. Heck, everyone but
Nina is recoiling.

...Well, he’s a Rank A adventurer with a title. Let’s believe his mental
fortitude matches.

Eventually, Bruce’s HP gauge depleted, cementing his defeat. Having fainted from
sheer terror, he was carried off on a stretcher.

The sight of Caron and Nina gleefully celebrating left quite an impression.
