# Chapter 5: Love’s Path

As July arrived, the season gradually shifted into one where sweat began to bead
on the skin. With the long holiday approaching, the students’ spirits seemed
slightly buoyant. Even though twenty-seven first-year students had vanished,
only those close to them seemed to care. The academy continued its usual
operations.

The issue concerning the “Outers” was progressing more smoothly than expected.
Not only had low-level dealers been apprehended, but even those in positions
akin to executive candidates had been captured. Now, our reach extended to
several key figures. Most of the surviving hostages had also been freed.

However, there was a part of this that couldn’t be celebrated outright.

“There’s been no counterattack at all,” I muttered to myself. “Sure, they resist
when we move in, but there’s no preparation in the information war beforehand.
It’s almost too easy.”

Given the number of arrests and the inclusion of a viscount among the
executives, there was no doubt this was a large organization. Yet, their
defenselessness was suspiciously extreme.

I’ve instructed my subordinates to consider the possibility of a trap, but what
might happen remains to be seen.

On the first holiday of early summer, harboring such doubts, Caron and I visited
a party hall located in a corner of the academy. The event was a buffet-style
banquet, attended by fewer than thirty students, including ourselves, along with
teachers and other administrative adults.

The occasion? The student council’s “Gathering of Top Performers.” To put it
simply, it was a gathering of the top ten students from each grade. From our
grade, there were Princess Arianorth, the Hero Yudai, and Yurika. From other
grades, there were Tara, the Third Prince Negro, Eclara, Kiteros... and also
Louise, Arianorth’s escort, among others.

Our grade is something else, I thought. The Hero’s side aside, only Orca made it
into the top ranks from the Saintess’s faction. Should we intervene to bolster
their performance?

No, hold on. The Saintess’s assistance has become a lower priority, and for them
to surpass the current top ten would require significant support. Unless they
fall into utter ruin, it’s probably better to leave them be.

Back to the matter at hand.

Originally, this party was intended as a progress report on the “Tournament
System,” meant to gather feedback from top performers about their experiences.
It was initially scheduled for mid-May to early June.

So why the delay of over a month?

The answer isn’t complicated. Due to the circulation of “Outers,” twenty-seven
first-year students were arrested, including three who were in the top ten. The
adjustments and aftermath pushed the event to this timing.

Looking closely, the student council officers and teachers on the administrative
side looked worn out. Even with Princess Arianorth, a light magic user, present,
their haggard expressions suggested how busy they’d been. My condolences.

The party proceeded smoothly. Perhaps because opportunities to interact with
other grades were rare, many students showed keen interest, engaging in lively
conversations without hesitation.

Our second-year group drew particular attention. Naturally, the Foranada
contingent stood out, but having the once-in-a-century Hero among us also
helped. He was busy navigating the event, which made him quite popular in his
own right.

I noticed Nina’s younger sister, Rina, who was assisting with the event, subtly
warding off female students approaching Yudai. Has she entered the “Rina route”?
I mused. Well, the root of her vengeance has been mostly eradicated, so it
definitely won’t unfold like the original story.

Caron and the others seemed to be enjoying themselves for the most part. Skia,
however, was overwhelmed by the crowd, but I entrusted her to Shion and the
other attendants we brought along, so there was no issue.

Oh, I was participating normally too, though in a low-key way. As the only head
of a household, students tended to feel intimidated by me. Thus, I was busy for
only the first thirty minutes or so. After that, I lingered by the wall,
leisurely savoring my meal.

“You’re neglecting your fiancées. What are you doing?” said Minerva, approaching
me.

“It’d be troublesome if you forgot about your fiancées,” added Nina, following
her.

I was idly sipping a drink to quench my thirst when Minerva and Nina approached.
“I’m not so tactless as to interrupt carefree student interactions,” I replied.
“Are you two already done?”

Until just a moment ago, they had been at the center of their respective groups.
Was it really okay for them to come to the edge of the venue like this?

The two exchanged glances and sighed.

“What are you talking about? There’s no one more important than you,” said
Minerva.

“You called yourself a ‘carefree student,’” pointed out Nina.

“Exactly,” Minerva continued. “Students without titles or positions can be dealt
with when we have spare time. It’s true that building connections for the future
is wise, but not at the expense of neglecting our husband.”

“Prioritizing love is also a student’s privilege,” said Nina.

“Y-Yes, that’s right. Love... You’d better pay attention to us so we don’t feel
neglected,” stammered Minerva.

“You don’t have to shy away from saying it, Minerva,” teased Nina.

“I’m not shying away\!” retorted Minerva, her voice rising.

Their lively banter suggested they were prioritizing spending time with me. I
felt their affection, and my heart warmed. I was aware of the slight upturn at
the corners of my mouth.

“Thank you,” I said sincerely.

“Hmph. Don’t just say it—escort us properly,” demanded Minerva.

“It’s not even worth thanking us for. We’re happy just being with you, Zex,”
said Nina warmly.

“Honestly, Nina, you’re too direct. It makes me look like the difficult one,”
complained Minerva.

“Difficult? More like... tsundere?” teased Nina.

“Who’s a tsundere?\!” snapped Minerva.

All I did was express gratitude, yet the conversation unfolded rapidly.

Come to think of it, I hadn’t often seen Minerva and Nina converse just the two
of them. I knew they got along, but watching their direct interaction now, I
realized their chemistry was quite good.

Perhaps because they’re Caron’s close friends, their slightly bold exchanges
were endearing in their own way.

As the three of us enjoyed our casual chat, someone approached and addressed us.

“Foranada-dono, Duke Loramber’s daughter-dono, Nina-dono. My apologies for
interrupting your conversation,” said Kiteros, standing before us with his usual
dubious smile, exuding a clownish aura.

Noticing that we had turned our attention to him, Kiteros bowed courteously.
“This is my first time meeting the two ladies. I am Kiteros Toneorima Yu Nan
Iraka. I thought it would be rude not to exchange a word on such an occasion, so
here I am,” he said smoothly.

“I am Minerva Allrain de Cali Loramber. Pleased to make your acquaintance,”
replied Minerva formally.

“Nina Gosilane Harneus. Nice to meet you,” said Nina curtly.

“Yes, a pleasure to meet you both,” responded Kiteros politely.

Though Minerva and Nina replied, their tones were tinged with suspicion. It was
understandable—Kiteros’s demeanor was consistently dubious, his gestures overly
theatrical.

I realized I should respond too, or it might seem odd. “It’s been a while,
Kiteros-dono,” I said. “This is a student council party, not exactly a
free-for-all, but you don’t need to be so formal. Right, you two?”

“Yes,” agreed Minerva simply.

“Being overly respectful is more bothersome,” said Nina bluntly.

Kiteros nodded quietly at my suggestion. “Understood. I’ll relax a bit then,” he
said, loosening his posture.

With greetings out of the way, we moved to small talk, but our common ground was
limited. Given our differences in status, the safest topic was academy life.

“It’s been about three months since enrollment,” I began. “Have you settled into
the new environment, Kiteros-dono?”

“I’m still adjusting, I’d say,” he replied. “As you know, my family is only a
barony, so dorm life isn’t a hardship. In fact, the facilities here are an
improvement over my usual lifestyle. However, the academic rigor is quite high.
I’m barely keeping up.”

“Yet you still made it into the top ten,” I pointed out.

“That’s thanks to the foundation I built before enrolling,” he explained.
“Fortunately, my family has ample wealth, so I was able to prepare thoroughly.”

Indeed, as Kiteros himself noted, the Iraka family was wealthy for a
barony—possibly richer than some earldoms in terms of funds.

How did they amass such wealth?

“I believe it was a business venture you started about five years ago that
succeeded, wasn’t it?” I asked.

“Exactly as you say, Count,” Kiteros confirmed. “Our trade business with the
city-state cluster took off successfully.”

I’d heard the Iraka family originally stemmed from a merchant house dealing in
trade, ennobled a few generations ago. My intelligence department had confirmed
this quickly. However, until five years ago, they were a modest house, barely
notable. Their business venture changed that dramatically.

“Starting trade in that war-torn region was quite a bold decision,” I remarked.

“We had little to lose,” Kiteros replied. “And while it’s risky, goods sell well
in such chaotic regions.”

“The risks don’t seem to outweigh the rewards,” I countered skeptically.

“That’s where our mercantile expertise came into play,” he said confidently.
“And it did succeed, after all.”

“I see,” I said, nodding.

Though I acknowledged his explanation, I wasn’t convinced it was that simple. No
matter the demand or the strength of their escorts, trading in a conflict zone
seemed reckless. The profits might be substantial, but the scale of wealth the
Iraka family had accumulated was hard to believe.

Still, I had no proof. Since their trade was with a foreign city-state, our
intelligence couldn’t dig deeper. I suspected something illicit, but it could be
an entirely unrelated matter. The world isn’t full of clean dealings, after all.

My conversation with Kiteros continued for a while until, abruptly, a
\[Telepathy\] message from Caron came through.

“_Onii-sama, it’s an emergency_\!” she exclaimed. “_Orca... Orca’s been proposed
to\!”_

It seemed a strange situation was unfolding beyond my knowledge.

---

The evening gathering was held under the pretext of fostering interaction among
the top ten students from each grade. To be honest, I—Caroline—felt somewhat
underwhelmed. While there were certainly a few noteworthy individuals, most
students struck me as inconsequential.

No, that’s not quite right—it’s unfair to put it that way. Even if they don’t
measure up to us, they are still budding powerhouses in their own right.

How should I describe it? ...Yes, “common” is the word. Their attempts to curry
favor with the beautiful and influential, like myself, Minerva, or Princess
Arianorth-dono, reek of vulgarity.

I’ll admit my ideal of a true powerhouse is lofty. Someone strong, wise,
compassionate, yet humble and cautious—such a person could only be my Onii-sama.
Compared to him, even I am no more than a roadside pebble.

With that perspective, I can accept the students’ behavior as inevitable. If I
haven’t caught up to my Onii-sama, how could anyone else act as he does?

Quietly reconciling myself, I continued my courteous interactions.

Truthfully, I’d rather be at my Onii-sama’s side above all else, but that’s an
unattainable wish. Neglecting others at such a social event would go against a
lady’s decorum, and my Onii-sama wouldn’t approve.

Everyone else seemed occupied with other students, so I resolved to do my best
as well.

Still, what a tedious time. If only a close friend were here, I could enjoy an
engaging moment, but unfortunately, aside from the usual group, no one else made
the top ten. The sole exception was Tara, but as the top student in her grade,
she was swarmed by others.

About an hour into the event, feeling the need for a break, I glanced at Garuna,
who was serving as my attendant.

Though she’s usually lighthearted, she performs her duties impeccably. She
caught my intent and acted swiftly.

“My apologies for interrupting your conversations,” Garuna announced. “Lady
Caroline is feeling a bit fatigued, so—”

This gave me an excuse to slip away from the crowd.

We quickly left the venue, heading outside. Her phrasing implied I might need to
freshen up, so moving to a separate room was the most convenient option.

In the dimly lit corridor, illuminated by the faint glow of magical devices, I
felt a slight chill as we walked. I spoke up.

“Thank you, Garuna,” I said gratefully.

“No trouble at all—it’s my job,” she replied, handing me a glass with a straw.
“Here’s a drink.”

“Thank you,” I said, accepting it and draining it in one go. I hadn’t realized
how parched I was. Talking nonstop for nearly an hour would do that, I supposed.

“Lady Caroline, you have it tough, surrounded by people you’re not the least bit
interested in,” Garuna said, her formal tone dissipating into her usual playful
one, though still within the bounds of respect.

I had no issue with her attitude—it was her true self, and I appreciated her
warmth as a Foranada companion. In public, she knew to keep it in check.

Returning the empty glass, I shrugged. “It’s unavoidable given my position,” I
said. “It’s certainly bothersome, but it’s part of my duty as my Onii-sama’s
sister. I’ve received enough benefits to warrant it.”

Some nobles—children included—shirk their responsibilities, but I can’t fathom
it. We don’t choose where we’re born, but it’s undeniable we live more
comfortably than most commoners. I believe we should fulfill the obligations
that come with that.

I’ll allow for differing opinions—it’s my personal view, and my Onii-sama has
said, “Don’t dismiss opposing perspectives outright.”

Thus, I have no complaints about the hassles of high society—or rather, I have
them, but I don’t use them as an excuse to grumble.

As Garuna and I chatted idly, we neared the powder room. But then—

“Orca?” I said, catching sight of him.

I glimpsed Orca turning a corner in the distance. His expression, visible for
only a moment, seemed unusually tense. I couldn’t be certain, but something felt
off.

“Let’s go,” I said decisively.

“Eh, Lady Caroline?\!” exclaimed Garuna, startled.

Feeling an inexplicable unease, I hurried after Orca.

Rounding the corner, I saw Orca’s back ahead, with Eclara-san walking in front
of him, as if leading the way.

Indeed, there was an undeniable tension between them.

Fortunately, they hadn’t noticed us. Normally, Orca’s detection range would
include us, but he must be entirely focused on Eclara-san.

Determined to uncover the cause of this heavy atmosphere, I decided to continue
tailing them.

Garuna and I followed at a safe distance.

After a few minutes, we arrived at a meeting room within the same building as
the party hall. The unlit room was faintly illuminated by moonlight and
starlight streaming through the windows.

We didn’t enter but peered through the entrance’s window. Orca and Eclara-san
stood facing each other in the center of the room, both with deeply serious
expressions.

The setting and atmosphere made it clear this was no mere extension of casual
conversation. Holding our breath, we observed.

I may not be as skilled as Shion or Garuna, but I’ve been trained by my
Onii-sama. I was confident I could evade the detection of a distracted Orca or
Eclara-san.

“What’s this important matter?” Orca asked, his tone slightly stiff. “Calling me
out to a place like this.”

“You already know, don’t you, Orca?” replied Eclara-san, her voice as carefree
as ever.

Despite sharing the same serious air, their expressions gave opposite
impressions. After a brief pause, Orca spoke.

“Exactly, Orca. Well done,” replied Eclara-san, her tone light but resolute.

“—\!” I gasped, nearly letting my voice slip out.

That was close. His words were so shocking I almost gave myself away. Reviving a
fallen noble house is an endeavor far more arduous than establishing a new one.
After all, a house collapses for significant reasons, and those reasons become a
negative stigma that hinders progress.

In the case of the Lampros family, their extinction stemmed from the fallout of
a civil war, so their ability to govern a territory would undoubtedly be
questioned. For a noble, that’s a fatal flaw. Few would rally to support
incompetence.

“By the way, Garuna doesn’t seem surprised,” I remarked, suddenly recalling the
maid who was spying alongside me.

Despite hearing their conversation, she remained completely composed. Garuna
gave a slight shrug.

“Of course not,” she said casually. “That textbook prideful noble lady wouldn’t
be satisfied staying a commoner. Plus, we got reports that early in the
semester, she was frantically visiting noble children she had connections with.”

“Oh, there was a report like that, wasn’t there?” I said, vaguely recalling it.

“...Lady Caroline,” Garuna said, her eyes narrowing. “I don’t like pointing
fingers, but even if you’re not interested, you should at least read the reports
thoroughly.”

“I-I know that\!” I protested. “This was just a coincidence\!”

Yes, truly a coincidence. I merely skimmed the report because I didn’t want to
waste mental energy on ‘that irritating woman.’ Normally, I read reports
meticulously. Honestly\!

Ignoring Garuna’s skeptical gaze, I focused on Orca and Eclara-san’s
conversation.

Their exchange seemed to have grown heated, with both breathing slightly
heavily. From what I caught, Orca had been urging Eclara-san to reconsider,
though it appeared futile.

Orca sighed and asked, “I understand your resolve is firm. But why tell me
this?”

“I’m asking for your assistance,” replied Eclara-san.

Her request was expected. Given the nature of this conversation, it was only
natural she’d seek cooperation.

However—

“No way. There’s no benefit in it,” said Orca flatly.

A perfectly reasonable judgment. As he said, there was no advantage for us.
Refusing a friend’s request might weigh on the heart, but deciding matters
involving the house’s interests based on personal feelings is nonsense.

Despite Orca’s blunt refusal, Eclara-san showed no signs of panic. Did she have
some hidden strategy?

She gave a wry smile. “Well, please hold on,” she said. “I’ll present the
benefits now.”

“Is there even such a thing?” asked Orca, his voice tinged with suspicion.

I tilted my head, equally puzzled. I couldn’t imagine a fallen noble lady
offering benefits compelling enough to sway Foranada.

Unfazed by his reaction, Eclara-san continued, “If you bring me into your
fold... you can secure four-fifths of the noble houses currently declaring
neutrality.”

“That’s...” Orca faltered, his words catching.

It was no wonder. The benefit Eclara-san proposed was indeed significant enough
to be considered a reward for Foranada.

The truth is, Foranada has few allies among other houses, largely due to our
unique position.

The Holy Kingdom is currently divided by two main criteria: allegiance to the
royal palace or the nobility, and adherence to monotheism or polytheism. This
results in three primary factions: Palace Polytheists, Noble Monotheists, and
Noble Polytheists.

To be precise, there are more nuanced divisions, but broadly, these are the
three.

What about Palace Monotheists, you ask?

To my knowledge, they don’t exist. The Holy Royal Family has long championed
polytheism, leaving little room for monotheists to align with them.

Consequently, the Noble faction tends to lean heavily monotheist. Historically,
the Holy Kingdom’s factional struggles have mirrored religious conflicts.

Now, where does Foranada fit in?

Undoubtedly, we’re polytheists. We don’t discriminate against beastmen or other
non-humans, after all.

As for Palace or Noble allegiance, we lean toward the Noble faction. Aside from
the First Prince, we keep our distance from the current Holy Royal Family.

Thus, Foranada can be classified as Noble Polytheists.

However, it’s not simply that we have few allies because our faction is the
smallest. While we bear the label of Noble Polytheists, we haven’t formed
cooperative ties with other houses in our faction.

Why? Because the majority of noble houses declaring neutrality—fearing
Foranada’s immense power—are Noble Polytheists. In an already small faction, our
allies are further diminished.

I understand their desire to bide their time until the situation stabilizes. For
a weaker faction, taking risks is undesirable—they don’t want to be crushed by
drawing attention.

Still, couldn’t they be a bit more astute in seizing opportunities? Their noble
status is practically weeping.

Back to the point.

Due to these circumstances, Foranada has only a handful of allied noble houses.

Our territory alone boasts significant influence for now, but that prosperity
isn’t guaranteed forever. As long as my Onii-sama is here, we’re fine, but
there’s no assurance the next generation or beyond will be as exceptional.

Thus, for the future, securing allied noble houses is desirable. Eclara-san’s
proposal was indeed a valuable benefit.

However, to make that benefit effective—

“Simply cooperating won’t convince neutral houses to switch sides,” muttered
Orca, his expression darkening.

Eclara-san, in contrast, beamed brightly. “Indeed,” she said. “Some houses might
respond to mere cooperation, but it’s not guaranteed. In that case...”

“You’d join the family,” finished Orca.

“Hehe, exactly,” she confirmed. “With someone as astute as you, I’m confident
we’d get along well. So, Orca, would you marry me?”

What an outrageous turn of events\!

It wasn’t entirely unexpected, but the sudden escalation left me reeling. I
hurriedly activated my magical communicator.

_“Onii-sama, it’s an emergency_\!” I exclaimed via \[Telepathy\]. “_Orca...
Orca’s been proposed to\!”_

Where will this situation lead?

As I observed the silent Orca and Eclara-san, a small seed of unease sprouted in
my chest.

---

“I’m not pressing for an immediate answer,” said Eclara-san. “Take your time to
think it over.”

With that, the meeting concluded, and they parted ways. Garuna and I hurriedly
retreated to avoid being caught spying.

Meanwhile, my Onii-sama responded, _“I’ll look into it,”_ before ending the
communication. I have no doubts about his capability, but I hope he finds a path
that’s best for Orca. He’s an important family member to me too, after all.

That said, I have no intention of sitting idly by. The morning after the party,
I summoned Minerva, Nina, Shion, Marina, and, of course, Orca. Since it was a
day off from the academy, we didn’t need to worry about time constraints—a
fortunate convenience.

And so, in a small room with a round table, I declared, “Let us commence the
fifty-ninth Bride Conference.”

“Thank you all for attending this emergency meeting,” I added, expressing my
gratitude.

The attendees responded in their own ways.

“No need to mention it,” said Shion calmly.

“As Shion says, don’t worry about it,” agreed Nina. “The Bride Conference takes
precedence over most things.”

“Nina-chan’s exaggerating a bit, but I’d show up for most calls,” said Marina
cheerfully.

“No thanks necessary,” said Minerva curtly. “I didn’t have any urgent matters
anyway.”

Minerva’s usual roundabout way of speaking was, as always, tiresome, but I
decided to let it slide for now. We had more pressing concerns than her quirks.

Orca, who had been nervously glancing around until just moments ago, spoke up
with a puzzled expression. “W-Wait a second,” he said. “Caron-chan dragged me
here, but where even is this place? I’ve never heard of a room like this in
Foranada Castle. And what’s this ‘Bride Conference’?”

A fair question. As a first-time attendee, his confusion was understandable. The
fact that he could pinpoint the necessary questions spoke to his sharpness.

We exchanged glances and began to explain.

“The Bride Conference is exactly as it sounds,” I said.

“To be precise, it’s the ‘Prospective Brides Conference,’” clarified Shion. “A
gathering for those confirmed to be accepted by Zex-sama.”

“It’s for sharing info about Zex-sama or dealing with sudden issues,” added
Marina. “Though, honestly, it’s mostly reports about how cool or cute Zex-sama
was, plus a lot of lovey-dovey talk.”

“The chairperson is me, the primary wife,” said Minerva. “I handle scheduling,
with Shion’s help, though Caroline called this meeting, so she’s running it.”

“As for this room,” I said, “you can accept that it was ‘arranged at Onii-sama’s
request.’ It utilizes \[Spatial Expansion\] to make use of the castle’s dead
space.”

This conference, in truth, is less than a year old. The first meeting was during
summer break, so it’s been about eleven months.

The reason? Until we enrolled in the academy, there weren’t enough members to
warrant a conference. Just Minerva, Shion, and me in the sister role were too
few.

By the way, I was the one who proposed it. I suggested to Minerva, “Since
Onii-sama’s brides are likely to increase, shouldn’t we hold regular meetings to
coordinate?” Without that initiative, I, as a sister, wouldn’t have a seat here.
I have at least that much sense, you know.

Well, it’s not entirely inaccurate. I’m aware of my love for my Onii-sama, and
he seems ready to accept me once he’s sorted out his feelings.

But the origins of the Bride Conference aren’t the focus now. We have a pressing
agenda.

“Uh, okay,” said Orca, giving a half-hearted response.

His reaction was a bit underwhelming. I’d hoped for more engagement.

He didn’t seem confused by the explanation itself, but rather, he hadn’t grasped
why he was summoned here.

No need to worry, Orca. We’ll get to the point soon enough.

Setting aside his bewilderment, I took charge as the moderator. “Now, time is
finite, so let’s move to the main topic,” I said. “The reason for this emergency
meeting is that Orca was proposed to by Eclara-san.”

““““Oh?”””” chorused the group.

They all nodded with feigned interest, but beneath the surface, I could sense a
subtle irritation.

It seemed everyone shared my sentiments. That was reassuring.

To clarify, this wasn’t jealousy.

But one person reacted differently.

“What?\! How do you know?\!” exclaimed Orca, leaping to his feet, genuinely
shocked.

Come to think of it, Orca had only told my Onii-sama about the proposal, saying,
“I want to think it over alone first.” My Onii-sama, respecting that, hadn’t
delved deeper. So, it was natural that Orca was stunned to learn I, an outsider,
knew.

Still, his reaction was uncharacteristically flustered for someone usually so
composed. Was the matter so sensitive he didn’t want others to know? It was
certainly a delicate issue.

I set aside my speculations. Calming Orca was the priority, lest he mistakenly
think my Onii-sama leaked the information.

“Please calm down, Orca,” I said gently. “Garuna and I were present at the scene
of the proposal. Seeing you two head to a secluded place raised my concern, so
we followed. I apologize for eavesdropping.”

“Oh, uh, sorry,” said Orca, quickly regaining his composure. “I was careless
too.”

Thanks to my sincere apology, he calmed down, lowering his head slightly and
sitting back down quietly.

However, his confusion wasn’t entirely dispelled. No, perhaps because he’d
regained his composure, a new doubt surfaced. Tilting his head with an “Huh?” he
asked, “Why is my proposal a topic for the ‘Bride Conference’?”

“““““...””””” The room fell silent.

We couldn’t answer. All we could do was exchange glances, searching for a
response.

Orca was far more oblivious than I’d expected. No, I don’t have the right to
criticize, given I only realized my own feelings after a demon pointed them out.
Still, I couldn’t help but feel exasperated.

Orca is in love with my Onii-sama. That’s an undeniable fact. The way he looks
at and acts toward my Onii-sama clearly goes beyond mere sibling camaraderie.
Having actual older Onii-samas provides a clear comparison.

My Onii-sama likely senses it too, so the only one unaware is Orca himself. The
beloved knows, but the lover doesn’t—what a bizarre situation.

“Caroline, this is your responsibility. Do something,” said Minerva abruptly,
thrusting the burden onto me.

I widened my eyes. “Why is it my responsibility?” I demanded.

It’s Orca’s issue if he’s unaware of his own feelings.

My objection was met with exasperated looks—not just from Minerva, but from
everyone except Orca.

“Lady Caroline...” sighed Shion.

“Clueless,” muttered Nina.

“No way, Caron-chan,” said Marina.

I was utterly unsupported, surrounded on all sides—what’s that phrase? Four
sides, all enemies?

Caught off guard by this unexpected turn, Minerva sighed and spoke. “Since
you’re oblivious, I’ll explain,” she said. “Orca has been close to you and Zex
for years. He’s watched a relationship where love was mistaken for sibling
affection. Growing up exposed to such extreme Onii-sama-sister complexes, it’s
only natural his sense of romantic boundaries would blur.”

“I see,” I said, nodding slowly.

Her explanation was convincing.

Regrettably, my relationship with my Onii-sama isn’t typical for siblings. It’s
only logical that Orca, having observed us closely, would have a skewed
perception.

In short, because of my own obliviousness, Orca became oblivious too. Indeed,
this situation could be considered my responsibility.
