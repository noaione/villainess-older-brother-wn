# Chapter 2: New Students

The shocking first period ended, and it was time for a break. The next class was
a lecture held in the classroom, so we began moving to the designated room.

The second period was also a mandatory subject, so our classmates were attending
the same lecture. However, as we walked, I couldn’t shake the feeling that there
was slightly more distance between us and the others than usual. ...No, it
wasn’t my imagination. It was obvious that the earlier incident was still
lingering in their minds.

There was nothing to be done about it. I’d just have to wait for the tension to
cool off with time. Fortunately, Caron and the others didn’t seem to care at
all.

That said, not everyone was keeping their distance. Some approached us without
hesitation, calling out cheerfully.

“Hey, Caron\!”

“Good morning, everyone\!”

“G-Good morning, everyone\!”

It was our childhood friends Dan and Miria, along with the rabbit beastman
Yurika. Dan was a burly guy with brown hair and eyes, Miria had green,
shoulder-length hair, and Yurika stood out with her dew-colored, straight, long
hair and droopy eyes.

After exchanging greetings, the conversation naturally shifted to the fact that
Dan and Miria had barely made it to class on time.

“Did you oversleep or something?” Orca asked, his tone a bit sharp. Having
looked after their studies for nearly a year, he probably had some strong
feelings about their tardiness.

At his question, both Dan and Miria wore sheepish expressions.

“Well, you’re not wrong about the oversleeping part...” Dan mumbled.

“It’s just that this time, it couldn’t be helped...” Miria added.

For a pair as straightforward as they usually were, their responses were
unusually vague. What happened?

All of us, myself included, looked at them suspiciously, waiting for them to
elaborate.

After a brief pause, Miria spoke up.

“Yesterday, we were getting lectured by Tara-chan until late at night. You know,
since our grades last year were... well, \*_that_\*.”

Miria let out a dry laugh.

Our reactions to her explanation split into two camps: those who understood and
those who didn’t. The former group consisted of the long-time Foranada
members—myself, Caron, Orca, Nina, Minerva, and Shion. The latter included
Marina, who tilted her head curiously.

“Um... Tara-chan is Dan-kun’s little sister, right?” Marina asked.

“That’s right,” I replied simply.

That only made the skeptical group more confused.

“Your \*_little sister_\* lectured you? Late into the night, enough to make you
oversleep?” Yurika asked, her tone laced with doubt.

Dan and Miria shrank even further under her gaze.

It was a fair question. It’s hard to imagine a younger girl lecturing her older
siblings until midnight. Maybe it’s a lack of imagination on their part...
though, I noticed Skia, who had been silent, had a distant look in her eyes, as
if she understood perfectly.

\*\*Oh, I get it.\*\*

“Yurika, are you an only child, by any chance?” I asked.

“N-No, I have a younger brother... ten years younger,” she replied.

“Got it. That makes sense.”

“??”

Yurika tilted her head, clearly puzzled, but I had pieced it together.

It was simple. Marina, an only child, and Yurika, who only had a much younger
brother, didn’t fully grasp the dynamics of close-aged siblings. Having seen the
extreme example of me and Caron, they were probably thrown off by Dan and
Miria’s situation.

To the two still tilting their heads, I explained, “That’s just how normal
siblings are.” They didn’t seem entirely convinced, but they nodded along for
now.

As the conversation settled, Caron murmured with a hint of nostalgia, “Tara-chan
hasn’t changed a bit, has she?”

“With how reckless these two can be, it’s only natural she’s grown into that
role,” Minerva added.

“Tara-chan’s always been the one keeping them in check,” Orca chimed in.

The three of them wore expressions tinged with pity, likely picturing Tara
running around after her troublemaking brother and childhood friend. It was hard
to deny that Tara had always been the responsible one. The silver lining, if you
could call it that, was that Dan and Miria were good at heart. Their “mishaps”
were mostly harmless, the kind of mistakes kids make.

Perhaps picking up on the mood, Marina and Yurika gave wry smiles. Skia,
meanwhile, seemed to be lost in her own memories, her gaze even more distant
than before. As the youngest of six siblings, she probably related to Tara’s
struggles more than most.

Until the second period began, we continued chatting about Tara, the
conversation lively and warm.

Lunch break arrived. By this point, most classes had been briefed on the “Combat
System,” and the academy was buzzing with excitement. Many students seemed eager
to participate, their voices filled with chatter about “who they’d fight.”

For us, though, it felt like a distant topic. I overheard comments like, “I’d
never challenge Foranada,” scattered among the conversations. We’d become
something of an untouchable presence.

And for good reason. In last year’s individual tournaments by grade, we’d
displayed overwhelming strength. Few would be foolish enough to challenge a
fight they knew they’d lose.

“Alright, shall we head to lunch?” I said, gathering my things and calling out
to the Foranada group. Since the morning classes were mandatory lectures,
everyone was still in the classroom.

Most responded promptly, but Nina was doing something unusual.

“Yurika, come eat with us,” she said.

It was rare for her to invite Yurika like that. It’s not that we ignored her or
anything—our lunches were typically held in private rooms reserved for nobles,
so we avoided inviting commoners outside the Foranada circle. You never know
when someone might misinterpret the gesture and cause trouble.

That’s why we didn’t usually eat with Dan or Miria at the academy either. The
moment class ended, those two had already dashed off to the cafeteria.

So why was Nina inviting Yurika now?

Orca answered my unspoken question.

“Oh, right. Because of the ‘Combat System,’” he said.

“That makes sense,” I replied.

“It’s a necessary precaution, isn’t it?” Minerva added, shrugging lightly.

Orca’s single sentence was enough for me to understand, and Minerva clearly got
it too. But the others—including Yurika herself—seemed confused. Caron spoke up
on their behalf.

“What do you mean, Onii-sama?”

I explained.

“The ‘Combat System’ is an academy-sanctioned event where anyone can challenge
anyone else. It’s meant to help students aim higher, but not everyone will use
it with pure intentions.”

“You mean some people will misuse it?” Caron asked.

“Exactly. There’ll definitely be those who use it for less-than-honest reasons.”

“There are plenty of nobles who aren’t happy with their grades,” Minerva added,
elaborating on my point.

At this, Caron’s eyes widened slightly as the realization hit her.

“So nobles jealous of Yurika-san, a special-admission student, might flood her
with challenge requests under the ‘Combat System’?”

Orca nodded at her surprised tone.

“No matter how much the academy promotes meritocracy, the kingdom itself is
still rooted in a class system. Not everyone can switch their mindset so easily.
That’s why A-Class commoners like Yurika are likely to be targeted first.”

“That’s...” Caron covered her mouth with both hands, her expression heavy with
concern.

I reached out and ruffled her hair gently. “It’s not that serious, so don’t
worry too much. It’s just that she’ll probably get a ton of challenge requests.
The academy’s got measures in place to handle things like intimidation or
underhanded tactics.”

Rigging matches using status differences is an obvious cheat anyone could think
of. Naturally, neither I nor Arianorth, who spearheaded the system, would let
that slide. We’d already taken steps to manage such issues.

So why invite Yurika to join us?

“While we can’t stop the flood of challengers, it’ll be a hassle for her to deal
with them all. This is just a way to help her avoid some of that trouble,” I
said simply.

It boiled down to looking out for a friend. It’d be hard to ignore a friend’s
struggles.

Nina, the one who’d made the suggestion, added, “Fighting a bunch of nobodies is
less useful than training.”

Her words cut through the crowd of students like a master swordsman’s blade,
dismissing most of them as beneath her notice.

With that, everyone seemed to understand. No one had been opposed to Yurika
joining us in the first place, so the matter was settled.

With the discussion wrapped up, it was time to head to our usual room for lunch.
Lunch break wasn’t infinite, after all.

But just as we were about to leave, we were stopped once again.

A familiar figure appeared at the classroom door.

“Hey, Zex-san. It’s been a while.”

Standing at the entrance was one of our childhood friends and the top new
student of the year, Tara Bremil Magra.

---

Tara is Dan’s younger sister and someone who used to play with me, Caron, and
Orca often when we were kids. Despite being younger, she always took on the role
of keeping Dan and his antics in check, and even as a child, she struck me as
remarkably sharp.

Since I inherited my title, my busy schedule has reduced my chances to see her
in person, though I’ve heard from Caron and the others that they’ve met with her
regularly. That said, those meetings were before the academy term began. For all
of us, including me, this was our first encounter with Tara since starting
school. For Marina and Skia, it was their first time meeting her altogether.

Seeing Tara after so long, I couldn’t help but notice how beautifully she’d
grown. She didn’t have the same flamboyant charm as Caron and the others, but
her natural qualities had matured gracefully. With a bit of polish, she’d
undoubtedly shine even brighter.

“I’m Tara Bremil Magra, Dan Biled Magra’s younger sister. My brother’s always
been in your care. I’ve joined the academy this year, so I look forward to
getting to know you all,” Tara said, bowing slightly, her neatly trimmed, short
brown bob swaying as she did.

We were in one of the academy’s private rooms reserved for nobles. Tara had
apparently come to see her brother and Miria, but unfortunately, they’d already
dashed off to the cafeteria. Since it was a rare opportunity, we invited her to
join us for lunch. Given her similar status to Yurika, it wasn’t exactly an
afterthought, but the timing was perfect.

Her introduction was directed at Marina and Skia, the two meeting her for the
first time.

“I’m Marina Aloera Crus. Nice to meet you\!” Marina replied cheerfully.

“I-I’m Skia Thornbull Yu Ga Tali Cheyni. P-P-Pleased to meet you\!” Skia
stammered, her usual nervous demeanor on full display.

I’d already looked into their backgrounds, and it seemed Skia held no prejudice
against commoners. Judging by her emotional aura, she was neither particularly
interested nor hostile—just indifferent. She wouldn’t go out of her way to
interact, but she’d engage normally if the opportunity arose. That was a relief.

As I quietly sighed in relief, Marina continued the conversation.

“I’m a full-fledged commoner, so feel free to treat me casually. Oh, and I’d
love to hear about Zex-sama’s childhood sometime\!” she said with a grin.

“Oh, I see. Got it,” Tara replied, relaxing slightly. “But why ask about
Zex-san’s past? Can’t you just ask Caron-san and the others?”

Marina gave a wry smile. “Well, you know, when it’s Caron-chan and the others,
they... get a bit \*_too_\* enthusiastic.”

“Oh...” Tara’s eyes grew distant as she let out a sound of understanding.

Wait, what? Are Caron and the others going overboard when they talk about me?

I rarely get the chance to hear others discuss my childhood, so this was news to
me. While part of me was curious about what they said, another part felt a
creeping dread. ...Maybe it’s better if I don’t know.

As I tried to shut down that train of thought and escape reality, someone
interrupted the conversation. Naturally, it was the two who apparently got “too
enthusiastic” about my past.

“I couldn’t help but notice a rather rude implication in that,” Orca said,
sounding mildly offended.

“All we’ve done is answer questions honestly,” Caron added. “I’ll admit we
might’ve gotten a bit carried away, but when it’s about Onii-sama, can you blame
us?”

“Exactly\!” Orca agreed.

Their protests, however, found no allies in the room. Everyone else sided with
Marina.

Caron and Orca doubled down, their frustration only growing. The discussion
heated up, and before long, they started recounting my so-called “heroic tales”
(or so they claimed) with exaggerated, bard-like flair.

Guys, I appreciate the enthusiasm, but could you please save this for when I’m
not around?

Sipping the tea I’d brewed myself, I let out a quiet sigh.

“Oh, if it isn’t the top student of the year\!”

We’d finished lunch and were leaving the private room when a voice called out.

The only top students here were me and Tara, and since the voice didn’t specify,
I stopped and turned around to see who it was.

The speaker must have been exiting another private room at the same time. A
young man with hibiscus-colored hair and eyes stood before us, a gentle smile on
his face, accompanied by two attendants.

Everyone except Nina and Marina immediately recognized him. We quickly offered a
polite bow in accordance with etiquette.

“Your Highness, Prince Negro the Third. We hope you are in good spirits today—”

“Oh, no need for formalities,” Negro interrupted. “We’re at the academy, so
let’s keep things casual.”

“As you wish,” I replied.

With Negro’s permission, we relaxed our postures, though only slightly.

Prince Negro the Third. It had been six or seven years since I last saw him in
person—since that duel, if I recall correctly.

Unlike Arianorth, he hasn’t drawn much attention from intelligence reports.
Meeting him face-to-face confirmed it: he seemed the very picture of
harmlessness.

Yet, there was something... off about him. It’s hard to describe—a kind of
prickling sensation, like catching something flickering at the edge of your
vision, only to find nothing when you look directly at it.

“Congratulations on your enrollment, Your Highness. Were you also having lunch?”
I asked.

“Thank you. Yes, though unlike you, Earl, I was dining alone,” Negro said with a
shrug. His tone wasn’t sarcastic but rather carried a hint of genuine envy.

If he were an ordinary noble, this would be the moment to invite him to join us
next time. But for me, that wasn’t an option.

Foranada is aligned with the First Prince’s faction. If I, the head of the
house, were to dine with the Third Prince, it would spark all sorts of
speculation and rumors.

So, I let his comment pass with a vague smile and steered the conversation
elsewhere.

“By ‘top student,’ were you referring to Tara, perhaps?”

“Oh, right, you're at the top of your year too, aren’t you, Earl? My apologies
for the confusion,” Negro said.

“No need to apologize, Your Highness.”

“Is that so? Well, let’s get back on track. As you guessed, I was addressing
Tara-jou. I wanted to meet the commoner who outshone nobles and royals to claim
the top spot. It’s hard to approach her in the classroom with all the noble eyes
around, you know. I didn’t expect her to be dining with the Earl of Foranada,
though.”

His expression was cheerful, but his eyes were sharp, studying us closely.

Hmm. The information he mentioned checks out, not that I doubted it.

Prince Negro’s reputation is... average. He’s less capable than First Prince
Wiemlay but broad-minded, less skilled in combat than Second Prince Gray but
still adept, and less politically savvy than First Princess Arianorth but
competent enough. He’s above average compared to most but unremarkable among
royalty.

The refreshing air he shares with Wiemlay feels like a calculated act, meant to
lower defenses and draw out information. That’s likely his way of navigating the
world.

Compared to Arianorth, though, his approach is somewhat clumsy. It won’t work on
me.

Keeping a smile on my face, I responded to his inquiry.

“Tara is our childhood friend. She’s from the Foranada castle town, so we used
to play together when we were kids. The same goes for her brother.”

I spoke honestly but laced my voice with a subtle warning, a hint of pressure to
make my point clear.

Someone as sharp as Negro would pick up on it. I’m not keen on seeing my
childhood friend dragged into the kingdom’s murky politics.

“...I see. Playing in the castle town sounds enviable. We royals weren’t allowed
such freedoms,” Negro replied.

“I wasn’t either, believe it or not. I was quite the mischievous kid,” I said.

“Oh, I see. So even the Earl had a playful side back then,” Negro said,
chuckling.

The conversation shifted to safer, more neutral topics. He seemed to have
understood my intent.

That was fine, but what irritated me was Caron and the others tilting their
heads at the “believe it or not” part, as if I were some perpetual troublemaker.

After exchanging a few more words, Negro took his leave.

Despite a few annoyances, I managed to navigate the encounter without stirring
up trouble.

A month had passed since the start of the new term, and no major issues had
arisen. The newly introduced “Combat System” had been used a few times by
second- and third-year students, and the system Noma and I had developed was
running smoothly.

As for the first-years, they seemed preoccupied with adjusting to their new
lives. City living, pseudo-independence, and a barrage of advanced classes—even
talented students needed time to acclimate. They hardly had the leisure to
engage in “Combat.”

That said, it was about time for some first-years to start making moves. While
second- and third-years were familiar with the academy’s dynamics, the
first-years, unaware of the reality, were a bit of an unknown variable.

Come to think of it, in the original story, interactions between different
grades were rare, but one avenue for such exchanges was the “Combat System.”
Opponents were chosen randomly, and sometimes, those included students from
other years.

If that’s the case, I should anticipate the possibility of challenges in reality
as well. First-years, who haven’t witnessed our strength firsthand, are
especially likely to act recklessly. I’m a prime target as a non-elemental, of
course, but Marina, a commoner, and Skia, who hasn’t publicly displayed her
abilities, should be cautious too.

Just as I was mulling this over—

“You there, the commoner Marina. I, Idio Roasas Yu Tan Zakotto, challenge you to
a ‘Combat’\!”

At last, someone had the audacity to challenge our Foranada group.

The scene unfolded in the second-year A1 classroom, right after a mandatory
lecture, with many classmates still present. The room buzzed with
murmurs—reactions like “What an idiot,” “He’s about to get humbled,” and “So
reckless.”

Idio appeared to be a first-year. He had a decent build, and his red hair and
eyes suggested an affinity for fire magic.

His earlier words and the fact that he strode out of the classroom without
considering the possibility of refusal revealed him to be an elitist brimming
with confidence.

Well, as the son of a viscount, he \*\*is\*\* part of the elite, but his
attitude toward Marina, who’s closely tied to an earl’s family, spoke volumes
about his warped personality. As a noble, he’d score a failing grade.

“Uh...” Marina said, her expression one of bewilderment as Idio stormed out of
the classroom.

I turned to her and said, “Do what you want, Marina.”

My words carried the implicit message that it was fine to decline the challenge.

After about ten seconds of hesitation, she made her decision.

“Thanks for looking out for me. But I think showing my strength now will make
things easier later.”

She had a point. In last year’s individual tournament, Marina fought as a
standard mage, not revealing her spirit magic abilities. The students here
didn’t know how much stronger she’d become. Displaying a fraction of her power
now would likely deter reckless challengers in the future.

Before the second-year A-Class building, Marina’s “Combat” with Idio began.

The process was straightforward. Declaring the start of a “Combat” to the
magical wristband activated its functions, ensuring no serious harm would come
to either participant.

\*_Beep_.\* “Ten seconds until the ‘Combat’ begins.”

Marina and Idio faced each other from a distance of twenty meters. The only
sound between them was the wristband’s automated voice.

Our classmates, including me, gathered as spectators around them, maintaining a
tense silence. While Idio was of little interest, everyone was curious to see
how Marina, a member of Foranada, would fight.

“Go, Marina\!” Caron cheered quietly.

“You’ve got this, Marina-chan\!” Orca added.

“Fight hard, Marina,” Nina said.

“Don’t embarrass us,” Minerva chimed in.

Their voices were soft to avoid breaking Marina’s focus, but their trust in her
was unmistakable.

Shion and Skia, though silent, were likely cheering for her in their hearts.

\*\*Three, two, one... Begin the battle.\*\*

The signal to start rang out.

Idio struck first.

“\[Boost\]\! \[Heat Veil\]\!”

After casting two low-tier fire spells, he gripped his two-handed spear and
charged.

A strength-enhancing buff and a spell to imbue his weapon with heat—Idio was
clearly a frontliner. But starting with low-tier magic? His skill level was easy
to gauge.

Even with a buff, the boost was minimal. Marina, a pure backliner capable of
high-level \[Body Enhancement\], easily outpaced him. His sharply thrust spear
sliced through empty air.

If Idio had prioritized speed, he might’ve had a chance. But his magic choices
screamed offense-focused, and against Marina, who could dodge with ease, his
true potential remained untapped.

His relentless attacks didn’t land a single hit. Marina evaded every strike with
minimal, graceful movements.

After about five minutes, she muttered, “Alright, I’ve got his pattern down.”

Despite the ongoing barrage, she stopped moving entirely.

Idio didn’t hesitate, thrusting his spear mercilessly.

But—

“What\?!”

The spear missed. Without moving her feet, Marina tilted her upper body just
enough to dodge by a hair’s breadth.

His subsequent thrusts met the same fate. Her movements grew even more refined,
making it seem as if the \*_spear_\* was avoiding \*_her_\*.

“Impressive,” I said, letting out a breath of admiration.

Marina wasn’t particularly athletic—her close-combat talent was practically
nonexistent. Yet, she was holding her own against a decently skilled spearman,
thanks to her extraordinary perception.

Recently, she’d formed a contract with a spirit. Her water spirit, Maimu,
possessed perception abilities that could surpass even mine in certain
situations. Those skills, though slightly diminished, were imparted to Marina as
her contractor.

Using that heightened perception, she was likely predicting the spear’s
movements. It was a technique akin to my \[Magic Vision\].

Idio attacked with full force, while Marina dodged with minimal effort. Given
the stamina difference between a frontliner and a backliner, the outcome was
obvious.

“Haa... huff...” Idio panted, his steps faltering.

Marina, meanwhile, hadn’t broken a sweat. The victor was clear.

“Maimu-chan, whirlpool,” Marina said.

“Got it\!” the water spirit perched on her shoulder replied adorably.

In an instant, a massive whirlpool of water engulfed the exhausted Idio. The
mid-tier spell wasn’t overwhelmingly powerful, but in his drained state, he
couldn’t resist. Swept away by the current, he was flung into the air and
crashed to the ground.

Thanks to the wristband, he was unharmed, but he’d depleted his stamina and
passed out.

A brief silence followed, broken by a shrill buzzer.

\*\*“The ‘Combat’ has concluded.”\*\*

The wristband’s declaration sparked an “Ooh\!” from the crowd.

The match wasn’t flashy, but it showcased Marina’s strength as an A1-Class
student. Her childhood friend, the hero Yudai, gaped in astonishment.

Caron and the others rushed to Marina, showering her with praise.

Watching them, I gave a small smile.

Well done, Marina.

The day after Marina defeated the first-year, “Combats” began popping up all
over the academy.

It was no surprise that most participants were first-years. Upperclassmen, who
had a better grasp of their own and others’ abilities, weren’t in a rush to
challenge anyone. Students in C-Class and below, where skill levels were more
evenly matched, fought frequently, but others mostly observed.

The first-years, however, threw themselves into battles with a “who cares”
attitude. Whether due to youthful impulsiveness or ignorance of their limits,
they seemed to relish the straightforward cycle of fighting and proving
themselves. Some even challenged upperclassmen.

The “Combat” we stumbled upon was one such match between first-years. Orca,
Nina, and I—free after a canceled lecture—were on our way to grab some tea when
we came across the fight.

One combatant was a young man wielding a one-handed sword. His technique was
overly textbook but well-balanced, blending swordsmanship and magic—a solid
magic swordsman.

The other was a jackal-type beastwoman dual-wielding swords of different lengths
with impressive dexterity. Both her swordplay and magic were of a high caliber.

The match leaned heavily in the beastwoman’s favor. The young man aimed for an
upset, but the skill gap was wider than he’d anticipated.

A glance was enough to grasp the fight’s flow. Normally, I’d have moved on
without much thought, but something caught my interest.

I recognized the woman. Not personally, but one-sidedly. She was Eclara Velrade
Lampros, the third-ranked student among the new first-years.

Her skill wasn’t what drew my attention—it was her family name, Lampros.

The Lampros family was once an earl’s house in the Holy Kingdom, closely allied
with the Garbauda earldom and part of the same faction. But because of that
affiliation, they were caught up in the civil war years ago. While not directly
consumed by the conflict, the fallout destroyed them. No nobles bearing the
Lampros name remain in the kingdom; most were scattered as slaves.

Eclara was a survivor of that family. According to intelligence, she spent her
first five years shuffled between owners as a slave before being purchased by
the Iraka barony. Her enrollment in the academy suggests she’s been freed, but
her life up to now must have been grueling.

“Eclara-sama...” Orca murmured beside me.

As his words suggested, Orca and Eclara were old acquaintances. Unlike Nina, who
was under house arrest and had no contact, Orca had played with Eclara a few
times as a child due to their families’ alliance.

It was over a decade ago, so Orca’s memories of her were faint. There were no
dramatic, manga-like moments of shared significance. That he even vaguely
recalled those days spoke to his impressive memory.

We’d actually identified Eclara at the entrance ceremony when Orca spotted her
name on the top students’ list. Foranada’s intelligence unit investigated, and
despite delays due to her frequent change of owners, we confirmed a few days ago
that the Eclara fighting before us was indeed the former earl’s daughter.

Perhaps because of those faint childhood memories, Orca’s feelings toward her
were complex, despite their lack of deep connection. He’d been shaken when he
found her name, and seeing her in person now stirred his emotions further.

“There’s not much to see here. Let’s move on,” I said.

“Agreed,” Nina replied.

But Orca shook his head.

“I’m fine. She’s noticed us, so leaving now might give the wrong impression,” he
said with a weak laugh.

He was right. Though Eclara was focused on the “Combat,” most of her attention
was on us—or rather, Orca. It was clear she had business with him.

We could’ve ignored her, but since Orca was willing to talk, I decided to
respect his choice. His feelings took precedence.

The “Combat” ended shortly after, with Eclara securing an overwhelming victory
despite her divided focus.

Ignoring the defeated swordsman slumped on the ground, Eclara approached us, her
shoes clicking purposefully.

Her appearance was striking, the word “beautiful” fitting her perfectly. Her
glossy, indigo hair flowed smoothly, and her piercing, twin-star-like eyes
exuded strength. Her dignified, mature aura belied her fifteen years, making her
seem far older than a new student.

Reaching us, Eclara offered an elegant curtsy.

“A pleasure to meet you, Earl of Foranada and ‘Dragon Buster’ Nina-sama. I am
Eclara Velrade Lampros, a first-year. You may know of me, but I am a descendant
of the fallen Lampros earldom. If you would deign to remember my name, even
faintly, I would be most honored.”

Her curtsy, lifting her uniform skirt, was flawless—a testament to skills
ingrained in her despite no longer being a noble.

Raising her head, she turned her gaze to Orca.

Orca’s expression remained steady, but his shoulders trembled slightly.

“It’s been a while, Orca... Oh, I should address you properly, as you now
outrank me. My apologies, Orca-sama,” Eclara said.

“Please, no honorifics, Eclara-sama,” Orca replied. “Even if it’s old history,
it feels wrong to be addressed so formally by someone from a family once equal
to my former guardians. It’s bad for my heart.”

“I could say the same,” Eclara countered. “Being addressed with an honorific by
an active noble makes \*_me_\* uneasy.”

“...Fine, Eclara. Is this alright?” Orca asked.

“Yes, that will do, Orca. Please forgive my formal speech—it’s my default,” she
replied.

“That’s not playing fair, is it?” Orca teased.

Since their reunion, Eclara had started visiting Orca frequently. They usually

deepening. It felt like they’d gone from old acquaintances to something closer
to regular friends. Right now, they were even planning to hang out on the

With so many sharing the same sentiment, it’s likely there’s \*something\* about
Eclara. Whether it’s serious or not remains unclear, but staying cautious
wouldn’t hurt. I’d need to keep my guard up.

The next class period was free, so I found myself idly watching Orca and Eclara
chat in the nearly empty classroom.

“A-Are you... jealous?”

Since it was just me, Orca, and Eclara in the room, she seemed less hesitant

“Jealous of what?” I asked, tilting my head. Her question came out of nowhere

Skia, however, grew even more animated.

“U-Unaware\! Tch, this... this is a classic trope\! It’s perfect\!”

Her words didn’t seem directed at me anymore. They were more like a monologue,

Still, I let out a wry chuckle without any real anger. I was well aware of
Skia’s social awkwardness. And upon reflection, I realized what had her so
worked up.

I’d been watching Orca and Eclara this whole time, so she must’ve mistaken me
for being jealous of Eclara.

Skia was a huge fan of BL fiction, particularly stories featuring younger,
boyish characters as the “heroine.” Orca and I were apparently right in her
strike zone. She’d even started drawing manga based on us in secret, so there
was no mistaking it.

For someone like Skia, who shipped us so intensely, this situation must’ve been
pure bliss. A third party swooping in to steal the “heroine,” sparking jealousy
in the “hero”—yep, as she said, a classic trope. I’ll admit, I kind of like
those stories too.

I don’t have anything against same-sex relationships. It’s possible to love
someone regardless of gender. But right now, I didn’t have \*those\* kinds of
feelings for Orca. I might feel a bit lonely as he grows more independent, but

Correcting her, though, felt like too much effort. Skia was already lost in her

ever make—while sporting a sloppy grin.

Let sleeping gods lie. Engaging with her now would be a terrible idea.

\*_Oh_,\* I thought, nodding inwardly.

“Pleased to meet you, heir of the Iraka barony.”

Was his borderline-impolite attitude a calculated way to lower my guard? I’d
have to dig into that later.

Indeed, Kiteros was the one who had freed Eclara from slavery. According to our

He genuinely wanted to rebuild their friendship, but her calculated approach

The gap between his old friend and his current ones might be something he was
still grappling with.
