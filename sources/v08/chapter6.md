# Chapter 6: Successor and Merchant

The day after Orca’s confession, I—Zex—headed to my office before attending the
academy. Seated at my desk, I was reviewing two sets of documents.

It was nearly dawn, so I was alone in the office—

“Excuse me,” said Minerva, entering after a knock.

“I’m intruding,” added Nina, following her.

It was highly unusual for these two to visit the office.

One is my fiancée but still a lady of another house; the other, despite her
title, is merely an adventurer. Considering their positions, I’d heard they
avoided places like this, where sensitive information could be accessed.

Bracing for an emergency, I tensed, but Minerva gave a wry smile. “No need to be
on guard,” she said. “It’s not an urgent matter.”

“If it was urgent, I’d use the magical communicator,” said Nina.

“That makes sense,” I replied.

The magical communicator, constantly updated, is a reliable means of contact.
For urgent matters, there’d be no reason not to use it.

Still, while that cleared one thing up, the core question remained. “So, what’s
up?” I asked. “It’s rare for you two to come here.”

We have plenty of chances to meet—at breakfast, on the way to the academy. If
it’s not urgent, there’s no reason to visit the office.

Nina answered concisely. “Orca’s situation,” she said. “We were worried about
how it turned out.”

“I see,” I said. “It might be awkward to ask elsewhere.”

I knew they’d dragged Orca to that round-table room the day before yesterday,
likely to discuss Eclara’s proposal. They must be curious about our
conversation, though it seems they’d already reached a conclusion during their
discussion.

Hmm, how should I respond?

After a moment’s hesitation, I gave a brief answer. “To cut to the chase, Orca
confessed to me,” I said.

““Huh?”” they exclaimed in unison, eyes wide and frozen.

Apparently, Minerva and Nina hadn’t known Orca had realized his feelings.

Come to think of it, he’d said, “I reflected last night.” So, while they sparked
the realization, he came to it alone. If someone they thought was oblivious
acted so quickly, it would indeed feel like a bolt from the blue.

“Well, we did nudge him a bit...” said Minerva, trailing off.

“I didn’t expect him to realize it,” said Nina.

“Exactly,” said Minerva. “We only got his honest thoughts about the Lampros
matter.”

“They called him oblivious, and that’s what tipped him off,” I added.

The two, still bewildered, nodded in unison, saying, “Oh.”

“It’s like he reasoned it out,” said Minerva. “Very Orca, but what a strange way
to realize it.”

“Confessing the same day takes guts,” said Nina. “Most people can’t move that
fast after realizing.”

“Despite everything, Orca’s a boy, isn’t he?” said Minerva. “I keep forgetting
that.”

“Yeah, I forgot too,” said Nina. “Or rather, I’m forgetting now.”

They carried on a conversation that would likely upset Orca if he heard it.

I understood their feelings, though. As Nina said, the gap between realization
and confession was short. A relationship that had crawled along suddenly sped up
like a bullet train. Their confusion was natural.

If I were a third party, I’d brush it off as “these things happen,” but being
involved made it tricky.

Not that I object to Orca’s confession—it’s just that I had no time to prepare.
My half-hearted response feels pathetic.

—Enough about my shortcomings.

“Anyway, no need to worry about Orca,” I said. “He’s recovering well, I think.”

“Good,” said Minerva.

“That’s a relief,” said Nina.

As I wrapped up, they both sighed in relief, visibly concerned.

That made me wonder. “By the way, where’s Caron?” I asked. “I’d expect her to
rush here for updates.”

For someone who’d do anything for family, I thought she’d be the first to check
in.

Minerva and Nina gave wry smiles.

“She did rush over—to your bedroom,” said Minerva.

“She tried something like a night visit and got caught by Shion, who’s lecturing
her,” said Nina.

“We considered the time and headed to your bedroom first,” said Minerva. “That’s
where we saw Caroline sitting in seiza.”

Assuming I was still asleep, Caron must have snuck in, only to be spotted by
Shion.

“She’s getting more extreme, isn’t she?” I said, exasperated.

“You just have to accept her as she is,” said Minerva. “You know that, right?”

“Resignation is key,” said Nina.

I sighed at their utterly unhelpful advice.

Indeed, I have to accept Caron, but I wish she’d calm down a bit.

Seeing my reaction, Minerva spoke up. “She’s just anxious because her position
is ambiguous,” she said. “Once you accept her, she’ll settle down somewhat.”

“So I just endure for now?” I asked.

“Exactly,” said Minerva.

I might need to let her blow off steam appropriately to prevent things from
escalating further. I already make time for her daily, so maybe increasing that
would help.

A brief silence fell as the topic paused.

Breaking the gap, Minerva spoke. “By the way,” she said, “what work are you
doing this early? And are you actually sleeping?”

“I was wondering about that too,” said Nina, joining in. “Especially the
latter.”

Their pressure seemed to intensify slightly.

Was it concern for my well-being? Prodding might stir trouble, so I answered
calmly.

I shrugged. “I’m sleeping fine,” I said. “Thanks to \[Time Stoplll\], I’m
effectively cutting sleep time. As for work, I was reading reports on Eclara-jou
and the ‘Outers’ that finally came in.”

“\[Time Stop\]... that insane magic,” said Minerva. “Fine, I’ll let that slide.
But the ‘Outers’ aside, the Lampros report coming now is awfully late. Weren’t
you investigating her for a while?”

“Her masters changed frequently during her slave days,” I explained. “She
bounced around so much it was hard to gather info.”

“It was all tangled up?” asked Nina.

“Yeah,” I said. “The clincher is that she was sold to another country for a
time. It wasn’t long, but details from that period are unclear. Gathering info
from the chaotic city-state cluster is tough.”

The city-state cluster is a real hassle, like Japan’s Warring States period.
Small states constantly fight, and it’s a wonder the conflict doesn’t spill over
here.

Given such turmoil, information collection is understandably difficult.

Minerva frowned at my response. “She was in the city-state cluster for a time?”
she asked.

“For a bit,” I said. “Until just before being picked up by the Iraka Barony, she
was with a merchant house in the Kashiyo Kingdom.”

“Hmm,” said Minerva, her voice skeptical.

I understood her suspicion. Eclara, already suspicious for aiming to revive her
house, having opaque parts in her history invites speculation. I suspect she’s
hiding something too.

Still, there’s no evidence yet. Using authority is an option, but that’s a last
resort. Such methods create distortions and often backfire painfully.

“For now, we keep an eye on Eclara-jou,” I said. “We stay cautious, but there’s
little we can do proactively.”

“No helping it, then,” said Minerva, sighing but nodding in understanding.

“What about the ‘Outers’?” asked Nina.

She seemed interested—or rather, she was asking since the topic came up.

Since neither seemed too invested, I gave a summary. “We identified the
ringleader,” I said. “A decently sized merchant house called Ludenshi.”

“Never heard of it,” said Minerva.

“No surprise,” I said. “They operate in the northeast, a region far from us.”

Minerva’s confusion was expected. Despite their size, they’re just a local shop.

Still, it was surprising that a mere merchant house led such a large
organization. Was their small size a cover? We should consider a bigger
mastermind.

“Any arrests?” asked Nina.

I shook my head. “We formed a joint raid team with Wimley and searched the
house,” I said. “But the main suspect, the Ludenshi head, was absent. His son
and employees said he’s been uninvolved in operations for years, focusing on
activities abroad.”

We found some “Outers,” confirming Ludenshi’s involvement, but the key figure
was missing.

“The country he’s been active in is the Kashiyo Kingdom,” I added.

“That’s...” started Minerva.

“Where Eclara was?” finished Nina.

Exactly. The pieces connected here. Or rather, there’s a common thread, but no
confirmed link. Still, it’s too suspicious to dismiss as coincidence.

“So, investigating the Kashiyo Kingdom’s affairs is my current task,” I said.

“Telling us not to suspect something is impossible,” said Minerva.

“Too blatant,” said Nina.

As Nina said, it’s too obvious. We should consider the possibility of a trap.

But with no other leads, we have no choice but to push forward, trap or not.

“Once personnel are secured, I’ll head to Kashiyo,” I said. “I’ll need you to
hold down the fort.”

“You’re going too?” asked Nina, her tone questioning why a count would go.

Before I could answer, Minerva spoke. “Because it’s a country with no diplomatic
ties,” she said. “Even for a banned substance investigation, we can’t probe
openly. That’s why Zex, who can operate covertly with \[Alternate World: Baule
de Tesoro\], is needed.”

“Exactly,” I said. “This requires power that absolutely can’t be detected.”

If the kingdom noticed, it could spark a war, given their unstable situation.

You might think avoiding the hornet’s nest is better.

Honestly, without me, we’d likely have let it go. My exceptional abilities make
covert investigation possible.

Hearing this, Nina frowned slightly. “Drawing the short straw?” she asked.

“Not quite,” I said. “This is a request from the Holy Kingdom. It’ll create
significant leverage for Foranada, so it’s worth my effort. Otherwise, I
wouldn’t bother.”

Resolving the “Outers” case is enough to silence loudmouths. It’s worth my
involvement.

“When do you leave?” asked Minerva.

“Within a week at the latest,” I said.

“Alright,” said Minerva. “Leave the home front to us.”

“Got it covered,” said Nina.

Minerva puffed out her chest confidently, and Nina clenched her fist lightly.

Their dependable attitudes brought a smile to my face. “Right, I’m counting on
you,” I said. “Oh, it’s about breakfast time. Let’s go together.”

With that, I gently took their hands to escort them.

Initially surprised, they soon smiled happily. The smiles of those I cherish are
irreplaceable.

The news arrived during lunch with the usual group.

“Huh?\!” I exclaimed, my voice rising.

“““““““...”””””””” The others stared at me.

The report was so unexpected I forgot my surroundings and raised my voice,
drawing everyone’s attention.

Taking a breath, I apologized calmly. “Sorry,” I said. “It was an unexpected
report.”

“Is everything okay?” asked Minerva, speaking for the group.

I nodded. “It’s fine,” I said. “They found a potential issue, not an incident. I
just need to handle the aftermath.”

“I see,” said Minerva, her expression skeptical. “If you need help, don’t
hesitate.”

Perhaps sensing something from my phrasing, she pressed me not to handle it
alone. Her tone was grumpy, but her concern was pure kindness. My fiancée, as
always.

“I know,” I said with a wry smile. “I won’t hide anything.”

I fully understand the limits of solving things alone. That’s why I’m training
them, and my words were sincere.

“Good,” said Minerva. “Do your best at work.”

““““““Have a good day\!”””””” chorused the others.

My intent seemed to get through, and Minerva responded as such, with everyone
else sending me off warmly.

“Be back soon,” I said, smiling and opening a \[Phase Connection Gate\].

The destination was Foranada Castle, specifically a room for storing mail.
External deliveries are collected here, inspected, and then passed to the
intended staff.

Three people were already present.

The first was Noma, the earth spirit who reported to me and my contracted
partner. Floating with her palm-sized body, she was vigilantly watching the
exit.

The second was Kaiser Gauera Byakudai, Orca’s older brother, now working as a
civil servant in Foranada. He looked troubled, deep in thought.

The last was Gad Zauschuz Byakudai, Orca’s other older brother and Kaiser’s
younger sibling. Unlike them, he was burly and served in our knight corps. Like
Kaiser, he was frowning deeply.

As I entered, their gazes focused on me, their expressions easing slightly, a
sign of their wariness toward outsiders.

Stopping the two from kneeling in greeting, I set up an anti-eavesdropping
barrier before addressing the issue.

“Is that the letter from the report?” I asked, eyeing two envelopes on the
central table, addressed to Kaiser and Gad.

Noma answered. “That’s right,” she said. “As your eyes can probably tell, those
letters are imbued with attributeless magic.”

—Letters imbued with attributeless magic, addressed to both Kaiser and Gad.

That was the urgent report. Apparently, Noma discovered them while passing by.
Kaiser and Gad were here separately, summoned about incoming mail.

The issue wasn’t just the “magic” but the “attributeless magic” specifically. It
couldn’t be ignored.

Attributeless magic’s essence is mental magic. My method of materializing magic
into weapons is unorthodox; mental magic is its true domain. In other words,
these letters might carry magic to manipulate someone’s mind.

Such dangerous items couldn’t be left alone. Since Noma, an earth spirit,
couldn’t discern details, I, the expert, rushed over.

Noma, her brown eyes serious, explained. “Even if I hadn’t found them, an
inspector with \[Magic Sight Glasses\] would’ve,” she said. “But the magic is
too strong. Unless you have resistance like you or me, it’s best not to look
directly.”

“Seeing has its pros and cons,” I said.

“Yeah,” said Noma. “Seeing creates a connection, making the spell easier to
affect you.”

“We should add protection to the \[Magic Sight Glasses\],” I suggested.

“Definitely,” said Noma. “With a bomb like this found, we can’t assume it won’t
happen again.”

Indeed, this was an unexpected event.

The technique of imbuing magic, used in creating magic tools, is quite advanced.
It demands greater skill than casting the spell directly. To put it simply,
imbuing level 5 fire magic requires level 8 proficiency.

In this world, mental magic is an unknown category. It was recognized in the era
of magic priests, but no trace remains today, not even in ancient texts.
White-haired individuals, adept at mental magic, are branded as incompetent and
persecuted.

Thus, another mental magic user appearing is astronomically unlikely. Imbuing it
is even more unthinkable.

It’s a headache. If Noma calls the magic “too strong,” the caster capable of
imbuing it into letters is a serious threat. Depending on the spell, we may have
to treat them as an enemy of Foranada.

Mental magic is troublesome when turned against you. While Foranada’s upper
echelons are fine, those not constantly using \[Body Enhancement\] could be
easily manipulated.

I avoid such uses to not be swayed by power, but there’s no guarantee the
opponent shares that ethic. What a pain.

“For now, let’s examine the letters,” I said.

Thinking can wait. First, I need to identify the imbued magic.

Activating mental defenses and \[Body Enhancement: Overflow\], I ensured no
mental magic could penetrate.

Then, I picked up the letters. “Indeed, they’re imbued with mental magic,” I
confirmed.

The spell’s details were unclear, but it was on the contents, not the envelopes.

Enclosing myself in a strong barrier to prevent magic leakage, I opened the
letters.

If I had to describe it, it’d be a “whoosh.” The magic that leaked upon opening
felt as sticky and unpleasant as a summer locker room.

I sighed. “Ugh...” I muttered.

I understood immediately. The sender was an enemy of Foranada. Why?

“The magic is \[Charm\],” I said. “And it’s at the level of a magic eye.”

“\[Charm\]...” said Kaiser, his voice low.

“That’s bad,” said Gad.

“A magic eye?\!” exclaimed Noma.

Each reacted differently.

Kaiser and Gad, silent due to their positions, couldn’t stay quiet at “charm.”
Though mental magic isn’t widely known, the term’s implications were clear.

Noma reacted strongly to the “magic eye” part, as she was the only one here
familiar with it, an expected response.

Addressing them wouldn’t progress things, so I read the contents.

It took mere seconds; the message was concise:

“The true successor of Garbauda rises now. Comrades, raise your swords and stand
against the current regime.”

It was unmistakably a call for rebellion—armed uprising. The \[Charm\]’s initial
command, “Amass forces while living normally,” ruled out misinterpretation.
Their hostility was confirmed.

Garbauda, huh. In the original story, it was merely a trigger for Orca and
Rina’s arcs, but now it’s causing trouble.

It makes sense. It’s more unnatural that peace lasted this long after such
fierce conflict. The hidden issues are just surfacing after a decade.

I set aside speculation on the backstory. Practical matters come first. I
activated my left eye’s magic eye, \[Magic eye of White Radiance\], to analyze
the letters.

“Tracking the magic’s source is impossible,” I said. “But I identified the
caster’s name.”

“Who is it, Master?” asked Noma, leaning forward, aware of the danger a magic
eye user poses to enemies.

I answered plainly. “Ward Isente Garbauda,” I said. “From the letter, likely a
member of the former Garbauda Earl family. Do either of you know the name?”

“Not at all,” said Gad.

“Unfortunately, neither do I,” said Kaiser. “But it’s odd. Even as mere barons,
we knew our liege’s lineage...”

As Kaiser said, it’s strange. For vassals like them not to know is nearly
impossible. Plus, Foranada’s intelligence network didn’t catch it.

One possibility: the Garbauda family hid Ward’s existence thoroughly.

An earl house hiding a successor is usually unthinkable, but it’s plausible
here. Ward likely possesses a \[Charm\] magic eye, possibly a mental magic user.

Anyone can use mental magic, but only those with attributeless
aptitude—colorless—face no restrictions.

A colorless individual born into an earl house would damage its reputation
significantly. Garbauda’s cover-up was understandable. Foranada’s response is
the abnormal one.

Still, I can’t be certain. Charm spells can be used by fire, light, or dark
aptitude holders. Magic eye manifestation depends on the user’s magic quality.
Like my fire-attribute \[Blazing Heaven Eye\], non-colorless users can wield
attributeless magic eyes.

“Speculating with insufficient info is pointless,” I said. “Kaiser, Gad, contact
everyone you know from the old days. Similar items were likely sent out.
Investigate and report if anyone’s under \[Charm\]’s influence. I’ll handle it.
This task takes top priority. I’ll notify your department heads.”

““Understood,”” they replied in unison, leaving together.

“For us, it’s investigating areas they can’t cover,” I said to Noma. “Postpone
lower-priority tasks for this. ...We’re short on manpower.”

Even Foranada struggles with such a broad scope. Garbauda’s involvement
implicates many beastmen as potential targets.

I considered informing Wimley but decided against it. “Let’s hold off,” I said.

It could cause unnecessary chaos. I doubt the Holy Royal Family could handle
\[Charm\]-affected individuals properly.

It also risks creating pointless friction between humans and beastmen.

“Noma, we’ll analyze the \[Charm\] spell and craft counter-magic tools,” I said.

“Haha, sleepless nights for days?” she teased.

“No worries,” I said. “With \[Time Stop\], it’s a moment in reality, and I can
secure sleep.”

Would my magic hold until the tools were complete? That was my only concern.

through interrogation.

I twisted my neck and shoulders to loosen up, then spoke calmly. “Our paths are
completely opposite.”

There was no need to discuss further. To resolve the situation, I prepared to
unleash a finishing spell.

But a voice interrupted me.

“Zex-nii,” Orca called.

It wasn’t loud, but it resonated clearly through the area.

There’s no way I’d ignore my adorable little brother’s words. Fortunately,
Eclara was too wary to make a move.

“What’s up?” I asked.

“Can I make a selfish request?” Orca said.

“Depends on the request...” I replied.

Even as a doting brother, I don’t make promises lightly. I believe a good older
brother firmly refuses requests that could have negative consequences.

Orca gave a wry smile. “Can you leave this to me?”

“You want to take on Eclara?” I asked.

“No,” he said. “I want to take on everyone here.”

“...” I fell silent, stunned.

It was an uncharacteristically bold proposal from Orca.

He values emotions but leans toward rational thinking, much like Arianorth. I
never would’ve expected him to want to face not just Eclara but all the shadowy
eija as well. Normally, he’d choose the safest, most certain approach.

Orca pressed on. “It’s okay. I’ve got a solid plan to win. I want to be the one
to put an end to her.”

“...Alright. It’s yours,” I said.

My hesitation was brief. Seeing the unwavering resolve in Orca’s eyes, I
couldn’t dismiss his determination.

He said he had a strategy, and if anything unexpected happened, I could step in.
With that in mind, I relaxed my shoulders and shifted to spectator mode.

“Eclara,” Orca said, his voice steady. “As your former friend, I’ll be the one
to stop you.”

Eclara’s eyes flicked toward me, not Orca. “I was bracing myself for what you’d
do, but... entrusting everyone to Orca alone? Are you mocking me?” she said, her
words directed at me, the strongest person present.

I didn’t respond. I’d already handed the stage to Orca. Now, it was just a
matter of watching his actions.

Eclara clicked her tongue. “Tch. Fine. Orca, I’ll crush you first and take you
hostage. That’ll give me leverage against Foranada-haku. He doesn’t seem
inclined to interfere further.”

At Eclara’s battle-ready words, the eija, previously standing motionless,
shifted into combat stances.

Now, let’s see how Orca handles this.

Honestly, the odds are in Eclara’s favor. Even with Orca’s skill in magic
manipulation, he’s not specialized for combat. He could probably take down three
eija unscathed, maybe six at his limit, but any more would be tough.

“Go\!” Eclara commanded.

At her order, the eija charged. Unlike Kiteros’s clumsy rush, their attack was
calculated, each one striking with precise timing.

Does the skill of the summoned depend on the summoner? What a hassle Gruewen’s
magic is.

The eija launched a ferocious assault. Orca, however, focused solely on evasion,
dodging their eleven attacks with minimal movements.

It reminded me of Marina’s technique. Lacking her level of sensory ability, Orca
compensated by simultaneously deploying multiple detection spells. His adept use
of heat, vibration, and sound detection was only possible because of his
exceptional magic control.

And that wasn’t the only astonishing thing. While maintaining those detection
spells, Orca was also constructing another spell—likely for a counterattack.
Even I couldn’t help but frown at the mind-bogglingly complex magic he was
weaving.

“Whoa, what the heck is that?\!” Garuna’s voice blurted out, unable to hold
back.

I completely agreed. It was unthinkable to evade enemy attacks while
simultaneously casting three separate spells. It was like running while sewing
four different patterns with both hands and feet—an impossible feat.

Soon, Orca’s spell was complete. Without chanting to reinforce it, he unleashed
it silently.

At first glance, it appeared as glowing particles, like grains of sand dancing
in the wind around him.

But those with keen eyes could tell: those particles were tiny needles. Forged
from earth magic as iron needles, they were coated with searing flames and
high-speed wind. A mere touch would shred a target to pieces—a lethal weapon.

Tens of thousands of these near-invisible, silent needles flew freely through
the air.

The moment this spell was unleashed, the battle was decided.

In the next instant, every eija disintegrated into dust. And—

“...” Eclara collapsed, unconscious, without even a chance to scream. Her limbs
were torn apart, her eyes crushed.

Fortunately, the heat from the needles had cauterized her wounds, preventing her
from bleeding out.

“It’s over, Zex-nii,” Orca said, his voice heavy.

He glanced at Eclara with sadness for only a moment before announcing the end of
the fight. His expression was, as expected, one of deep sorrow.

I approached him, gave him a light hug, and ruffled his hair. “You did great,” I
said.

“...Yeah,” he replied, his voice trembling slightly.

I pretended not to notice the quiver in his voice. He’s a boy with his pride,
after all.

Shortly after, the facility was fully secured.

Though it left a bitter aftertaste, the incident was, for now, resolved.
