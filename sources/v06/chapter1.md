# Chapter 1: The Suspicious Honor Student

September. In the midst of the lingering heat of summer, the second semester at
the academy has begun.

With the second semester comes the full-fledged start of practical magic
classes. As a result, the usual group of friends will have more opportunities to
act separately than before. This is solely dependent on magical aptitude, and no
matter how hard one tries, it cannot be changed.

My mission—Zex Levito San Foranada—is to protect my beloved sister, Caron, from
the fate of death. Although I would prefer to always be by her side, I can only
entrust the parts that become impossible for me to the subordinates. With Caron
and their skills, they should be more than capable of buying enough time until I
can arrive.

Additionally, due to my white hair, I have a higher degree of flexibility in my
curriculum. It’s fortunate that I have more chances to align my classes with my
sister’s.

With that, the practical magic class was upon us. The class I was currently
attending focused on offensive fire magic, and my companions for this subject
were two.

One of them was Caron, formally known as Caroline Flamel de San Foranada. She
was characterized by her wavy blonde hair pulled into a ponytail and her
jewel-like red eyes. With her translucent white skin and well-defined features,
she possessed an impressive physical beauty. Even setting aside my bias as her
older brother, there was no doubt that she was a stunning woman.

In the original game, she was portrayed as a spoiled villainess, but in reality,
she was quite different. Thanks to her upbringing, she had grown into a
kind-hearted girl who showed compassion to everyone. While she did have a bit of
a brother complex, she was a sister I could proudly show off anywhere.

The other was Minerva Allrain de Cali Loramber. She was a petite woman with
black hair styled in twin tails, and she was my fiancée. Her magical talent was
exceptional, and she possessed high aptitude for nearly everything she tried,
making her a gifted young lady from a ducal family. She was almost too good for
me. Well, she was also a classic tsundere, which was an endearing trait.

So, the three of us were attending the fire magic lecture.

Marina, from a different class, was likely in another classroom attending earth
magic lessons with Orca and Nina. Shion was probably accompanying them as well.

“—That concludes the important points for this class. Are there any questions?”

The teacher’s lengthy explanation came to an end, and she directed her attention
toward us.

The instructor for this class was the familiar Merla-sensei, the homeroom
teacher of Class A1. As usual, she wore a somewhat nervous expression, and her
sharp gaze behind her glasses was intense.

No questions arose from the students present, and we quickly transitioned into
the practical part of the lesson.

“As I mentioned earlier, today you will be shooting Fire Arrows at the targets.
Don’t underestimate this low-level spell. Because it is easy to fire rapidly, it
is frequently used in actual combat.”

When Merla-sensei instructed us to “begin at your own pace,” the students
scattered across the spacious training ground. Targets had already been set up
throughout the area, and everyone positioned themselves in front of one.

“Well then, excuse me, Onii-sama,” Caron said politely as she left.

“Make sure to think up a good excuse for yourself,” Minerva added with a rather
sharp retort before walking away.

Her words were biting, but the concern hidden behind them showed that she was
genuinely looking out for me. She was still as difficult to read as ever.

I smiled wryly and crossed my arms, thinking, “What should I do now?”

I had moved in front of a target, following the example of the other students,
but I couldn’t use fire magic. With my white hair, I had no magical affinity
whatsoever—no elemental magic aptitude at all.

You might wonder why I was attending a fire magic class then, but as previously
mentioned, it was because I wanted to stay close to Caron.

Additionally, I needed to attend a certain number of practical magic classes to
avoid failing. While graduating from the academy wasn’t crucial for me, I hoped
Caron would understand the pride I wanted to maintain in front of her and the
others.

That said, standing around like this was also a bit problematic. After all,
Merla-sensei was currently glaring intensely in my direction.

…Ah, it seemed she was getting impatient; she was striding toward me with
determination.

She fixed me with a cold gaze and asked,

“Baron Foranada. Stop standing around in a daze and start shooting your Fire
Arrows. The other students have already begun.”

Her voice carried a hint of mockery. She was aware that I couldn’t use fire
magic and was instructing me nonetheless.

In this regard, I couldn’t complain. I was participating in a class I couldn’t
even manage. From the teacher’s perspective, I was likely a nuisance. Given her
skepticism about my abilities, I could understand why she would act this way.
Therefore, I had no reason to feel angry.

However, I was anxious. Caron, positioned a short distance away, was firing off
Fire Arrows in rapid succession, her gaze fixed intensely on us—specifically, on
Merla-sensei.

I wished she wouldn’t stare at us with such an intense expression while firing
magic. It was incredibly intimidating. Moreover, while she had started off
taking it easy, the increasing power of her shots suggested that the target
might soon be destroyed. Calm down, Caron.

Meanwhile, Minerva was simply completing her tasks. Although she seemed to be
concerned about me, she was managing her emotions well.

It was clear I needed to quickly persuade Merla-sensei; otherwise, this class
would turn into a disaster.

I did my best to remain calm.

“I apologize, Merla-sensei. I am unable to use fire magic, so I cannot fulfill
the assignment this time.”

The moment she heard my response, Merla-sensei’s mockery deepened. Given that I
had admitted I couldn’t do it, it was only natural.

She maintained her composure and asked again, “Then why are you taking my class?
If you cannot use fire magic, it renders the class pointless. Ah, don’t think
I’ll accept the excuse of ‘for the credits.’ You are already the current head of
your family with proven achievements. Falling behind in the academy shouldn’t
affect your standing.”

Yeah, I had anticipated that question. Anyone would throw that at me. It was
more about her desire to eliminate impurities from her class than a genuine
inquiry, but it was still a valid motivation.

That said, I couldn’t simply let myself be dismissed. I had my own reputation to
uphold.

However, I couldn’t answer with the blunt truth of wanting to impress my
sisters, so I offered a different reason. I had prepared for this expected
question, after all.

“Even if I cannot use fire magic, I believe there is still value in learning it.
There may come a time when I must face a fire mage as an opponent. Whether or
not I understand their fighting style will change how I approach the situation.
Therefore, Merla-sensei’s practical class is very beneficial, and I see value in
attending it. Is that reasoning insufficient?”

“…Just don’t disturb the other students.”

“Yes.”

Merla-sensei did not offer any rebuttal to my explanation.

Although it was unusual for the current head of a family to directly engage in
combat with a mage, she couldn’t deny my eagerness to learn. She was somewhat
stubborn, but after all, she was an educator as well.

After that, no further issues arose, and the practical magic class concluded
without incident.

---

“Honestly! Just honestly!”

After school, Caron was fuming inside the carriage on the way back to the annex.
While she didn’t lash out in a fit of rage, her voice was rough, and her cheeks
were puffed up in indignation.

Everyone else in the carriage, including me, could only smile wryly at her
outburst.

“What’s got you so worked up, Caron-onechan? Well, there’s only one reason you’d
be this upset,” Orca, my younger brother-in-law, asked on behalf of everyone. He
was a beastman, with sleek brown hair that revealed a pair of fox ears peeking
through. Coupled with his adorable face, he was incredibly cute.

By the way, he was wearing a skirt as part of his uniform, but he was definitely
male. Those around him often forgot this fact, and I sometimes did as well, but
biologically, he was undoubtedly a boy.

Caron, pouting, answered Orca’s question.

“During most of today’s practical class, the teachers tried to kick Onii-sama
out! I’m so frustrated!”

“So it was about Zex, huh?” Orca chuckled dryly at the predictable answer.

Since it was the first class, there were a lot of practical exercises, and just
as Caron said, I was treated as an inconvenience in most of the lessons.
Although Merla-sensei was more direct, the others subtly requested my departure.

Well, I managed to silence them with the same retort and stayed until the end of
the class, so I had no complaints. I could understand their feelings.

However, it seemed Caron couldn’t take it as easily. She looked utterly vexed.

“I understand how you feel, but it’s a fact that Zex can’t use attribute magic.
You might have to accept that.”

The one echoing my sentiments was Nina, another beastman. With her striking
features, a single braid of brown hair, and her height exceeding 170 cm, she was
the opposite of Orca in terms of appearance.

Though she usually maintained a blank expression with little emotional
fluctuation, she actually harbored feelings for me. Well, I suppose I don’t need
the word “actually.” Her feelings were quite evident through her actions. Right
now, she was snuggling against my arm, her ample chest pressed against me,
testing my resolve.

Nina, an A-rank adventurer, had a more pragmatic outlook, which likely prevented
her from getting swept away by emotions as Caron did. Though her eyes weren’t
smiling, they held a certain depth.

“I know that! But I just can’t accept it!”

Caron typically behaved like a lady in public, but among our usual group, she
was the most emotionally expressive. Given her older brother complex, it was
only natural that she would struggle to accept the situation, even if she
understood it.

Just then,

“By the way, Zex—Zex-sama can’t use attribute magic, right? I totally forgot,
considering how strong he is…”

Marina chimed in with a carefree tone.

With her deep blue hair and pale lavender eyes, she was the only true commoner
in our group. Originally, she had been a love interest for the hero, the main
character of the story, but after some twists and turns, she ended up having
feelings for me.

While she didn’t have a talent for normal magic, she was a rare find with a
strong affinity for spirit magic.

Being the newest member, it seemed she hadn’t yet reconciled the gap between my
strength and my inability to use attribute magic.

At her words, Minerva nodded.

“Well, it’s only natural to feel uneasy at first. When I first learned of his
abilities, I couldn’t believe my ears. Trying to apply common sense to him is a
futile endeavor, so you should adjust your perception sooner rather than later.”

“Yeah, I’ll do my best!”

How rude. It’s as if she’s saying I lack common sense. It’s not that I don’t
know common sense; I simply choose to ignore it. It’s a misunderstanding that
I’d rather not have.

That said, I’ve pointed this out multiple times before. If they continue to say
it, it seems I’ll just have to accept it. Sigh.

After that, the women—one of them is a man—began to chat excitedly about me. I
really wish they’d do that when I’m not around. It’s just so embarrassing.

“Are you going to protest?”

Suddenly, Shion, the last person in the carriage, asked.

She was my personal secretary and a cherished member of my family. Although she
was an elf despised in the Holy Kingdom, my feelings for her remained unchanged.

Her emerald-green eyes were fixed on me, filled with seriousness. If I casually
approved her suggestion, it seemed she would carry it out without hesitation.

I shook my head for a moment.

“I allow the protest, but don’t go all out.”

While maintaining the dignity of the head of the family required a protest,
going all out would likely result in the teachers losing their jobs. If the
headmaster, who feared me, were to take it that far, it could happen.

I didn’t want to accumulate unnecessary grudges. So, just a warning to get the
teachers to adjust their attitudes would be sufficient.

“Understood.”

Though she seemed somewhat dissatisfied, Shion responded affirmatively.

It’s hard to believe just how much I’m loved. It feels a bit
self-congratulatory, but there’s no denying it.

I genuinely wish to respond to their affections as quickly as possible… while
also being careful to overturn Caron’s fate of death. That’s my earnest hope.

The second semester had started a few days ago. Life at the academy flowed along
without any significant issues... at least on the surface.

“Haah.”

During lunch, gathered in the private room designated for nobles, I noticed
Minerva let out a sigh. It was barely loud enough to be heard, but it was
definitely there.

“What’s wrong?”

I quickly asked.

Minerva, who possessed more than enough talent and continued to work hard
without being arrogant, typically maintained a haughty demeanor. For her to
exhale a sigh that wasn’t merely exasperated was quite unusual.

It seemed she hadn’t expected me to notice, as her eyes widened slightly.

“You’re unnervingly perceptive. If I didn’t know your daily behavior, I’d almost
suspect you were stalking me.”

Despite her usual sharp tongue, her tone seemed a bit subdued. There was indeed
something bothering her.

As I watched her with concern, Minerva raised both hands slightly, as if
surrendering.

“Since the practical classes began, I’ve heard voices questioning your
abilities. Are you aware of that?”

“Ah, I’ve noticed.”

While it was still just a murmur among some students, I was aware such opinions
existed.

She continued.

“Since the day you challenged the Holy Royal Family, you’ve certainly stopped
holding back. When necessary, you wield your extraordinary power without
hesitation. But in the end, you only use it when it’s needed. Especially during
academy life, there’s hardly any occasion where you’re pressed to use it.”

That was true. Even the most frequently used [Phase Connection] rarely found an
opportunity to activate. Most of the time, it was merely for moving from one
private room to another. For other spells, there hadn’t been any timing to
showcase them at all.

“In other words, the majority of students have never witnessed your true
abilities. They have no material to judge you by other than vague rumors and the
performance the academy guarantees. If they then hear your claim during the
practical classes that ‘you can’t use attribute magic’—”

“It’s understandable that they would question my abilities, right?”

I finished her last sentence, and Minerva nodded in agreement.

I had anticipated this development to some extent. The spread of rumors was
happening a bit quicker than expected, but the issue itself was as I had
predicted.

Therefore, I had already considered a few solutions to address it—

“Um, excuse me, can I ask a question?”

As I was pondering how to explain things in order, Marina timidly raised her
hand. It seemed the others had started listening to our conversation from a bit
earlier. Caron was exuding a heavy atmosphere, almost like thunder.

Since I needed a little more time to organize my thoughts, I internally gave a
thumbs-up and thought, “Nice timing,” as I prompted Marina to continue.

“Why do the other students have doubts? I heard that the academy’s grades are
measured by absolute skill…”

Given the academy's founding principles, even the Holy Royal Family cannot
engage in unfair practices. There is no favoritism based on status; the true
abilities of each student are reflected in their grades.

With that in mind, it isn’t surprising that my abilities, as the top of my
class, are being questioned. That’s what Marina seems to be implying.

Her observation was spot on. However, this time, there are special circumstances
involved.

“Perhaps it’s because of Foranada,” Orca suddenly spoke up.

“What do you mean?”

“The current influence that Foranada wields over the country is significant.
Even just looking at their financial holdings, they account for over one-fifth
of the entire nation.”

As expected from someone involved in domestic affairs, Orca seemed to grasp the
issue at hand.

On the other hand, Marina, who until recently was just an ordinary commoner,
seemed overwhelmed by the sheer scale of the numbers. She tilted her head in
confusion.

“Is that a lot?”

“Well… would it help if I said they’re wealthier than any other noble in the
country?”

“Wait, they’re wealthier than the king!?”

“Yeah. Immediately after clashing with the Holy Royal Family, they had nearly
two-fifths of the country’s wealth, but that would cause the economy to
stagnate. Now, they’re just a bit above the Holy Royal Family.”

“Whaaaat?”

Oh no, the sheer magnitude of the money was enough to make Marina, the
representative of the commoners, dizzy. She’s usually quite composed, but when
it comes to money, it might be a different story.

“To continue, even just with their wealth, their influence surpasses that of the
Holy Royal Family. Moreover, Foranada is also expanding its business ventures
domestically. In other words, they have become an indispensable entity for the
Holy Kingdom.”

Well, if everything becomes too centralized, it could lead to the decline of the
nation, so they are somewhat distributing their power. They haven’t been
expanding their business recently, though.

As Orca finished explaining, Nina interjected with a thoughtful “I see.”

“So, Foranada’s power is why you’re being doubted?”

“Ah, yes. People think, ‘If we anger Foranada, the nation could collapse.
Therefore, they must have fabricated the current head’s grades.’ That’s the
reasoning of those around us.”

Following Nina, Caron also showed understanding.

Overall, their reasoning was largely accurate. Essentially, the talk of my
rumored abilities was overshadowed by discussions of Foranada’s strength. The
misconception that it was my territory’s might, rather than my personal
abilities, took root.

Once I saw that everyone had grasped the situation, I finally spoke up.

“The solution is quite simple. I just need to demonstrate my abilities in front
of a large audience.”

“That’s true. But the problem is where to showcase it,” Minerva responded
accurately.

That’s precisely the tricky part of this issue.

As it stands, even if I show my strength during classes, it could be interpreted
as, “They fabricated the story with Foranada’s power.” If I’m going to display
my abilities, it needs to be in front of a substantial number of people.

The nobles, starting with Minerva, wore troubled expressions. They were likely
pondering a good method.

To ease their worries, I spoke in a gentle tone.

“There’s no need to fret. I have a few countermeasures in mind.”

“...Really?”

Minerva was the first to question me suspiciously.

I shrugged my shoulders.

“I’m serious. It’s just that I can’t put it into action yet. I apologize for
causing you worry for a while.”

“Hmph. This is nothing to worry about.”

“For Onii-sama, I don’t mind at all.”

“Compared to Zex-niisan, the person involved, this is hardly anything.”

“IAre you okay?.”

“W-Well, in my case, there have already been various rumors circulating about
me…”

The five students each offered reassuring responses.

They really are good kids. Their kindness warms my heart.

In that case, I need to respond to their concern. Well, there’s a
straightforward way to do that… but what should I do?

After that, while having lunch, I continued to contemplate my plans for the
future.

Several days had passed since rumors about me began to circulate. I had
requested the shadowy factions to calm things down, but so far, there had been
no visible effects. Rather, it seemed to be expanding.

At this point, a certain factor had to be considered... but the motive was too
unclear. What would anyone gain from this?

That said, I still had no solid proof. If things went as I expected, I wouldn’t
be making any mistakes in securing evidence, and it seemed I would have to
resort to that method to resolve this. It was becoming clear that I needed to
make a decision soon.

That aside, I was currently headed to the school library. Due to today’s
curriculum, I found myself with some free time. Thus, I had come at the
suggestion of Minerva, who was in the same situation.

She exchanged light greetings with the librarian at the front desk and then
turned back to me.

“This is your first time in the library, isn’t it? Here’s a simplified map.
There are maps placed around the library as well, so you don’t need to worry
about getting lost. It’s hard to imagine you getting lost, but just in case.
Also, remember to check out books at the front desk. The librarian can answer
most questions, too. After that…”

“You seem quite familiar with this place.”

I was a bit surprised by how efficiently Minerva was conveying the necessary
information to me.

Well, I knew she had been frequenting the library since before summer break. It
wasn’t that; it was her nurturing demeanor that stood out. She felt more like an
older sister who likes to take care of others or a caring teacher.

It’s strange that when she acts this way, which is so different from her usual
prickly demeanor, I find it endearing. I felt a bit moved.

Noticing my warm gaze upon her, Minerva snorted and turned her head away.

“Do you have any questions? If not, I’ll be taking a different path from here. I
have my own research to do.”

Having finished her words in a bit of a rush, Minerva disappeared into the
stacks of books.

I watched her leave with a wry smile. My fiancée is truly an adorable person.

“Well then, what should I do?”

I muttered to myself.

I had joined her because she was going, but to be honest, I didn’t have any
particular books in mind. It could be called curiosity. I had heard that the
library had a considerable collection compared to Orca's.

I believe I heard there were around ninety thousand volumes. It wasn’t as
extensive as a national library, but it was certainly a large number. That was
evident from the numerous bookshelves crammed with books.

However, randomly searching for a book among these would be somewhat reckless.
If I didn’t have a specific idea of what I was looking for, I might genuinely
end up lost.

After a moment’s hesitation, I decided to head toward the section for novels.
The reason was that I had detected a somewhat intriguing magical response while
scanning the expansive library. It was a small yet potent and strange magical
presence.

It had been quite a while since I had picked up on such a peculiar response. The
last time might have been when I first hunted a thief? However, I had changed
the foundation of my detection technique since then, so this was likely not the
same as intentionally minimizing magical power. Rather, it felt more like it was
fading.

Suppressing my presence, I walked toward the mysterious magical aura. According
to the map in my hand, the subject I was looking for seemed to be in the novel
section.

After navigating through the shelves for a few minutes, I finally arrived at my
destination. Just to be safe, I peeked over the bookshelf to observe the person
in question.

“If I remember correctly, she is…”

There she was, a familiar figure to me. Although we had never met, I knew her
from one-sided knowledge.

Her details were recorded in documents created by my subordinates, distinct from
the protagonist or the target characters from the original game, as they were
deemed necessary for understanding.

The woman’s name was Skia Thornbull Yu Ga Tali Cheyni. She was the fourth
daughter of the Cheyni viscount's branch family, which was famously large for a
noble household. After all, despite having only one wife, the Cheyni viscount
had six children. Quite a feat!

However, the main reason Skia was listed had to do with her appearance, which
was evident at a glance.

This was because her eyes were golden. The color served as proof of her aptitude
for light attribute magic, evident in her twin orbs.

I should clarify that there were only three light mages in the Holy Kingdom, and
Skia was not among them. Although she possessed the aptitude, she could not
wield light magic.

I believe I mentioned this before: unlike other attributes, light magic requires
not only aptitude but also a "heart that cares for others." Well, since it’s a
tradition, we can’t expect perfect accuracy, but many people do exist who have
the aptitude yet cannot activate it, so the current situation cannot be denied.

Ultimately, Skia was one of those people. The mere fact that she had aptitude
made her a rarity on the list.

However, seeing her directly changed my impression of Skia a bit. The earlier
strange magical response, coupled with her deep purple hair, led me to form a
certain hypothesis.

Still, I wouldn’t be able to grasp the details just by observing her here. It
seemed I would need to employ another type of magic.

Curiosity killed the cat. There's a saying about that, but I made the decision
to engage with Skia.

My curiosity was, of course, coupled with the desire to increase the number of
cooperative light mages on our side. If this matter went well, there was a
possibility she could learn to use light magic. With more hands on deck, Caron’s
burden would be significantly reduced in the event of the Demon King's revival
in the west. It was an incredibly enticing prospect.

Skia, who was already small at a height of 150 cm, was hunched over while
holding a large stack of books, searching for more. In that posture, it was only
natural that she would stagger. Additionally, her strong curls obscured her
vision, and she seemed about to take a major tumble.

Perfect timing. I could give her a warning while also creating a light
acquaintance. It would be awkward if she fell right in front of me.

I approached Skia slowly.

However, it seemed my action was a bit too late. As I got closer, she
dramatically toppled over, throwing the books she was holding into the air and
landing on the floor in a pose reminiscent of a victory celebration.

“Oh no.”

Slightly exasperated by the predictably chaotic situation, I rushed forward to
catch her as she fell. At the same time, I used manifested magic to catch the
tumbling books in midair.

“Wha—? Huh? Huh?”

It seemed Skia had never considered that someone would catch her; she looked
quite surprised, her golden eyes widening.

Hmm. Now that I was this close, I could see that Skia was living quite an
unhealthy lifestyle. Although she tried to hide it with makeup, the dark circles
under her eyes were clearly visible. She didn’t seem to exercise much, as her
arms and legs were thin, and her skin had a pale, almost bluish hue. Despite
that, her chest had quite a bit of volume. She was the epitome of an indoor
type—one might even say a reclusive girl.

To calm her down, I made an effort to speak in a gentle tone.

“Are you alright?”

“Ah, y-yes! I-I’m fine!”

Once I helped her stand up, she darted away from me with surprising speed,
creating a considerable distance between us. How did such a slender body produce
such agility?

However, it was understandable for her to be on guard. After all, a stranger had
suddenly touched her to help her.

Skia looked at me suspiciously, and I raised both hands in a gesture of
harmlessness.

“I was just passing by when I noticed you were about to fall. I apologize for
touching you without permission. I’m very sorry.”

Due to my position, I couldn’t bow my head in a place where an unspecified
number of people might see me. However, I poured sincerity into my voice as I
apologized.

At that, Skia immediately began to fluster.

“Ah, ah, ah! Y-you don’t have to worry about that! I-I’m not someone of such
value to be apologized to by a c-c-count like you… No, rather, I should be the
one to thank you first! I-I’m so sorry!”

She spoke while stammering quite a bit. This girl was even worse than Marina or
Yurika.

It seemed her nervousness wasn’t solely because she was speaking to me; I sensed
that she simply wasn’t accustomed to talking at all. It would be rude to say so,
but her personality seemed to match her appearance.

Moreover, it appeared she was quite aware of my background. Despite being
perplexed, her understanding was surprisingly high, even with the obvious cue of
my white hair. Though her appearance and behavior had some unfortunate aspects,
her cognitive abilities might be quite sharp. Now that I think about it, she had
ranked eleventh in her studies; that makes sense.

What a mismatch. I pondered how to evaluate Skia as I continued the
conversation.

“There’s no need for you to apologize either. There was certainly a better way
to handle this.”

“B-but…”

“Let’s do this. We’ll consider it mutual fault and call it even. How does that
sound?”

“Uh, um…”

When I asked with a smile, Skia’s eyes darted around in a rather amusing way.

It was clear she was not just unaccustomed to speaking; she seemed to have an
astonishing lack of immunity to interacting with others. I was left wondering
how she had managed to live up until now.

After a minute or two of her fidgeting, Skia finally nodded.

“I-I see. If that’s alright with you, my lord, then I-I don’t mind at all.”

“Great. Then this matter is settled. Carrying this many books must be quite the
task, right? It would be unfortunate if you fell again, so I’ll help you carry
them.”

“Oh, my books! …Wait, they’re floating!?”

Perhaps because she had been focusing on me, she hadn’t noticed the books
hovering around us.

Leaving her in confusion, I continued speaking.

“You're carrying a lot of books, right? Then you must want some help. Please
don’t hesitate to rely on me.”

“W-well, that’s a bit… too much for me to ask…”

I nodded inwardly.

For the fourth daughter of a viscount’s family to burden the current head of a
count’s household was far from a trivial matter. The expression "too much to
ask" was her way of wrapping her feelings in a layer of politeness.

However, this reaction was something I had anticipated. It was obvious that I
would need to have a plan in place.

“In that case, let’s do this. Instead of carrying your books, I’d like you to be
the subject of a magical experiment.”

“W-wait, a magical experiment?”

“There’s no need to be so frightened. It’s not an offensive type; it’s one that
examines bodily information. Besides, I’ve conducted this experiment many times
before, so it’s more of a final confirmation.”

In truth, the magic was already complete. It was a non-attribute spell called
[Scan] that scrutinized physical information containing magical power. While I
