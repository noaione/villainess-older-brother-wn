# Chapter 2: Towards the Tournament

A few days after my participation in the individual tournament was decided.
Today is a school holiday, and with relatively less work to do, I was planning
to relax and spend some quality time with everyone for the first time in a
while.

However, it seems that not everyone would be joining us. While we were leisurely
chatting after breakfast, Minerva suddenly stood up.

“Well then. I’m going to train for the tournament.”

“You're starting already?”

I tilted my head at her words.

The individual tournament would take place about a month from now, in
mid-October. Due to the number of students, it would span a long period until
the end of November. Well, matches before the top 16, commonly known as the
“prelims,” would be held quietly during class intervals.

Hearing that the event was a month away might make it feel like preparations
could begin. That is both true and false.

After all, we are already strong enough. Aside from our daily training, there’s
no need to specifically train for the tournament.

I figured Minerva must have some kind of plan in mind, but…

As I pondered this, Minerva responded with a hint of exasperation.

“What are you talking about? You’re participating too, right? If anything,
there’s not enough time.”

“‘Huh?!’”

The moment those words reached my ears, the others—Caron, Orca, and Nina—stood
up, their shoulders trembling.

“R-right! Onii-sama is participating too! There’s no time to sip tea leisurely!”

“If we don’t directly clash and lose, then it’s already set that we’ll have to
fight Zex-niisan, right? …We really don’t have enough time.”

“Shion, you take care of the opponent!”

“Eh, wait, Nina-san?!”

Caron and Orca began to grasp their heads in anguish, and Nina, having dragged
Shion along with her, started to put their plan into action.

Watching the chaos unfold among them, I couldn’t help but interject.

“Wait, wait. It’s true that I’m participating, but is there really a need to
panic this much?”

As Orca pointed out, the likelihood of Caron and the others facing me in a match
is indeed high. I figured I’d at least end up fighting one of them.

Even so, it was surprising to see them so frightened. There’s no way I’d beat
them up.

As I wore a puzzled expression, Minerva calmly replied, “You really don’t get
it, do you?” and continued.

“Everyone, myself included, genuinely wants to fight you. We want to give it our
all and have our strength recognized by you. I understand it’s an incredibly
difficult thing to achieve, but it’s still a desire we can’t give up on.”

“There’s no need for me to confirm it; I already recognize everyone, you know?”

“I don’t think that’s what we mean. Caron and the others want to be acknowledged
as reliable figures, not just someone to be protected.”

“…I see.”

Her words, tinged with a hint of sorrow, stirred my heart.

Minerva was right. I see Caron and the others as those I need to protect. While
there are opportunities for them to support me emotionally, ultimately, I
believe it's my duty to safeguard them.

That’s why I haven’t shared much about the information regarding the dark
factions or the machinations within the royal castle. A recent conflict with the
Forest Kingdom serves as a good example. Had Laurel not been involved, I
wouldn't have disclosed any information about that incident at all. Even now,
I’ve only shared a portion of the details.

However, I don’t think that’s wrong. I understand their feelings, and it’s true
that I’ve been swayed emotionally. But that doesn’t mean I’m willing to reveal
aspects that wouldn’t impact them.

“You don’t need to think too hard about it.”

Noticing my internal struggle, Minerva spoke with a slight smile.

“It’s not unreasonable to ask for everything to be shown. I’ve received an
education in a ducal family, so I understand that there are matters that can’t
be shared with others. Naturally, Caron and the others understand that as well.
But still, they want you to rely on them a little more. It’s not about you
shouldering everything from one to ten; even if it’s just a fraction—no, even a
tiny part of it—they want you to share some of the burden with them.”

“I see.”

They don’t want me to carry everything on my own, huh?

I hadn’t been aware of that. I thought I was delegating tasks I couldn’t handle
to capable subordinates and cutting out troublesome matters I didn’t want to be
involved in. Yet from Minerva and the others' perspective, it seems I’m “taking
on too much.”

Even when pointed out, I couldn’t help but tilt my head in confusion. I
genuinely had no idea.

That said, I didn’t doubt her words. I might have unknowingly allowed such
burdens to surface.

Seeing that I was deep in thought, Minerva shrugged.

“Because of that, everyone will work hard in their training. So, you’d better
treat them sincerely. While it’s understandable to hold back due to differences
in ability, I won’t accept careless treatment.”

“I understand. If you all are going to give it your all, then I’ll respond in
kind.”

“Good. That’s all I ask.”

Minerva snorted and placed her hands on her hips. Her demeanor was just as proud
as usual, but after hearing her previous words, it now seemed both generous and
gentle.

I couldn’t help but smile.

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

When I expressed my gratitude, Minerva responded curtly.

But her true feelings were obvious. She mostly pretended to represent Caron's
opinions, but she was the one who first said, "Everyone, including myself, wants
to fight you seriously." In other words, she was practically admitting that she
shared the same sentiment. She seemed unaware of it, so I decided not to mention
it. If I pointed it out, she would probably deny it with all her might. It would
be a shame to retract Minerva's rare, straightforward words.

“Well then, I’ll be going too.”

With a nonchalant air, Minerva left the room. She must have felt a bit
embarrassed. My fiancée, as always, was adorably charming.

“How cute, Minerva-san.”

Suddenly, the same sentiment that I held slipped out.

Of course, it wasn’t from me. It came from Marina, the last person remaining in
the room.

She had been silent since the talk about the tournament began. Given her
abilities, she was still far from Caron and the others, so it was
understandable.

However, it wasn’t as if Marina had nothing to do with the tournament.

“Marina.”

“Y-yes!?”

She hadn’t expected to be called and jumped slightly. It seemed she bit her
tongue at a bad moment, as she pressed her mouth and looked ready to cry.

“Ah… Are you okay?”

“Y-yes, I’m fine!”

“That doesn’t sound fine at all.”

Given the circumstances, I decided to wait for her tongue to recover before
continuing the conversation.

“I wanted to continue from earlier.”

“Um, are we still talking about the tournament?”

Over the past few months, Marina had finally started to get used to interacting
with me, and her stuttering had decreased. While she was still more stiff than
when she spoke with others, she was better than before.

I nodded at Marina's words.

“That’s right. It seems Caron and the others are going to train individually, so
I thought I’d personally supervise your training.”

“Eh!?”

Marina let out a strange sound that mixed surprise and fear, along with a hint
of joy. Her expression reflected a complex swirl of emotions.

I had grown accustomed to this reaction, but I didn’t expect that Marina, who
had only known me for less than six months, would have such an understanding.

I considered changing the subject but quickly dismissed the idea. If I made the
training too easy, it would inevitably backfire. This was non-negotiable.

“W-why is Zex going to supervise directly…?”

Marina asked cautiously.

With a slight sigh of exasperation, I answered her question.

“That’s a reasonable question. At the same time, the answer is quite simple. If
you perform well in this tournament, you might end up being my classmate.”

“Is that true!?”

Marina shook off her earlier fear and her eyes sparkled with excitement.

Her reaction was more enthusiastic than I expected. It seemed her
dissatisfaction with the current class division was greater than I had thought.

“The results of the tournament tend to be weighted more heavily than regular
exams. Even if you don’t win, if you can make it to the top 16—essentially, if
you can clear the preliminary rounds—getting into Class A1 is almost
guaranteed.”

A similar phenomenon could be seen in the original game. While the protagonists,
like the hero and the saintess, started off in Class A1, the other characters
had different paths. If they achieved results that allowed them to clear the
preliminaries, they would become classmates in the third term.

However, since this was not certain, I confirmed it with the headmaster. While
there are other factors to consider, making it to the top 16 would undoubtedly
secure a spot in Class A1.

Without a spirit, Marina would barely make it to Class A even with her best
efforts. Yet, considering she had been perpetually in Class D in the original
game, this was a significant improvement. Still, she wasn’t satisfied with just
that. Moreover, from a protective standpoint, it would be easier to stick
together if they were in the same class.

Ideally, I would want to find fire or water spirits for her, but those were hard
to come by. There were plenty in the Forest Kingdom, but most were already
contracted. Those flowing into the Holy Kingdom were also not up to par at this
point.

According to Noma, there was no choice but to leave this matter to fate.

Thus, this tournament. The evaluation criteria for practical skills were
extensive, making it difficult for Marina to score high. However, in a personal
battle where winning against an opponent was everything, there was still hope.

As I explained this, Marina nodded vigorously, her determination evident.

“I’ll do my best!”

She clenched her fists and let out an enthusiastic huff.

It seemed there would be no issues with her attitude. All that was left was for
me to provide the appropriate guidance so that her hopes could be realized.

With about a month left until the individual tournament by grade, special
training began, each of us holding our own aspirations.

---

A week had passed since Marina's special training began. The rumors about me
circulating within the academy remained unchanged. Fortunately, it seemed that
there were fewer people taking them at face value than I had anticipated, but my
intention to demonstrate my strength in the individual competition had not
wavered.

Currently, Marina and I were visiting her hometown. However, we had not traveled
to the village itself, but rather to the mountain towering beside it.

This time, we had an additional companion with us.

“Oh, how nostalgic. This is the place where I first met you and Marina.”

It was Noma, the small earth spirit, whose palm-sized form looked around with
shining brown eyes at the cave nestled within the forest.

I had a feeling that our first encounter with Noma had been a traumatic
experience for her, but... it was probably best not to bring it up. It would be
troublesome if I inadvertently triggered a panic.

As Noma whirled around in the air, Marina gasped in wonder.

“This is the place where I first met you, Your Highness... wow!”

Her voice alone wasn’t enough; her expression was one of rapture, and she seemed
somewhat spaced out. It was a little frightening.

If I left them to their own devices, it seemed unlikely we would achieve our
goal. I gathered magic into both of my hands and clapped them together. The
magic burst forth with a dry, loud sound.

“‘Eek!?’”

Hearing that, the two of them snapped back to reality with a cute shriek. It was
quite effective.

“Listen, both of you. We didn’t come here to play today, so let’s not let our
guard down too much.”

““S-Sorry!””

Perhaps my slightly cold tone prompted them, as they quickly apologized, looking
somewhat chastened. Given their reaction, it seemed unlikely they would fall
back into their previous state of distraction.

Regaining our focus, we began walking toward the cave.

Before us stood a cave that had likely been formed by a fault line, along with a
large flat-topped rock and a ceilingless expanse of trees. The view was nearly
the same as it had been eight years ago.

And there was one more thing that hadn’t changed.

“The magic essence is abundant. There shouldn’t be any problems.”

“Of course. The flow of magic essence doesn’t just change all of a sudden.”

Noma responded to my words.

Magic essence—the source of the magic that humans possess, a natural energy
abundant in the environment. Noma and I were reaffirming its presence.

The reason for our visit was, of course, for Marina’s training. The environment
here was crucial for this round of training.

Due to the incident eight years ago, Marina had attained a magic density
comparable to mine at that time. As a result, her ability to manipulate magic
had become more difficult than usual. To put it simply, it was like trying to
wield a sword heavier than one’s own body without having learned the basics of
swordsmanship. It was impossible for her to handle it properly.

Until now, she had been honing her fundamentals through the standard practice of
meditation. Progress had been slow, but even under unusual circumstances,
following established methods was the most efficient approach.

That said, there were limits to conventional methods. While she had managed to
swing the heavy weapon, she had hit a wall at that point. At her current level,
all she could do was swing the weapon in a basic manner. Swift spell casting or
mastering advanced spells was essentially impossible.

This was where unconventional training came into play. When conventional methods
reached a standstill, it was time to devise a means to break from the norm.

Today’s training involved repeatedly performing the actions of “release” and
“absorb” of magic in the place where she had been born and raised.

So why had we come deep into the mountains? The essence of this “place where she
had grown up” was that it was the location that had shaped her magic. Therefore,
for Marina, this was the place where her magic had been altered due to my
influence.

“Marina, sit on the rock.”

“Okay!”

Following my instructions, Marina sat on the large rock. Since I had informed
her beforehand about what we were going to do, she naturally assumed a
meditative posture without needing further guidance.

“Alright, let’s begin the training.”

As I spoke, I wrapped my entire body in a barrier. I had to do this to prevent
her from absorbing my magic during the “absorption” process. It would not only
defeat the purpose but could also worsen the situation. Noma was performing the
same task, of course.

With her eyes closed, Marina soon began to “release” the magic within her body.

As Noma and I observed, we prompted her with guidance whenever necessary.

“Your release speed is too fast. Slow it down.”

“Release from your whole body. If you concentrate it in one area, it’s
pointless.”

Marina repeated cycles of Magic [Release] and [Absorption] within her body. It
might sound simple when put into words, but in truth, this training was
extraordinarily difficult.

First, there was the issue of managing the amount of magic released. If she
allowed her magic power to reach zero, she’d faint, so she couldn’t let it
deplete completely. However, if she held back too much, the training would lose
its purpose. Therefore, she had to push herself to the very limit.

Next was the speed of release. If it was too fast, there was a risk of
shock-induced death due to the drastic fluctuation in her internal magic levels.
On the other hand, if it was too slow, natural recovery would catch up, and her
magic levels wouldn’t drop at all. Judging the right limit was essential here as
well.

Finally, there was the method of release. This training wasn’t about carelessly
letting out magic. If she didn’t release it evenly from every part of her body,
it would be utterly meaningless.

Training while paying attention to these three points was something that
ordinarily only a highly skilled practitioner could undertake. Given that Marina
struggled with magic manipulation, this was no small feat for her.

However, that was only the case when she was alone. With the assistance of me
and Noma, it was a different story.

The reason was that we could visualize magic in fine detail. By observing
Marina’s training together and pointing out mistakes, we could minimize her
burden.

If this training continued for about two weeks, she would likely acquire at
least average magic manipulation skills. If I were to be optimistic, she might
even reach a level on par with Nina. The outcome would depend on her
determination.

From there, Noma and I repeatedly issued instructions, and Marina adjusted her
actions while looping between [Release] and [Absorption].

The task of emptying her magic to the limit must have imposed considerable
mental strain. Additionally, her precise control was being tested, so I imagined
her fatigue was immense. Indeed, her expression twisted in pain, and beads of
sweat were forming on her brow.

Even so, Marina didn’t utter a single complaint. She gritted her teeth and
continued the training imposed upon her, even more than I was required to
endure.

“How commendable,” Noma muttered softly.

“She’s desperately clinging to the wall in front of her, trying to uphold the
beliefs she holds dear. People are often bound by their origins, environments,
and talents—things they may not fully understand—but she is shaking them off and
striving to catch up to you, my lord. Truly, she is a person of great intellect,
favored by spirits.”

“Indeed.”

As she spoke passionately, I nodded slightly.

Marina was unwavering. She possessed a strength that was different from Nina's
purity—a determination to see her convictions through to the end.

Even if she longed to be with the person who saved her life and whom she
admired, it would be unreasonable for her to endure the harsh training designed
to combat death without a single complaint. Normally, one would expect to give
up in pursuit of the next romance.

But Marina was different. She never betrayed her resolved feelings. No matter
the hardships that lay ahead, she persevered with all her might.

I had known this from the original game. In that story, she also chose not to
abandon her romantic pursuit with the hero. No matter how hard she tried, how
much bitterness she tasted, or how low she sank, even after realizing she
couldn't fight beside him, she wrestled with her feelings but never once
considered giving up. Not once did she entertain thoughts of resignation.

That single-minded determination, which could easily become an obsession, was
what defined Marina as a heroine.

It was overwhelmingly radiant. Her unwavering nature shone brightly, and because
I yearned for that brilliance I lacked, she had been my favorite character in my
previous life.

Though I felt a bit shy knowing that such conviction was directed toward me,
witnessing her efforts made it impossible to escape. I didn't know how things
would settle in the future, but following her example, I felt I, too, needed to
look straight ahead.

While my thoughts wandered in this way, Marina’s training continued to progress
smoothly. By the time the sun began to set, we decided to wrap up for the day.

“Haa, haa, haa.”

After nearly an entire day of circulating magic, she was panting heavily, her
shoulders rising and falling.

“I can’t move a single finger anymore~”

Despite her exhaustion, Marina joked with a laugh. She was quite resilient.

Then, Noma flashed a grin. She leaned in to whisper to me.

“How about giving her a reward for today’s training, my lord? Specifically,
something like gonyo gonyo.”

Huh, are we really doing that?

I made a reluctant face at her suggestion.

However, outright dismissing it felt wrong. She had worked so hard up to this
point. I thought it might be okay to grant her one or two rewards. Besides,
Caron and the others had been a great help as well.

My hesitation lasted no longer than ten seconds.

In situations like this, overthinking wouldn’t lead to a conclusion. So, it was
best to decide quickly based on my instincts.

I silently approached Marina and lifted her up effortlessly… like a princess.

“W-wait, what!? Your highness!?”

Startled by the sudden turn of events, Marina spoke in a voice like a broken
record, her face flushing bright red.

She had gotten used to light conversation, but it seemed that direct contact was
still quite a burden for her.

“This is just a thank-you for everything. I’m not sure if this is really a
reward, though.”

“N-no, no! I’m incredibly grateful! Thank you so much!!”

“Is that so?”

I often wondered if saying “thank you” in the context of a reward was trending
among the women in our group. It seemed to pop up frequently. …Well, I guess I
was just avoiding reality. I understood the meaning behind it.

With a dry smile on my face, I carried Marina back home. For now, I thought it
would be fine to deliver her reward to her personal room. It was an extra touch
that we wouldn’t directly connect to her room using [Phase Link].

As a result, Caron and the others, who witnessed us, would inevitably ask for
something similar, but that was another story.

---

The sound of wind cutting past my ears was chilling, brushing against my cheeks
and hinting at the impending arrival of autumn.

I was currently hovering above the royal capital, standing on a solidified
platform of magical energy at an altitude of about five hundred meters.

Below me, the city lay in darkness. It was well past midnight, a time that could
only be described as the witching hour, and all human activity had ceased.
Despite advancements in magical technology, the city didn’t operate around the
clock like in modern times.

“It’s definitely getting colder.”

Though I had only been waiting for about five minutes, considering my plans to
move soon, I wanted to avoid my extremities becoming numb, even if just a
little.

I briefly released the platform and reconstructed it into a transparent cube
surrounding me. It resembled a barrier but was a different technique altogether.

_‘The encirclement is complete.’_

_‘Understood. The operation will commence at the scheduled time. However,
consider the possibility of moving it up based on the monitor’s judgment.’_

_‘Acknowledged.’_

A report came in from my subordinate through [Telepathy], prompting me to give
my orders.

Many could probably guess from our conversation. We were about to execute a
capture operation. Tonight, we would raid the gathering of the Demon King cult
that was being held in this royal capital.

How did we track their movements while blending in with the crowd? It goes
without saying; it was knowledge from the original game. I knew that they would
soon cause a certain disturbance, allowing us to tighten our surveillance
network to detect even the slightest movements.

The cult members had taken the bait, and they would soon be captured by my
subordinates. With the encirclement reported as established, they had no escape
route left.

I had come here to prepare for any contingencies. There might still be users of
teleportation magic like the ‘Western Witch’ within the Demon King cult. The
odds were low, and even if they were present, they likely wouldn’t show
themselves this time. But since I couldn’t deny the possibility, I wanted to be
cautious.

“It’s time.”

As the moment for the operation approached, I activated [Alternate World]. The
designated area included myself, my subordinates, and the individuals gathering
in the underground space where the meeting was being held. It was reportedly an
emergency shelter used over a hundred years ago.

Not long ago, using [Alternate World] required opening an entrance and engulfing
the target. However, now, simply wrapping them in magic would suffice, and I
could even designate the range covered by the barrier. In other words, I had
gained the ability to selectively take anything within the entire detection
range.

“A professional kidnapper, am I?”

I chuckled self-deprecatingly.

…Though I said it myself, it was quite an unflattering title. I vowed never to
speak those words again.

Having completely copied the royal capital, the visible scenery remained
unchanged. However, this was undeniably a [Alternate World] that was separate
from reality.

Before a minute had passed, an explosion erupted from a section of the city. It
seemed the assault had begun. While I had thought the suppression might be
completed unilaterally, it appeared there were capable fighters on the other
side as well. Even so, the sounds of battle were resonating somewhat.

However, the forces of Foranada were extraordinarily strong. The dark faction
could take down a dragon with just five or six of their members. Even those who
were “capable” would surely be instantaneously defeated.

My prediction was correct; mere seconds later, the cacophony of battle ceased.
The interior of [Alternate World] returned to silence.

_‘The suppression has been completed.’_

_‘Good work. Interrogate the survivors. No need for leniency.’_

_‘Yes! Understood.’_

As the report of our success came in, I dispelled the spell. While the world
appeared unchanged, the magical energy enveloping it vanished.

Now, it all depended on the results of the interrogation.

Since it would likely take some time, I decided to return to the estate. I had
classes at the academy in the morning, so I should take a light nap beforehand.

Using [Phase connection], I returned to my other residence and secured about
three hours of rest.

Early morning. As soon as I awoke from my nap, I was informed of the results of
the interrogation. With over twenty individuals detained, it seemed there had
been some difficulty, but they had managed to extract all the information.
Completing the job in just three hours was impressive.

First, it should be noted that the individuals we captured were mostly
low-ranking members of the cult. There were a few who held slightly higher
positions, but the difference between them was negligible. Therefore, the
information obtained was mostly of little significance.

However, there were a few key details I wanted to know beforehand. Among them
was the "Ritual of Demon Summoning" the cult planned to carry out at the end of
November.

The subordinate who reported back seemed somewhat skeptical, but I was not. This
ritual would indeed take place, and it would be successful.

This was tied to an event from the original game involving the Saintess. During
the awards ceremony for the winners of the grade-based individual competition, a
horde of demons suddenly attacks the royal capital.

Each demon isn’t particularly strong, so initially, students and knights of the
Holy Kingdom put up a fight. However, as the demons keep coming without
dwindling in number, casualties begin to rise.

In the midst of this chaos, the Saintess and one of the key characters, Zigrald,
shine. Zigrald, in particular, is a descendant of the mages who conducted the
summoning rituals and is the only one capable of dispelling the summoned demons.
Together with the Saintess, he works to save everyone in the royal capital.

If the cult's plans are true, it seems that the event will indeed unfold as in
the original story.

Given that this incident would involve the entire royal capital, I wanted to nip
it in the bud if possible—but this posed a tricky dilemma.

After all, this was an event related to the Saintess. If I were to meddle too
much, it could negatively impact her story. Moreover, this incident was also a
key component in her power-up event, so I wanted to avoid any rash actions.

While ensuring Caron and the others' safety was paramount, prioritizing
immediate gains could jeopardize our ultimate goal of sealing the "Western Demon
King." What to do?

“For now, I’ll put it on hold.”

Since the whereabouts of the ritual’s items were unknown, even if I chose to
sabotage the event, there was nothing I could do at this point. I could only
raise the alert level in the royal capital.

While this would merely postpone the problem, there were still about two months
left until the event. I would need to devise a solution during that time.

---

One afternoon after school, I made my way to a certain facility on the academy
grounds. Its exterior resembled a dome venue, and the texture of the walls
suggested it was a newly constructed building—indeed, it had just been completed
three days prior.

This was the new stage for the magical beast, Magical Piece. Commissioned by
Foranada, construction had been underway since spring.

I was given free rein to decide the stage's design, so… I may have gone a bit
overboard. The theme was a near-future urban landscape, and I fully replicated
the skyscrapers reminiscent of a city from my previous life.

However, this ambitious design resulted in a slight shortage of space, so I
incorporated spatial expansion through a barrier. It was a prototype technology,
but it functioned better than I had expected. I believe it expanded the area to
about one and a half times its original size.

Since this was an experimental introduction, I also added automatic magical
power generation as a bonus to maintain the barrier.

Of course, because this was such advanced technology, security measures to
prevent theft were robust.

When I relayed this information, the headmaster was surprised but accepted it
fairly quickly. She remarked, "Your technological innovation is not new, and
this skyscraper direction is certainly plausible." Having lived a long life, she
likely had the foresight to predict human development.

The reason I had come here was to observe the trial operation of the stage.
Although it was built well, I wanted to ensure there were no flaws.

Since I needed to observe the entire setup, I had to occupy the spectator seats.
Therefore, I arranged for a separate group of players to actually use the stage.

One team comprised my club members: Caron the "Red Mage," Minerva the "Purple
Mage," and Yurika the "Green Mage," along with the new members, Dan and Miria.

The opposing team was from a top club, who quickly agreed to participate, eager
to be among the first to use the new stage. Jet's eyes sparkled when I brought
it up.

A match was currently underway, with explosions echoing throughout the urban
landscape. To account for the trial operation, Caron had orchestrated the
players to separate into different areas, allowing us to analyze a variety of
patterns.

While the multiple perspectives made it somewhat difficult to follow as
entertainment, that was of no concern this time. With my ability to detect, I
had no trouble grasping the situation.

"Looks like Dan and Miria are holding their own," Orca remarked, pointing to one
of the battle sites.

The two of them, childhood friends of Caron and me, were putting up a good fight
against the top club. Dan was matched against Jet the "Green Mage," while Miria
faced off against the "Blue Mage."

"Since Caron taught them, they should be able to manage this much," I replied.

"That's a lot of pressure, Zex-niisan," Orca said with an exasperated tone, but
I thought it was only natural.

I hadn’t taught them everything I knew; I only shared knowledge and techniques
that would likely become widespread later on. Still, it was undeniable that Dan
and the others were handling advanced magic for the time being. There was no way
they could lose to those who were lagging behind. In a team battle, it might be
a different story, but with the current one-on-one format, that was even less
likely.

That said…

"Can't Dan find a better way to fight?"

"Haha… that's just so typical of him," Orca chuckled.

"Charging in headfirst."

"That's a strategy that's hardly useful in actual combat."

Orca chuckled wryly, followed by commentary from Nina and Shion.

As their comments suggested, Dan’s fighting style was pretty gritty. He would
solidify his defenses with earth magic, then go in with a brawler approach,
ignoring any counterattacks as he charged straight at his opponent. It was all
about relentless, straightforward offense.

I understood that, as a "Brown Mage," he needed to focus on counter-attacks to
win against a "Green Mage."

But charging in headlong? Like Shion said, that was a strategy I couldn’t
approve of in real combat. It was only feasible here in Magical Piece thanks to
the barrier that protected players from damage.

With the HP gauges set by the role cards, the “Brown Mage” had an advantage in
direct brawling. Still, his opponent was Jet, who used mobility to dodge
attacks, making it an even match. Jet might have a slight upper hand.

That said, even if Dan lost, he’d done more than enough just by holding his
ground solo against Jet for this long.

Meanwhile, Miria was playing things much more steadily. Though the “Blue Mage”
had almost zero offensive power, she was stacking buffs to boost her attack. Her
opponent was doing the same, but the difference in the potency of their buffs
made it her game. I could watch her match with confidence.

“Miria’s surprisingly sharp, given her academic standing,” Nina remarked.

“Yeah. Dan’s influence really helped her out there—she’s got a good head on her
shoulders,” Orca added.

“Love makes one blind.”

“You know what a boomerang is?”

Nina and Orca were bantering again, but I decided to stay out of it. If I jumped
in, they’d probably drag me into their back-and-forth.

With the two newcomers doing well, I decided to check in on the experienced
members.

“One Red Mage from the top club has been eliminated.”

Yurika had just taken down her opponent.

She had become even stronger since last month, her movements swift as lightning.
There were likely few who could keep up with her under Magical Piece’s
constraints. Caron and the other back-line members would probably struggle.

The defeated Red Mage stood in shock, completely outmatched by Yurika’s
blistering speed.

“Whoa, Minerva’s really something,” Orca murmured.

“As expected,” Nina nodded.

I looked over to see Minerva’s battle reaching its climax.

She was facing another “Purple Mage.” At the start, they had engaged in a
weakening contest, but Minerva now had her opponent fully under her spells.

She had sunk the enemy into decayed ground using a combined spell of earth,
water, and wind, blinded them with darkness, and messed with their perception of
temperature with fire. Her opponent was completely immobilized.

At this point, the opposing Purple Mage from the top club was helpless, their HP
gauge slowly dwindling away.

I could see that Minerva was continually improving. She had always been
brilliant, but she also worked harder than anyone else—she was a true genius.
Despite spending less time in my supervised training than the others, she kept
pushing herself without ever giving up. As a result, she could now go toe-to-toe
with Caron and the others.

Since joining the academy, she’d refined her skills even more. Controlling five
combined elements simultaneously was a highly intricate technique. Her recent
achievement of full mastery in magic control only highlighted her skill.

Normally, she came across as an “adorably awkward girl,” but there was no doubt
Minerva was a genius mage with unmatched resilience.

Finally, there was Caron.

I shifted my gaze to watch her match with a "Brown Mage" opponent, when suddenly
a flash of light engulfed the entire stage, whitening every skyscraper.

“What the—?!”

Orca and the others were fine. They let out small gasps, but it didn’t seem to
have affected them physically. I, of course, was unharmed as well.

The brilliance had only lasted for a moment, and the stage quickly returned to
its original colors. But the scene that greeted us was entirely unexpected.

Because the top club had been completely wiped out. They stood there in a daze,
clearly unable to grasp what had just happened.

I turned my gaze toward the person responsible.

There stood Caron, a small orb of light floating between her outstretched palms.
She noticed me looking and flashed a bright smile.

There was no doubt—the light from earlier was her doing. And she was also the
one who had taken down the remaining members of the top club. An all-range
attack that could distinguish between friend and foe… she’d created a truly
extraordinary spell.

And with that unimaginable strike, the match drew to a close.

---

“That spell… isn’t it a bit too underhanded?”

In the post-match review, the first thing out of Minerva’s mouth as we gathered
in our usual rundown clubroom was that.

Since her words were directed at Caron, there was no question what “that spell”
referred to.

The magic Caron used at the end of the recent Magical-Piece match was her own
original spell, one I hadn’t even been told about. It seemed she’d secretly been
developing it without letting anyone in on it.

Caron replied with a carefree smile.

“It worked out well this time, but it’s not quite as powerful as you might
think.”

Then, she explained the spell.

That blinding light that wiped out the top club was a composite spell of light
and fire called [Perennial Radiance], Caron’s own original creation. It’s a
wide-area annihilation spell that detonates a condensed blaze of light.

“Wait a minute. If that’s the case, doesn’t that make the match earlier kind of
strange? Based on what you’re saying, it sounds like it would have hit everyone
nearby, friend and foe alike.”

Minerva’s question echoed the doubts that everyone in the room had.

With the mechanics of the spell Caron described, the blast would have flattened
everything in the area, including her.

But in reality, only the opponents were wiped out, and the structures on the
stage remained untouched.

It probably meant she could select her targets, but… it was hard to see how she
managed it. Caron’s not particularly adept at precise magical control.

In her usual calm manner, Caron began to explain.

“As for the buildings, [Perennial Radiance] only targets living beings and magic
sources. I modified the range of a healing spell and adapted it. The ally
selection aspect is a technique from light magic. By diffusing the light, I
could detect allies’ locations and exclude them from the attack, similar to my
spell [Diagnosis] or your [Scan], Onii-sama.”

“Ah, so that initial flash was for target selection.”

“Indeed, Onii-sama. As you said, there’s no power in the flash itself. Though
with how quickly the attack launches, there’s hardly any noticeable delay.”

Hmm. Caron didn’t seem to realize it, but she had come up with a completely
over-the-top spell.

First of all, that line about “adapting the range of a healing spell” was
insane.

Generally, magic is constructed from one’s imagination. While pre-existing
spells have established frameworks that simplify them, magic creation always
depends on the caster’s mind. And with original spells, it requires even more
creativity.

What I’m getting at is that to take something as opposite as healing and
wide-area destruction and synchronize effects between the two… that level of
thinking is highly unusual. Normally, it would be far easier to imagine a new
spell from scratch.

Maybe it’s the unique thinking process of a true genius. She’s developed magic
in a way that’s really out there.

Then, there’s the spell’s range and power to consider. [Perennial Radiance]
likely performs its attack by “burning with light.” Meaning, as long as the
light can reach, the attack will land.

As for its strength, I’d say it’s beyond high-level magic. It seemed like it
could incinerate a dozen fire-resistant dragons in one blow.

Now that I think about it, in the original game, Caroline was a fire mage with a
focus on pure firepower. During her boss fight, she’d keep casting the
high-level fire spell [Avalanche Blaze]—an avalanche of flames—in rapid
succession. If you got hit directly, you’d be left on the brink of death, so
dodging was crucial.

Her personality may differ from the original, but her affinity for powerful
spells hasn’t changed. Although it’s a bit unsettling to see traces of her
original villainess nature, it’s nothing to lose sleep over. My affection for my
little sister isn’t shallow enough to waver over something like this.

As Caron finished her explanation, Minerva, Orca, and Shion—versed in magic as
they were—looked utterly dumbfounded. It was a reasonable reaction; I felt like
holding my head in my hands too.

Meanwhile, the commoner group was blissfully unfazed.

“I dunno the details, but it sounds like Caron’s amazing!”

“You’re amazing, Caron-chan!”

“Uh, um, you’re incredible, Caroline-san!”

Are their IQs dropping or something?

…Well, Dan and Miria have always been like this. Yurika seems to be just going
along with the flow.

Well, if you’re not knowledgeable about magic, you might only grasp that
“something complicated and difficult was done.” While Nina isn’t quite joining
in on Dan and the others’ vibe, she tilts her head slightly in confusion.

“I’d like to ask something,” Orca said.

“What is it, Orca?” Caron replied.

“You mentioned it’s not as strong as you say. Is there some kind of flaw?”

“One flaw is that its power diminishes in direct sunlight. Another is that I can
only exclude targets that I’m aware of. And a third is that it can’t affect
physically enclosed spaces at all. Just off the top of my head, those are a few
drawbacks.”

Despite being a wide-area attack, it seems to perform better indoors. The
selection of targets is rather limited, and there are definite ways to defend
against it. It certainly isn’t the most user-friendly spell; there’s plenty of
room for improvement.

But—

“It might be enough as a trump card,” I said.

As Orca pointed out, it was indeed a useful card to have. Even in its current
form, it seemed like it could be an immediate asset.

After Caron finished explaining her new spell, we proceeded with the review as
planned.

The four of them, excluding Dan, received positive evaluations. Each had shown
progress from their training, so their areas for improvement were mostly minor.

As for Dan… well, he could only be described as a wild boar, and everyone was
giving him some hard truths. He had that look of “Why?” on his face, but it was
a natural conclusion.

Our club activities continued in a cheerful atmosphere.

However, there was one thing that bothered me, just a little. It was a subtle
sense of discomfort that only I, being sensitive to emotions, could perceive.

The fact that Minerva, who should have been part of our lively circle, seemed to
have a slight cloud over her heart was something I found very concerning.

---

That night, I found myself out on the balcony just outside her room. With the
lingering warmth of late summer fading, the temperature had grown a bit chilly
now that the sun had set.

So why was I out here in the cold? To talk with an early visitor, of course.

Sure enough, Minerva was there. The dark-haired girl, the owner of the room, had
her hands resting on the balcony railing, gazing up at the stars that filled the
night sky.

A beautiful girl admiring the starlit sky. Coupled with her youthful features,
the sight was truly breathtaking. Capturing this moment in a painting would
surely create a piece of art admired for generations.

It was a scene that could make anyone stop and stare, but I couldn’t stay idle
forever. Feeling a bit intrusive, I quietly walked over to stand beside her.

"…"

Even when I came to her side, she didn’t react. She simply kept her eyes fixed
straight ahead, looking up at the sky, fully aware of my presence.

There was no need to rush. I decided to match her pace and gaze up at the starry
sky alongside her.

A rare, peaceful moment where we simply stood and looked at the sky. Usually —
if I were alone, I’d probably get bored after a while, but just being with my
fiancée changed how I felt. There was a warmth that made me forget the chill in
the air.

Moments like these make me realize how much I care about Minerva. After these
past few years together, I knew I had truly fallen for her.

Considering I still held memories of my previous life, I couldn’t help but laugh
at myself for being so fickle. But, well, emotions are just like that… You can’t
always control them, which is why you try to respect and honor them.

…Not exactly the words of a saint, I suppose. If I were in Japan, I’d probably
be called a scumbag.

But for now, I set aside my own thoughts. Tonight, I was here for Minerva.

I had come to check on her, worried about her since she had seemed down after
the Magical Piece match review. Her emotions had remained clouded, and I wanted
to see how she was doing.

Even now, as she continued to silently stare at the sky, I could sense that she
was troubled.

The silence between us lingered for a while until, suddenly, Minerva spoke up.

“…What do you want?”

She asked, still looking at the sky, in a voice that carried a hint of
sulkiness.

But beneath that tone, I could detect a slight note of happiness. Since I’d
arrived, she’d been a bit restless, probably pleased that someone cared enough
to check on her. She was as stubborn as ever, hiding her true feelings.

Amused by her endearing display, I replied with a smile.

“If something’s bothering you, I’m here to listen.”

Minerva was stubborn. If I asked indirectly, she’d probably put on a brave face
and keep quiet. Honestly, asking directly might not get a different result, but
it was worth a shot — maybe being blunt would get her to open up.

“It’s nothing,” she replied, predictably denying it. Her emotions were all over
the place, yet she kept a calm facade.

Alright, time for the direct approach.

“It hurts, you know, having my fiancée lie to me.”

I pretended to sniffle, covering my eyes with my hand.

“If you’re just going to joke around, maybe you should leave…” she snapped,
finally turning to look at me, a hint of irritation showing.

This was the moment I’d been waiting for. Not missing my chance, I quickly took
her hands in mine, kneeling right there beside her. From the outside, it must
have looked like a knight swearing loyalty to his princess.

“Wh-what are you doing?!”

"I’m not joking. I’m serious — I want to help you."

Looking her straight in the eyes as she expressed her confusion, I spoke
earnestly.

"There’s something weighing on your mind, right? Then let me share that burden.
We’re engaged, aren’t we?"

"This is something I have to solve on my own."

"Still, just let me hear it. I promise I won’t try to force any solutions.
Sometimes, just talking to someone can help ease things."

"…"

"Minerva."

"Alright, fine, I’ll tell you! Is that good enough? Now let go of my hands."

She finally gave in under my steady gaze, her cheeks flushed as she looked away,
avoiding my eyes.

"Thank you."

"You really are pushy, aren’t you? It wouldn’t hurt to be a little more
considerate."

"I’ll try to be more careful."

Minerva puffed out her cheeks in frustration, and I responded with a slight
smile.

Still, despite her complaints, her mood seemed lighter. Though I had been a bit
forceful, she didn’t seem genuinely angry. That was a relief.

"Since you insisted, I’ll tell you. But it’s not some grand tale or anything."

After I let go of her hands, Minerva adjusted her posture and continued.

"Lately, I’ve been painfully aware of my lack of skill. Orca has her magical
control, Nina her swordsmanship, Shion her stealth, Marina her spirit magic… and
Caroline her light magic. Each of them has a remarkable strength they excel at.
But I… don’t. Sure, I’m adept at all five elemental attributes and can handle
them skillfully, but I don’t stand out like they do. When it comes to magic,
Caroline and Orca are much more specialized."

It was true — Minerva didn’t have a sharply defined talent. Her strength was in
her ability to excel across all areas at a consistently high level.

But that didn’t make her lacking. She was still plenty strong. Being versatile
is a gift in its own right.

Perhaps sensing my thoughts, Minerva added, "I know."

"I understand that I don’t have to specialize. I know I have my own kind of
strength. But even so, I can’t help but admire people like Caroline, who have
something unique. I want to have something that makes me special, too."

So it’s a matter of feelings, not logic.

I’d grasped Minerva’s worry. Her resolve to handle it herself made sense, and I
wanted to respect that.

So, there was only one thing I could say.

"I’ll be cheering you on, hoping you find that ‘something’ for yourself. And
I’ll help however I can."

"If I rely on others, it wouldn’t really be mine."

"But having someone to talk to doesn’t hurt, right? Sometimes discussing things
can help clarify your thoughts."

"Well, that’s… true…"

If she was working hard to find her "something," I wanted to support her fully.
I was prepared to help her every step of the way.

She seemed unable to dismiss my reasoning so easily, pausing for a moment before
sighing softly.

"Alright. If you’re offering, then I suppose it would be a waste to refuse.
Every now and then, I might ask for your advice."

"Gladly."

"Thank you. …You really are pushy."

Minerva pursed her lips slightly.

I shrugged in response.

"Is that a problem?"

"…You’re such a troublesome fiancé. I hate that about you."

"Sorry, sorry."

She feigned irritation and turned her gaze back to the sky.

Knowing it was all an act, I followed her lead, offering a mild apology as I
joined her.

The stars sparkled across the vast sky. Together, we enjoyed the view of this
celestial jewel box.
