# Chapter 5: The Demon Invasion

“My apologies, Zex-sama. We were unable to locate the item in question.”

The report came from Shion as we returned to the waiting room to prepare for the
award ceremony following the tournament’s conclusion.

“As expected,” I replied, my voice tinged with resignation as I nodded.

What had I tasked her with finding? The catalyst for the commotion the Demon
King Cult was about to unleash—the “Demon Summoning Ritual.” By offering a price
to this catalyst, demons could be summoned in nigh-infinite numbers.

I had ordered a thorough investigation, but this outcome was within
expectations. The catalyst was likely provided to the cult by the scheming
demons, who would keep it under their own protection until the ritual’s
commencement to avoid interference.

Well, no matter. I had no intention of disrupting the ritual itself—it was
necessary for the Saintess and her party to grow. All that remained was to let
events unfold as scripted.

“Preparations?” I asked curtly.

“They are complete,” Shion answered promptly, her voice steady with the
confidence that everything was in place.

From her demeanor, it seemed we were ready.

We had no plans to interfere with the demon summoning. Plucking the buds of
growth from the Saintess and her allies was not our way.

However, we wouldn’t simply stand by either. This event would cause harm to
many, and though it wasn’t explicitly stated in the original story, there were
likely casualties as well.

I refused to dismiss those as “necessary sacrifices.” Doing so would surely rob
Caron and the others of their smiles. The scars of conflict would torment their
kind hearts. Thus, we had made extensive preparations in advance.

Our mission was to provide the hero, the Saintess, and their parties with the
tension they needed to grow while minimizing harm to civilians. It was a
daunting task, but I believed we could succeed. We had built a strong foundation
for it.

“Very well. Hold your positions until the event begins. If you detect any
anomalies, report immediately,” I instructed.

“Understood,” Shion replied, bowing deeply before operating her magical
communicator, MagiCru, to relay my orders to the team.

At that moment, Caron and the others, who had been waiting in the room with us,
spoke up.

“We’ll give it our all\!” Caron declared.

“I’ll do my very best too\!” Orca added.

“Preparations are perfect,” Nina said confidently.

“I-I’ll do my best as well\!” Marina chimed in.

Their clenched fists and spirited attitudes showed their readiness.

As you might have guessed, Caron and the others were part of this strategy. The
more capable hands we had, the better.

As I mentioned before, the summoned demons weren’t particularly strong. A
B-class student could defeat one single-handedly, and even weaker students could
manage in teams.

The real threat was their numbers—a relentless swarm that never ceased. To face
them, stamina was critical.

In that regard, Caron and the others were more than adequate. Marina was a
slight concern, but as long as they stuck together, injuries were unlikely.

Moreover, they were thrilled to be relied upon, their morale soaring. I could
entrust them with the task without worry.

“I’ll—” Minerva began.

“You’re on standby, Minerva. Your magic hasn’t recovered yet,” I interrupted.

“…Tch. You’re right, I suppose,” she admitted, her shoulders slumping in
frustration.

As I pointed out, Minerva had nearly depleted her magic in the tournament’s
final match. Though there was some time before the commotion, she wouldn’t
recover enough to fight effectively. With chaotic combat expected, she had no
choice but to sit this one out.

I ruffled Minerva’s hair roughly as she sulked.

“Everyone here knows your strength, Minerva. And we know how much you poured
into that final match. You stayed true to your convictions. Don’t beat yourself
up over this,” I said.

“I-I get it\! Don’t mess up my hair so casually\!” Minerva huffed, turning away
with a pout.

But she didn’t brush my hand away. So tsundere.

As we bantered, the time for the award ceremony arrived. With a faint tension in
our hearts, we stepped onto the stage.

The award ceremony encompassed all academic years. The stands were packed with
even more spectators than during the finals, and the stage was lined with the
top four winners from each year. As expected, Louise had won in the second year,
and Jet had claimed victory in the third.

The ceremony proceeded smoothly. The supervising instructors gave their
critiques, high-ranking nobles led by Duke Loramber offered greetings, and the
principal delivered a summary. It was all rather drowsy, unfolding at a
leisurely pace.

Then came the moment to honor the top four. That’s when the event began.

My detection network, spread across the capital, suddenly picked up foreign
entities—swarms of demons and what appeared to be members of the Demon King
Cult, divided across four locations.

I furrowed my brow.

“…Four locations?”

That wasn’t right. In the original story, the cult operated from a single site.
Upon closer inspection, I detected what seemed to be the ritual’s catalyst at
each of the four locations. Why were there four of these precious
demon-summoning catalysts?

The most likely explanation was the ripple effect of my actions. The demons
pulling the strings must have been influenced in some way.

This called for a course correction. I had planned to guide the Saintess to the
catalyst’s location, but there was no time to have her visit all four. Given her
party’s strength and the risks of prolonged combat, that wasn’t feasible.

If neutralizing one catalyst could halt the summoning chain, that would be
ideal, but I couldn’t gamble on uncertainty.

Thus, an additional task: gather the other three catalysts at the site the
Saintess would head to. Zigrald could disable them all at once.

However, each site was guarded by cult combatants, and demons might be lurking
as well. Sending a half-hearted force would be foolish, and overwhelming numbers
weren’t an option given the need to defend against the demons. A small, elite
team was the best approach.

I connected via telepathy.

“Caron, Orca, Nina, Shion. Change of plans. Pair up and launch an assault on the
locations I’ll designate. Secure the demon-summoning catalysts and bring them to
a single point.”

Their affirmations came swiftly. Orca and Nina, seated in the stands,
immediately sprang into action, passing through a 【Phase Connection Gate】 I
had opened nearby to retrieve one of the catalysts.

Meanwhile, Caron, Shion, and I remained on standby.

Soon, the instructors and guests began to stir. They must have received external
reports or detected the demons’ vanguard entering their range. A barrier was
hastily erected around the venue, and the ceremony was interrupted.

“Everyone, listen calmly\!” the principal bellowed. “Unknown creatures have
appeared across the capital. Do not move from your positions until safety is
confirmed\!”

The crowd began to murmur, confusion and unease spreading, but no one made
reckless moves—yet.

That fragile order wouldn’t last.

The next moment, the vanguard—a hundred demons—slammed into the barrier above.
Black skin, piercing eyes, sharp fangs, gaping red maws, claws designed to maim,
and bat-like wings. These grotesque monstrosities hurled themselves at the
barrier with terrifying force.

They seemed devoid of reason, unconcerned with their own injuries, relentlessly
crashing into the shield.

Though individually weak, a hundred demons attacking in unison strained the
barrier. It creaked with high-pitched cracks, its surface rippling dangerously.

The spectators screamed. It was only natural—grotesque creatures unlike anything
of this world threatened their safety.

The precarious peace shattered easily. One person’s attempt to flee sparked a
wave of panic.

“Calm yourselves\!” the principal shouted, but her voice couldn’t reach a crowd
lost to fear.

This was all as expected. The game’s original scenario had unfolded similarly,
so I had anticipated this panic.

And naturally, I had prepared countermeasures. That’s why I remained here.

“【Silence\!】”

My voice, laced with 【Calm】 and 【Intimidation】, echoed through the venue.
The combined effect drew every eye to me, the earlier chaos replaced by stunned
silence.

Mental magic, as expected, was incredibly reliable—though far too dangerous for
casual use.

Satisfied with the situation, I declared loudly, “As of this moment, House
Foranada will take charge of this matter. I will protect your lives. So, calm
yourselves and wait. We, House Foranada, guarantee your safety\!”

I exuded absolute confidence, standing tall and resolute.

The crowd’s reactions varied—some sighed in relief, others looked skeptical, and
a few still harbored unease. I could sense their emotions clearly.

Yet, their behavior was unified: all watched my every move, holding their
breath.

“The enemy forces—henceforth called demons—are scattered throughout the capital.
Those attacking the barrier are merely the vanguard, and their numbers exceed
ten thousand.”

Murmurs of unease rippled through the crowd at the mention of the enemy’s
numbers, but no further chaos erupted, thanks to 【Calm】 and my immediate
reassurance: “It’s alright.”

“I swear that not a single one will lay a finger on you. House Foranada and I
will protect you.”

I overwrote the existing barrier with my own. The faltering shield was
reinforced, and the vibrations from the demons’ assaults ceased. There was no
sign of them breaching it.

But I didn’t stop there. I opened multiple 【Phase Connection Gates】 around the
venue.

From them emerged a flood of servants and knights—all in service to House
Foranada, a veritable army of dragon slayers.

They charged out as soon as they arrived, some taking to the skies with
magic-enabled flight. They began hunting the demons with ferocious momentum,
steadily expanding their control.

Given the demons’ low individual strength, our servants could dispatch them
single-handedly. It was only a matter of time before they secured the capital.

The sight of even mere servants slaughtering demons left everyone outside House
Foranada dumbfounded.

Understandable, but I didn’t have the luxury of time to coddle their shock. I
continued, “The demons are numerous. We will protect this venue, but other
students and staff are scattered across the academy, and beyond its walls are
the capital’s citizens. To protect them, I ask those confident in their skills
to lend a hand.”

In truth, we could handle it without assistance. With my involvement, total
annihilation was possible. But I needed to say this to ensure the Saintess and
her party could act. Avoiding a narrative derailment required this call for aid.

“I’ll lend my strength, Count Foranada,” Jet, standing beside me on the stage,
was the first to respond.

“I will too,” another voice followed.

“So will I,” added another.

“I’m sure I can be of use\!” a third chimed in.

The other winners joined in, their voices rising in unison.

The momentum spread to the audience, and nearly a hundred volunteers stepped
forward, including the Saintess and Zigrald.

Perfect. This would allow us to protect everyone while keeping the scenario on
track.

Mentally pumping my fist, I began explaining the next steps to the volunteers.

“Operate in groups of at least three. Each demon is weak, but their numbers are
vast. Their limits are unknown, so prepare for a prolonged battle. Avoid
reckless engagements and prioritize protecting non-combatants.”

I emphasized safety to minimize casualties among the volunteers. After
addressing their questions, they ventured beyond the barrier.

Caron remained by my side.

“Caron, I’m counting on you,” I said.

“Leave it to me, Onii-sama\!” she replied enthusiastically.

“Shion, look after Caron.”

“Understood,” Shion affirmed.

As instructed earlier, they would head out to retrieve a catalyst. Sending my
beloved sister and the others—four in total, including the advance team—into
battle pained my heart, but their skills left no room for doubt. Holding them
back would be foolish and selfish.

I sent them through a 【Phase Connection Gate】 and let out a breath.

For the final location, I dispatched Garuna, Teria, and Maron, the three maids.
Despite being newcomers, their skills were exceptional. The catalysts would soon
be gathered in one place. I had also secretly assigned guards to the Saintess,
so there was nothing to worry about.

“Now, time for me to get to work,” I muttered, lightly slapping my cheeks before
leaping upward.

I passed through the barrier, soared past my subordinates engaging the demons in
the sky, and reached an altitude where I could survey the entire academy
grounds. Encasing myself in a personal barrier, I quietly observed the scene
below.

The demons were being steadily defeated, but their overall numbers hadn’t
decreased. More were being summoned continuously.

Worse still—

“It looks like the ones we kill are coming back,” I noted grimly.

A quick scan confirmed my suspicion: the demons were reviving after being
defeated. Ordinary attacks likely couldn’t destroy them permanently. What a
hassle.

The original game had no such depiction—probably because no one understood the
demons’ nature back then. I really wished these unexpected complications would
stop.

My original plan was to wipe them out with 【Shot】, but if they could revive,
that would be a waste of magic. My reserves were vast but not infinite, and
non-attribute magic consumed far more energy. I didn’t want to expend it
fruitlessly.

That left only one option.

“Noma,” I called, summoning the earth spirit from 【Phase Concealment】.

A palm-sized, adorable girl appeared before me, her short, silky brown hair
swaying as she tilted her head.

“What’s up, Master? I thought I had no role this time,” she said.

“Plans changed. I need to focus on constructing a spell, so I’m counting on you
to keep watch.”

“No problem, but… a spell that even you need to focus on?” she asked, curious.

“It’s one I’m not particularly suited for, and it’s tricky to use. But it’s
useful for this situation,” I explained.

“Got it. Leave the lookout to me,” Noma replied, patting her chest and scanning
the surroundings.

She didn’t press further, likely sensing my urgency. That kind of thoughtfulness
made her an invaluable partner.

I closed my eyes, focusing on the magic flowing within me.

The spell I was about to cast was a form of ritual magic, activated at the cost
of my blood and eyes—a manifestation of demonic power. It was the only technique
humans possessed capable of matching the extraordinary.

Its name: the Magic Eye.

---

“Why can humans wield magic?”

I once posed that question to my mentor, Akatsuki.

Initially, I had dismissed it due to the premise of this being a “game world,”
but I reconsidered that such a mindset was inadequate. This world nurtures life
and operates according to its own logic.

Akatsuki’s answer was strikingly concise: “Magic exists because mana does.”

…Well, that’s probably true. But it didn’t satisfy me. If magic arises from
mana, then where does mana come from? I understood the process of absorbing
ambient magical essence and converting it into mana via 【Coloring】, but the
origin remained a mystery.

Magical essence is one thing—it’s an energy inherent to this world, and I could
accept that without delving into the universe’s genesis, which would be far too
fruitless.

The real question was: how does the human body convert magical essence into mana
through 【Coloring】?

Is there an organ that generates mana?

No, that’s impossible. After examining numerous bodies following this inquiry, I
found no such structure. The human body in this world, at least, seems nearly
identical to that of my previous life.

If it’s not a physical factor, then it must be conceptual—something akin to
magic itself.

With this hypothesis, I began my investigation. I traced the source of mana,
excavated forgotten ruins, and restored tattered documents.

It was a pursuit driven by curiosity, progressing slowly in my spare time, but
my research steadily advanced.

Last year, I arrived at a possibility.

Could it be that humans have formed a contract with the world?

It sounds absurd, but based on my findings, no other explanation fits.

Let me break it down logically.

In ancient times, humans lacked mana. Relics and records suggest that in
primordial eras, they relied on physical prowess and ingenuity to fend off
threats.

But humanity stumbled. Whether due to magical beasts or natural disasters—the
cause is unclear—they faced a crisis threatening their survival.

This led to the development of ritual magic. As I’ve mentioned before, ritual
magic allows spellcasting without mana by expending stamina or life force. In
game terms, it’s like using HP as a cost for magic.

This breakthrough spurred humanity to advance magical technology, expanding
their dominion.

Yet, they hit another wall: the limits of ritual magic.

Ritual magic is inherently inefficient. Using substitutes for mana results in
poor “fuel economy.”

Thus, humanity sought new magical possibilities. Documents indicate that
developing new magic was arduous until a genius proposed a radical idea.

A contract with the gods. At the time, religion already existed, and they sought
to gain power from a supreme, supernatural entity in exchange for a price.

The gods they believed in likely didn’t exist, but the world itself may have
acted as a proxy. This is largely my speculation, but by offering a price,
humans rewrote the laws governing magical essence to suit their needs.

It might seem impossible for humans to bend the world’s laws, but not if the
contract encompassed all of humanity—past, present, and future.

The price was “blood.” By offering the blood that flows through generations,
humanity gained mana. Through a daily, minute sacrifice of blood, they acquired
the 【Coloring】 system.

In essence, every living human is a contractor with the world.

Now, that was a lengthy preamble, but it’s merely the foundation. The real
matter begins here.

I hypothesized: “If a small amount of blood grants mana, could a greater price
yield even greater power?”

No time to waste. I began experimenting to deepen this contract with the world.
It was no easy feat, taking a full year to manifest.

But I succeeded. One side… remains unstable, but I undoubtedly awakened a new
power.

That power is the Magic Eye. A force that steps into the truths of the world. A
great eye peering into the abyss of magic.

I opened my closed eyes.

The world before me remained unchanged, but my right eye had transformed.

“Whoa, what’s with your eye, Master\!?” Noma, who had been keeping watch, let
out a startled yelp.

She must have glanced over, drawn by the surge of mana, and been shocked by the
unexpected sight.

My right eye had become a Magic Eye. Its sclera was white with red edges,
intricate red lines etched at its center, and it burned with white flames.

This was my Magic Eye: 【Blazing Flame Heaven Eye】, a fire-attribute eye.

“Hey, Master, your eye’s bleeding\! Are you okay?” Noma, recovering from her
shock, began to panic.

I had noticed the pain, and as expected, the backlash had come.

Normally, using a Magic Eye shouldn’t cause a reaction, as the “eye” itself is
the price.

So why the bleeding? It’s a matter of aptitude. Lacking fire affinity, my use of
a fire-based Magic Eye triggered a rejection response.

Given House Foranada’s fire affinity, this eye likely manifested through my
bloodline, but I’d have preferred something more versatile.

“It’s fine. Nothing serious,” I reassured her.

“If you say so…” Noma replied, her concern lingering.

Blood dripping from my eye would worry anyone. Best to wrap this up quickly.

I poured more mana into 【Blazing Flame Heaven Eye】. The flames flared
brighter, blazing intensely.

My detection was complete. Now, it was time to act.

“—Burn,” I muttered softly.

At that moment, white flames erupted across the academy.

“What the—\!?” Noma exclaimed in astonishment.

It was simple: every demon was ablaze.

The core ability of my 【Blazing Flame Heaven Eye】 is to imbue targets with
white flames that burn until extinguished—lethal in a single strike.

Revival was no concern. The flames cling to the soul, burning through
reincarnation or resurrection. The demons, incinerated instantly upon revival,
were rendered utterly incapacitated.

I closed my eyes, deactivating the Magic Eye.

With the demons annihilated, 【Blazing Flame Heaven Eye】 had served its
purpose. I wanted to minimize the backlash.

Tch, it still hurts. My right eye would be unusable for a while.

The bleeding and pain were temporary, with no lasting damage, but the intensity
was troublesome. I’d be blind in that eye for about ten minutes. Improving this
Magic Eye would be a future task.

As I pondered, I sensed someone approaching.

“Master\!” Noma noticed it too.

The mana signature suggested…

Soon, the figure revealed itself.

---

The figure that arrived was a man. A strikingly handsome man with flowing black
hair, but no mere human. He was a demon. His tanned skin bore curled, goat-like
horns at his temples and grotesque, fleshy wings sprouting from his back.

His name was Septem. In the original story, he was a recurring rival who
repeatedly challenged the Saintess, the strongest demon on her side. Across the
game, his level of 69 was second only to the final boss.

Unlike typical demons, he held a sense of conviction. While he sowed chaos for
the Demon King’s revival, he honored promises and upheld principles. This made
him a fan favorite in popularity polls, with many clamoring for him to be a
romance option—only to be met with the official response, “He’s a demon, so it’s
impossible.”

Thus, his involvement in the demon summoning was unexpected. This event didn’t
align with his character. The mastermind was supposed to be another demon.

《Urgent report: The hero’s party has engaged a suspected demon in combat.》

《Urgent report: The Saintess’s party has engaged a suspected demon in combat.》

As Septem appeared, reports came through 【Telepathy】 from subordinates
monitoring the protagonists.

I narrowed my eyes slightly at the news.

Four demons in the capital?

In the original story, there were only two: the mastermind and the one attacking
the Saintess. Combined with the four catalysts, the narrative had diverged
significantly. Should I rejoice that fate could be altered, or lament that my
knowledge was becoming unreliable?

One additional demon could be attributed to the hero’s party joining the story.
But Septem’s presence was a mystery. Perhaps my existence drew him out, despite
his secrecy in the original story.

No, it’s too early to conclude. I lacked information. Engaging Septem in
conversation might yield answers.

Now, how to handle him?

I had intended to let him go unless forced to confront him. He wasn’t pure evil
and served as a valuable influence on the Saintess.

But facing him now, combat was inevitable. Demons are humanity’s enemies—letting
him go without a fight would be unnatural.

Killing him, however, was undesirable. It would hinder the Saintess’s growth.
The best course was to rough him up just enough to make him flee voluntarily.

It’s always like this, isn’t it? Given my strength, I could probably obliterate
anyone but Akatsuki with a glare. Moderation is the challenge.

Septem would likely make his move soon. Even with 【Body Enhancement】 and
mental magic boosting my thought speed, I couldn’t yet use 【Time Stop】. For
someone of his caliber, five seconds of observation should suffice.

As predicted, Septem narrowed his crimson eyes and spoke.

“Those white flames were your doing, weren’t they?”

“Who knows?” I replied evasively.

“No use hiding it. I’ve already traced the mana to you.”

I let out a small sigh.

I had hoped denial might make him leave, but it was a shallow ploy. Demons, born
from curses, can see mana as clearly as spirits. Tracking a Magic Eye user would
be trivial for him.

Should I unleash my full mana?

No, that might render him incapacitated. Even a spirit king was broken by a mere
brush of my power. Septem still has a role to play, so I should avoid
overwhelming him until I know the right balance.

For now, I’d either drive him off with words or force. His actions would decide.

Septem eyed me suspiciously.

“You’re awfully calm facing me. Have you already encountered my kind?”

“Oh, yeah. Took one down while hunting a witch,” I said casually.

“…I see. You’re no ordinary foe,” he replied, his aura intensifying.

My offhand mention of defeating a demon—likely one using possession—had
heightened his caution. Defeating a demon, even a scheming one, was no small
feat, marking me as a formidable opponent.

Perfect. His earlier mild caution made restraint tricky. Demons are durable,
unlike humans, but with so few combat samples, gauging non-lethal force was
hard. His full commitment would reduce the risk of accidental overkill.

He flapped his wings dramatically, raising both hands as he declared, “I am
Septem, the First Sword Kead of the Great Light Magic Administrator
Gruewen-sama. Far stronger than the demon you claim to have defeated. Face me
with resolve\!”

“…Huh?”

Wait, what was that introduction? It was completely different from what I knew.

In our first encounter in the original story, Septem declared:

_I am Septem, a servant of what you call the ‘Western Demon King.’ Far stronger
than any demon you’ve faced. Come at me with everything you’ve got._

This new introduction was littered with unfamiliar terms. Great Light Magic
Administrator? First Sword Kead? I’d never heard of them. From context, the
Great Light Magic Administrator likely referred to the Western Demon King, and
Kead was his top subordinate. It wasn’t a stretch, but I couldn’t be certain.

Did my experience defeating a demon alter his introduction? In the original, the
Saintess’s party had fought demons but hadn’t killed any by this point.

A bad feeling crept in. This wasn’t just a difference in phrasing—it felt
significant.

Change of plans. I’d still drive Septem off, but I’d extract information during
combat. His knowledge seemed critical.

I needed to collect information without crushing his pride, while ensuring he
could retreat intact. A high-stakes task, but as the strongest—and the brother
Caron and the others admired—I’d rise to the challenge.

I drew two daggers from 【Phase Concealment】, clicking them into position.

“I’m Zex. Zex Levito San Foranada. Let’s hear about this Great Light Magic
Administrator,” I said coolly.

Septem responded with a faint, confident smile.

“Foolhardy human, taste my power\!”

At an altitude of five hundred meters, the battle began.

“I’ll let you have the first move,” I said.

“You’ll regret that,” Septem replied, maintaining his composed demeanor.

Inwardly, I smirked while feigning irritation.

Currently, I was suppressing my mana to the level of an average level-60 human.
He had completely misjudged my strength—a mistake I could exploit.

First, I’d reduce his mobility. Tearing his wings to shreds would be easy, but
since he fought airborne in the original story, I’d limit the damage to what he
could heal.

I unleashed a carefully calibrated 【Gunshot| Shot】 without looking. Ten
flashes of light pierced his grotesque wings, targeting key points to disrupt
his balance without crippling him.

“Tch\!” Septem clicked his tongue.

He used wind magic to avoid plummeting, but his flight became noticeably
clumsier.

“My turn\!” he shouted, a trace of urgency in his voice as he realized my
strength exceeded his expectations.

Such precision shooting was impossible without exceptional mana control, a feat
beyond human norms. His reaction was understandable.

But he wasn’t the First Sword Kead for nothing. His momentary unease vanished,
and he launched a counterattack.

Raising his right hand skyward, he conjured five massive lances behind him, each
over ten meters in diameter. They radiated fire, water, wind, earth, and
darkness, spinning with terrifying force.

Getting hit by those would do more than tear a body apart.

Summoning five near-high-tier spells in an instant was a testament to his status
as the strongest demon. Outside Akatsuki-sensei or House Foranada, he was likely
unmatched.

As he swung his arm down, the lances shot forward at supersonic speed, homing in
on my vitals.

Evading or intercepting them would be simple, but I held back. The latter,
especially, risked breaking his spirit.

Instead, I chose a third option: deflection. By executing it calmly, I’d make
him think I was only slightly stronger than him.

I imbued my daggers with 【Magic Envelopment】, extending their blades slightly
with mana, and swung at the approaching threats.

Five slashes in a second. The remnants of 【Magic Envelopment】 traced five
lines in the air. The lances stalled briefly upon contact before dissipating
into mist, not a shred of their mana reaching me.

—Wait, what\!?

That was too much\! I’d overdone the reinforcement and ended up intercepting
them outright. Deflecting was better than annihilation, but I’d still chosen the
second-most showy option\!

Outwardly, I maintained a faint smile, but inwardly, I was panicking.

This is why I hate facing mid-tier opponents. They’re stronger than Foranada’s
servants but weaker than Caron and the others—the exact range I have the least
experience with. Demons are usually one-shot kills, so gauging restraint is
tough.

No use dwelling on it. I just hoped I hadn’t broken Septem’s spirit.

I glanced at his expression.

“Huh?”

I couldn’t believe my eyes. Septem was conjuring over a hundred of the same
lances, poised to launch.

Why was he preparing a barrage?

My confusion was quickly resolved upon seeing his face. His intentions became
clear.

Septem was terrified. He had accurately gauged the gap in our strength and was
gripped by fear.

This barrage was likely a diversion to pin me down while he escaped.

His ability to assess my power after a single exchange was proof of his skill.
It’s harder for combatants to gauge an opponent mid-battle, as focus often
narrows to the fight itself.

As a warrior, he was impressive, but I couldn’t let him escape. I needed his
information.

Fortunately, his pride was bruised but not shattered. There was a solution.

I swept away the incoming lances with 【Compress Cube】 and leaped into his
escape path.

Seeing me appear before him in an instant, Septem’s face twitched.

“Monster\!” he spat, clicking his tongue.

That defiance was promising. He could still serve as the Saintess’s opponent.

Inwardly satisfied, I maintained a calm exterior.

“I propose a deal. Accept, and I’ll let you go this time.”

“…I’m listening,” he replied.

“Answer my questions. I can discern truth from lies, so don’t try to deceive
me.”

It wasn’t entirely accurate—my judgment relied on reading emotions—but I didn’t
need to admit that. I just needed him to comply.

Septem sighed, his shoulders relaxing.

“Fine. I accept.”

“Deal sealed,” I said.

He understood he had no choice. I appreciated his ability to read the situation.

The ground seemed to be stabilizing, so it was time to ask my question—the only
one that mattered.

“You mentioned the ‘Great Light Magic Administrator.’ Is that another name for
the Western Demon King? And what exactly is a Magic Administrator?”

Septem’s eyes widened slightly, and he gave a self-deprecating smirk.

“So you’re not one of them. Even a powerhouse like you hasn’t reached that
level.”

“What are you talking about?” I pressed.

“I’ll answer. Yes, the Great Light Magic Administrator-sama is indeed the one
you call the Western Demon King. As for your second question, it’s not complex.
A Magic Administrator is one who has mastered magic, attaining its truths. They
govern magic, hence the title.”

This was new. The original story never mentioned “Magic Administrators,” even as
the protagonist grew stronger, nor did any other trainable characters.

“It doesn’t sound like a mere title,” I noted.

“I don’t know the details. But those who become Magic Administrators transcend
humanity,” he said.

“Transcend humanity…” I muttered.

This was getting murky. He claimed ignorance, and further probing about Magic
Administrators seemed fruitless for now.

Time to shift gears.

“So the Great Light Magic Administrator mastered light magic? A Demon King who
spreads curses and manipulates hearts’ darkness?” I asked skeptically.

That was the hardest to believe. The Western Demon King cursed the world and
exploited people’s darkness, like Caron’s in the original story. With immunity
to non-light magic, mastery of light seemed unlikely. Darkness would fit better.

But Septem refuted me outright.

“The stronger the light, the deeper the shadow. And intense light can obliterate
all things. Light’s essence isn’t just healing.”

I couldn’t argue.

Light magic wasn’t limited to recovery; it included potent offensive spells,
like Caron’s 【Perennial Radiance | Amaterasu】.

So… our enemy is a light magic master? Is that why the Saintess, a light magic
user, was chosen to seal them?

No, don’t rush to conclusions. This was based solely on Septem’s words. I needed
more perspectives before deciding.

《Zex-sama, the Saintess Seira’s party has halted the demon summoning ritual.》

Perfect timing.

With the Saintess resolving the scenario as planned, it was time for us to
handle the aftermath. Many mysteries remained, but this was a good stopping
point.

“One last thing. Do you know what the other demons are up to?” I asked.

“No idea. We demons don’t exactly cooperate,” he replied.

As expected, consistent with the original story. No further questions, then.

“Go. As promised, I’ll let you off this time.”

“That’s it?” he asked, wary.

“Yeah.”

Septem backed away cautiously, then flew off at high speed.

I watched him go, letting out a small sigh.

“The Demon King, a light magic master, huh?”

This new information, absent from the original story, filled me with an ominous
premonition. My instincts screamed that this wasn’t to be ignored.

I had planned to follow the original story and reseal the Western Demon King.
But with this new knowledge, perhaps another path needed exploration.

With an uneasy feeling, I began formulating new strategies.
