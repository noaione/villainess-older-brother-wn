# Chapter 4: Individual Competition

November had arrived. It had been a fortnight since the individual competition
by grade level had commenced.

That said, it was still the preliminary rounds. Until the top sixteen, the
matches were held in the gaps between classes, so unless one was particularly
keen, spectators were rare. Generally, only the two competing students and the
supervising teacher would be present, allowing the schedule to proceed quietly.

As expected, Caron and the others, including Marina, seemed to be advancing
smoothly. Given their current form, securing a spot in the top sixteen of the
knockout rounds was all but guaranteed.

On the other hand, the revival of the summoning ritual that had begun last month
was already complete. In the original game, it would typically be finished just
before the finals, but with Minerva and me lending a hand, a slight advance in
timing was only natural.

Although not as intense as her father, the magic fanatic, Minerva had her own
share of enthusiasm for magic. I, too, had been thoroughly combing through
various materials and continuing my research. With seventy percent of the
research already completed and knowledge of the final form, guiding it to
completion was relatively easy. Of course, the significant contributions from
Zigrald and the others, who had already completed seventy percent of the work,
could not be overlooked.

Thus, without any concerns, I was prepared to participate in the individual
competition.

By the way, the Demon King’s followers, who were rumored to cause a commotion
during the awards ceremony, had yet to be detected.

While I suspected they were steadily gathering in the royal capital, they seemed
to be acting like ordinary citizens, showing no suspicious behavior. They were
likely planning to stay hidden until the last moment. Since I knew the location
for the ritual, there wasn’t much of a problem.

Well, this event was designed to encourage the growth of the Saint—Seira, and
her peers. While I would take various precautions, I didn’t intend to interfere
with the summoning of demons. They could enjoy their brief moment of joy.

Back to the topic at hand.

“Guess it’s time to go.”

Due to a canceled class, I had been passing the time in the library. With
everyone else in class, I was alone.

However, the time for my match was drawing near. I closed the book in my hands
and stood up.

I cast a small \[Phase Connection\] to return the book to its shelf, then opened
a regular \[Phase Connection\] to move to the training ground where the match
was scheduled to take place.

According to my detection, my opponent was already inside the venue.
Additionally, there were a few spectators for some reason. Given that they were
sitting together, I surmised they had come to watch their friends’ matches.

Yet, I could sense a feeling of derision from both my opponent and the
spectators. It didn’t seem like this match would end well.

“Ugh.”

Having faced only reasonable opponents in the previous preliminaries, I felt an
increase in mental fatigue. A sigh slipped out involuntarily.

That said, I couldn’t just turn back on my heel. The reason I entered the
individual competition was to showcase my abilities to those around me and
eliminate unnecessary conflicts. There was no option but to advance to the
finals.

I stretched my arms toward the sky and relaxed my body.

It didn’t really matter. Whatever my opponent thought inwardly was their own
business. I had no intention of interfering with others' inner thoughts.

If Minerva were to hear this, she might scold me for being “too soft.”

With a wry smile, I stepped into the training ground.

The interior matched what I had detected. Near the center of the expansive
circular ground stood the male teacher acting as the referee and my male
opponent. In one corner of the castle, five spectators, presumably a mix of
males and females, were seated. All of them, except for the referee, wore
strange smiles directed at me.

The atmosphere was unpleasant. I wanted to wrap this up quickly.

Suppressing my gaze slightly, I approached the referee.

“You’ve arrived. Now, please put on this device, Lord Foranada.”

“Yes.”

I received a ring from the referee.

This was a magical tool designed to prevent serious injuries during the match.
To put it another way, it was something I had prepared. Since not only Caron but
I would be participating this year, I developed it because I believed the
traditional system would be insufficient.

In broad strokes, this ring was a magical tool of \[Phase Concealment\]. It
converts incoming damage into magical power—that’s how it works.

Let’s call this—this ring that converts damage into magic power—the “Damage
Conversion Ring.” Developing it was quite a challenge. I had originally
conceived the design for \[Phase Concealment\] tools, but the issue of energy
consumption was a significant hurdle. After all, it consumed vast amounts of
magical power every time an item was brought in or out. Without the increased
magical capacity provided by the Magic Fragrance Flower, we couldn’t handle it
easily.

So, the proposed solution was to limit what was converted to magical power. It
was demonstrated that if the damage was restricted to the wearer, it
significantly reduced magical power consumption.

However, even then, it wasn’t something students could use repeatedly.
Therefore, I restricted it further. The ring was set to be usable only within
the barrier of a separately manufactured device.

As a result, it achieved a level that could be utilized by ordinary students.

Well, due to the two improvements mentioned earlier, it’s limited to events like
this tournament. While I have no concerns about it being misused, it’s still
somewhat of a niche application.

To summarize my lengthy explanation, it’s sufficient to understand it as “a ring
that converts physical damage into magical power consumption.” I believe most
students would perceive it that way. The difference in understanding doesn’t
change its performance.

The referee, having acknowledged that I was wearing the ring, began to explain
with fervor.

“For this match, I, Teach, will be officiating. Since matches have already been
held multiple times, I don’t believe there’s a need to explain again, but for
the sake of thoroughness, I will go over the rules.”

Firstly, the outcome will be decided by either the opponent being incapacitated
or surrendering, or by the referee's judgment.

Secondly, all participants must absolutely comply with the referee's decisions.

Thirdly, any attacking actions are prohibited after the match’s outcome has been
determined.

—And so on. He elaborated patiently.

In essence, it was about following the referee while refraining from actions
that could lead to accidents.

“Both participants, please stand on the marks on the ground ten meters behind
you.”

““Yes.””

My opponent and I complied with the instructions, creating a distance of
approximately twenty meters between us.

Upon confirming this, the referee was just about to declare the start of the
match.

However, he was interrupted.

“Excuse me, sir.”

My opponent, a male student, raised his hand lightly to call for a halt.

The referee, unable to ignore him, altered his words.

“...What is it?”

Perhaps due to being interrupted, the referee’s voice sounded somewhat stiff.

Not paying any mind to this, the male student grinned as he replied.

“Nothing much. I just wanted to give my opponent a little advice before the
match.”

“Advice?”

The referee’s expression darkened at the rude tone of the remark. I shared the
same frown.

I understood he was looking down on us, but the situation was turning sour. It
would be best not to let him speak any further.

The referee seemed to come to the same conclusion, speaking quickly.

“Chit-chat before the match is not a commendable act.”

“But there’s no rule against it, right? I made sure to review the match
regulations.”

“That’s…”

The referee stumbled over his words, having been hit in a sensitive spot.

As the male student had said, there was no rule prohibiting talking to one’s
opponent before a match. While it stated that matches should commence as swiftly
as possible, there were no strict regulations.

Seeing the referee fall silent, my opponent threw a glance my way, filled with a
mocking, vulgar tint.

“Better for you to withdraw before you get hurt. You might have bought your way
here, but I’m not about to give up victory for that. You’re worthless, so just
step aside.”

As he spoke, laughter erupted from the gallery. The atmosphere was completely
one of condescension towards me.

“I made the right choice in participating.”

I rolled the words around in my mouth.

I had heard rumors casting doubt on my abilities, thanks to my subordinates.
However, witnessing this situation forced me to admit that my understanding had
been naive. There was a vast difference between hearing about something and
experiencing it firsthand.

I pinched the bridge of my nose and asked, “Where are you from?”

“Huh? Why should I have to tell you that?”

“Just tell me.”

“...A small village in the Kanai Barony.”

“I see, I see.”

The Kanai Barony was a remote area on the outskirts of the Holy Kingdom’s
western region. Knowing he hailed from a village there made his outrageous
behavior more understandable.

Ordinary commoners do not seek to associate with nobles. Nobles do not reach out
to commoners. Maintaining a certain distance from each other to keep peace is
the norm in this country. In fact, the groups within the academy are distinctly
divided between nobles and commoners. There are certainly exceptions, but they
are rare.

This understanding is widespread. Even the impoverished living in slums—no,
especially because they are impoverished—tend to maneuver skillfully to avoid
being crushed as social underdogs.

The man before me had clearly lacked this common sense due to his rural
background. It was likely that the Kanai Baron was the type to work hand-in-hand
with his subjects, but that didn’t mean it would work the same way outside of
his territory. This situation had revealed the negative aspect of producing
someone who lacked awareness of common sense.

I turned my gaze toward the referee.

He nodded as if he had given up on everything. It was a relief to see that he
seemed to understand the situation.

I raised my index finger and pointed it at the male student.

“I have a piece of advice. You should refrain from publicly mocking a noble,
especially a current lord. You could lose your head on the spot.”

I conveyed this in a voice that echoed throughout the venue.

Noticing the shift in atmosphere, my opponent showed a hint of fear in his
expression. Regret seeped through his emotions.

“W-what are you talking about?”

But it was too late for that. The saying “Regret comes too late” couldn’t be
more accurate.

“Oh, this advice wasn’t directed at you. It was meant for the audience.”

“Huh?”

Caught off guard, he was met with the swift end of his life just a moment later.
A small hole opened in his forehead as he collapsed onto his back. A copious
amount of blood flowed from the wound, making it clear he was dead on the spot.

The training ground fell silent, but the stillness lasted only a moment. The
students in the gallery screamed and fled like startled rabbits.

I had no intention of pursuing them. At present, I had not shown any clear
contempt.

Once I confirmed that the audience had vanished, I stored the corpse using the
phase concealment magic tool. I also collected the spilled blood, returning the
training ground to a pristine state.

“Teacher, I would appreciate it if you kept this matter confidential.”

“I don’t mind, but… why?”

“If this incident becomes public, his family will be implicated as well. That
would be absurd, wouldn’t it?”

Even if I didn’t act directly, the Kanai Baron, fearing the wrath of the
Foranada, would surely take action. It would be too cruel for his family to
suffer because of their foolish son.

“You’re hard to read; I can’t tell if you’re being lenient or strict.”

“Feel free to choose whichever you prefer.”

I shrugged in response to the fatigued-looking referee.

From an external perspective, it would likely be deemed that I was being
lenient. Minerva had certainly made that point clear numerous times.

Yes, I am lenient. I don’t possess the kindness to care for complete strangers.

Leniency is not synonymous with kindness. When I show leniency, it’s always
towards someone I deem inconsequential. I wouldn’t care if they repeated the
same mistake and found themselves in a fatal situation, thus I would let it
slide.

Of course, if it escalates to an undeniable situation like this one, I must
impose strict punishment. It’s a tough job to maintain the dignity of the
nobility.

I suddenly pondered: Am I a heartless person? I don’t consider everyone but my
kin as “inconsequential” in such extreme terms, but I lacked sufficient evidence
to dispel that suspicion.

“There’s no point in overthinking it.”

“Did you say something?”

“No. I will handle the follow-up here, so you just need to keep quiet.”

“Understood.”

“Well then, I’ll be heading back.”

Now, I should stop by the headmaster’s office. I need to take care of this
promptly. The students who ran away would likely keep quiet if my subordinates
applied a little pressure, so that wouldn’t be a problem.

What was supposed to be just a preliminary match had left me feeling utterly
exhausted. I wanted to be comforted by Caron and the others soon.

---

As November approached its end, the top 16 had finally been decided. The
breakdown was as follows:

Me, Caron, Orca, Nina, Minerva, Marina, Dan, Miria, Yurika, Yuudai, Rina,
Ariarnorth, Seira, Gray, Zigrald, and Eric.

Everyone from Caron’s Magical Piece Club had made it through. In fact, it was
all too familiar faces. Given that many of the original story's main characters
are exceptionally talented, it was hardly surprising.

From the top 16 onward, the matches are commonly referred to as the finals and
take place in front of a large audience. All classes are canceled, allowing
students to come and watch. Additionally, important figures wishing to assess
the quality of the students also make an appearance.

On the morning of the first day of the finals, one such important figure had
arrived at the Folanada’s villa.

“I apologize for the sudden visit, Count Foranada.”

The stern man who entered the reception room began with an apology. His name was
Joobah Stemhols Kan Loramberg, the current head of the duke's family and
Minerva’s father.

“Please, there’s no need for concern. I understand you’ve had a tight schedule,
and you contacted me last night. It hasn’t been much trouble at all, as you
might think.”

I responded with a smile.

In reality, Duke Loramberg was quite busy. He was involved in various magical
enterprises, as well as managing his territory. Without a phase connection gate,
he also had to account for travel time, making time management quite
challenging.

I understood that he had taken precious time out of his schedule to meet with
me.

It seemed he accepted that there was no hidden meaning behind my words, as the
duke simply replied, “I see.”

“Minerva should be here shortly as well.”

“No need to worry. I know that women require more preparation than men. Please
tell my daughter that there’s no need to rush—”

Knock knock.

Before he could finish his sentence, there was a knock at the entrance. A
waiting servant confirmed that Minerva had arrived.

“It seems my consideration was unnecessary.”

He smiled wryly.

I returned the smile, signaling the servant to let Minerva in.

“Father, it’s been a while. The weather today is quite nice—”

Upon entering, Minerva gracefully curtsied. Her seasonal greeting was smooth and
fluent, and in this moment, I was reminded once again of her noble upbringing.

After exchanging the series of greetings, Minerva took a seat next to me.

At that moment, Duke Loramberg unexpectedly relaxed his expression.

“What’s the matter?” I asked, puzzled.

“Oh, nothing. I simply thought that you two have grown quite close since I last
saw you. It makes me very happy to see your relationship flourishing.”

It seemed he had inferred the nature of our relationship from just our current
demeanor. I suppose it was to be expected from the head of a duke's family. Or
perhaps our feelings were simply that apparent.

I felt a bit embarrassed, but after all, we were engaged, so worrying about it
wouldn’t change anything.

However, it appeared I was the only one able to brush it off.

“F-Father\!?” Minerva exclaimed, her face flushed with embarrassment as she
protested.

Seeing this, the duke’s smile widened even further, and he let out a hearty
laugh.

A father-in-law laughing at his fiancée’s embarrassment—it was a rather chaotic
situation. Realizing that allowing it to continue would only waste time, I
decided to steer the conversation back on track.

“By the way, your visit to the capital at this time means you’ll be watching the
individual matches as well, right, Duke?”

“Ah, indeed,” the duke confirmed, falling into my train of thought. He nodded
graciously, still in good spirits.

“Every year, I make it a point to attend the final tournament. If I spot any
exceptional talent, I’ll extend an invitation, and if I get to see some
interesting magic, that’s a bonus.”

“It certainly seems like the perfect opportunity to gauge the students'
abilities.”

“Indeed. While I can’t grasp everything, it’s undoubtedly a valuable chance to
witness their individual combat prowess from the employer’s perspective.”

“I see.”

If that’s the case, then my abilities would likely resonate throughout the
kingdom by the end of the year. Until now, only a select few in the royal court
had known of my skills, but showcasing them in the spotlight of the individual
matches would leave no room for concealment.

Not that I really needed to hide anything. I hadn’t felt the need to demonstrate
my full strength before because there was no necessity to make a grand display
of power. Since inheriting my title, I hadn’t put any effort into hiding my
capabilities.

“Under normal circumstances, I would be watching the upperclassmen’s tournament,
but this year I will likely be observing the first-year students.”

“Is it to support Minerva?”

“That’s part of it. —But my main focus is on you, Count Foranada.”

The duke's crimson eyes sparkled with anticipation as he fixed his gaze on me.

“I have been eagerly awaiting this day ever since I heard you would be
participating. I’m excited to see how you will wield your unknown magic. Heh heh
heh, I can hardly contain my excitement\!”

The smile he wore was tinged with a hint of madness, true to his nickname as the
"Magic Maniac." It seemed he had already forgotten about his original purpose
for attending the matches: recruiting talented individuals.

Glancing over, I saw Minerva sitting beside me, her head in her hands. Given her
expression, it was clear that no one would be able to stop this now.

I managed a wry smile and offered a noncommittal response.

“I will do my best to meet your expectations.”

It was a rather political, gray answer. But nobles are much like politicians, so
I figured this level of ambiguity was acceptable.

In any case, there aren’t many opportunities to showcase new magic in matches
between students. Perhaps the only chance would be when I matched up against
Caron and the others.

Still, with expectations set this high, I would have to make some adjustments.
After all, it was a request from my future father-in-law.

After some light banter, we decided to part ways, as the final tournament
awaited us, along with the duke’s observation of it.

That said, we were cutting it pretty close on time, so the three of us decided
to use 【Phase Connection】 to travel. During that process, I was somewhat… no,
completely taken aback by Duke Loramberg’s level of excitement. It truly struck
me how intense a person could become when they dedicated their life to magic.

---

Classes were not held during the final tournament, so students headed directly
to the match venue instead of the classrooms.

The match venue was divided by grade, and each individual could choose where to
watch. Typically, the higher the grade, the more spectators there would be.

However, this year was different. The audience at the first-grade venue was
overwhelmingly large.

There were several reasons for this.

One was the presence of heroes and saints, individuals who appeared only once in
a hundred years. Many were curious to see how they would fight.

Another reason was the multitude of those with notable titles. With participants
like Caron, Nina, and Ariarnorth, attention was unavoidable.

And one final reason was my own participation. For a colorless individual to
make it to the top sixteen was unprecedented, and there were many eager to
confirm the strange rumors surrounding me.

It seemed that no matter the world, human curiosity never changed.

While I idly pondered this as I sensed the area surrounding the waiting room,
Caron called out to me. For some reason, the members of the Foranada group had
gathered in my waiting room.

“Onii-sama, the matchups have been announced.”

The matchups referred to the tournament bracket. From the top sixteen onward,
the tournament was shuffled and announced on the day of the matches.

“How did it turn out?” I asked.

“Here you go.”

She handed me a paper about the size of A4.

I quickly glanced over it.

“What a hassle,” was my immediate reaction.

The first-round matchups are as follows:

1\. Match 1: Miria vs. Minerva

2\. Match 2: Marina vs. Yudai

3\. Match 3: Gray vs. Zigrald

4\. Match 4: Eric vs. Yurika

5\. Match 5: Nina vs. Dan

6\. Match 6: Caron vs. Seira

7\. Match 7: Orca vs. Ariarnorth

8\. Match 8: Zex vs. Rina

Indeed, the matchups were filled with deep-seated connections.

Given the participants involved, I had anticipated such combinations, but the
reality was worse than I imagined. I, along with Caron and Marina, found
ourselves facing precisely the opponents we dreaded.

“Zex,” Nina murmured unexpectedly.

Her eyes were faintly shaken. While it didn’t reach the level of panic, it was
clear that she harbored concerns about the battle between her love interest and
her younger sister.

There was no use lamenting the situation. Once the matches were set, there was
no way to change them, and more importantly, I didn’t want to add to Nina’s
emotional burden.

I gently patted her head.

“It’s going to be okay.”

It was a vague statement, lacking specificity. Still, it conveyed the intention
that no matter what happened, everything would be fine.

I couldn’t tell how much she understood, but she nodded in response, saying, “I
get it.” Her emotional turmoil seemed to have settled, so I felt reassured.

Having provided sufficient support to Nina, I turned my gaze to the others.

Everyone displayed a suitable level of tension. I was concerned about Marina’s
feelings, but from what I could see, she appeared stable. It seemed like there
wouldn’t be any issues.

I couldn’t predict how this individual competition would unfold, but I hoped it
would turn into a meaningful experience for all of them.

---

The first round of matches progressed smoothly. The results settled largely as
expected, with Minerva, Nina, Caron, and Orca all securing their victories
without difficulty.

I had been wary that something might happen during Caron’s match, but it ended
up being surprisingly uneventful. The only notable issue was that the Saint
Seira seemed overly tense, but she relaxed during the match, so it wasn’t a
cause for concern. It was a relief that nothing significant occurred.

As for Marina, she unfortunately lost. She put up a good fight until the end,
but given her current level, it was understandable.

However, it wasn’t all bad news. It seemed that after a heated argument during
the match, she and Yudai managed to reconcile. I was genuinely happy to see
their relationship improve, which had become strained since I took her under my
wing. They were childhood friends who had always been close, and I hoped they
would cherish that bond.

The match between Prince Gray and Zigrald was closely contested, but Gray
emerged victorious, proving his skill in close combat. It seemed that in
individual matches, being an all-rounder was indeed advantageous.

Lastly, the battle between Yurika and Eric had an unexpected outcome: Eric
managed to win. After a fiercely intense close-quarters battle, he claimed
victory. Although Yurika had been training hard, Eric surpassed her abilities.

From the context, it seemed that he had rigorously trained the body enhancement
skills conveyed through First Prince Wiemlay. Achieving such a high level of
proficiency in under six months was no trivial feat—it certainly wasn’t the
result of mere casual training. He too must have been growing significantly.

---

And now, the eighth match—the final battle of the first round—was about to take
place.

As I stepped into the venue, I glanced at the audience. Compared to the previous
matches, only about a quarter of the seats remained occupied. It seemed that
those interested in watching a colorless match were in the minority. Well,
considering that Rina was still relatively unknown at this point, it was
understandable that many wouldn't find this match particularly appealing. The
fact that it was the last match of the day, with the sun beginning to set,
likely contributed as well.

At this rate, it might not matter how I demonstrated my abilities until the
finals or semifinals. I would aim for victory as originally planned.

I returned my gaze to the front.

In the ring surrounded by a barrier stood my opponent, Rina. She seemed to be
concentrating, standing firmly with her eyes closed.

As I approached the stage, I observed her.

She was equipped with a one-handed sword and a small buckler. I had heard she
possessed magical talent, so her appearance resembled that of a conventional
magic swordsman.

She reminded me of my sister Nina, but the reality was likely quite different.
Given her petite stature, Rina was more suited for an agile, aggressive fighting
style, and her talents likely leaned towards magic for her offensive
capabilities. Most likely, she had only tried to emulate her beloved sister’s
appearance.

Like Yudai, Rina had also improved her skills. In fact, she had grown stronger
than Yudai, albeit by a slim margin. This was probably the result of her intense
determination.

I knew she harbored resentment towards the Foranada family. It was almost a case
of misplaced anger, but I could sympathize with her feelings, especially since
she was Nina's younger sister. I had chosen to remain hands-off, partially
leaving it to Yudai and Ariarnorth to manage the situation.

Thanks to the two of them, she hadn't caused any trouble for us thus far, but it
seemed her pent-up emotions were still festering inside her. Despite her closed
eyes, I could feel her hostility directed at me.

“Lord Foranada, please step forward.”

As I ascended the stage, the supervising teacher handed me a ring. I slipped it
onto my finger and moved to the designated position.

The distance between Rina and me was about twenty meters. In general, this
distance wouldn't allow for an immediate attack, whether with a sword or magic.

As the match was about to begin, Lina finally opened her eyes. Her gaze, filled
with murky hatred, pierced through me.

Facing her like this reminded me once again: despite being twins, Nina and Lina
looked almost nothing alike. In stark contrast to Nina’s nearly 180-centimeter
tall stature, Lina barely reached 150 centimeters. Compared to Nina’s pure and
straightforward personality, Lina was self-centered and prone to emotional
outbursts. Where Nina had talent with the sword, Lina excelled in magic. Almost
everything about them was opposite.

Well, I could understand the blood relation; they shared similar facial
features. But the truth was that there were hardly any other commonalities
between them.

What should I do here?

With the brief moment I had before the match began, I pondered my strategy.

While I had boldly declared my intentions to Caron and the others, I was
actually unsure how to handle Lina. While a victory was a foregone conclusion,
the manner of that victory was the real issue.

Instantly defeating her was off the table. If I did that, it would likely only
postpone the problem. Someone like Lina would probably never recognize a defeat
that occurred without a fight as a true loss.

Then, should I stage a close contest to give her a proper fight? That was also
not feasible. If I engaged in a close match, I would stray from my original
purpose.

So, aiming for a middle ground seemed to be the best option. It was a hassle,
but considering it was for my beloved Nina, I felt I could take on this
troublesome challenge.

"Now, we will begin the first match of the final tournament\!"

As my thoughts wrapped up, the announcement came at just the right moment.

The few remaining spectators began to stir restlessly, and the referee raised
one hand straight up. With the phrase "Begin\!" he brought it down.

Finally, the match had begun. Lina seemed ready to charge forward, sword at the
ready.

Before she could take off, I made my move.

From the \[Cover Texture\] of \[Phase Concealment\], I summoned a weapon similar
to the one Lina wielded and traced a circle at my feet. It was a circle with a
radius of less than one meter, barely enough space for me to take a step—or
rather, half a step.

This perplexing behavior caused everyone in the venue to look on in suspicion,
and Lina herself frowned in confusion.

But "What does that matter?" she thought as she sprinted forward, combining her
beastkin-derived strength with a buff from Earth magic, running at a speed
recognized as superior by the public.

The spectators erupted in cheers as she closed the twenty meters in just a few
seconds. Her athletic ability was nothing short of awe-inspiring. Some even
optimistically proclaimed that the match was already decided, so captivated were
they by Lina's movements.

However,

“Just as I investigated, there’s no error in her skill level,” I murmured
nonchalantly while keeping my eyes on the descending sword aimed at my head. I
then deflected the approaching dull-colored blade.

“What?\!”

It seemed she had intended to end the match in one strike, as Lina let out a
surprised exclamation. Stunned, she left her side open, leaving herself
vulnerable.

I was not so kind as to let that slide. I launched a middle kick aimed at her
stomach.

The kick connected cleanly, and unable to absorb the force, Lina was sent flying
backward. That said, she suffered no damage; the ring's effect ensured she
remained unharmed despite being knocked away by the impact.

As Lina rolled to the edge of the stage, the venue fell silent. An uncomfortable
stillness settled over the area.

It seemed most of the spectators hadn’t expected me to mount a successful
counterattack. Perhaps they thought that even if I did manage to fight back, it
wouldn’t be so easy to handle.

Either way, it indicated that my strength had been underestimated. I reaffirmed
that joining Minerva's proposal had been the right choice.

Well, I’d set aside the reactions of the crowd for now. It was better to focus
on Lina.

She had already risen to her feet, sword in hand, glaring at me with eyes filled
with determination and hostility.

However, unlike before, she didn’t charge at me again. It seemed she had learned
that rushing in without a plan wouldn’t lead to victory. Though she was a
muscle-brained fighter, she wasn’t completely devoid of learning ability.

It looked like she wouldn’t attack me immediately, so I decided to say something
now.

Waving my sword back and forth, I declared, "In this match, I won’t step outside
this circle. If I do, I’ll accept defeat."

"Huh?"
