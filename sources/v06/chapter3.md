# Chapter 3: Protagonists

The morning classes had ended, and the lunch break had begun. Minerva had been
in the same class, but the others had attended different lectures, so we planned
to meet up now.

It was when I went to visit Caron, who was the closest.

“C-Caroline-san, how about having lunch together?”

As I entered the classroom, I was met with the sight of Yudai, the hero,
nervously inviting Caron to lunch.

“You’re letting your bloodlust slip,” Minerva remarked.

“Whoops, that was close,” I replied.

At Minerva’s warning, I quickly suppressed my emotions. A few students near the
entrance fainted from the intensity, but I caught them before they hit the
ground, so no injuries. I’d have to send an apology gift later.

What was this situation, though?

Caron and Yudai stood face-to-face, while the other students watched from a
distance with warm, amused gazes, as if witnessing a bittersweet moment of
youth.

Come to think of it, Yudai had shown signs of having a crush on Caron. That was
seven or eight years ago, so I’d completely forgotten about it. He hadn’t
approached her since we enrolled, either.

Behind Caron stood Shion, keeping a wary eye on Yudai. It wasn’t a situation to
worry about too much, but it was mildly irritating. Not just Yudai trying to woo
Caron, but the way the onlookers seemed to think they were “perfect for each
other” was particularly grating.

Well, considering Yudai’s reputation, it made sense. Ever since our clash the
day after enrollment, he’d been living diligently. True to the original story,
he’d handled several minor events and even took on student council duties. To
the average student, Yudai embodied the ideal hero.

A hero and a saintess getting together—what a picturesque couple. It was only
natural the masses would be intrigued.

But, but—this was unacceptable. As a noble and as an individual, letting Caron
end up with Yudai was not an option. I would absolutely prevent that future.

“Let’s go,” I said.

“Ugh, such a siscon,” Minerva muttered.

“That’s nothing new,” I retorted.

“Don’t just own it!” she snapped back.

I shrugged at Minerva’s exasperated comment, only to be met with more criticism.

Sure, even I admit my sister complex might be a bit much, but it’s just who I
am. Deal with it.

Feeling a tad guilty, we pressed forward into the heart of the scene.

Meanwhile, Caron and Yudai’s conversation continued.

“I appreciate the invitation, but I must decline. I already have plans,” Caron
said, offering a polite smile as she gently refused.

But Yudai, seemingly too excited, didn’t catch the hint. “Then how about another
time that works for you?” he pressed.

His eyes shone with obvious affection, impossible to conceal, and the
crowd—especially the romance-obsessed female students—grew noisier.

In contrast, Caron, the object of his feelings, responded calmly, “I’m sorry,
but I’ll have to decline.”

Her expression was puzzled, her head slightly tilted.

Minerva, observing this, murmured, “I’ve thought this for a while, but that girl
is _incredibly_ dense.”

I couldn’t agree more.

For her to remain oblivious despite Yudai’s blatant attitude... I felt just a
tiny bit of sympathy for him. Really, just a smidge.

It was baffling. Caron was usually so perceptive about others’ feelings, yet
when it came to romance, she turned utterly clueless, rivaling the densest
protagonists out there.

Honestly, I couldn’t figure out why. I thought I’d raised her to be loved by
everyone.

“Where did I go wrong?” I muttered.

“Probably everything,” Minerva shot back sharply, glaring at me.

I hadn’t said it out loud, but our long acquaintance must’ve let her guess my
thoughts.

Oh well. Caron’s denseness had thrown me off, but for now, we needed to reach
those two.

“Caron,” I called.

“Onii-sama!” Caron’s polite smile transformed into a radiant, genuine one, her
eyes sparkling. Truly, a self-proclaimed and universally acknowledged brocon.

Leaving Yudai behind, she rushed over to me.

“Did you come to pick me up?” she asked.

“As usual, let’s have lunch with everyone,” I said.

“Yes!” she replied cheerfully, her enthusiasm so lively she’d probably be
wagging her tail if she were a beastman.

Then, naturally, she took my hand and urged me toward the classroom exit.

“The others will join us soon, right? Let’s go. They must be waiting for
Onii-sama too,” she said.

“Uh, yeah,” I agreed.

I started walking as she prompted, with Minerva and Shion following behind.

Just before leaving the classroom, I glanced at Yudai.

His jaw dropped in shock, exuding a profound sense of pathos. He’d been the
center of attention, only to be left out in the cold. Understandable.

...Yeah, as a fellow guy, I genuinely felt for him. Stay strong, hero. Your true
heroine is out there somewhere.

After school, having finished my business with the principal, I was walking
alone through the academy grounds. Using [Phase Connection Gate] would’ve been
faster, but sometimes it’s nice to stretch my legs. I’d sent Caron and the
others home to handle their own errands, so even if I returned immediately, I’d
just be twiddling my thumbs. For once, there was hardly any lordly work left
today.

Walking like this, I was reminded of how vast this academy truly was. I only
used a small fraction of its facilities, and most areas I never visited. It
hadn’t even been six months since enrollment, but the sheer scale was
unbelievable.

On a whim, I decided to take a detour. Instead of heading straight to the exit,
I chose a slightly longer route.

Leaving the academic building, I passed by the shared clubroom facility. As the
name suggests, it’s like an apartment where multiple clubs share a single
building, mostly smaller or mid-sized ones.

Unlike our dilapidated but independent clubroom, some groups opt for these
shared spaces for better facilities. Each choice has its pros and cons.

I rarely came to this area. The clubs I’d dealt with were all top-tier, so this
place felt unfamiliar. For minor errands, I could just send a servant.

Around the shared clubroom, sports clubs were warming up or jogging. Clubs at
this level likely couldn’t reserve proper fields or training grounds, or perhaps
they trained outdoors to maximize time at better facilities. I’d heard about
such logistical struggles secondhand.

Even just walking, I drew a lot of attention. My white hair stood out, and I
felt countless glances. No one stared blatantly, but the sheer number of eyes
created pressure.

I’d only meant to take a stroll, but maybe this was a bad idea. I seemed to be
disrupting the focus of these diligently training students.

I wanted to keep exploring, but disturbing them further wasn’t my intent. I’d
continue my walk elsewhere.

Decision made, I turned to leave the clubroom area.

But my retreat was a tad too late.

As I took a step to withdraw, a deafening roar echoed from the shared clubroom
behind me. The ground trembled, instantly signaling an explosion.

Turning reflexively, I saw part of the building collapsing, black smoke
billowing upward. The sound confirmed it—an explosion had occurred.

A quick scan showed no students caught in the blast outside, but a few remained
inside. Were they the culprits?

They didn’t seem injured. For now, I’d assume they were suspects and act
accordingly.

I couldn’t ignore a commotion happening right in front of me. The academy would
respond, but I doubted they’d have enough personnel to secure the scene and
prevent secondary damage in time.

Sighing inwardly, I waved my fingers. Instantly, a barrier enveloped the
explosion site, securing the suspects and preventing further harm.

The surrounding students, already rattled by the explosion, grew more confused
by the barrier’s appearance, but that wasn’t my concern.

“Haa,” I sighed, stepping toward the barrier.

The issue was that the suspect students had started brawling. They were
grappling and even attempting magic, though I remotely disrupted the latter to
prevent escalation. Still, they were seriously fighting, and I couldn’t just
leave them be.

Passing through the barrier, I entered the collapsed building. The dust from the
explosion had settled somewhat, and with detection magic compensating for poor
visibility, it wasn’t a major issue.

As expected, the scene matched my scan: six male students—three against
three—engaged in a fight. No, “fight” was too grand a term. It was a childish
scuffle. There was some blood, but nothing life-threatening.

I couldn’t discern the cause. All I heard were insults hurled at each other,
with no clear explanation of what sparked this.

Still, I could guess the gist. Most likely, an argument escalated, magic was
misused, and this mess ensued.

Magic, if mishandled, becomes a weapon. Inexperienced students are especially
prone to letting emotions take over. Incidents like this, caused by magical
outbursts, weren’t unheard of at the academy. Last year, there were five such
cases, if I recalled correctly.

In my previous life’s terms, it’s like everyone carrying a handgun. Mishaps are
inevitable. Regulation is futile since it depends on individual intent.
Ultimately, only environment and values can curb such behavior.

It’s strange, though. Despite minimal regulation, this world maintains order.
With everyone wielding powerful weapons, you’d expect a dog-eat-dog society, yet
it functions.

Enough theorizing—I’m no cultural anthropologist. Right now, I needed to subdue
these students.

Thanks to concealing my presence, they hadn’t noticed me. Capturing them would
be easy.

The question was how. Magic was the quickest method, but with no idea when
academy staff would arrive, conserving mana was preferable. I was confident I
could last days without running dry, but human nature leans toward minimal
effort.

“Might as well test this,” I muttered, drawing ultra-fine threads from [Phase
Concealment].

These weren’t ordinary threads. Made of pseudo-mithril, they boasted exceptional
durability and high mana conductivity.

I planned to infuse them with [Magic Coating] to restrain the students. For mere
students, minimal mana would suffice, and the mithril ensured efficiency.
Top-tier cost-performance.

I’d made these but hadn’t had a chance to use them. Capturing someone alive
usually meant knocking them out or intimidating them.

Seizing the opportunity, I channeled mana into the wires and tossed them at the
brawling students. Pre-programmed with [Design], the threads autonomously bound
all six. Caught off guard, they couldn’t resist.

“What the hell is this?!”

“Damn it, I can’t break free!”

“Ow, it’s cutting into my arm!”

“My magic’s not working—what’s going on?!”

“Damn, damn, damn!!”

“Shut up, you!”

The students spewed crude curses, struggling against the bindings.

I’d tied them tightly to prevent further violence, but they were obnoxiously
loud. Even in this situation, their tempers hadn’t cooled.

I enveloped them in a soundproof barrier. Now I could wait for academy
reinforcements in peace.

Five minutes later, help arrived. My detection picked up approaching figures.

“Ugh,” I let slip.

The academy’s response team included Princess Arianorth, the student council
president, her guard Baroness Louise, Yudai the hero, and Rina, Nina’s sister.
All were troublesome for me.

There were about ten adults too, but those four—especially Yudai and
Rina—weren’t likely to be helpful if things got messy.

It made sense that the powerful student council would handle this, but why did
_they_ always show up when I was involved? I’d have preferred senior members
over anyone but the president.

Complaining wouldn’t help, though. I had no choice but to work through them.

I dispelled the barrier around the site to let Arianorth’s group approach.

Soon, the group, including those three, arrived.

“Ugh,” Yudai blurted, his face twisting in distaste.

His reaction mirrored mine from moments ago... and quietly, I vowed to
reconsider my attitude toward him going forward.

“Until the matter is settled, you six will be detained by the student council.
Understood?”

“Yes...”

The six students responsible for the incident nodded meekly at Arianorth’s icy
tone.

Once the academy’s reinforcements arrived, the aftermath was handled swiftly.
The shared clubroom building was immediately sealed off, and over half of the
debris had already been cleared.

Simultaneously, the suspect students were questioned. Apparently, it started
with a trivial argument that escalated, leading to this mess. What a nuisance.

Well, the students had cooled off and seemed genuinely remorseful, and
fortunately, there were no injuries. Their punishment would likely be
light—perhaps a temporary suspension of their club activities and a brief
suspension from classes.

After watching the six be escorted away by the adults, I let out a small
sigh—not of relief, but of exasperation.

The real hassle for me was what came next.

Arianorth approached, accompanied by Louise.

“My apologies for keeping you waiting, Zex-san. Now, if you could explain the
circumstances of this incident,” she said.

As expected, I was also subject to questioning.

The six had already confirmed that I’d intervened to suppress the situation. But
as a direct participant, I had a duty to explain. While I had no desire to get
deeply involved with them, I wasn’t outright refusing either. Best to get this
over with quickly.

I recounted the events as concisely and honestly as possible, leaving nothing
out.

Arianorth placed a hand to her chin, murmuring, “Hmm,” as she pondered.

Bracing myself for what she might say, I watched as she lowered her hand.

“I understand the situation. There seem to be no inconsistencies, so you’re free
to go. Thank you for your cooperation,” she said with a slight bow.

I narrowed my eyes, assessing whether her words hid any ulterior motive, but I
concluded there was none.

Relieved inwardly, I responded, “No, it’s only natural to help in times of
trouble. If I could be of service to Your Highness or the academy, it’s an
honor.”

I didn’t care about the “honor” part, but the first sentiment was genuine. I’m
not so arrogant as to meddle in everything, but lending a hand when needed feels
important.

“Your words are appreciated, Zex-san. They’re a great help to us,” Arianorth
replied with a faint smile.

Her signature chilling smile gave the illusion that she could peer into one’s
soul. I had no idea how much of my thoughts she actually grasped.

With my business concluded, I nodded to Arianorth and Louise, then turned to
leave. Opening a [Phase Connection Gate], I prepared to make a swift exit. As
The mood for a leisurely stroll was gone.

But someone wasn’t about to let me go so easily.

“Wait,” a voice called.

It was Yudai. He’d just finished his assigned task and entered the scene.

I could’ve ignored him and left, but that would only strain our already tenuous
relationship. Disregarding someone of his growing reputation wasn’t the wisest
move.

I stopped and glanced at him.

His eyes were resolute, not clouded by delusion. His demeanor had improved since
our last encounter. Perhaps the events he’d faced had fostered some mental
growth.

Given our history, this was bound to be troublesome. But seeing his expression,
I figured it might be worth engaging him a little. After all, spurring the
protagonist’s growth is part of a villain’s role.

With a wry internal chuckle, I closed the [Phase Connection Gate] and faced
Yudai.

Meeting his gaze directly, I exerted a slight pressure—not full [Intimidation],
but enough to make my presence felt.

Yudai flinched but didn’t look away.

Good. As [Scan] suggested, he’d grown. I felt a bit reassured.

“What’s your business, Hero?” I asked, ignoring the intrigued looks from
Arianorth and the others.

Yudai replied earnestly, “I want... to fight you.”

“A mock battle, you mean?”

“Yeah.”

I raised an eyebrow, but he nodded immediately.

What logic led to challenging me to a mock battle? He didn’t still think I was
the root of all evil, did he?

Suppressing my unease, I kept my expression neutral and asked, “Why a mock
battle?”

“Because I want to move forward,” he answered without hesitation.

“Over the past six months, I’ve grown. I’ve had help from Rina, Aria, and
Louise-san, but I’ve overcome all sorts of challenges.” He raised both hands,
staring at them and said.

I knew that. With my dedicated surveillance, I was fully aware of every incident
Yudai had resolved.

He’d tackled challenges just within his capabilities, solving them with his
allies, like a classic protagonist straight out of the original game.

“In doing so, I realized this world is exactly as you said. My values don’t hold
much weight here, and few people share them. If I want to uphold my beliefs, I’d
need to start a revolution or persuade everyone. I learned how daunting that
is.”

He really had grown. Arianorth and I had secretly orchestrated a few minor
noble-related troubles for him, but I was surprised they’d had such a profound
effect.

Those issues should’ve been manageable for him, but for someone clinging to
their previous world’s values, they must’ve been jarring.

“I’ve gotten stronger. But deep down, the weak version of myself still lingers.
I need to overcome that to grow further.”

“And how does fighting me fit into that?”

“You’re the reason I wanted to get stronger in the first place. Without you, I’d
probably have taken my time training.”

Interesting. His intuition was correct. In the original story, Yudai would’ve
been weaker at this point. I wasn’t thrilled to be the cause, but spurring his
growth wasn’t a bad outcome.

“I want to surpass you. So please, have a mock battle with me. I’m begging you!”
Yudai concluded, bowing deeply, his body bent at a sharp angle.

I took my time to think it over.

He had indeed grown. The old Yudai wouldn’t have bowed to a noble, let alone
engaged in dialogue. Recognizing that his values weren’t absolute was a step
forward.

But he was still naive.

First, his manner of addressing an earl like me was utterly improper. If he were
a commoner or a lesser noble, he could’ve been punished. Even noble children
weren’t exempt. His status as a hero was the only thing shielding him. He needed
to rethink that.

Second, he hadn’t considered my perspective. He’d only spoken of his own
benefits. When making a request of someone you’re not close to, offering some
form of compensation is basic courtesy.

Lastly, he hadn’t fully shed his arrogance, complacency, or overconfidence. He
acted self-reflective, but deep down, his pride loomed large. His belief that he
could beat me said it all.

Confidence is fine, but complacency is not. That’s a core combat principle in
Foranada. Know your enemy, prepare thoroughly, and end the fight before it
begins. That’s how you survive in this world.

Still, his overconfidence was somewhat understandable.

He’d never experienced true defeat. He’d faced failures but never a crushing
loss. Subconsciously, he likely believed he was invincible.

This mock battle request was a perfect chance to teach him defeat.

Without looking, I used detection to check Arianorth and Louise.

Arianorth was unchanged, but Louise showed slight agitation—perhaps tension. She
was watching the situation intently.

This was undoubtedly Arianorth’s doing. She’d recognized Yudai’s inflated ego
and chose me as his opponent, cleverly framing it as his own initiative. Sneaky.

I sighed.

“Fine. I accept your challenge.”

“Really?!”

“Yes. My word is final.”

I could’ve refused, but seeing his decent progress over six months, I believed
he’d keep growing. If a little effort could be an investment in his future, I’d
swallow my annoyance.

The mock battle was arranged without much delay. Leaving the remaining cleanup
to the adults, we headed to a nearby training ground.

I was dumbfounded. They weren’t even hiding it anymore. The cleanup didn’t need
Arianorth, Louise, or Yudai, and this training ground was already reserved. The
preparations were _too_ perfect.

Seeing Arianorth’s faint smirk, I felt an urge to punch her. I wouldn’t, though.

Only Arianorth would be overseeing the match. Due to the incident with Nina,
Rina still resented me, and meeting her could escalate into trouble.

Come to think of it, I’d sensed Rina earlier but hadn’t seen her today. Knowing
this was likely part of Arianorth’s plan left me with mixed feelings.

Louise’s absence was to guide Rina elsewhere efficiently. A guard leaving her
post seemed questionable, but when Arianorth declared, “With Zex-san and Yudai
here, we’ll be fine,” I had no rebuttal. It was true.

Time to focus.

Yudai and I faced each other in the center of the training ground, about fifteen
meters apart, standing naturally. Each of us held a wooden sword sized for
one-handed use.

A short distance away, Arianorth, the referee, was already enveloped in a light
magic barrier.

All that remained was the signal to start.

Judging by Arianorth’s demeanor, she’d likely begin in about a minute. Her
emotions were unreadable, but her subtle movements gave me a clue.

I used the brief time to strategize. How should I approach this mock battle?

There were two options: crush him instantly to demonstrate overwhelming
superiority or hold back to make it feel like a proper fight.

Both had pros and cons. The former would humble Yudai but risked breaking his
spirit. The latter was the opposite.

Hmm. The best balance might be to dominate while leaving a sliver of hope for
victory. That’s an incredibly delicate adjustment.

If normal restraint is measuring by the bucket, this required precision by the
gram. It demanded finesse.

It was doable but ridiculously tedious. Why was I going this far for him? I
wanted him to handle the Eastern Demon King’s seal reinforcement, but he needed
to grow mentally and physically to at least defeat demons.

Ugh, what a hassle. If it weren’t for other demons capable of possession, I
could just focus on boosting his strength.

No use complaining. My goal is to steer Caron away from her doomed fate. To
deviate from the original story, I must prepare for other irregularities—like
the hero falling to darkness.

So, as long as it aids his growth without crossing my bottom line, I’ll
cooperate.

“Now, we’ll begin the mock battle between Yudai and Zex-san. I, Arianorth, will
serve as referee. The match ends when one is incapacitated, I deem one
incapacitated, or upon surrender. Understood?”

“Yes,” I replied.

“Yeah,” Yudai said.

We nodded at Arianorth’s final confirmation.

She raised one hand skyward, then swung it down.

“Begin!”

Yudai moved first, kicking up dust as he charged with [Body Enhancement].

Right, protagonists often use [Body Enhancement]. Thanks to their past-life
knowledge, they bear less strain and gain slightly greater effects than this
world’s natives.

But in this reality, [Body Enhancement] isn’t their exclusive privilege. It’s
ours. Unlike their subconscious use, we wield it with full awareness, achieving
far greater potency.

As Yudai closed in, he raised his wooden sword overhead and swung down with
force. Seeing me unmoving, he likely thought victory was assured. I could sense
his complacency and overconfidence.

This was bad.

His carelessness was that of an unseasoned fighter. Relaxing before securing the
outcome—lacking zanshin, the state of sustained readiness. His swordsmanship and
physical skills had improved, but his combat mindset was half-baked.

No surprise there. Coming from modern Japan, he’d primarily hunted weaker
magical beasts, and his events were tailored to his level. With little
experience against stronger opponents, this inconsistency was inevitable.

I’d planned to observe longer, but it was time to act. His initial strike gave
me a clear read on his abilities, so I’d focus on tempering his mentality.

Swaying slightly, I dodged his attack by a hair’s breadth.

To his overconfident mind, this close evasion would seem like a fluke—exactly my
intent.

Having swung with the intent to end it in one blow, Yudai couldn’t transition to
his next move. His weight had shifted fully forward, his sword striking the
ground.

I could’ve countered to knock him out, but that wouldn’t serve my purpose.
Instead, I opted for a more humiliating response.

I seized both of his hands gripping the sword. With his weight tilted forward,
he couldn’t easily shake me off.

A moment’s immobilization was enough. I swiftly swept his legs. Leaning forward,
he toppled face-first to the ground.

“Guh!”

“Lie there dawdling, and you’ll get crushed,” I taunted.

“?!”

My warning spurred Yudai to roll away, creating distance.

Immediately after, I stomped the ground where he’d been, the impact sinking the
earth with a thud.

Yudai, now on one knee, gaped at the sight, his face paling as he likely
imagined what would’ve happened if he hadn’t dodged.

He rose warily, gripping his sword and sidestepping cautiously to gauge
distance. Unlike before, he was far more prudent.

Good. That’s the kind of vigilance I wanted. I’m not an opponent you can afford
to underestimate.

Though, caution alone wouldn’t guarantee survival.

Deciding the match wouldn’t progress otherwise, I took the offensive. I darted
lightly, circling behind Yudai.

I hadn’t used any special techniques, but he couldn’t track my movement,
glancing around frantically.

He only noticed me as I swung my sword. He barely managed to block, intercepting
my strike mid-arc.

But his reaction was too slow. Unable to fully absorb the impact, he was sent
flying over ten meters, tumbling across the ground.

I’d held back, but the impact might’ve cracked a bone or two.

With a casual thought, I watched Yudai sprawled on the ground. With [Body
Enhancement] active, he shouldn’t be unconscious from that.

“Guh...”

As expected, he was still conscious, groaning in pain as he struggled to rise.

Time to end it.

I caught a faint trace of fear in his emotions. Continuing further would only
harm him. Stopping here would fuel his resolve.

I closed the distance instantly, pressing my sword to his throat, then glanced
at Arianorth.

Catching my intent, she called out, “That’s enough! The winner is Zex-san!”

Her words dispelled the tension in the air.

I let my heightened battle intent fade.

“Damn it,” Yudai muttered, his voice thick with frustration.

Still on the ground, he clenched his fists, trembling. The loss clearly stung.

One last push.

“You’re too careless. Holding back is fine, but never underestimate any
opponent,” I said, lacing my tone with a hint of mockery, knowing his
personality would turn it into motivation.

“?!”

As expected, he tensed further, his fighting spirit flaring as he gritted his
teeth.

It worked. I’d leave the rest to Arianorth.

“I’ll take my leave, Your Highness,” I said.

“Thank you for indulging us,” she replied.

After a brief nod to Arianorth, I opened a [Phase Connection Gate] and returned
to my estate.

October had arrived. With the preliminary rounds of the grade-level individual
tournament just half a month away, the academy buzzed with an unusual
excitement.

For us, though, nothing changed. We hadn’t trained so softly that we’d panic
now, and our skills were backed by solid experience. I was slightly concerned
about Marina, but her resilience was formidable, rendering my worries unfounded.

“Well, I’m going to browse some magic theory books,” Minerva said.

“If anything comes up, use the [Magic Communicator],” I replied.

“I know,” she said.

In the library’s reading area, Minerva and I had this exchange.

Since our last visit, we’d been coming here together whenever our schedules
aligned. Minerva buried herself in magic research materials, while I looked for
something to pass the time. Sitting shoulder-to-shoulder reading felt a bit
ticklish, but it was a quintessential youthful student moment. Wanting Caron and
Minerva to enjoy their school life, I saw this as a positive trend.

After seeing Minerva off toward the bookshelves, I set out to find a book of my
own.

I’d never disliked reading, so scanning for interesting titles was surprisingly
enjoyable. The library’s vast collection could easily kill time. But since
staying too long would cut into my time with Minerva, I decided to pick quickly.

“No one’s here today,” I noted.

A quick scan with [Detection] revealed no other users besides Minerva and me.

I was referring to Viscountess Skia.

Since making that proposal to her, we’d only met once. I’d hoped to check on her
progress, but her absence meant it couldn’t be helped. I was aware I’d stirred
things up for her, so I was trying to be considerate.

Well, I’d told her I’d wait six months, so I’d be patient. She must have her own
thoughts to sort through.

On another note—

I decided to read some history books from other territories today and was eyeing
a relevant bookshelf when a [Telepathy] message came from one of my
subordinates.

_“Zex-sama, may I have a moment of your time?”_

“Go ahead. What’s up?”

_“The Saintess under surveillance is heading your way. To the library, to be

“What?”

The sudden report made me frown, but I quickly connected the dots.

“Is the Saintess with Zigrald?”

_“Er, yes, as you say.”_

Caught off guard, my subordinate confirmed with a hint of confusion.

As I thought.

As mentioned before, the first-year individual tournament was an event centered
on Zigrald. And it had already begun before the tournament itself.

In truth, the Deimann family’s summoning ritual techniques, Zigrald’s lineage,
had long been lost, with no one left who could perform them. But Zigrald, with
his keen interest in magic, was tirelessly researching to revive this lost art.
His frequent library visits were part of that effort.

In the original story, the protagonist Saintess, learning of this by chance,
offered to help, and together they succeeded in reviving the summoning ritual.
So, it was natural for them to visit the library at this time.

The timing, however, was less than ideal. At this rate, they’d run into Minerva,
who was already back at the reading area.

...Wait.

“No, maybe that’s fine,” I reconsidered after a brief moment of thought.

The reason I’d avoided the Saintess was to minimize contact between her and
Caron, who were destined to be enemies in the original story, to improve Caron’s
survival odds.

In other words, this had little to do with Minerva. In the original story, they
became close friends, so I judged there was no cause for concern.

Of course, there was a chance the Saintess might exert some odd influence, but
it wasn’t worth forcibly moving Minerva over.

There was a slight worry, but I’d trust my fiancée.

For now, I’d head back. I trusted Minerva, but being by her side would put me
more at ease.

Grabbing a random book from the shelf, I hurried back to the reading area where
she waited.

No [Phase Connection Gate] this time, by the way. Using it in a non-emergency
would raise suspicion instantly.

When I arrived, the Saintess and Zigrald were already speaking to Minerva.
They’d just exchanged greetings and hadn’t yet moved to small talk.

“Well, greetings to you, Saintess-dono and Deimann-dono,” I said, standing
beside Minerva with a friendly smile.

Minerva aside, the Saintess and Zigrald hadn’t noticed my approach. They
flinched at my voice, eyes widening.

“Oh, it’s you. Greetings,” the Saintess said.

“—?! G-Greetings,” Zigrald stammered.

Both quickly relaxed and responded, but... was it my imagination, or was the
Saintess’s reaction a bit excessive?

“Earl Foranada, please, call me Seira. I may be the Saintess, but I’m just a
commoner at heart. Titles feel so stiff,” she said smoothly, before I could
dwell on my suspicions. Her demeanor was entirely natural, showing no trace of
her earlier agitation.

Maybe I was overthinking it? Still, I’d keep an eye on her.

The suspicion lingered, but there was no way to address it here. I didn’t even
know what exactly was bothering me, so I set it aside for now.

Come to think of it, the Saintess’s name was Seira. I’d read it in reports but
always thought of her as “Saintess,” so it slipped my mind. Calling her by name
felt oddly jarring. I’d have to get used to it.

“Understood, Seira-dono,” I said.

“No need for formalities either. I understand my position, but at least in the
academy, I’d prefer to be treated casually.”

“...Alright. Is this better?”

“Yes!” Seira beamed brightly.

So this is the protagonist.

This was my first direct conversation with her, and it was eye-opening. Her
expressions, voice, gestures—her entire aura seemed to say, “Let’s be friends!”
It was like water soaking into a dry sponge, effortlessly instilling goodwill.

Seira must have a natural talent for disarming others, perfectly suited for both
a protagonist and a Saintess. No wonder she could bond with the prickly heroes
of the original story.

I’d need to stay sharp. Getting too close could cloud my judgment.

Still—

“It’s only fair. You can call me Zex too, Seira-dono,” I offered.

“Thank you, Zex-san,” she replied.

With her taking a step toward friendliness, I had to reciprocate to avoid
looking standoffish.

Then Zigrald spoke up. “Please call me Zigrald as well, Earl Foranada. And feel
free to speak informally.”

“Got it. I’ll do that. You can be casual with me too, in unofficial settings.”

I blinked for a moment but caught on after seeing his sour expression.

Zigrald seemed jealous of our exchange. It was just polite small talk, but love
is blind, I suppose.

To think Seira had won over Zigrald—the second-hardest capture target—in just
six months was impressive.

Suppressing my unease, I continued the conversation. “What were you three
talking about?”

“Nothing much,” Minerva replied, sounding slightly annoyed. “You showed up right
after we exchanged greetings.”

Oh? Could it be—jealousy from you too? Adorable.

“Hey, cut it out in front of people!” she snapped.

“Sorry,” I said.

I’d unconsciously started patting Minerva’s head, charmed by her reaction. I
quickly pulled my hand back and apologized.

She was scowling, but her emotions brimmed with joy—she was just embarrassed.
I’d have to pat her again later.

“You two are close, aren’t you?” Seira said, eyes wide, having watched our
exchange.

It might’ve looked like I was being a nuisance, but she seemed to see through to
Minerva’s true feelings. Even if they were destined to be friends, that was some
sharp insight.

Zigrald, meanwhile, looked at Seira as if to say, “They’re close?”

But Minerva, caught up in the moment, didn’t have room for doubt. “N-No way! I
don’t l-l-like this guy or anything!” she blurted, her face bright red,
delivering a textbook tsundere line.

She’d been more open lately, but her embarrassment had apparently hit its limit.

It was cute, but not the best reaction for the setting.

“Minerva, this is a library,” I reminded her.

“Oh... S-Sorry,” she said.

Libraries demand silence worldwide.

The librarian at the desk was staring at us.

Noticing this, Minerva hurriedly apologized, lowering her voice. She then
covered her face with both hands, retreating like a turtle, so I decided to
change the topic. Further teasing would be too much.

“Why are you two in the library?” I asked.

Seira, picking up on my intent, answered with a wry smile. “Zigrald-kun’s doing
some research, and I’m helping out.”

“What kind of research?” I asked.

I knew the details but couldn’t admit it, so I kept the conversation neutral.

Zigrald answered, “You wouldn’t understand.”

His tone carried a hint of hostility.

Understandable. The Deimann family’s summoning ritual was once mocked by society
and eventually lost. Reviving it wasn’t something he’d easily share with
outsiders.

My only thought was, “Fair enough.”

But someone present didn’t know the context.

“You won’t know unless you ask,” Minerva said sharply.

I realized I’d picked the worst possible topic.

Since coming to Foranada, Minerva had toned down her pride, but she was still a
proud girl at heart. Being dismissed with “you wouldn’t understand” naturally
provoked her.

I’d completely forgotten her personality. There were safer topics—why did I step
into this minefield?

I wanted to bury my face in my hands, but there was no time for that. You can’t
take back words once spoken.

“No, you definitely wouldn’t,” Zigrald insisted.

“That’s why dismissing it outright is pointless. Nothing’s absolute in this
world,” Minerva shot back.

“Saying ‘nothing’s absolute’ is contradictory.”

“Don’t get hung up on wordplay! I’m not here for games. Spit it out already.”

“No way. I’m not obligated to tell you.”

“What did you say?”

Both Minerva and Zigrald were strong-willed magic enthusiasts. Once they started
arguing, it was inevitable they’d heat up. Their voices grew louder.

Noticing the librarian’s raised eyebrow, I decided it was time to intervene.
Exchanging a glance with Seira, we each restrained our companions.

“Calm down, Minerva. I just said this is a library,” I said.

“Zigrald-kun, please calm down. This isn’t like you,” Seira urged.

“Mgh...”

Both still had enough sense to recognize the situation. They quieted down,
though their glares still clashed. It was better than shouting.

As silence returned, Seira spoke up. “I think we should tell them.”

“Seira?!” Zigrald exclaimed, eyes wide with surprise.

Unfazed, she raised both hands and continued, “One’s the top student of our
year, and the other’s the daughter of the Loramber dukedom. They surely have
wisdom we lack. Doesn’t passing up this chance go against your goal,
Zigrald-kun?”

“Guh... Nngh.”

Unable to counter her logic, Zigrald fell silent.

Shortly after, he sighed and said, “Fine. I’ll talk.”

What an unexpected turn. To think I’d get involved in Zigrald’s research.

Then again, considering Seira’s personality, it wasn’t surprising.

In the original story, they’d revive the summoning ritual on their own, but they
didn’t know that. For Zigrald’s goal, choosing a path with higher success was
only natural.

With a reluctant sigh, Zigrald began, “Do you two know about ritual magic?”

“It’s a technique that enhances magic’s power or effects by paying a price,
right?” Minerva answered.

In this world, magic is shaped by mana and intent. While all magic follows this
principle, ritual magic—also called cost magic—involves offering something
beyond mana as a price.

A clear example is sacrificing vitality instead of mana, which falls under
ritual magic. It allows effects beyond one’s mana capacity by offering vitality
as a cost.

Other costs include livestock, blood, harvested crops, and more. The types of
offerings are diverse.

However, ritual magic is merely a supplement to existing magic. You can cast
magic without a cost, and if you’re going to pay one, you might as well use a
higher-tier spell. Thus, ritual magic has gradually faded.

But some unique forms of magic exist.

“My Deimann family was once skilled in a form of ritual magic—a summoning
ritual. As you know, ritual magic is often mocked as outdated, and our family’s
magic was lost as a result,” Zigrald explained.

“And Zigrald-kun’s been researching to revive it!” Seira added brightly.

“Summoning? As in, paying a price to call something?” Minerva asked, tilting her
head at the unfamiliar term.

Zigrald nodded. “You’re quick. The summoning ritual involves offering a price to
call magical beings from another realm.”

“Another realm, huh?” Minerva murmured.

“You don’t believe me? Then this conversation’s over. I can’t entrust my
research to someone who doesn’t accept the premise,” Zigrald said sharply,
speaking quickly and moving to leave.

He’d likely been ridiculed for this before and wanted to avoid that pain again.

But that was premature. My fiancée wasn’t like those small-minded critics.

“Wait,” Minerva said, her clear voice resonating despite its low volume,
striking the heart.

Zigrald stopped and turned back. “What?”

“No one said they don’t believe you,” she replied.

“But—”

“No buts. I don’t know if other realms exist, but dismissing it outright is
foolish. Research is about exploring the unknown and making it known.”

Minerva flashed a fearless smile, radiant enough to dispel dark clouds.

“You... believe me?” Zigrald asked hesitantly.

“Not yet. I’ll believe it once it’s verified and proven. That’s what research is
for, right?”

“Uh, yeah.”

Her relentlessly pragmatic approach left Zigrald flustered.

But the once-tense conversation had neatly resolved.

“Hehe, we’ve got new allies, Zigrald-kun,” Seira said with a smile.

“...Yeah. Thanks to your advice, Seira,” he replied, returning her smile.

Oh, wow. It seemed we’d been used to boost their rapport. Whether Seira realized
it or not, she was quite adept at seizing opportunities.

“For now, tell us your progress. I’ll point out what’s lacking. That works,
right, Zex?” Minerva asked.

“I’m fine with it,” I said.

“Please, we’re counting on you both,” Seira said.

“I’m relying on you,” Zigrald added.

How did it come to this?

I tilted my head inwardly.

Somehow, I’d been roped into deeply assisting Zigrald. I’d meant to minimize
interference with the protagonists’ events.

I could’ve stopped it, but intervening in that flow was impossible. Minerva’s
nerdy passion had been fully ignited.

At this point, I had no choice but to commit. The process might differ, but the
outcome would be the same. I’d focus on fostering Seira and Zigrald’s bond while
aiming to complete the summoning ritual.
