# Chapter 1: The Cheyni Family

It was early August. The height of summer brought a blazing sun that shone
brilliantly, with temperatures rising steadily, steaming the body.

Well, compared to Japan, the humidity was lower, so it wasn’t _deathly_ hot.
Additionally, magical cooling devices were commonplace, so there was no issue
staying indoors.

However...

“It’s hot.”

“It’s hot, isn’t it...”

“I’m gonna die.”

In the scorching heat of the Foranada Castle gardens, three peculiar individuals
were holding a tea party outdoors, even going so far as to use a parasol.

One of them, needless to say, was me, Zex Levito San Foranada. I was wearing a
lightweight short-sleeved shirt and shorts, but I was already drenched in sweat.
At this point, even my slimming physique couldn’t keep up, and my well-trained
muscles were practically saying hello. My trademark white hair clung to my
forehead, feeling rather annoying.

Another was the blonde, red-eyed beauty sitting to my right. Her name was
Caroline Flamel de San Foranada—my genuine younger sister. Not only was her
appearance stunning, but her proportions were also exceptional, making her the
envy of all.

In truth, she carried the fate of dying a year and a half from now, but at this
moment, she looked so wilted she might collapse even sooner. Somehow, even her
ponytail, tied neatly, seemed to droop...

Unlike me, however, her clothes weren’t see-through. As a proper lady, she was
apparently using light magic to conceal any transparency.

The final person, sitting—or rather, practically sprawling with her upper body
across my lap—was Nina, a wolf beastman woman. Normally, she wore her hair in a
single braid, but today, citing the heat, she had tied it into a bun.

However, Nina was no less beautiful or well-proportioned than my sister Caron,
so our current position was quite problematic. With various things pressing
against me, my rationality was being put to the test.

The silver lining in this misfortune was that, thanks to sweating profusely, my
discomfort level was currently outweighing any other concerns.

While stroking Nina’s head and beast ears on my lap, I said, “If you’re dying,
get off me. It’s too hot already.”

“That’s right, Nina. Such... shameless behavior is unacceptable. It’s also
troubling for Onii-sama,” Caron chimed in, perhaps lending me support? But Nina
stubbornly refused to budge.

If anything, she doubled down, saying, “If you’re so jealous, Caron, just climb
onto his lap too. I’ll share one side.”

She even vacated one of my knees, tempting her.

Oh, come on, give me a break. That verbal attack was practically a critical hit
for Caron. After all, she was a severe brocon who had developed romantic
feelings for her real brother. There was no way she could resist such
temptation.

“Thank you very much, I’ll dig in\!”

As expected, Caron immediately plopped her head onto my lap. The already close
distance became zero, making my body temperature rise even further.

The table was spacious, yet the three of us were crammed together at zero
distance. And under a parasol in this sweltering heat, no less. What were we
even doing?

Well, I was just as bad for not refusing them when they were clearly enjoying
themselves.

For about five minutes, we held our ground in that position, but—

“I’m at my limit. I’m using magic,” I declared.

“Indeed, any more of this, and we might actually harm our health,” Caron agreed.

“No choice, then,” Nina conceded.

I activated 【Heavenly Change】, altering the environment within a few meters
around us. I adjusted the temperature and humidity to comfortable levels,
lowering them gradually to avoid shocking our bodies.

Next, Caron seemed to use the advanced light magic 【Purification】, instantly
cleaning our sweat-soaked bodies and clothes.

“Phew. Holding a tea party outside in this season was reckless, after all,” I
muttered after quenching my thirst with the lukewarm tea on the table.

The two on my lap spoke up as well.

“Indeed. Even for a tea party, air conditioning is a must,” Caron said.

“Why are we even having tea outside in the first place?” Nina questioned.

Nina’s question was fair, but I didn’t think _she_ had the right to ask it.

Caron seemed to agree, letting out a sigh. “Who do you think suggested this? It
was none other than you, Nina, who said, ‘Since our vacation at the summer
resort got canceled, I want to experience summer.’”

Exactly. The mastermind behind this tea party was Nina. Since no one else wanted
to join, her fiancé—me—and her close friend Caron had no choice but to tag
along.

In response, Nina casually averted her gaze. “I’ve come to appreciate the
importance of modern conveniences.”

Well, that’s true, but it feels a bit off coming from you. Look, even Caron’s
smile is stiffening.

“Nina\~\!”

“Wait, Caron\?!”

Caron launched a tickle attack, and Nina desperately resisted. Their adorable
skirmish unfolded... right on my lap.

I wished they’d at least change the stage. It should’ve been amusing to watch,
but being so close, all I could feel was annoyance.

Clutching my tea protectively, I reflected on how this all came about.

As mentioned, Nina’s reckless suggestion stemmed from the cancellation of our
summer resort vacation.

Foranada had a moderately famous lakeside town, a popular summer tourist
destination. Every year, we spent our vacation at a cottage there, but due to a
certain incident, it was canceled this year.

The issue was the destruction of the cottage. Apparently, our foolish parents,
who had been enjoying their vacation earlier, were the culprits.

Talk about a rare topic coming up as a mishap. It was oddly reassuring to know
they hadn’t changed.

As a result, we were left to laze around Foranada Castle this year.

Nina’s antics were likely a way to blow off steam. It wasn’t surprising that
she’d be frustrated after our traditional vacation was ruined. That she kept it
to this level showed her restraint. When the incident first came to light, Caron
was ready to hunt down our parents and execute them. I had to stop her with all
my might.

Still, if even the relatively lenient Nina was acting like this, the others must
be harboring some degree of stress.

In that case, I should come up with an alternative. A full vacation like usual
might be impossible, but a discreet trip could be feasible. If I put in my all,
I could pull it off without causing trouble.

First, I’d need to choose a location. Picking carelessly could lead to chaos, so
I had to be cautious.

While Caron and Nina continued their noisy antics, I mentally listed and
discarded potential summer resorts in the country.

After much deliberation, still unable to decide, I was interrupted.

“Onii-sama.”

“We have a guest.”

I noticed Caron and Nina had stopped their playful struggle and were looking
toward one side of the garden.

Following their gaze, I saw someone standing near the garden’s entrance.

She had curly, deep purple hair, dazzling golden eyes, and a decent figure
marred by her slouched posture—an unmistakably indoor-type woman.

It was Skia Thornbull Yu Ga Tali Cheyni. The fourth daughter of the Cheyni
viscount branch family, she was slated to work in Foranada in the future. As a
classmate, we often acted together, and she had come to Foranada this summer as
well.

For someone like Skia, a severe recluse who usually stayed indoors reading, to
venture into the scorching garden was highly unusual.

Given her extreme shut-in tendencies, this situation was quite bizarre.

I woke up Caron and Nina, who were sitting on my lap, and hurriedly approached
Skia. Caron and the others followed suit.

Normally, I’d wait for her to come to me, but being formal even in private is a
hassle. Besides, waiting for someone as socially awkward as her would probably
waste a ton of time.

“S-sorry\!” Skia stammered, bowing hastily as I approached. “T-to think you’d go
out of your way to come to me...\!”

I told her there was no need to apologize and then asked, “What’s going on?”

“W-well, um, it’s a long story, but...” she began hesitantly.

Skia started explaining the situation. To sum it up concisely, she received a
troubling letter from her family asking for advice. Apparently, she first
brought it up with Shion, who passed it to Minerva, and it eventually reached
me.

If Shion and Minerva thought I should handle it, their judgment is probably
sound. But why me, of all people, as the go-to for advice?

Resigning myself to the likelihood of this being a headache, I listened to
Skia’s concerns.

“F-from my family, I got a letter saying, ‘D-don’t come home for a while,’” she
said nervously.

“Any details?” I asked.

“N-nothing...” she replied.

“...” I said nothing, processing her words.

I could see why this got dumped on me. With practically no information, giving
advice was nearly impossible.

During my regular meetings with the servants, I sometimes play the role of an
advisor, so I guess that’s why I was chosen this time. Still, what a mess.

With so little to go on, careless advice could make things worse. I covered my
mouth with my hand, trying to organize my thoughts.

“Do you know when the letter was sent?” I asked.

“A-about a week ago,” Skia replied.

“When was the letter before that? And did it mention anything about not coming
home?” I pressed.

“The one before that... I-I think it was early April. N-nothing special was
written in it,” she said.

“When was the last time you went back to the Cheyni territory?” I asked.

“Th-the New Year holidays,” she answered.

“Did anything feel off when you last visited?” I continued.

“N-no, nothing in particular comes to mind,” she said.

“Hmm,” I mused.

So, something must have changed in the Cheyni viscount branch family between
early April and late July—roughly four months. There’s definitely a problem in
the Cheyni territory, but for them to stop their own daughter from returning
home, it’s hard to imagine what could be so serious. The letter’s use of “for a
while” suggests the issue might drag on for some time.

Honestly, if it were a major crisis, I’d expect to have heard about it by now.
With the extra bandwidth from not monitoring the hero or Saintess recently, my
information network has widened considerably.

That means my initial assumption might be off. It’s possible this isn’t about a
massive crisis preventing Skia from returning but rather something specifically
targeting her with the instruction not to come home.

“Oh, I get it,” I said, realization dawning.

Skia leaned forward slightly at my reaction. “D-did you figure something out?”
she asked eagerly.

Her forward-leaning posture was rare for her. She must be really worried about
her family.

I’d heard before that the Cheyni family is exceptionally close-knit, even more
so than the tight-knit Loramber ducal family. With only one wife yet many
children, they’re a rare household these days. Personally, I find it quite
likable.

I urged Skia to calm down before continuing. “This is just a guess, so bear that
in mind,” I said. “It’s likely that because you awakened as a light magic user,
some unsavory types are now sniffing around the Cheyni viscount branch.”

“That makes sense,” Caron agreed, listening nearby.

“Light magic users are rare,” Nina added.

Caron and Nina nodded in agreement with my theory.

Light magic is not only rare in terms of aptitude but even rarer in those who
can actually wield it. In the Holy Kingdom, only four people, including Caron
and Skia, can use it. In the Forest Kingdom, there are none. In neighboring
countries, only one to three are known. Because of this, light magic users are
often the subject of fierce competition. In the current Holy Kingdom, however,
the presence of the Saintess chosen by God, a member of the Holy Royal Family,
and the backing of Foranada have kept such schemes at bay.

Considering this, let’s review Skia’s status. As the fourth daughter of a
viscount’s branch family, her rank is significantly lower than others. Given the
advantage of acquiring a light magic user, it wouldn’t be surprising if someone
tried to kidnap her.

The only reason she’s been safe so far is Foranada’s protection. Traveling
openly with us has kept her out of harm’s way.

“Honestly, I thought being associated with Foranada would be enough to deter any
domestic nobles,” I said. “If my guess is right, this is a surprising
development.”

“A-ah, I-I’m causing trouble for everyone again\!” Skia cried, trembling.

“Whoa, calm down,” I said.

“Skia, easy now,” Caron soothed.

“It’s not your fault, Skia-san,” Nina reassured. “The ones who started this are
to blame.”

I tried to keep things light, but Skia clearly couldn’t take it that way. She
was shaking all over and crouched down on the spot.

Come to think of it, didn’t she once say she wanted to be independent because
she felt she was a burden on her family due to her inability to use magic?
Learning she might be causing more trouble would naturally weigh heavily on her.

Still, as Caron said, this isn’t her fault.

I cast \[Calm\] to ease her nerves and spoke as gently as I could. “I know it’s
hard not to feel responsible, but don’t worry. We’ll handle this.”

“B-but...” Skia started, lifting her face but faltering awkwardly.

And no wonder. Dragging her boss into her family’s problems isn’t something
she’d willingly do, especially when that boss is a noble of higher rank.

But this time, she doesn’t need to feel guilty.

“This all started because I cured your illness,” I said. “I promised to provide
proper aftercare, didn’t I? So don’t worry about it.”

Exactly. If the Cheyni viscount branch is dealing with trouble related to a
light magic user, I’m the one who caused it. If I hadn’t gotten involved, this
issue wouldn’t have arisen.

This is just me cleaning up my own mess. Skia doesn’t need to feel responsible.

“B-but...” Skia protested, her expression still troubled.

Well, if she could be convinced by my words that easily, she wouldn’t be so
distressed in the first place. But this is a done deal. I need her to accept it.

Ignoring her flustered state, I started planning the trip to the Cheyni
territory.

Then, something occurred to me. “Skia, I have a question,” I said.

“W-w-what is it?” she asked, visibly on edge.

Given the current conversation, her wariness was understandable, but my question
was completely unrelated, and I felt a bit bad for startling her.

“Your hometown is at the foot of a mountain, in a colder region of the kingdom,
right?” I asked.

“Y-yes,” she replied. “I-it’s prone to cold damage, so it’s not great for
farming.”

“I seem to recall a town with a lake there,” I added.

“Y-yes, there is,” she confirmed. “S-some wealthy people visit it for tourism.”

Hearing her response, a grin spread across my face.

Caron and Nina’s eyes widened as they noticed.

“Brother, you don’t mean...” Caron said, catching on.

“A blind spot,” Nina remarked.

They figured it out.

A cool climate and a lake that’s a tourist attraction—perfect for a discreet
getaway.

Sure, Skia’s issue means it won’t be a pure vacation, but it’ll still offer a
moment of respite.

No worries. I’ll make sure to resolve the problem before we have fun. Nothing to
fret about.

And so, the plan for the rest of the summer break was set.

When you hear “travel,” what comes to mind first? Eating local delicacies,
trying traditional crafts, visiting tourist attractions—everyone has their own
way of enjoying it based on their hobbies.

“Traveling with \[Phase Connection Gate\] is convenient, but it’s a bit bland,
don’t you think?” said Minerva, a stunning beauty with black hair tied in twin
tails, as we teleported near the Cheyni territory.

The petite and adorable Minerva is my fiancée and a duke’s daughter. She’s
gifted in nearly everything and never slacks on effort—a truly perfect genius.

Well, her private tsundere remarks are a flaw, but I think that just adds to her
charm.

I could relate to her sentiment. For some people, the journey to a destination
is part of the fun of travel.

Caron, however, shot back sharply. “If you feel that way, why don’t you travel
by carriage alone, Minerva-san? Meanwhile, we’ll have our fill of fun.”

“Why does it have to be like that?” Minerva retorted. “I was just feeling a bit
sentimental\!”

“You’re being awfully bold for someone relying on Brother’s power,” Caron said.

“Mmph, that may be true, but... you don’t have to rub it in\!” Minerva huffed.

“Oh, quite an honest reaction,” Caron teased.

“Hmph. I grow, you know. Unlike a certain someone,” Minerva shot back.

“Oh? And who might that someone be?” Caron asked.

“What, you don’t know?” Minerva replied coyly.

...What started as casual banter had somehow escalated into an argument.

I decided to let it slide—it’s just their usual bickering. Caron and Minerva
never seem to stop clashing. It’s the “the more you fight, the closer you are”
kind of thing, so it’s not a big deal.

“They never learn, do they?” said Orca, a fox beastman, with a wry smile as he
approached me.

“Those two are quite the pair,” added Marina, a human, with a chuckle.

Orca is my adoptive younger brother. His reddish-brown short hair sways softly,
and his yellow-green eyes are bright and charming, but he’s definitely a guy—not
a tomboyish girl, despite appearances.

Marina is the childhood friend of the current hero. Her deep aqua hair, swept
over her right shoulder, is glossy, and her face, adorned with pale blue-purple
eyes, is strikingly beautiful—hardly what you’d expect from a commoner.

She has a soft, gentle air about her, but she’s actually the kingdom’s only
spirit magic user, with power rivaling Caron’s. Underestimate her, and you’ll
regret it.

“It just shows how comfortable they are with each other,” I said. “Still, this
isn’t Foranada. We should probably stop them soon.”

“Yeah,” Orca agreed. “No need for you to step in, Zex-nii. I’ll handle it.”

“I’ll help too\~,” Marina chimed in.

With that, Orca and Marina approached the bickering duo.

Those two are the top communicators in our group. Even Caron and Minerva, who
don’t back down easily, should be manageable for them.

Time to refocus and go over the plan.

“Shion, Skia, come here,” I called.

“Yes,” they replied in unison.

Skia, our guide for this trip, and Shion, my right-hand woman, responded
promptly.

Shion, a servant, always takes on the role of my secretary, even during
vacations. Though dressed casually, her straight posture and composed expression
give off a “capable woman” vibe.

However—

“Hweh?” Shion yelped, suddenly tripping as she approached. There was nothing on
the ground. She just... tripped.

As you can tell, Shion is quite the klutz. She’s highly capable in many ways,
but she has her clumsy moments.

With a mental facepalm, I rushed to her side.

Her pale blue-purple hair and fair skin were smudged with dirt, and her emerald
eyes were slightly teary. The “capable woman” image crumbled in an instant.

I brushed off the dirt and helped her stand. “It’s fine,” I said magnanimously.
“Just watch your step.”

“S-sorry...” Shion apologized, shrinking in embarrassment.

I thought to myself, _It’s just Shion being Shion,_ but I wasn’t tactless enough
to say it out loud.

Skia, approaching from another direction, hurried over. “A-a-are you okay?” she
asked, clearly worried after Shion’s face-plant.

“I’m fine,” Shion replied coolly, switching to a composed, adult demeanor. Her
hair was slightly disheveled, though, so it didn’t quite land.

Then—

“Shion, your hair’s a mess,” Nina said, appearing behind her and brushing it
with a comb she’d thoughtfully brought.

“S-sorry,” Shion mumbled.

“Don’t worry about it,” Nina replied.

Shion’s face flushed with embarrassment, while Nina remained completely
expressionless.

Once Nina finished fixing her hair, she turned to me. “I’ll keep an eye on our
surroundings,” she said. “We’ve got covert escorts, but better safe than sorry,
right?”

“Go for it,” I said.

Since this was a discreet trip, we only had a few elite shadow guards besides
the eight of us. Their skill allows for a small team, but extra vigilance is
always welcome.

With my approval, Nina shifted into alert mode, looking sharp and cool.

Now, after that little hiccup, let’s wrap this up quickly. We can’t stand around
forever in a grassy field near the Cheyni territory’s capital.

“Skia, do you know where we are?” I asked.

“Y-yes,” she replied, nodding vigorously. “N-no problem. I-I can guide us.”

I nodded, satisfied. “Good. Shion, what’s the schedule?”

“Today, we check into the reserved inn and pay a courtesy visit to the head of
the Cheyni viscount branch,” Shion answered. “That’s all. Since we don’t know
the exact nature of their issue, that’s the plan for now.”

As discussed, today’s mostly free. My theory about Skia being the cause is just
a hunch, so this schedule makes sense.

“And the advance notice?” I asked.

“Sent three days ago, including that this is a discreet visit,” Shion confirmed.

“Sounds good,” I said. “If they don’t need to roll out the red carpet, their
preparations should be minimal.”

“...Th-though their stomachs are probably in knots,” Skia muttered.

I gave a wry smile. “They’ll just have to deal with it.”

I get that welcoming a big shot is nerve-wracking, but that’s not something time
will fix. Resignation is key.

“Alright, let’s move out,” I said, calling to the others before pulling a plain
carriage set from \[Cover Texture\] for our discreet trip. It’s high-performance
on the inside, though.

After about ten minutes of travel by carriage, we finally reached the capital of
the Cheyni viscount branch.

A majestic mountain loomed in the background, and a lake stretched along the
town’s edge—a beautiful city in harmony with nature.

“W-welcome to my hometown,” Skia said softly, her modest greeting reaching us as
we admired the splendid scenery.

The inn we secured was the most luxurious place in the Cheyni territory. It was
what you’d call a suite, with a living room at its center and individual rooms
for each of us branching off from it.

Once we’d unpacked and gathered in the living room, I spoke up. “After this,
Skia and I will go pay our respects to Viscount Cheyni. According to the advance
messenger who just returned, they’ve already made preparations.”

The reason things were proceeding so smoothly was likely because we’d informed
them of our arrival time three days ago. Thanks to the 【Phase Connection
Gate】, there was no chance of our schedule shifting, which was, as expected,
quite convenient.

I turned to the others. “Those of us besides Skia and me have free time, but
what are your plans? I’d like to get a rough idea of what everyone’s up to.”

At that, everyone started to speak all at once. Sensing trouble, I quickly
added, “—Going with me is not an option.”

Instantly, Caron and the others, who had started to open their mouths, shut
them. It was obvious what they’d been about to say.

I shot them a half-lidded glare, and they turned their eyes away with a huff.
Skia, the only one accompanying me, gave a dry smile.

Letting out a sigh, I reiterated, “Only Skia and I are going to Viscount
Cheyni’s. Showing up with a big group would inconvenience them, and more
importantly, it would defeat the purpose of keeping this low-key. Even without
our shadow escorts, we’re eight people. That’s bound to stand out if we all
parade through the lord’s castle.”

Even with our cover as wealthy merchants and the ability to disguise ourselves
with 【Concealment】, we’d still draw attention. One wrong move, and our
identities could be exposed in no time.

Plus, I was a little worried about the stress this might put on Skia’s father.
Hosting the head of the Foranada family was burden enough, but if a duke’s
daughter, the ‘Saintess of Sunlight,’ or the ‘Dragon-Busting Swordmaster’ showed
up too, it might give him a stomach ulcer.

Since my reasoning was sound, Caron and the others reluctantly conceded, albeit
with some grumbling. After a moment of exchanging silent glances, Minerva spoke
up on behalf of the group. “In that case, we’ll go explore the town. It’s a
tourist destination, after all, so there should be plenty to see.”

“Try to—” I began.

“Stick together, I know,” Minerva interrupted smoothly, flashing a smug grin.
“Even if it’s a vacation, we won’t make things difficult for our escorts. Don’t
worry.”

She’d stolen my line with ease, practically saying, _I know exactly what you’re
thinking._ After eight years since our engagement and seven years of living
together, this kind of unspoken understanding was second nature.

One person, however, didn’t seem pleased by this exchange. No need to say who.

Since she was sitting right next to me, I quietly patted her head. It was a
clumsy gesture, but it was enough to ease the jealousy radiating from her.

Personally, I found it endearing—and a little concerning—how easy it was to
placate her. With a wry smile to myself, I nodded at Minerva. “Alright. Be
careful.”

“You too,” she replied.

After some light chatter, we dispersed.

Now it was time to face Skia’s family. I wondered what kind of trouble awaited
us.

Thanks to the appointment we’d made, we were able to enter Cheyni Castle
quickly. Skia’s presence as their daughter likely helped smooth things over.

The gatekeeper seemed surprised that it was just the two of us, but when I
whispered, “We’re here incognito,” he nodded in understanding. That should keep
unnecessary rumors from spreading.

Guided by a butler, we made our way through the castle. There wasn’t anything
particularly noteworthy about the viscount’s branch family castle. It was
appropriately sized and dignified for their rank—neither exceptional nor
lacking.

As such, it was easy to guess the layout. Important areas were likely concealed,
but I could tell where we were being led. It wasn’t a reception room but
something more private, like a personal chamber.

In this world’s customs, being received in a private area was reserved for close
relations, regardless of status—be it a liege lord or someone connected through
political marriage.

In other words, the Cheyni branch family was operating under a misunderstanding.
A big one.

Skia, naturally familiar with her family home’s layout, realized the situation
and started to panic, her expression flustered.

As a member of the household, she probably wanted to stop this, but given her
social awkwardness, that was asking too much. That left it to me to correct the
misunderstanding directly with the head of the house. Fortunately, since this
was an incognito visit, the situation wouldn’t escalate too much. If questioned,
we could play dumb.

Soon, we arrived at a room. After the customary exchange at the door, we
entered.

The room, likely the head’s private study, was lined with bookshelves on every
wall, including the side with the entrance. It was clear the man was an avid
book lover—a fitting space for Skia’s father.

In the center stood a sofa and table, where the head of the Cheyni family and
his wife were waiting, standing side by side.

They performed the noble’s bow and spoke. “Welcome to our domain, Earl
Foranada,” the man said. “I am Arvas Biono Ga Tan Cheyni, the lord entrusted
with this land.”

“And I am his wife, Flonoa,” the woman added.

As Skia’s parents, they were unsurprisingly a striking pair, both handsome and
beautiful. They gave off a refined, indoor-type impression, with delicate
features.

Their tension was palpable. They’d likely heard rumors about the Foranada family
and seemed to harbor a mix of awe and apprehension.

“I thank you for your warm welcome, Viscount Cheyni,” I said, returning the bow.
“I am Zex Levito San Foranada. It’s a pleasure to meet you.”

The need to act appropriately haughty due to our difference in rank was a
hassle, but it couldn’t be helped.

Still, this was an informal visit. “This is an unofficial meeting,” I continued.
“I know it’s difficult given our positions, but I’d appreciate it if you could
relax a bit. Can I count on you?”

“If Earl Foranada requests it, we have no objections,” Arvas replied.

“Nor do I,” Flonoa added.

It was a stark reminder of the rigid hierarchy of status. It made me appreciate
the academy’s efforts to maintain a merit-based environment and the relative
peace of the Foranada household.

After they welcomed Skia back with an “Welcome home,” we took our seats. The
greeting was lighter than expected, but I supposed that’s how families are.

Sipping the prepared tea without hesitation, I spoke. “First, let me express my
gratitude. Thank you for accommodating our trip. We ran into an issue where the
place we visit every year wasn’t available this time. You’ve really saved us.”

Arvas shook his head at my sincere thanks. “It’s not something worthy of such
gratitude, Earl Foranada. We’re practically family, so there’s no need for such
formalities.”

“Exactly as my husband says,” Flonoa chimed in. “That Skia has secured her
future path is a blessing. If anything, we’re the ones who should be offering
our thanks.”

She seemed on the verge of tears, overwhelmed with emotion.

As expected, they were misunderstanding the situation. Their reactions were so
transparent I didn’t even need to read their emotions.

I couldn’t let this misunderstanding drag on. Just as I was about to correct
