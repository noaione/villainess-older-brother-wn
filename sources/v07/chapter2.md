# Chapter 2: Field Trip

"Yo, Zex\!"

"...It's you."

While I was going through some paperwork in my office, the person who entered
with a casual tone was someone who happened to be my mentor.

The man with white hair and black eyes, strikingly handsome, was
Akatsuki-sensei. He was once an apostle of the gods and, in the original game,
stood as the final boss before the protagonists. And without a doubt, he is also
the second strongest person in the world.

Who’s the first, you ask? Naturally, it's me. Not to brag, but I’ve been on an
undefeated streak in recent mock battles.

While moving my pen, I glanced at Akatsuki-sensei.

"You always seem to show up when I'm alone, huh?"

Due to my position, I don't often have the chance to act alone. Maybe when I'm
researching mental magic or during sleep. Most of the time, Shion is by my side.

Right now, Shion just happened to be away, but it was definitely a time he was
aiming for.

Akatsuki-sensei plopped down onto the sofa, dramatically shrugging his
shoulders.

"Of course I show up when you're alone. I used to be an apostle of the gods, you
know? I’m not a settled figure in this world, and it’s better for me not to get
too involved with the public. So, I prefer to keep my face known to as few
people as possible, and I don’t share much of my knowledge either."

"But we’re closely involved, aren’t we?"

"You’re fine. You’re my disciple, after all."

"...I see. So you're just casual with everything, huh?"

"You get it, huh?"

Akatsuki-sensei flashed a mischievous grin.

Although he's an extraordinary existence, Akatsuki-sensei is actually very rough
and fickle. At heart, he's good-natured, so he would never steer things toward
harming the world. But as his disciple, I’ve often found myself being tossed
around by him. Even the situation where the protagonists face him as the final
boss was just because of his impulsive nature.

I sighed and asked him,

"So, what brings you here this time? It's been about six months since I last saw
you."

The last mock battle we had was at the end of September last year. I hadn’t
really been paying attention, since I was busy with other things, but this felt
like the longest break between our battles.

Given who he was, I wasn’t worried about his health or life, but I couldn’t
shake the thought that he might be plotting something troublesome.

Akatsuki-sensei, however, didn’t seem concerned and casually replied while
relaxing on the sofa.

"Obviously, it’s training. Training."

"Training?"

"I’ve been developing new tactics and magic, since I’ve been losing lately.
Gotta show my disciple the dignity of his master."

"That’s a futile effort."

From the start, Akatsuki-sensei never had any dignity to show.

However, his words weren’t something to ignore. The fact that the former apostle
of the gods, who was already incredibly strong, had gotten even stronger was
nothing short of a nightmare. If the Saintess fought him like in the original,
she probably wouldn’t win even with some mercy.

While I was still pondering the implications of this, Akatsuki-sensei cheerfully
added,

"So, let’s have a mock battle, huh?"

"Impossible. I’m working."

I shook my head. The task I was working on needed to be finished by today. I
didn’t have time to humor Akatsuki’s antics.

But before I could even respond, he had teleported behind me and was leaning
against me.

"Come on, just a little bit. I’ve been training for this, so let’s have a mock
battle\!"

"Ugh, so annoying\! I didn’t say I wouldn’t do it, just wait a bit\!"

I used one hand to throw Akatsuki-sensei off of me, then quickly used a fake
Orichalcum wire to entangle him. I infused it with my magic, so he wouldn’t be
able to escape easily. It should buy me some time.

"Whoa, what is this? I can’t get out\!?"

It was noisy and chaotic, but at least I could get my work done without further
interruption. I wasn’t sure how long the wire would hold, so I needed to wrap
things up quickly.

I fully activated my \[Body Enhancement\] and began writing even faster.

Thirty minutes later.

"Finally got out..."

Akatsuki-sensei, panting heavily, tossed the wire aside. It seemed he'd finally
managed to escape.

It took longer than I expected. If I'd known, I would've taken my time with the
work.

About ten minutes ago, I had been tidying up the documents. Now, while sipping
the tea I had prepared for myself, I leisurely let my thoughts wander.

Then, Akatsuki-sensei slammed his hand onto the table I was sitting at.

"Why the hell are you just sipping tea? Time's ticking\! Let's get the mock
battle started already\!"

"How much do you want to fight, anyway?"

Seeing him so eager, it seemed he was pretty confident in the results of his
training. I wasn’t sure if I wanted to know or not.

The situation was a bit draining, but it was hard to refuse. Even though I was
stronger, the gap was minimal. There was no way to avoid being dragged into
this.

"Fine, fine. I get it."

I put the half-drunk tea away with \[Phase Concealment\], stood up, and then
casually waved my hand to cancel \[Alternate World\]. As soon as I did, the
world I had shifted away from returned.

And at the same time—

"Huh?"

Akatsuki-sensei stared wide-eyed, looking more shocked than I had ever seen him.
His mouth hung open, and a stunned voice escaped him.

Less than two seconds later, he recovered and, in a flustered tone, asked,

"W-when did you cast \[Alternate World\]?"

"It was right after you entered the room. Judging by your reaction, it seems you
didn’t notice. Yeah, I’d say the experiment was a success."

"...Wait, did you actually improve \[Alternate World\] so others can’t detect
it?"

His eyes widened in disbelief.

I smirked, tilting my mouth upward.

"It’s not just \[Alternate World\]. I applied the \[Magic Concealment\] I
developed before and worked on a technique to hide the signs of any magic in
general. If I can fool someone like you with a large-scale magic like
\[Alternate World\], I think I can consider it complete."

I had even gotten help from Noma, a spirit with high magic detection abilities.
If I can deceive someone like a former apostle of the gods, there’s nothing to
worry about. It also seems like it could be used in magical tools, so this would
broaden the possibilities for covert operations.

Upon hearing this, Akatsuki-sensei frowned and lowered his head slightly.

No surprise there. He was already shocked when I first developed \[Magic
Concealment\], so advancing it even further must have been beyond his
expectations. I was hoping to surprise him.

However, his gesture of holding his head immediately stopped. It seemed he
sensed something, and he suddenly looked up.

"Wait a minute. This feeling... no way, you can’t be serious\!?"

Akatsuki-sensei locked eyes with me, then grabbed both my shoulders with
tremendous force. Way too close.

"W-what’s going on?"

"'What's going on?' That's what\! That \[Alternate World\] you used was
completely isolated from the flow of time, wasn’t it? Only five minutes have
passed since I entered this room\!"

"Oh, so you noticed after all?"

I figured he might not realize since there’s no clock in this room, but as
expected of a former apostle of the gods—he could sense the discrepancy without
even looking at a clock.

As Akatsuki-sensei said, only five minutes had passed since he came in. Despite
the fact it took him thirty minutes to escape, time hadn’t moved at all while
\[Alternate World\] was active. I’d successfully reproduced time-stopping magic.

The trick? It was exactly what Akatsuki-sensei guessed—I generated the
pseudo-space of \[Alternate World\] outside the bounds of the world itself. By
creating it outside, it naturally freed itself from the world’s rules and
avoided the passage of time.

The phrase “easier said than done” fits perfectly here. Even though I’d been
studying time-stopping magic for ages, I couldn’t get anywhere with it until
recently.

What triggered my breakthrough was assisting in researching the ritual to summon
Zigraldo. The summoning was essentially magic that connected to the “outside” of
the world, so by analyzing the spell, I finally understood how to define this
“outside.” With that key in place, it was just a matter of dedicated research. I
worked tirelessly—well, maybe not that far—but with intense focus, I completed
the spell.

Time-stopping is incredible. For now, I can only use it in controlled spaces
like work or sleeping quarters due to the nature of \[Alternate World\], but
that alone is more than enough. I can take on as much paperwork as I want
without losing time, and I can completely skip sleep. Having a full twenty-four
hours every day is honestly amazing.

"Right now, I’m working on creating a time-stop spell that doesn’t rely on
\[Alternate World\]. I’m also testing whether I can stop specific objects
selectively. So why not use this mock battle to test it out?"

Running small-scale tests is valuable, but a real fight against a high-level
opponent like him is priceless. He seemed eager, but honestly, so was I—this
mock battle came at a perfect time.

But something seemed off with Akatsuki-sensei. His eyes darted around, and beads
of sweat ran down his forehead.

"A-actually… maybe we should save this mock battle for another day—"

"Haha, you’re not getting away."

Sensing his intent to escape via teleportation, I swiftly grabbed hold of him
and smiled as I opened the entrance to \[Alternate World\].

He’s always the one dragging me around, but I figured it wouldn’t hurt to turn
the tables once in a while.

---

Inside the \[Alternate World\] I had created, a large hole had opened up in the
middle of the grassland where we usually held our mock battles. At the center of
it, Akatsuki lay, his expression twisted in pain.

At first glance, he didn’t seem to have any visible wounds, but he was lying
flat on his back, unmoving. Well, aside from the occasional clenching of his
teeth.

Anyone with a good sense would probably realize the cause of his predicament: it
was me. I had used the non-elemental magic \[Compression\] to slam him into the
ground.

By the way, a simple \[Compression\] wouldn't be enough to keep someone like
Akatsuki-sensei to be pinned, so I had layered it about fifty times and boosted
its power with a magical ritual to overcharge the magic. Even with just a normal
\[Compression\], I could easily apply over a hundred tons of force at maximum
output, but this time I had pushed it even further.

As you might have guessed, this is a mock battle. After a fierce exchange, I had
managed to capture Akatsuki.

Normally, Akatsuki-sensei wouldn’t be caught by a simple spell like
\[Compression\], but thanks to \[Time Stop\], everything had gone according to
plan.

At first, I wasn’t able to activate \[Time Stop\] well, but as the battle went
on, I started to get a feel for it. The spell only works within my controlled
areas—like my barriers or \[Alternate World\]—but it allows me to freeze parts
of the surroundings or even just halt consciousness. Right now, I had frozen
Akatsuki-sensei's magic and stopped his mana from circulating.

Fighting a strong opponent like him really gave me a lot of experience. I don’t
think I would have grown this much in such a short time just with my own
research. Having a well-matched rival is something truly invaluable.

Now, my mana reserves were getting low. It was time to end this.

I narrowed the area of \[Compression\] and approached Akatsuki-sensei, making
sure to be cautious in case of a last-minute reversal.

When I reached his side, I drew a dagger from \[Phase Concealment\] and pressed
it to his neck. At our level, even in this situation, a reversal was possible,
but in a mock battle, this was the losing condition. There was no point in
dragging it out any further.

With my victory confirmed, I released \[Compression\]. Immediately,
Akatsuki-sensei gasped and began breathing heavily.

Ah, I guess the pressure was so intense that he couldn’t breathe properly. This
spell has that kind of offensive potential, huh. I should remember that.

“Damn it… I lost. I told you, time manipulation is cheating\!”

While still muttering something odd, Akatsuki-sensei revived and jumped up,
stomping his feet in frustration.

I couldn’t help but feel exasperated.

“If you’re going to call me cheating, then you’re pretty much cheating yourself.
What’s up with that divine enhancement of yours? There’s no way I could ever
replicate that.”

This mock battle had originally been instigated by Akatsuki, who wanted to show
off his new magic. Naturally, he had used it on me during the fight.

Divine enhancement. Akatsuki had utilized the powers of his identity as “an
apostle of the gods.” By literally enhancing his “divinity,” he had elevated
himself into a superior being and launched attacks of an entirely different
scale.

The difference in scale was so vast that both his martial arts and magic were
overwhelming... and if I had just clashed head-on, I would have lost for sure. I
had to dodge constantly. If I hadn’t developed \[Time Stop\], I definitely would
have lost this mock battle.

“You’re using those magic eyes, a human-exclusive ability. It’s even.”

“The scale’s completely different between a human and an apostle of the gods,
though.”

Magic eyes are a technique humans use to fight against non-humans, but when even
those non-humans advance a step further, it becomes meaningless.

...Wait a minute. Did Akatsuki-sensei ever use a technique like divine
enhancement in the original story?

“……………”

It's not like the Saintess would ever actually challenge Akatsuki-sensei in the
real world, so there's no need to worry. He’d probably hold back anyway. Yeah,
it'll be fine. Definitely.

“What’s wrong?”

“Ah, nothing.”

I must’ve zoned out a bit, since Akatsuki-sensei was giving me a puzzled look. I
shook my head lightly in response.

Then, I decided to change the subject.

“By the way, there’s something I wanted to ask you, Akatsuki-sensei.”

“Something you want to ask?”

“Do you know anything about ‘Great Magic Administrator’?”

The Great Magic Administrator. It was a title the demon Septem used to refer to
his master, the "Demon Lord of the West."

Septem had described the Great Magic Administrator as "_one who has mastered
magic, reaching the truth of it, and reigning over all magic,"_ but it was
unclear how much of that was actually true. Septem himself didn’t seem to know
all the details.

That’s why I wanted to ask Akatsuki-sensei, who was likely to know something
about this kind of topic. Any information regarding the Demon Lord of the West
was valuable, as it tied into Caron’s fated death. I couldn’t afford to overlook
even the smallest clue.

Akatsuki-sensei’s expression turned serious in response to my question.

“This is about the ‘Demon Lord of the West,’ isn’t it?”

“You knew?”

“Yeah. But, unfortunately, I don’t know much about it myself. I arrived in this
world after the Demon Lord of the West was sealed. The only reason I’ve even
heard of the Great Magic Administrator is because I’ve roamed the world long
enough. From what I gathered, All the Great Magic Administrators vanished
without a trace one day, long ago.”

“I see…”

I had hoped Akatsuki-sensei would know something, but it looks like I was out of
luck this time.

Still, I couldn’t give up. If I kept gathering information from various sources,
I was bound to find some clue eventually. Luckily, the people of Foranada were
highly skilled.

Besides, at least I’d learned that the Magic Administrator existed in an era
before Akatsuki-sensei’s arrival—around the time of the Demon Lord of the West’s
sealing. I’d just have to focus on searching for records from that period.

“Oh yeah, speaking of that…”

Suddenly, Akatsuki-sensei seemed to remember something.

“There might be something in a dungeon.”

“A dungeon?”

I tilted my head.

Could there really be information on the Magic Administrators in a place that
was nothing but a den for monsters? Or maybe there’s something hidden in the
deepest part?

While I was lost in thought, he continued.

“Dungeons are actually anchors that help keep the balance of power in this
world. They naturally draw in magic, and as a side effect, monsters gather there
too.”

“Anchors?”

“Yeah. Without them, this world would burst apart in no time. Like an
overinflated balloon.”

Akatsuki-sensei spoke casually, but the content of his words was far from
reassuring.

"So, the collapse of the dungeon equals the collapse of the world, right? With
the 'Western Demon King' cursing the entire world, isn’t the survival of this
world hanging by a thread?"

As I fretted anxiously, Akatsuki-sensei shrugged and laughed.

"You don’t have to be that scared. The dungeon was created by the gods. It won’t
break unless you or I go all out."

"Well, that's good to hear."

"Let’s get back on topic. Like I said, the dungeon was created by the gods. It
can’t be destroyed by half-hearted attacks, and inside it, barrier-type magic
can’t be used. That's because it's a place that's practically under divine
dominion."

"So, if barrier magic doesn't work, that means things like \[Alternate World\],
\[Phase Connection\], and \[Phase Concealment\] won’t work either, right?"

"We won’t know until we try, but I think \[Phase Concealment\] might just work.
Everything else, though, won’t. When I tried to visit before, teleportation was
impossible."

"That's a pain..."

I let out a sigh without thinking, and Akatsuki-sensei gave a wry smile.

"Dungeons are a pain, yeah. Even we think so, so it’s no surprise humanity
hasn’t figured it out yet, right?"

"…I think I get what you're trying to say. If it's a place untouched by anyone,
under divine dominion, there might be a chance of learning something about those
'who have reached the truth of magic,' like those grandiose 'Magic
Administrator.' Is that what you mean?"

"Exactly\!"

Akatsuki-sensei flashed a grin and gave me a thumbs up.

Honestly, I felt it was a rather simplistic take. Even if it's the gods' domain,
that doesn't mean there will be any information about Magic Administrators. In
fact, considering the nature of keeping the world's energy balance stable, it
might be more efficient not to place anything unnecessary in the deepest part of
the dungeon.

However, there was no other lead to follow. And since we were already going on a
field trip to the dungeon, I might as well investigate the deepest part while I
was at it.

"Thanks. I’ll look into it while I’m there."

"I'm glad I could help."

After that, we exchanged our thoughts and some advice about each other's magic
before parting ways.

---

The day of the field trip finally arrived. Many students were gathered in front
of the academy’s main gate—though not in any significant way. With over a
hundred thousand students in each grade, gathering them all in one place was
impossible. Each large class had different schedules for the field exercises,
and Classes B and below were set to depart one by one as we in Class A returned.

As I mentioned before, our destination was a dungeon in Count Roeme’s territory.
Naturally, we’d be leaving the capital, so an escort was necessary. Since we
were protecting noble children, the guards had to be carefully chosen.

Normally, the knights would take on this duty, but this year, things were a bit
different. Due to the recent demon incident, the capital’s security had been
heightened. With no soldiers to spare, the knights were unavailable.

There was apparently some debate over this, but in the end, it didn’t take long
for the escort to be decided. Volunteers had stepped forward.

So, who were these volunteers…?

“Starting today, for the next ten days, we’ll be in your care, boss\!”

““““““““““Thank you, we’ll do our best\!\!””””””””””

A group of knights—no, combat-obsessed warmongers—bowed in unison before me.
Their eyes were all gleaming with intensity, as if they wouldn’t miss a single
move I made.

The group, consisting of eleven men and women, was the Feiberun clan—a
prestigious name known for producing legendary sword saints.

After I defeated the previous sword saint in a duel, they had sworn allegiance
to the kingdom of Foranada. Since then, they had undergone moderate training
under my guidance... and this was the result. They called me their boss, and
their loyalty—no, their devotion—had reached a level of madness. For them,
strength seemed to equal faith. Usually, they were entrusted with managing the
capital or the Feiberun territory, so it didn’t bother me much, but seeing them
up close made me feel overwhelmed. Would it kill them to be a bit more mellow?
Probably not.

Besides the eleven in front of me, more of Feiberun’s knights would be
participating in the escort. The group here was specifically for guarding Class
A1. Apparently, they had won the right to be near me through a battle
royale-style contest. I hoped they wouldn’t end up battered before the job even
began...

Well, their skills were undeniable. As battle-obsessed as they were, they could
likely defeat lower-ranked knights of Foranada. Monsters and bandits along the
way would be no match for them.

“I trust you all. With your skills, I’m confident I can leave it to you without
worry.”

“Such kind words\! I, Aurelia, will stake my life to protect you and meet your
expectations, Boss\!”

Aurelia, the leader of our A1 escort team, responded with an incredibly heavy
declaration.

With short brown hair and blue eyes, she was strikingly beautiful. At twenty
years old, it seemed like marriage proposals would come flooding in.

But in reality, she was one of Feiberun’s most fanatic members. She’d never find
a suitor, and today, as usual, she was busy with her training. Thanks to that,
she had been appointed as the head of the escort team, though whether it was a
blessing or a curse was up for debate. For Feiberun, it was probably a good
thing, but for me, it was a headache.

Aurelia and I had known each other since she was a student at the academy. She
was one of the first members of Feiberun’s elite who I had trained, and back
then, she wasn’t the fanatic she was now. In fact, she had been somewhat
arrogant, with a strong elitist mindset. Considering her top-tier skills for her
age, it was no wonder she had developed an inflated ego.

So, what caused her to change? Well, it was because I casually defeated her in
training. It was just part of the lessons, but it seemed to have been a shock to
her. Her inflated pride was shattered, and she ended up becoming a loyal
follower of mine, wagging her tail like a faithful dog.

Yeah, that’s on her.

She treated everyone else normally, and fortunately, she didn’t fawn over me
either. If she had started trying to curry favor, I would have set her straight.

Changing the subject...

Due to her fanaticism, she had fought desperately to win the right to be a part
of the escort. It was obvious from the way her whole body screamed for praise.

Well, it couldn’t be helped.

“I heard you won in the Feiberun internal battle royale, Aurelia. Well done.”

“\!\!\!\! I’m so honored\!”

Aurelia immediately dropped to one knee.

The sight of her, usually so dignified, trembling with emotion was quite the
contrast. As expected, the classmates around us were starting to make noise.

“Brother…”

“As expected of Zex, always so quick.”

“A real lady-killer.”

“You gave permission, but isn’t the rate at which you’re adding them a bit too
fast?”

“Haha…”

Meanwhile, Caron, Orca, Nina, Minerva, and even Marina seemed to be
misunderstanding something. I swear, there’s nothing going on between me and
Aurelia\! Though I know it’s pointless to try explaining.

As I sighed inwardly, one of the male knights standing beside Aurelia smacked
her on the head.

“Sister, stop fooling around and take command of the unit properly.”

The man, bearing a striking resemblance to Aurelia, was named Sadias. As you
might have guessed, he was her younger brother. He had just graduated from the
academy last year, but having received my training, he was strong. With him
around, I figured he would be able to keep his sister in check. I felt relieved.

“Thanks, Sadias. Well… do your best.”

Without needing to say it outright, he seemed to understand that I was asking
him to look after his sister. He smiled wryly and nodded.

It was certainly an interesting group, but as mentioned before, their skills
were undeniable. I was confident the journey would be safe.

---

The journey to the Roeme Count’s territory would take five days in total. The
trip was certainly safe. It was safe, but...

“You went too far, Aurelia.”

“I-I’m so sorry\!”

At a stopover village, I was scolding Aurelia.

It seemed that, since she could fight in front of me, she got a little too
carried away, and ended up repeatedly overkilling. Every beast that attacked us
was blown apart, and the path our A1 class took was drenched in a sea of blood.

Naturally, the students in the carriage witnessed the scene as well.

Not every student is accustomed to such violent scenes. While everyone had
undergone some basic training as part of the required curriculum, there were
still many who had never experienced real combat. How would they react when
confronted with such a bloody scene?

They would get sick. Several students started feeling nauseous, so we decided to
take a break at the village. Fortunately, we had plenty of time in our schedule,
so it didn’t cause any major problems, but it was definitely a trouble that
could have been avoided.

“I understand your enthusiasm, but I’ve told you this before, haven’t I? You can
show confidence, but don’t let your guard down. Using excessive force in a fight
is being reckless, don’t you think?”

“You’re absolutely right. I’m truly sorry\!”

It was clear from Aurelia’s deeply bowed head that she was genuinely remorseful.
There was no need to scold her further, as doing so would only have the opposite
effect. I decided to leave it at that for now.

“I’m glad you understand. Just don’t make the same mistake again.”

“I understand\!”

With that, the conversation was over, and I turned to leave. I headed toward the
inn where everyone was taking a temporary rest. Aurelia followed behind.

As I walked, I crossed paths with Minerva in the village square. She was
accompanied by a servant from Foranada.

“Finished with the lecture?”

“Ah, yeah. What about you?”

This was a small village, and there wasn’t much to see. It wasn’t really a place
where you’d find any exciting discoveries, even if you walked around.

“I was just getting some fresh air. Everyone in the inn looks so pale and
miserable. It’s a little depressing. I’d help take care of them, but with Caron,
Orca, and Shion around, they’ve got enough hands.”

“I see.”

Indeed, with Caron and the other two, they were well-equipped to handle things.
They were experienced with helping out in places like the church.

I extended my right hand to her.

“Well, in that case, would you like to go for a walk with me? We still have some
time.”

It was a perfect opportunity to make use of.

Minerva blinked in surprise for a moment before snorting in a playful manner.

“If you insist, I suppose I’ll accept.”

“I’d be happy to,” I replied, winking at her in response to her usual nonchalant
remark.

She looked away, but still took my hand, and we resumed walking. Minerva and I
walked side by side, with Aurelia and my maid trailing behind as guards.

As mentioned before, there wasn’t anything particularly noteworthy along the
way. Even so, this moment was incredibly enjoyable. Minerva wore a faint smile,
and her expression was filled with a pleasant warmth, so I believed she was
feeling the same way.

However, there was one troubling development. The news of the class tournament
and the demon incident had spread to this village as well.

What made it troublesome was that, because our aliases had become well-known, we
were treated like celebrities in the village. Our guards did their best to
protect us, but the attention was overwhelming.

What were our aliases, you ask?

...“Albida Monarch, the White Ruler” and “Colors Commander, the Conductor of
Hues.” Why are aliases often so embarrassingly grandiose? I’d love to confront
whoever named us.

After that, without any major trouble, we arrived at the Dungeon City “Dijot” in
the Roeme Count’s territory.

Although it’s called a Dungeon City, it doesn’t actually contain a dungeon
within the city itself. It simply refers to a city near a dungeon. As long as
it’s within a reasonable distance for adventurers to access and allows
monitoring to prevent magical beasts from overflowing, that’s all that matters.
No one would willingly embrace a den of magical beasts inside their city.

Given its proximity to a dungeon, Dijot has strong defense capabilities. The
entire city is surrounded by a ten-meter-tall city wall, and on top of it are
weapons capable of wreaking havoc on the ground. If a swarm of magical beasts
were to overflow from the dungeon, the city would be more than capable of
handling it.

That said, the stampede that will occur a few days from now will involve a
massive swarm that even these defenses cannot fully handle. No one could have
predicted an enemy force numbering in the tens of thousands. At most, one might
expect around a thousand or two thousand.

Well, we can leave that to the main group, the saint and hero, and the royalty.
As long as we provide support from the shadows, there shouldn’t be any
casualties.

But back to the story.

The exterior of Dijot is as described, but the interior was also quite unique.
The majority of the people in the city are adventurers, and as a result, many
shops and facilities cater to their needs. There are weapon shops, material
exchange stores, taverns, and more—practical establishments for adventurers.

from the third floor. Remaining were groups led by Yudai & Rina, Marina &
Yurika, the Saint and the Second Prince Gray, the Viscount Zigrard’s son, Dan &
Miria, and other students from different classes began to appear here and there.

Well, the remaining Class A1 groups should be withdrawing soon. At this point,
as long as they were careful on their way back, there shouldn’t be any issues.

—Or so I thought, but that’s when I made the mistake of thinking that.

“Huh?”

I couldn’t help but mutter aloud, my voice tinged with irritation.

The reason was simple: a powerful monster had suddenly appeared. Ten of them, to
be exact. They had emerged all over the third floor, and they were far stronger
than any monsters that should have appeared here. They were about level 50 or
so. In the original story, these enemies appeared near the end, and if any of
the students encountered them, they’d undoubtedly be killed.

“Damn it, the flag’s been triggered too early\!”

I cast my Gunshot magic at a distance — no, wait, that’s impossible. This spell,
when used in someone else’s domain, doesn't allow me to set the firing point far
away.

Unable to do that, I instead activated Gunshot close to me, directing the magic
toward the monsters. With the help of my detection magic, I had already
pinpointed their positions. In no time at all, a barrage of magic bullets was
unleashed upon the monsters.

I took down eight of the ten monsters. But it wasn’t a complete victory. Two of
them managed to escape the final blow.

This was the dungeon’s fault. The magic coursing through the dungeon caused my
Gunshot to go off-target. Additionally, those two remaining monsters were more
stronger than the other eight—around level 60\. The terrain here seemed to be
aiding the enemies.

And to make matters worse, the two remaining monsters were now close to the
groups of the Hero party and Marina’s group. No matter how fast I moved,
avoiding the encounter would be impossible.

“Damn it\!”

I clicked my tongue in frustration and placed my hand on the ground. Then, I
activated Telepathy to communicate with everyone in the third floor.

Normally, communicating with so many people at once would be difficult, but I
used the magic circulating throughout the dungeon to my advantage.

“_This is Zex Levito San Foranada, Count of the Levito family. I am using a
special magic to relay this message to everyone currently in the third floor.
This is an emergency, so I’ll be brief. Pay attention and do not miss
anything.”_

After introducing myself, I explained that powerful unknown monsters had
appeared on the third floor. While most of them had been dealt with, it was
still an unexpected situation. I instructed all students to withdraw
immediately.

I also warned the Hero group and Marina’s group that the monsters were closing
in on them.

_“Both of those groups, hurry and regroup. Work together to buy time. As long as
you hold out, I’ll handle the rest.”_

By the time I finished sending the message, Marina’s group had already started
to move. Naturally, they were quick to act. With their abilities, the worst-case
scenario could likely be avoided.

Now, it was my turn to rush over there.

“Body Enhancement: Divine Transformation”

Activating my ultimate skill, I dashed forward at a speed that approached the
speed of light. My feet tore through the ground, and I raced swiftly toward the
threat.

Ten seconds later, I had covered a distance of over ten kilometers and arrived
at the battlefield.

It was a scene of fierce combat. The teachers who had arrived and the student
group led by Yudai were each fighting a monster, but neither side seemed to be
in a favorable position.

The student group, in particular, was in terrible shape. Despite the short
amount of time that had passed, the frontliners—Yudai, Rina, and Yurika—were
covered in blood, and Marina, the only rear guard, had used up most of her magic
and was now pale-faced.

Only these four were fighting the monsters. The other six students were
trembling and huddled together in fear, unable to move in the face of such
formidable enemies.

I felt a strong urge to reprimand them, but asking ordinary students to fight
was unreasonable. As I've said repeatedly, no matter how excellent their
academic performance, this was their first real battle. In front of enemies far
beyond their strength, only a handful would be able to summon the courage to
stand and fight.

Though the teachers were in better condition, they were also battered. Given the
thirty-level difference, that was to be expected. It was a good thing I had
provided some support fire along the way; otherwise, they would likely have been
wiped out by now.

Tch. Why did such an unexpected situation have to occur? The countermeasures I
had prepared just in case last night had ended up being completely useless.

I cursed inwardly as I maintained my speed and leaped into the fray. Without
hesitation, I kicked the two monsters that were facing off against the
frontliners.

There was a disgusting squelching sound as I swung my leg.

The parts I had kicked were blown away, and the monsters’ bodies exploded into
pieces. Their organs scattered everywhere as they fell silent.

These monsters had just taken a blow from my Divine Transformation-enhanced
attack. No matter how strong they were, there was no way they could survive it.
In fact, I was surprised they hadn’t vaporized. Did they have some kind of
physical resistance?

For the first time, I truly took in the appearance of the monsters.

"What the hell is this?"

I couldn’t help but let out a voice of surprise.

Monsters were creatures that had been transformed from wild animals absorbing
magical energy from the atmosphere. Therefore, despite some structural
differences, their base form was always animal-like.

However, the monster I had just blown away was far too unusual. It had the face
of a lion, the forelegs of a bear, the body and hind legs of a horse, and a
serpent's tail. Each part of it came from a different creature. It was like
something out of a work of fiction—like a chimera.

—No, now’s not the time to focus on the monster’s corpse. There are injured
people here; we need to evacuate quickly. The investigation can wait until
later.

I used \[Phase Concealment\] to collect the monster’s remains and turned toward
the group behind me.

Everyone was sitting on the ground, overwhelmed by the battle they had just
survived. It was no surprise—they had fought for their lives. I wanted to let
them rest, but taking a break here was impossible. I steeled myself and called
out to them.

"I know you're exhausted, but get up. We need to get out of the dungeon as
quickly as possible."

"Yes," they all replied in unison.

Had it been ingrained in them through training with me, or was it just their
instinct, but Marina and Yurika immediately responded. Their voices were sharp,
showing no signs of fatigue from the battle. The others stared at them in
surprise.

Following them, the teachers also responded and moved to assist the students who
had been frozen in fear.

Finally, Yudai and Rina, both wearing complex expressions, slowly approached.

"Sorry, you saved us."

"Don't mention it... though, I suppose I can't say that. I'll accept your
thanks. For now, let's retreat quickly."

"…Ah, understood."

Yudai’s expression clearly showed frustration—not directed at me, but at his own
inability. It was the anger of someone who hated their own weakness.

The preparations to retreat were quickly underway. However, most of the members
who had fought were injured, and their movements were sluggish. Since there were
no Light Mages in the group, only basic first aid could be performed. Even I
couldn't compensate for that. The best I could do was dull the pain with Mental
Magic.

In about a minute, preparations were complete. I took the lead, with the
teachers bringing up the rear, and we began our retreat.

The journey was eerily quiet, as if the fierce battle had been a lie. Perhaps
the other monsters had been frightened by the chimera; I didn’t see any of them.

It was like the calm before a storm. My instincts told me that "something" was
about to happen. I sharpened my senses, ready to respond to any problems that
arose.

And, as expected, my instincts were right.

"GUAGAAAHHH\!\!\!"

Just before we reached the stairs leading to the second floor, a chimera
appeared once more, this time from the side of our formation. It burst out of
the dungeon wall like a ghost.

I see. Leaving aside how it was able to pass through the wall, the floor and
walls of the dungeon were flowing with magical energy. If it had been hiding
there, it was no surprise it didn’t show up on my detection.

The chimera attacked from an unexpected direction, aiming for Yudai. Its
lion-like fangs glinted as it aimed to tear through his throat.

Yudai, with a dumbfounded expression of "Huh?", managed to grab his sword and
adopt a defensive stance in time.

However, his defense was utterly powerless against the chimera's attack. If left
as is, it would easily break through. His throat would be torn apart in an
instant.

—That is, if I let it happen.

"Hah\!"

With a short breath, I swung my hand in a blade-like motion. A magical slash
flew and cleaved the chimera's neck.

In a last-ditch effort, the chimera’s head, still attacking, attempted to
charge, but that too was anticipated. I activated \[Compression\] and slammed
the foolish beast into the ground.

Once the chimera had fallen silent, I was about to resume the retreat when—

"Yudai, down\!"

"?\!"

From the shadow of the severed head, something resembling the chimera's tail
shot out from the ground, aiming for Yudai’s feet.

I immediately shouted a warning and ran toward him, but the enemy was faster.
Yudai, caught off guard, had frozen. I wouldn’t make it in time\!

But just before the chimera's tail could strike Yudai—

"Yudai-kun\!"

Someone else, closer to Yudai and faster than I was, managed to stop it.

It was Marina. She had been behind Yudai and, without thinking, had pushed him
out of the way. As a result, the chimera’s tail missed Yudai.

However, the cost was heavy.

"Kyah\!"

In her haste, Marina had leaped directly in front of the tail, and it quickly
ensnared her ankle. The tail began dragging her into the floor, and within an
instant, she had already sunk up to her knees.

"Get thereeee\!\!\!"

I sprinted with all my might and grabbed Marina’s arm just in time.

But, in that position, I couldn't hold my ground, and I had no strength left to
resist. Following Marina, I too was being sucked into the floor.

I could hear the screams around me, but I had no time to worry about that.

Using the little time left, I shouted out.

"Yurika\! Tell Caron and the others to do their jobs\!"

Since she was the closest to Caron and the others, I knew she would be able to
get the message across.

Just after, I was completely engulfed by the floor, and my vision was swallowed
by darkness.
