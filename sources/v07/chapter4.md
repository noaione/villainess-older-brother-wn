# Chapter 4: The Devotion of the Girls

When I, Caroline, heard the report that Onii-sama and Marina had been attacked
by an unidentified magical beast and swallowed into the dungeon’s floor, my
emotions were, quite simply, beyond description. Confusion, anger, sorrow, and
anguish—a tangled mess of every feeling swirled within me, plunging me into a
state I could scarcely comprehend myself.

An excess of emotion often paralyzes thought, and I was no exception at that
moment. The last time I felt such a sensation was perhaps when Shion suffered
grave injuries long ago.

The others were in a similar state. Though their reactions varied in intensity,
it seemed that every member of our usual group was stunned, frozen in place.

However, unlike us, who were overcome with such pathetic turmoil, there was one
person who could act with composure.

That person was none other than Orca. While we were thrown into disarray, he
issued precise instructions one after another.

First, he coordinated with Dijot to seal off the dungeon. Given the irregularity
of an unidentified magical beast’s appearance, this was an obvious necessity.

Second, he enforced an information blackout. The disappearance of the head of
the Foranada family would be nothing short of a catastrophic revelation. While
our territory is known for its strength beyond just Onii-sama, there are still
fools who might seize the opportunity for reckless actions. To prevent
unnecessary chaos, controlling the spread of information was critical.

Third, he organized an investigation within the dungeon and formed a search
party. Given Onii-sama’s prowess, he was likely unharmed, but with the head of
the family missing, a search was mandatory. We assembled teams while considering
precautions against the magical beast that had attacked Onii-sama and Marina, as
well as preparations for the stampede expected to occur soon.

It was a masterful display. Even now, as reports continued to pour in, Orca
handled the absence of Onii-sama with deft precision.

That said, it’s not as though he was “unconcerned for Onii-sama.” Having lived
with him for years, I could tell. Orca was suppressing his anxiety, focusing on
the tasks he needed to accomplish. I believe he was striving to remain calm,
following the message Onii-sama had relayed through Yurika.

With my adoptive brother working so diligently, I—we—could not afford to sit
idly by. Inspired by Orca’s efforts, we quickly snapped out of our stupor.

Minerva, accompanied by Noma and a portion of Feyburn, headed to investigate the
dungeon. With the wisdom of those two, there was a high chance they could
uncover clues about the magical beast that attacked Onii-sama.

Shion assisted Orca. Her experience supporting Onii-sama was not for naught. She
adeptly filled the gap left by Onii-sama, handling this emergency with
precision.

Nina was in her room, honing her focus. She wasn’t slacking off; she was
preparing for the stampede that would likely occur soon. In terms of pure combat
strength, she was the strongest among those remaining.

And I—

“Skia-san, please treat that patient over there.”

“Y-Yes\!” Skia replied.

Alongside Viscountess Skia, I was volunteering at Dijot’s church, providing
medical aid. The reason was simple: to improve Skia-san’s light magic skills as
much as possible in a short time.

It was already known that a stampede of magical beasts would occur soon. Due to
Onii-sama’s policies, many people would likely be injured in the event.

However, that didn’t mean we could stand by and do nothing. If people were to be
harmed, it was the duty of us light magic users to heal them. Thus, training
Skia-san was a way to bolster our numbers, even if only slightly.

For someone like her, who struggled with interacting with others, treating an
endless stream of patients was clearly taxing. But I hardened my heart. Having
awakened as a light magic user, she could not shy away from such situations.
This was especially true if she was to be part of Foranada. Onii-sama is quite
the Spartan, after all.

Even so...

“Where could Onii-sama and the others be...?”

As I watched Skia-san begin treating another patient, I murmured the words under
my breath.

Three days had already passed since Onii-sama and Marina went missing. The
memory of my initial panic, realizing that even someone like Onii-sama had been
out of contact for three days, now felt distant.

According to Minerva and Noma, who investigated the dungeon, “The dungeon’s
walls are swirling with dense magical energy, distorting the concepts of time
and space.” Thus, even if Onii-sama escaped in an instant, days could have
passed in our world.

They concluded that distortions spanning weeks were unlikely, so I resolved to
endure a little longer.

Oh, but it’s been three whole days since I last saw Onii-sama’s noble visage.
When I can prepare myself in advance, it’s manageable, but in sudden situations
like this, my fingers can’t help but tremble. Please return soon, Onii-sama.

As I dwelled on such trivial—yet to me, profoundly important—thoughts, the area
outside the clinic suddenly grew noisy.

The words weren’t clear enough to discern details, but it didn’t seem like a
minor disturbance. ...Yes, there was a familiar sensation. It was something akin
to the prelude to war.

“Caroline-sama\! Skia-sama\!”

Moments later, a priest from the church rushed in. His forehead was drenched in
sweat, and his expression was filled with palpable fear.

“_Cough_\! Both of you, _cough, cough, cough_\! Hurry\!”

He must have run here in a frenzy, as he was coughing so violently he could
barely speak. I could tell he was desperately trying to convey something, but
his words were incomprehensible.

I cast a stamina-recovery spell to help him catch his breath, but there was
little I could do for his parched throat—that was the speciality of water magic.

After a full five minutes, the priest was finally able to speak coherently. He
shouted urgently:

“It’s a stampede\! A horde of over ten thousand magical beasts has poured out of
the dungeon. Evacuate now\!”

I see. The event Onii-sama had warned us about has finally come to pass.

Having been informed in advance, neither I nor Skia-san were shaken. Well,
Skia-san, still relatively weak, showed signs of fear, but that was something
she’d have to overcome.

The fact that he went out of his way to urge us to evacuate, even when his own
safety was at stake, spoke to his good character. However, we could not accept
his kind gesture.

I offered the priest a gentle smile.

“Thank you for informing us. However, we will not evacuate.”

“Why\?!” he exclaimed, his eyes widening in shock at my unexpected response.

I continued in a calm tone, explaining patiently.

“Because we are of Foranada. Foranada does not flee from mere magical beasts. We
do not turn our backs on innocent people, abandoning them. We possess the
strength to protect everyone.”

Shifting my gaze from the stunned priest, I addressed Skia-san.

“Skia-san, let us return to the inn. We’ll regroup with the others.”

“Y-Y-Y-Yes, understood\!” she stammered, her shoulders trembling as she nodded.

There was no need to be _that_ frightened. Feeling a touch of exasperation, we
left the church behind.

Though Onii-sama was absent, I felt no anxiety. On the contrary, I was faintly
exhilarated.

When Onii-sama returns, I want him to see the strength of those he has nurtured.

It seemed we were the last to return to the inn. In the large room used as a
makeshift meeting hall, everyone was already gathered.

“Now that everyone’s here, let’s begin the strategy meeting,” Orca announced as
Skia-san and I entered. Though it was a serious meeting, his tone carried no
trace of strain. He was likely being mindful not to make us tense. And yet, he
was the one under the most pressure in this situation.

Aside from the newcomer Skia-san, everyone seemed to pick up on Orca’s state of
mind. However, no one was tactless enough to point it out. Orca was doing his
best to move forward, after all.

He shrugged lightly.

“I called it a strategy meeting, but there’s no new information or orders at
this stage. Things are unfolding exactly as Zex-nii predicted. The stampede’s
scale is roughly thirty thousand beasts. Of those, ten thousand are heading
toward Dijot.”

“We’ll leave Dijot to Princess Arianorth, the Hero, and the Saintess, while we
handle the remaining twenty thousand, correct?” Minerva confirmed.

Orca nodded deeply. “Yes. We can’t abandon the other towns.”

As they said, our assigned area was beyond Dijot. While Dijot is the closest
town to the dungeon, the other areas aren’t empty wastelands either. Thus, we
needed to strike the beast hordes before they could spread to those regions.

“However, we’ll be lending a portion of Feyburn to their side, so our burden
will increase accordingly. Keep that in mind,” Orca added.

That was unavoidable. With our elite, one-against-a-thousand forces, we could
handle twenty thousand beasts with a small, refined team. But the Hero and
Saintess staying in Dijot weren’t as strong. Lending troops from our
overabundant forces was only natural.

Then, Orca continued, “Oh, one more thing.”

“The beast hordes have scattered earlier than anticipated. So, we’ll split into
multiple groups to take them down individually. I’ll assign the teams now.”

The battlegrounds, excluding Dijot, were three locations. They would be handled
by three teams: myself with Shion, Orca with Minerva, and Nina. Feyburn’s forces
would be evenly distributed among the teams.

At that moment, Skia-san hesitantly raised her hand.

“U-U-U-Um, w-w-what should I do?” she stammered, visibly flustered.

As the only one not assigned a role, her reaction was understandable, but it
couldn’t be helped given the circumstances.

“Skia-san, you’re still in training, so we can’t send you to the front lines.
For now, I’d like you to use your light magic skills at Dijot’s relief
facilities,” Orca explained.

“U-Understood,” she replied.

“Any other questions?” Orca asked.

The room responded with silence.

With a firm nod, Orca continued.

“Alright. Time is limited, so let’s move out. If we’re quick, maybe we can wrap
this up before Zex-nii returns\!”

And so, the time for devastation began.

---

Leaving the academy’s inn, I dashed through the town with Shion. Feyburn’s
members were set to rendezvous at the destination, so we headed straight for the
outskirts of Dijot.

The stampede had thrown the town into chaos. Men and women, young and old, were
shoving their way toward shelters. Some areas saw fights breaking out, including
violent ones, creating a state of severe disorder.

Normally, I’d intervene, but my current priority lay elsewhere. I had no choice
but to suppress my emotions and look away.

However, things don’t always go as planned.

While avoiding the congestion on the ground by leaping from rooftop to rooftop,
I witnessed something I couldn’t ignore.

A young boy and girl had fallen on the street, and a carriage, recklessly
plowing through the crowd, was charging toward them. If left unchecked, the two
children would almost certainly die.

I had just said my priority lay elsewhere, but I couldn’t turn a blind eye to
this. If I allowed such an atrocity, I surely wouldn’t be able to face Onii-sama
with pride.

Without a moment’s hesitation, I used the enhanced leg strength from \[Body
Enhancement\] to leap toward the children. Scooping them up in one swift motion,
I narrowly dodged the carriage and landed in an alley away from the crowd.

It was a close call. Had my decision been even slightly delayed, I wouldn’t have
made it in time.

Breathing a sigh of relief, I set the two children down on the ground.

They seemed dazed, their faces blank, as if they hadn’t yet processed what had
happened.

It was understandable. The only humans capable of moving at such speeds were
likely those of Foranada, who could fully utilize \[Body Enhancement\].

Sensing Shion hurrying toward me, I addressed the children.

“Are you both unharmed?”

“Uh, y-yeah. I’m okay,” the boy replied.

“M-Me too,” the girl added.

They nodded vigorously. To be safe, I cast \[Diagnosis\], confirming they were
indeed uninjured. What a relief.

“Caroline-sama, please warn me before you rush off like that...” Shion said,
landing behind me with a reproachful tone.

I offered an apologetic smile. “My apologies. It was a time-sensitive
situation.”

“I understand, but if anything happened to you, Caroline-sama, it would be a
disaster.”

“I’ll be more careful in the future,” I promised.

“Please, truly be careful,” she insisted.

It seems I wasn’t entirely trusted. Well, given my usual behavior, it was a fair
response.

With a wry smile, I turned back to the children, finally taking a moment to
observe their appearance.

Their clothes were simple but well-maintained, suggesting they were likely from
an orphanage. Based on their builds, the boy seemed to be the older sibling,
with the girl as his younger sister.

To my surprise, the girl had golden hair. It was a radiant, divine gold that
made me squint instinctively. My intuition told me she possessed significant
potential for light magic.

Suppressing my inner astonishment, I spoke to the children.

“The town is dangerous right now, so go home.”

“But the magical beasts are attacking the town\!” the boy cried, his voice
trembling with fear.

I gave him a reassuring smile. “It’s alright,” I said firmly.

“The fangs and claws of those beasts will never reach you. We’ll make sure of
that with all our strength.”

“Are you and the other lady going to fight?” the girl asked, tilting her head
curiously.

It was a fair question. At a glance, Shion and I must have looked like mere
girls, hardly capable of defeating a horde of magical beasts.

“Yes, we’ll defeat them. We’re quite skilled mages, you know,” I replied,
puffing out my chest.

My words seemed to resonate with the girl, whose eyes sparkled brightly.

“Your hair is the same color as mine, right? Can I become a strong mage too?”

Oh, it seems her shared light affinity sparked her interest.

After a brief moment of hesitation, I answered.

“If you hold kindness in your heart and never neglect your efforts, you can
surely become strong. Keep at it\!”

“I will\!” she declared, clenching her fists with determination.

Her earnest resolve was utterly endearing.

Chuckling softly,I addressed them both.

“Now, hurry home. I’ve cast a spell on you, so I guarantee you won’t face danger
like that again.”

Having imbued them with \[Light Cocoon\], they should be safe unless struck
directly by magic.

“Thank you\!” the two exclaimed in unison, expressing their gratitude before
disappearing into the crowd.

“Well then, shall we press on?” I said lightly.

“Yes,” Shion replied with a calm nod.

It seemed she wasn’t too upset about the slight delay in our march. I had braced
for a lecture, so I was relieved.

Thereafter, we encountered no further obstacles and arrived at our designated
battlefield without incident.

---

I, Marina, am an ordinary girl. Born and raised in a rural village, I come from
no distinguished lineage. As the saying goes, “The child of a frog is a frog,” I
possess not a single remarkable talent. I’m somewhat skilled at housework, but
that’s the result of effort, not some exceptional gift.

As a child, I used to think, “Most of my life will probably play out in this
village.” I knew I’d spend three years in the capital as part of my obligations
as a citizen of the Holy Kingdom, but otherwise, I assumed I’d live quietly in
my hometown.

I imagined a safe, uneventful school life with the childhood friend I admired. I
might dream a little bigger after being exposed to the city, but in the end, I’d
choose a modest life in the village. Somehow, things would progress naturally
with my childhood friend, leading to marriage. I’d build a simple but warm
family, typical of a rural village. That’s the small, certain happiness I
believed I’d grasp.

After all, that’s the path most commoners follow. For an ordinary girl like me,
with no notable qualities, it was only natural to expect an easily predictable
future.

But my life didn’t turn out that way. I refused to let it end in mediocrity.

Even now, eight years later, I can vividly recall it when I close my eyes. I
went hiking with my two childhood friends, defying the adults’ warnings. We were
attacked by a horde of magical beasts. In the chaos of the battle, I was thrown
off a cliff. The desperate, wide-eyed faces of my friends, reaching out to me,
are still etched deeply in my memory.

Then, as my consciousness faintly returned, a dazzling white filled my vision.
Sparkling in the sunlight, it was seared into my memory with striking clarity.

From that moment, the presence of the “white-haired prince” took root in my
heart. His beautiful white hair and the twinkling figures—spirit mimics, I
think?—surrounding him were unforgettable. He was like a prince straight out of
a storybook, and I couldn’t shake his image from my mind.

He was my first love. I had admired my childhood friend at one point, but the
“white-haired prince” was the first time I consciously recognized it as love, so
I’m certain he was my first.

When I learned from my parents that the prince was the son of the lord, I became
obsessed with gathering information about him. My childhood friend Yudai-kun
gave me disapproving looks, but I couldn’t stop. Every time I heard about the
prince—Zex-sama—achieving something, I felt joy as if it were my own. It was as
if I were sharing emotions with my first love, and those moments were incredibly
fulfilling.

Oh, how nostalgic. I was so content with secondhand stories from merchants. If
someone had told the me from back then, “You’ll end up living with Zex-sama\!”
I’d never have believed it.

I genuinely cherish my current life—my time since enrolling at the academy. I’m
overjoyed, satisfied beyond measure. Being near the person I love, surrounded by
close friends I trust, and excelling in my studies thanks to them—it makes me
wonder if I’m the happiest girl in the world.

However, I can’t say I’m entirely free of concerns.

What troubles me, circling back to the start, is that I’m nothing more than an
ordinary girl. That’s the problem.

Zex-sama, Caron-chan, Orca-kun, Nina-chan, Minerva-chan, Shion-san, and the rest
of the Foranada members—all of them possess extraordinary talents. They’ve put
in tremendous effort and achieved tangible results. Compared to them, I, a mere
commoner, can’t help but feel intimidated by the brilliance of the person I love
and those around him.

To be honest, there were moments early on—and even now, occasionally—when I
wondered if stepping back might be the wiser choice. Having been told I have
little talent for combat and starting later than everyone else, I sometimes feel
that no matter how hard I try, I’ll never stand beside Zex-sama.

In truth, that assessment is correct. My magical talent had already hit its
limit, far below everyone else’s.

It was frustrating. I felt inadequate, sad, and heartbroken. I loved Zex-sama so
dearly, yet the reality that standing by his side was nearly impossible filled
me with resentment. The unbridgeable gap in status meant I had to close the
distance through ability, but even that was unattainable. The world felt cruel.

It was then that my fateful partner appeared. I couldn’t contain my inner joy.
If we hadn’t been stranded in the dungeon, I might have danced on the spot.
That’s how thrilled I was to meet Maimu-chan.

After forming a contract with a spirit, I grew stronger in ways that made my
past struggles seem like a lie. Well, most of that strength comes from
Maimu-chan, but Zex-sama said my ability to draw out her power stems from my own
efforts, so my joy isn’t entirely misplaced.

In short, I became dramatically stronger. I’m still slightly behind Caron-chan
and the others, but if I continue training with Maimu-chan, I’m certain I can
catch up.

The increased possibility of standing beside the person I love greatly boosted
my motivation.

But then, cold water was poured over that fervor. Maimu-chan went berserk.

Just moments before, she had been docile, but suddenly, she unleashed a raging
inferno of destructive flames. Ignoring my will, she consumed my magical
reserves without restraint. Like water gushing from a broken faucet, Maimu-chan
released torrents of fire.

As my consciousness grew hazy, I desperately pleaded with her. Through our
contractual link, I silently begged, “Maimu-chan, stop\!”

But my efforts were in vain. Her rampage showed no signs of stopping.

In the end, it was Zex-sama’s intervention that resolved the situation. We
managed to avoid disposing of Maimu-chan, but it was only a temporary reprieve.
If the same thing happened again, he would likely eliminate her without
hesitation.

That was something I couldn’t allow. I can’t deny there’s a selfish part of me
that fears growing weaker, but more than that, I genuinely wanted to save
Maimu-chan.

During her rampage, her emotions flooded into me. What I felt was a torrent of
intense feelings—loneliness, anger, and sorrow.

The loneliness was particularly profound. She harbored a ravenous hunger for
connection with others, yet simultaneously, an overwhelming fear of being
abandoned again if she failed. It was a distorted heart, far too heavy for a
young child to bear.

I wanted to help Maimu-chan. I didn’t have answers for how to do so, but
ignoring her was never an option.

After all, we’re partners bound by a contract. We’re comrades who share joys and
hardships.

If that’s the case, she’s as good as family. As a member of Foranada, I would
never abandon family.

“Ugh, uhh...”

It seemed Maimu-chan had regained consciousness. She let out a pained groan.

I wouldn’t waste the final chance Zex-sama had given me. Steel myself, I faced
my contracted spirit.

“Marina-neechan...?”

Maimu-chan woke up.

Her hair had returned to blue, confirming my assumption that she was no longer
in a berserk state. Seeing her respond so clearly brought me a sense of relief.

At first, she seemed confused, blinking blankly. She tilted her head and lightly
gripped the hem of my clothes.

Perhaps she didn’t remember what happened during her rampage. For a fleeting
moment, I entertained that optimistic thought.

But the situation wasn’t so kind.

“A-Ah, AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHH\!\!\!”

Without warning, Maimu-chan let out a piercing scream. Her incoherent cries
echoed as her entire body trembled violently.

“Maimu-chan\?!” I exclaimed.

Her reaction seemed excessive, even if she was lamenting the events of her
rampage. All she had done was drain my magic wastefully; she hadn’t directed her
flames at me.

To calm her, I pulled her into an embrace. Gently enveloping her small frame
with both hands, I brought my cheek close, as if to soothe her. I didn’t know
how to comfort a spirit, but I did my best to make her feel safe.

About thirty minutes passed, I think. Thanks to the time I invested, I managed
to calm Maimu-chan down. Her breathing was slightly ragged, but she was composed
enough to hold a conversation.

“What happened all of a sudden? If you’re okay with it, could you tell me what’s
going on?” I asked.

“...”

When I inquired about the reason for her distress, she kept her lips tightly
sealed. She responded to other topics, so there must be something she found
difficult to voice.

Normally, I wouldn’t pry into someone’s personal matters so rudely. Maintaining
proper distance is crucial for smooth communication. It’s only natural to
respect the boundaries of the person you’re speaking with.

But this time, I couldn’t afford to hold back.

Perhaps because of our contract, I could vaguely sense Maimu-chan’s thoughts.
She seemed resolved to never speak about whatever she was hiding. While the
details were unclear, I could feel her intense fear of “something she absolutely
didn’t want to touch.”

If time could loosen her tongue, I might have persuaded Zex-sama to delay this
confrontation. But if that wasn’t the case, I couldn’t back down now.

I took a small, deep breath to steady myself, preparing for the possibility of
reopening Maimu-chan’s wounds.

“Please tell me, Maimu-chan. I don’t know what you’re afraid of, but I promise I
won’t reject you. Could you let me help you?”

I asked in a gentle tone, but Maimu-chan didn’t respond. She shook her head
vigorously, clearly refusing.

“I know you’re scared of something. But if things stay like this, neither you
nor I can move forward. So, please,” I pressed.

I understood it was a harsh demand for someone so young. But words were the only
tool I had.

After all, I’m just an ordinary girl with no special powers. At best, I have the
“likability to spirits” that Noma-chan acknowledged.

Even if I managed to get her to open up, I’d likely be powerless to do anything.
All I could probably do is listen to my partner’s troubles.

Even so, I wouldn’t give up. If I ignored Maimu-chan now, I’d surely regret it.
My progress would stall completely, and Maimu-chan would meet a terrible fate. I
had a premonition of that outcome.

“I know I’m asking something difficult. But even so, I want you to talk to me. I
want to share your pain. There’s surely hope beyond that,” I said earnestly,
locking eyes with her.

As she tried to look away, averting her gaze, I continued to watch her closely.

Her voice trembling slightly, Maimu-chan asked, “Why... why are you trying so
hard to help me?”

“Because you’re my one and only partner in this world,” I answered immediately.

“Part...ne..r?” she repeated, puzzled.

“Yes, partner. We made a contract, didn’t we? That makes us partners,” I
explained.

My ideal was the kind of relationship Zex-sama and Noma-chan have—mutual respect
combined with a comfortable familiarity. It’s hard to put into words, but I
wanted to build that kind of easy yet precious trust.

Of course, that’s a bit premature for us now. We’ll need to make more memories
together first.

To do that, I wanted to support Maimu-chan through her struggles.

“The person I love,” I continued, “will solve any problem for those he considers
family.”

“The one with... the scary magic?” she asked.

“Yes, that’s him,” I confirmed.

“...”

I couldn’t help but giggle at the incredulous look on her face. I knew she could
see Zex-sama’s magic and was intimidated by it, but her reaction was so blatant
it caught me off guard.

Clearing my throat, I steered the conversation back.

“What I’m trying to say is, I want to be like him—someone who can help family in
times of need. I don’t have any special powers, but I still want to try. That’s
why I want to help you, my partner.”

“Me... family?” Maimu-chan asked, her eyes widening in surprise.

“Yes, family,” I said, nodding firmly and meeting her gaze again.

Our eyes locked.

“...Okay. I’ll talk,” she murmured after a long silence.

Thanking her, I listened intently to what she had to say.

According to her, “When I sense strong danger, I feel something crawling up from
deep inside me.” Whenever that “something” surfaces, it’s always accompanied by
intense anger. She believes it’s furious at her weakness and trying to consume
her.

She’s experienced similar situations multiple times in the past, and each time,
the encroachment grows stronger. She’s terrified that if it continues, she’ll
cease to be herself.

That’s why she was so reluctant to speak about it—she feared that mentioning it
might cause the “something” to emerge.

While stroking her head, I clenched my other fist.

“It’s okay. From now on, I’m with you. You’re not alone. If that ‘something’
tries to take you over, we’ll fight it off together\!”

“That’s...”

She trailed off, likely wanting to say it was impossible.

Indeed, I probably don’t have the power to defeat this “something.” But giving
up from the start means accomplishing nothing. Effort doesn’t always yield
results, but without effort, nothing begins.

Besides, I wasn’t making empty assurances. When Maimu-chan went berserk, her
emotions flowed into me. That means there’s a connection between our hearts. I
believed I could use that to support her.

“Let’s get stronger\! If we’re strong, we won’t have to fear this ‘something’
anymore. It might be tough alone, but together, we can make it work\!”

On my own, my strength had reached its limit, but partnering with Maimu-chan
allowed me to level up. I believed the reverse was true as well.

Maimu-chan gazed at me with wavering eyes.

After a long moment of staring, she spoke up.

“If I’m with Marina-neechan... I’ll try. I want to get stronger too\!”

Her voice was firm, and the fear had vanished from her expression.

Next was the battle against the dungeon boss. I had a feeling this fight would
determine our future.

After telling Zex-sama, “We’re ready,” we descended the stairs to the hundredth
floor. At the end of the staircase stood a massive double door, with nothing
else in sight. This was, without a doubt, the final barrier.

Maimu-chan and I stood before the door together. Zex-sama remained behind us,
ready to step in if necessary.

I whispered to my partner perched on my shoulder.

“Here we go.”

“Okay,” she replied.

With her brisk response, I flung the door open. It was a reckless move under
normal circumstances, but Zex-sama had assured us there was no immediate danger,
and Maimu-chan’s senses hadn’t detected any suspicious presence. So, I
prioritized momentum.

No surprise attack came—only silence greeted us, save for the creak of the
opening door.

The boss chamber was much as we’d been told. It was a vast cube, each side
measuring a hundred meters, empty of anything except the boss itself.

One detail hadn’t been mentioned: the color. The entire chamber was bathed in
unblemished white. The stark purity was almost painful to the eyes, making it
difficult to gauge distances accurately. This would undoubtedly put us at a
disadvantage in combat.

The boss itself stood silently in the center of the room. Its form seemed to
mimic a knight. Clad in faintly glowing white armor—truly emitting a subtle
light—it wielded a longsword in one hand and a large shield in the other.

Though it resembled a human in appearance, its size was anything but. Towering
over three meters tall, its equipment was proportionally massive. A single blow
from that weapon would likely cleave me in two in an instant.

And—

“It’s strong...” I murmured.

The boss exuded an aura similar to what Caron-chan and the others radiated when
fighting seriously. I’m not skilled at gauging strength, but the sheer hostility
directed at us was impossible to ignore.

In short, this fight would be a toss-up at best, and more likely, we were facing
a superior opponent. With Zex-sama here, I doubted we’d die, but he’s quite the
Spartan. I had a feeling he wouldn’t intervene until we were on the brink of
death.

After all, I had specifically requested to face the boss for the sake of my and
Maimu-chan’s growth. Relying on him in this battle would defeat the purpose. We
had to overcome this challenge with our own strength.

“Maimu-chan, let’s do this\!”

“Okay\!”

I stroked my partner on my right shoulder with my fingertip, raising my voice to
rally her.

Maimu-chan responded with a spirited shout, her resolve clearly already set. It
seemed I hadn’t needed to fire her up.

In that case, our focus was solely on the dungeon boss—the “White Knight”\!

I lowered my stance slightly, shifting into a combat-ready position. Maimu-chan
began circulating her magic, prepared to unleash spells at a moment’s notice.

Perhaps sensing our intent, the White Knight, which had been utterly still,
sprang into motion. With a clank of heavy armor, it readied its weapons in both
hands.

“...”

“...”

“...”

Silence fell among the three.

But it was shattered in an instant.

“Maimu-chan\!”

“Aye\!”

We seized the initiative. At my signal, Maimu-chan unleashed a barrage of water
arrows. Each arrow, rivaling high-tier magic, surged toward the White Knight.

If they hit, even a superior foe wouldn’t escape unscathed. ...Well, they didn’t
hit, of course.

The White Knight swayed its upper body slightly—and vanished. No, that wasn’t
it. It had moved at such high speed to dodge the water arrows that my eyes
couldn’t track it.

“Dome\!”

“Aye\!”

Without missing a beat, I issued the next command.

Maimu-chan conjured a dome of water enveloping us, layered fivefold.

Immediately after, a deafening boom echoed from the seven o’clock direction. The
White Knight had struck the water dome with its massive longsword. Glancing
back, I saw it had breached all but the final layer, which was on the verge of
breaking.

A chill ran down my spine. If my judgment had been a fraction slower or if I’d
skimped on even one layer of the dome, our lives would have ended here.

“It’s really strong\!”

In just one exchange, I grasped the stark gap in our abilities. The enemy was
unquestionably superior.

Yet, I also thought this: it wasn’t as overwhelmingly unfair as Zex-sama or as
crushing as Caron-chan and the others. The White Knight was stronger, but only
by a small margin.

If that was the case, a path to victory still existed\!

Creating a path of water beneath our feet, we glided away to put distance
between us and the enemy.

Having broken through the final dome, the White Knight charged straight at us.
Its sword would reach us in less than a second.

But even a fraction of a second was enough. For a spirit like Maimu-chan, there
was virtually no delay in casting magic.

“Barrage\!”

“Aye\!”

Just as the dull blade was about to cleave my head, a new spell was unleashed.

A torrent of water bullets erupted. Inspired by Zex-sama’s favored \[Gunshot\],
this water magic fired hundreds of high-density water projectiles in rapid
succession.

- At point-blank range, the White Knight couldn’t evade the curtain of bullets.
  It took every shot. The sharp, heavy clangs echoed as the barrage dented its
  gleamying armor. Naturally, it couldn’t reach me with its attack and was
  swiftly blown backward.

Even after the White Knight fell, we didn’t stop the barrage. There was no
guarantee this would defeat it. If we were going to strike, we had to be
thorough.

For nearly a minute, we pelted it with water bullets. During that time, the
White Knight showed no signs of stirring.

Did we beat it?

The thought crossed my mind for a split second.

“Maimu-chan\!”

“Aye\!”

Acting on the icy sensation creeping up my spine, I shouted another command.

Maimu-chan, who seemed confused, followed through obediently. Halting the
barrage, she conjured a massive water shield behind us.

That split-second decision was the right one.

Simultaneously with the shield’s appearance, a horizontal slash struck it. The
blow carried immense force, shattering the water shield and catching my torso
with its blade.

“Gah\!”

Swept away by the sword’s momentum, I was flung sideways like a piece of scrap
paper. After a prolonged tumble through the air, I crashed into the ground,
rolling uncontrollably across the floor. Only when I slammed into the room’s
wall did I finally come to a stop.

That single strike had sent me flying some fifty meters. Without the water
shield, my body might have been bisected by now.

Surviving by the skin of my teeth, I felt genuine terror. My limbs began to
tremble, and I instinctively glanced toward Zex-sama.

But I forced those feelings down with sheer willpower. There was no time to
cower. If I relied on him now, I’d never stand by his side. That was something I
absolutely refused to accept\!

I likely had a few broken bones, but fortunately, I could still move. Casting
\[Water Veil\] alongside the water shield had been the right call. Though a
low-tier spell, it had enough defensive power to prevent a fatal wound.

Ignoring the throbbing pain in my body, I stood and assessed the White Knight.

I finally understood what it had done. Its sword was floating in the air.
Apparently, the White Knight’s weapon could move independently. While we were
attacking its main body, it had maneuvered the sword behind us.

I hadn’t been careless, but my vigilance had been lacking. If I’d observed more
closely, I might have noticed the sword’s absence.

Cursing myself inwardly, I told myself to stay calm.

The White Knight, too, seemed to have taken considerable damage from the earlier
barrage and was rising slowly, refraining from an immediate counterattack.

We were injured, but so was the enemy. That meant we still had a chance to win.

“Maimu-chan\!”

“...”

“Maimu-chan?”

When I called out to plan our next move, there was no response from my partner.

Fearing she might have been hurt in the last attack, I hurriedly glanced at her
on my right shoulder.

“\?!”

I gasped. She wasn’t injured. Maimu-chan was unharmed. But the situation was far
graver. She was on the verge of going berserk again.

“No, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, no\!”

Clutching her head, Maimu-chan muttered incessantly, her wide eyes betraying a
glimpse of madness. Worse still, parts of her hair were turning red, and faint
flames were starting to flicker.

“Maimu-chan, calm down\! I’m right here with you\!”

I hugged her tightly, calling out to her with all my might.

But the situation only worsened. Her hair steadily shifted to red.

To make matters worse, the White Knight had rebooted. Retrieving its autonomous
sword, it began charging toward us.

“Not now\!”

I tried to fend it off with my own magic, but my meager talent was useless. The
enemy didn’t even bother dodging or defending; my spells simply dissipated upon
contact.

As I feared, I couldn’t do this alone.

Even so, I couldn’t stop. If I did nothing, we’d lose for sure.

Despite my efforts, the White Knight closed in. Raising its longsword high, it
prepared to deliver the finishing blow.

I deployed the low-tier water spells \[Water Wall\] and \[Water Veil\], then
dove sideways to evade the strike.

I managed to avoid a fatal hit, but the attack grazed my left leg. A dull sound
rang out, followed by sharp, searing pain.

Tumbling to create distance, I assessed the situation.

It was the worst-case scenario. My leg was injured. It wasn’t severed, but my
ankle was swollen and discolored, indicating a severe fracture. I no longer had
the means to dodge the White Knight’s attacks.

The only silver lining was that the White Knight, perhaps assuming it had
secured victory, was moving leisurely. Instead of attacking immediately, it
advanced slowly, as if savoring the fear it instilled.

What do I do?

The question raced through my mind. No matter how many times I asked myself, no
answer emerged.

The word “checkmate” flashed in my thoughts, but I shook my head furiously,
refusing to accept it. I didn’t want to give up. I kept racking my brain
desperately.

Reality was merciless and cruel. While I grappled with unanswerable questions,
the White Knight reached me. Raising its sword, it prepared to deliver the final
blow.

I still had magic left, but I’d already proven that magic alone couldn’t
withstand its attacks.

Even in this dire situation, I refused to close my eyes or give up. Determined
to fight until the last moment, I prepared to cast another spell.

But before I could, my magical reserves began to plummet.

Why? The question barely formed before the answer filled my vision.

Flames. Roaring, blazing flames ravaged our surroundings. Naturally, the White
Knight was caught in the inferno, swept away by the sheer mass of the fire.

The source of the flames was obvious. I immediately checked my hands.

There stood a spirit with flowing red hair. Gone was her earlier timidity; her
eyes now gleamed with fierce battle intent.

“Maimu-chan, snap out of it\!”

I called out instinctively, but she showed no response.

If this continued, Maimu-chan would be consumed. With that thought, I kept
calling to her.

But my efforts yielded nothing. The fire spirit continued to unleash flames,
draining my magic at an alarming rate.

Soon, my limited magical reserves began to run dry. Maintaining consciousness
was reaching its limit.

As my vision flickered, I refused to stop speaking. I felt that if I did,
Maimu-chan would vanish.

Effort doesn’t always guarantee results. Despite my struggles, my consciousness
began to fade.

But—just before darkness claimed me, I saw two lights: one red, one blue,
shining brightly.

“Where... am I?”

When I came to, I was standing in a pitch-black void. Looking up, down, left, or
right, everything was shrouded in darkness, with not a single object in sight.
Yet, it wasn’t complete darkness, as I could see my own hands and feet. Without
a light source, even my body should have been invisible.

I stood alone in this bizarre, incomprehensible space—an undeniably abnormal
situation. I was certain I had been fighting the dungeon boss. Maimu-chan had
gone berserk, my magic had run dry, and I had passed out. So why was I in a
place like this?

No matter how much I racked my brain, no answers emerged. For now, I had to
search for clues.

Wandering aimlessly for some time, I suddenly heard a sound. A roaring _whoosh_
like something burning fiercely. Mingled within it was a faint, barely audible
sob.

Someone might be here. Clinging to that hope, I ran toward the source, guided by
my hearing.

Fortunately, my instincts were correct. Within minutes, I spotted my target: a
large, ferocious red light and a small, frail blue one.

A sense of dread gripped me, and I poured more strength into my legs. My speed
increased slightly, and the true nature of the lights soon became clear.

The two lights were spirits—ones I knew well.

The blue light was, without a doubt, Maimu-chan. She was curled up on the
ground, face buried, hands covering her ears. She let out faint whimpers,
teetering on the edge of audible, her dim light flickering in sync. It was like
a candle flame, so fragile it might extinguish with a single breath.

In contrast, the red light was Maimu-chan in her berserk state. Standing tall
before the cowering water spirit, she glared down with an irritated expression.
As if to express her fury, intense red light and torrents of flames erupted from
her body, slowly encircling Maimu-chan.

The sight sparked an intuition. This must be Maimu-chan’s inner world. Right
now, her heart was on the verge of being consumed by the “something” dwelling
within her.

I sprinted at full speed. Ignoring the flames swirling around me, I scooped
Maimu-chan up and put distance between us and the fire spirit. I sustained minor
burns in the process, but it was a necessary cost to save her.

She seemed to have been scorched by the flames for quite some time. Though
conscious, she was limp and unresponsive, unable to hold a conversation.

Holding Maimu-chan tightly to my chest, I fixed a sharp glare on the fire
spirit. She clearly hadn’t anticipated my arrival, reacting with visible shock.
Her eyes widened as if they might pop out, and she froze in place. Even the
flames she had been unleashing ceased for the moment.

Seizing the opportunity to prepare for combat, I questioned the fire spirit.

“Who are you? Why are you hurting Maimu-chan?”

It was a question I asked despite already having a hunch about her identity.
Zex-sama had mentioned the experiments Maimu-chan might have undergone, making
it easy to piece together.

When Maimu-chan spoke of the “something” inside her, I initially thought it
might be an overwhelming power she couldn’t control. But facing the fire spirit
now, I understood. She wasn’t just a force—she had a distinct will, a clear
individual existence.

She was likely the soul of a fire spirit fused with Maimu-chan during those
experiments. That was her true nature.

If so, the reason for her actions became clear. Was she seeking revenge for
being fused? Trying to seize control of the body? Or perhaps both? Whatever the
case, she intended to harm Maimu-chan.

“—”

The fire spirit didn’t respond to my question. She might be incapable of speech,
but it was certain that communication was impossible.

This was troubling. I had hoped to negotiate if communication was possible, to
find a compromise and resolve the situation. But now, fighting seemed to be the
only option.

Was I resigned to that? Just as I began to steel myself, a flood of emotions
suddenly poured into me.

First came intense anger. But it didn’t stop there. Mixed within were concern,
regret, and even hope—not entirely negative emotions. It was hard to describe,
but it felt like... parental overprotectiveness? The clumsy affection of a
parent overly cautious for their child.

“Huh?”

A surprised sound escaped my lips.

Could it be? The fire spirit didn’t hate Maimu-chan? Was she actually trying to
help, out of worry?

—_Nod_.

As if on cue, the fire spirit nodded.

Wait—could she sense my thoughts, just as her emotions were flowing into me?

—_Nod_.

Another nod confirmed my suspicion. My guess was correct.

This was an unexpected turn of events. To think the fire spirit was Maimu-chan’s
ally\!

Come to think of it, Maimu-chan had said, “When I sense danger, ‘something’
crawls up from within.” Perhaps that “something” had been emerging to protect
her all along.

If so, the only question was why she had been scorching Maimu-chan with flames.
Had she not done that, I wouldn’t have misunderstood her intentions.

Another wave of emotions flowed from the fire spirit. Though hard to articulate,
concern and hope dominated.

“...You’re not going to say it was to train Maimu-chan, are you?”

—_Nod_.

When I asked hesitantly, she nodded immediately.

Seriously? Seriously?

I couldn’t help but want to clutch my head.

From what I gathered, she had been harsh on Maimu-chan to spur the growth of her
timid nature.

Considering Maimu-chan’s unique circumstances, it’s true that her current self
might struggle to survive. But this was clumsiness taken to an extreme. She’s
still a young child—surely there were gentler ways to approach this.

Feeling foolish for having been so tense, I let out a sigh.

“Honestly, I think it backfired,” I said bluntly. “Your actions scared
Maimu-chan.”

I spoke directly, believing she’d repeat the same mistake if left unchecked. As
I suspected, this clumsy spirit was likely oblivious to Maimu-chan’s mental
state.

Sure enough, the fire spirit’s eyes widened, and she froze, as if the revelation
hit her like a bolt from the blue. She really hadn’t realized.

“Neechan?”

It seemed Maimu-chan had recovered enough to speak. She looked up at me with a
puzzled expression.

Right—the exchange, particularly the fire spirit’s intentions, had only been
conveyed to me. Maimu-chan must be completely confused.

I adjusted my hold on her, turning her to face the fire spirit.

“Eek, Neechan\?!”

She panicked at the sudden movement.

Gently, I explained, “That fire spirit is the one who got mixed into you.”

“Mixed?”

“Yes, because of the humans who captured you.”

“S-So, she hates me?”

“No, not at all,” I reassured her softly, countering her fear at the shocking
truth.

“She’s been trying her hardest to protect you. She fought in your place when
danger arose, and the flames she directed at you were... like scolding, a plea
for you to get stronger.”

“Huh?”

Maimu-chan let out a bewildered sound.

I understood her feelings. That’s not how things usually work. But reality often
conjures up the strangest twists. I learned in Foranada that resignation is key
in such moments.

“I’ll be here from now on, and she’s promised not to do anything scary. So, I’d
like you to accept her, Maimu-chan.”

I didn’t say “forgive her.” Even if her actions stemmed from goodwill,
Maimu-chan had been terrified, and her feelings on that matter should be her own
to decide.

Still, I didn’t want her to reject the fire spirit. They shared the same body.
Even if they couldn’t get along perfectly, I hoped they could at least coexist
without conflict.

Maimu-chan gazed at the fire spirit with wavering eyes.

“Really?”

“You promise, right?” I asked the fire spirit.

—_Nod, nod, nod, nod, nod, nod._

The fire spirit nodded so vigorously I thought her head might fly off. Her
gesture, coupled with the sincere emotions I felt from her, convinced me she was
truthful.

“...Okay,” Maimu-chan said after a moment of silent contemplation.

“You were worried about me, right? Then I’ll forgive you. Or rather, I’m sorry
for being so scared of you all this time.”

Maimu-chan was far more magnanimous than I’d imagined. Her words weren’t mere
lip service; I could feel her genuine desire to get along.

The fire spirit smiled joyfully in response, her expression bursting with
delight as flames flared to express her happiness. Hastily, she bowed her head
in return.

“Well then, let’s get along from now on. Um...” I paused. “By the way, what’s
your name?”

She couldn’t speak, but perhaps we could communicate as we had before?

As I pondered, the fire spirit suddenly directed flames at me. Before I could
react, my body was engulfed in fire.

Was everything she said a lie\?! I panicked, but quickly realized it was a false
alarm. The flames weren’t hot at all. Instead, they felt as comforting as a
spring day’s warmth.

Soon, something flowed from the flames.

—Oh, I see. She was telling me her name.

With a wry smile at her clumsiness, the flames dissipated.

I addressed the fire spirit. “Your name is Esi, right? I’d be happy if you’d
lend us your strength, Esi-chan.”

—_Nod_.

With Esi-chan’s nod, both she and Maimu-chan amplified their respective lights.
The red and blue hues gradually dyed the surrounding blackness, eventually
filling the entire space with radiant brilliance.

---

“Hah\?!”

When I opened my eyes, I was back in the dungeon’s hundredth-floor boss chamber.
No longer in the dark void, I was in the painfully white room.

I quickly assessed the situation.

It seemed little time had passed since I entered that mental world. Most of the
room was engulfed in flames, and a slightly singed White Knight—the dungeon
boss—stood some distance away. The enemy was repositioning its sword, preparing
to attack again.

I, on the other hand, was in tatters and barely able to move. My magic had
recovered slightly, but it was a negligible amount.

Not everything was the same as before I lost consciousness, however. The one
change in the scene stood before me. A spirit cloaked in roaring flames hovered
in the air, her eyes gleaming with resolute intent.

“Esi-chan, please\!”

Enduring the pain wracking my body and mustering my scant remaining magic, I
issued a command to my second partner.

In response, Esi-chan, the fire spirit, grinned.

In an instant, the flames engulfing the room surged toward the White Knight.
They formed a spherical prison around the enemy, which began to shrink, clearly
compressing its contents.

But that alone wasn’t enough. The fiery sphere creaked and groaned, signaling
the White Knight’s attempts to break free.

In that case, we’d give it one final push.

“Maimu-chan\!”

Calling my other partner’s name, I squeezed out the last of my magic.

“Aye\!”

With her reply, the fire spirit switched to the water spirit. Her hair and eyes
shifted from red to blue in an instant.

Then, water conjured by Maimu-chan enveloped the fiery sphere.

—In that moment.

_BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM_\!\!\!

A massive explosion, accompanied by a cloud of steam, shook the entire room.

The sheer force nearly blew my battered body away, but a barrier of water caught
me. Maimu-chan must have protected me.

When the steam cleared, only scattered fragments of white armor remained where
the White Knight had stood.

“We... won...”

My magic was completely depleted. I was at my limit.

Perhaps the certainty of victory caused me to let my guard down, as I lost
consciousness once more.
