# Chapter 5: Magic Administrator

“Results beyond my expectations, huh.”

Landing beside the unconscious Marina, I—Zex—let out a sigh of admiration.

When she’d seized her chance, I’d sensed the potential for tremendous growth.
That’s why I didn’t force her to stop and instead let her act as she saw fit.

Even so, this outcome is astonishing. To form a contract with the two souls
residing within the composite spirit and perfectly control their power... The
use of \[Composite Spirit Magic\], a feat supposedly reserved for the Spirit
King, displayed breathtaking might.

To clarify one thing, even I didn’t know there was another soul inside Maimu. No
matter how skilled I am with mental magic, detecting a soul buried deep within
is no easy task, and there’s no room to discern a spirit’s soul during a sudden
rampage. It was only when Marina took control that I learned of the second soul
seated within Maimu.

This is just my take, but both Maimu and the fire spirit—Esi, was it?—seem like
the prickly sort. To negotiate smoothly with both, and wield them as a mage with
such finesse, is nothing short of remarkable.

Marina’s communication skills are truly something to behold. It’s precisely
because it was her that she could tame two spirits and overwhelmingly defeat a
dungeon boss in the level-ninety range. Sure, there were some dicey moments, but
once she wakes up, I’ll have to shower her with praise.

Now, putting my thoughts on Marina aside for the moment...

“Guess I’ll deal with some overtime.”

After retrieving the unconscious Maimu into \[Phase Concealment\], I turn my
gaze to the remains of the dungeon boss.

The white armor lies shattered in pieces, its dazzling light from when it was
active now gone. It’s nothing more than scrap now.

I cast \[Compress Cube\] at the wreckage. The transparent enclosure envelopes
every fragment, crushing it into something smaller than dust in an instant.

Once the spell concludes, the area should be nothing but ash.

—Or so I thought. The situation didn’t resolve so neatly. A single black stain
remains on the pristine white floor.

At first, the stain, darker than raven feathers, is no bigger than the tip of a
pinky. But as I stare, it begins to bubble and expand. It grows at an alarming
rate, spreading across the floor and even taking on a three-dimensional form.

What emerges is a black knight—an entity nearly identical to the dungeon boss
Marina and the others just defeated.

There’s no mistake: Marina’s group defeated the dungeon boss. The door to the
innermost chamber opened the moment the white knight fell, signaling the end of
the final trial.

So, what is this black knight before me? One thing’s certain—it’s not friendly.
I can feel its hostility radiating like a pulse.

“An extra stage, is it?”

I quip lightly, readying myself for combat.

Level-wise, I should have no trouble winning. But this thing is shrouded in too
many mysteries. If possible, I’d like to capture it alive and investigate
further.

“For starters, let’s try one.”

First move wins. Without any preparatory motion, I unleash nearly a hundred
\[Gunshot\] spells. Long-range sniping might be out of my reach, but
simultaneous deployment around me is no issue.

Not a single shot misses. The \[Gunshot\] barrage strikes the black knight
dead-on.

Yet, there’s no sign of damage. It staggers slightly from the impact but seems
unfazed, as if it felt no pain. It stands there, radiating hostility.

Four possibilities come to mind, the attacks were outright nullified, it’s
blocking pain, it’s reducing damage, or attacks outside its weak point are
meaningless.

For now, I’ll assume multiple scenarios and proceed cautiously.

I charge forward. The black knight mirrors me, running as well. The distance
between us closes in an instant, my dagger clashing with its longsword.

Having already activated \[Body Enhancement: Divine Transformation Overflow\],
the blade lock collapses immediately. I flick its longsword aside with a
scooping motion and slash upward with the dagger in my other hand, aiming for a
reverse diagonal cut.

The black knight can’t keep up with my spinning slash. It tries to dodge, but I
carve a gash from its flank to its chest.

“...”

Yet, there’s no joy in my heart. Frowning, I use the momentum of my spin to
deliver a mid-level kick. Unable to evade, the black knight is sent flying
backward.

I glance at the enemy tumbling across the ground while steadying my own
faltering stance.

The sensation of that attack was... bizarre. I know I hit the enemy’s body, but
there was no resistance. It’s a contradictory statement, I know, but I can’t
describe it any other way.

“\[Seraphim Edge: Thirteen Wings\]”

Deliberately chanting aloud, I unleash the attack on the black knight. Thirteen
blades, modeled after white angel wings, streak toward the enemy, each tracing
countless trajectories.

The black knight doesn’t dodge. It takes the assault head-on and doesn’t budge
an inch.

There’s no doubt now. This thing has nullification resistance. The black knight
possesses complete immunity to at least physical and non-elemental
attacks—likely a high-tier resistance. Otherwise, it couldn’t withstand attacks
in my divine transformation state. Lesser nullification abilities would’ve been
breached.

With such robust resistance, no wonder it doesn’t bother dodging. It makes sense
to focus on offense rather than evasion.

That arrogance, however, will be its downfall. I’ve bought enough time.

I blink once. That’s all it takes. In that fleeting moment, I activate my Magic
Eyes.

A white flame ignites in my right eye. The pupil transforms, its red rim framing
a white sclera with three red lines at the center. \[Blazing Flame Heaven
Eye\]—a Magic Eye that burns all I deem unworthy until nothingness.

And my left eye... gleams. Divine, radiant, and pure white, it illuminates the
surroundings. Its name is \[Magic Eye of White Radiance\]. This, my second Magic
Eye, possesses the fundamental ability to see through all.

With my burning right eye and gleaming left eye, the black knight is caught in
their gaze.

—The battle is already decided.

Less than a second later, I deactivated the Magic Eyes. The left is fine, but
the right still places a heavy burden on me. Wiping the blood trickling from my
eye, I fix my remaining left eye on the enemy.

The black knight is already ablaze. Its limbs and neck have vanished, white
flames still raging at the stumps.

I haven’t killed it. Thanks to the \[White Radiance Magic Eye\], I pinpointed
the exact line to neutralize it without ending its existence. I also confirmed
its nullification resistance couldn’t block my Magic Eyes.

“That said, what’s the point of capturing it alive?”

Thanks to the \[White Radiance Magic Eye\], I already know the black knight’s
true nature. Still, it could serve as a test subject for countermeasures against
this kind of magic... so it’s not entirely useless.

Yes, the black knight is magic—or rather, a byproduct of magic. It’s a
counterfeit of the dungeon boss, created by the light magic spell \[Extreme
Flash\].

The original \[Extreme Flash\] seems to be a unique spell that bathes a target
in blinding light, laying bare their heart. This was adapted to manipulate the
“shadow” born from that light, using it as a puppet. Quite the wild application.

More than that, it seems there were prior conquerors before us. Considering
Maimu’s imprisonment, it’s likely researchers or their collaborators. They’d
taken some countermeasures, as I couldn’t read the caster’s information. To
deceive my Magic Eyes, the other party might be a Magic Eye user themselves.

One troublesome revelation after another. I'm fed up with this now.

Sighing softly, I retrieve Marina from where I’d temporarily stored her and
begin first aid.

Her injuries are pretty severe, as expected. I doubt I can heal her fully. The
best I can do is channel magic through her body to stabilize broken bones and
keep them in place.

Since I need to maintain the magic flow for a while, I decide against returning
Marina to \[Phase Concealment\] and instead carry her on my back.

The dungeon boss is gone. There’s much to think about, but for now, I’ll press
forward. I’m worried about Caron and the others, too.

With a heavy heart, I pass through the dungeon’s final door.

“Welcome to my research—guh\!”

“Yeah, yeah, save it.”

The moment I step into the innermost chamber, an irritating voice echoes. I
silence it swiftly. There are countless other presences in the room, and I knock
them out with restrained \[Gunshot\] spells.

The room’s interior, as the voice suggested, resembles a research lab. It
should’ve been a vast, empty space with only a one-meter stone pillar at the
center. But now, wires snake across the floor, and various magical devices are
scattered about.

From a quick scan, the stone pillar seems to be the only original feature of the
dungeon, with everything else brought in later.

As suspected, the researchers who captured Maimu had conquered the dungeon. The
fact that a surprise \[Gunshot\] took them all down suggests the key figure in
the dungeon’s conquest isn’t here.

“Well, if it isn’t Earl Roeme.”

The man who greeted me first was, of all people, Earl Roeme.

...No, “of all people” might not be accurate. I had a bad feeling the moment I
heard about another research facility.

I produce faux mithril wires infused with magic, letting them autonomously bind
the researchers. With that handled, I prioritize observing the facility.

Ten minutes later, I’ve grasped the general picture.

First, it’s certain that Earl Roeme created the composite magical beasts—the
chimeras. His research led him to the simplistic conclusion that combining
various magical beasts might allow them to wield multiple types of magic.

It’s a crude hypothesis, but given that it produced powerful magical beasts, the
approach wasn’t entirely wrong. The ability to phase through dungeon walls was
apparently a magic effect that allowed free movement within magical energy.

Moreover, they were the ones who fused Maimu and Esi. They captured two young
spirits by chance and applied their chimera technology to merge them. There was
no grand purpose—just a flimsy rationale about “gathering data for magical beast
research.” That’s unforgivable.

Even more infuriating is that they caused the stampede. Their experiments to
harness the dungeon’s magical energy backfired, causing tens of thousands of
magical beasts to spill out. Their notes detail the suspected causes and
conditions of the failure.

Despite the crisis in their own domain, Earl Roeme showed no concern. This is
why fanatic researchers are such a headache.

Also, about the stampede—I’d thought less than six hours had passed, but
apparently, three days have elapsed. Was my sense of time warped within the
dungeon’s walls?

Thanks to a far-seeing magical device, I can observe the surface in real-time.

The battle seems to be at its climax, with most magical beasts already defeated.
In the city of Dijott, cheers of victory are rising. At the center are Yudai and
Princess Arianorth, who likely led the charge to turn the tide.

The Saintess... ah, there she is. She’s tending to the wounded from the front
lines. The treatment center is abuzz with “Saintess” chants. Oh, Skia’s here
too, looking as flustered as ever.

The areas outside Dijott are fine, too. As per my prior instructions, Caron and
the others have done their part, reducing the remaining magical beasts to fewer
than a thousand.

It should be safe now, but... maybe I’ll wrap things up to let them know I’m
alive. Since the far-seeing device is operational, I can use its pathway.

I scan the device and hijack the \[Far Vision\] circuit. From there, I unleash
\[Gunshot\] toward the surface. A hail of magical bullets rains down, wiping out
the few remaining magical beasts.

While I’m at it, I’ll use \[Telepathy\]. Reaching everyone is impossible, so
I’ll contact Orca, who’s likely the command hub.

“_Orca.”_

_“Zex-nii\!”_

A voice bursts back, lively and almost bouncing. Sure enough, Orca’s literally
jumping on the spot.

I let out a wry smile and tell her to calm down.

Orca, still brimming with excitement, presses on.

“_You’re okay\!”_

_“Yeah, sorry for worrying you.”_

_“Seriously\! I wish you’d come back a bit sooner.”_

_“My bad. My sense of time got messed up. I thought it’d only been about five
hours.”_

_“I knew it...”_

_“Someone predicted that?”_

_“Yup. Minerva-chan and Noma-chan did.”_

I nod inwardly, impressed. With their deep knowledge of magical theory, they’d
likely make such a deduction.

Keeping my admiration to myself, I convey the main point.

_“Right now, Marina and I are in the dungeon’s deepest part. We’ll investigate
here and then head back.”_

_“You conquered it\! Congrats\! I’ll let the others know.”_

_“Thanks.”_

After a brief exchange, I cut the \[Telepathy\].

Now, let’s wrap up the investigation quickly. It’s been three days—I want to
reunite with Caron and the others soon.

I lay the still-unconscious Marina in a corner of the room and store all the
magical devices in the chamber into \[Phase Concealment\]. I toss Earl Roeme and
the other bound researchers in there, too.

The room returns to its original state, with only the stone pillar remaining.
All that’s left is to examine it.

I approach the pillar and touch it. The top surface emits a soft hum and begins
to glow. The light shifts on its own, arranging itself into a keyboard-like
array of characters.

At the same time, a rectangular light appears in the air before me—likely a
substitute for a screen. Letters flicker on and off.

“It’s like a computer.”

It reminds me of a device commonplace in my previous life but unthinkable in
this one—a hallmark of advanced civilization. This alone convinces me this place
was created by a god.

The operation is similar to a computer, so I sift through the information with
ease.

The data gathered here seems limited to matters concerning magical energy: the
total amount of magical essence in the world, the quantity of magic amassed in
the dungeon, or how much magic each race converts. It’s the kind of data
researchers would kill for.

There’s also information on magical beasts, which Earl Roeme and his group
likely exploited.

However, this alone doesn’t explain everything. They lacked the technology to
create chimeras, conquer the dungeon, or produce that black knight.

There’s definitely a collaborator—someone potentially at a limit-breaking level.

This is critical information we must extract from the captured Earl Roeme and
his team. Their collaborator, aware of their research yet aiding them, likely
harbors malicious intent. We can’t let them roam free.

“Whoa, it’s really here\!”

While mulling over my thoughts, my fingers keep moving, and I stumble upon a key
piece of information.

It’s the reason I aimed for the dungeon’s deepest part: data on the Magic
Administrators.

The term “Magic Administrator” refers to those who govern magic. Each is
assigned to a single “color,” tasked with managing the “color” of intelligent
beings rooted in this world.

Their duties are vast: overseeing the circulation and balance of “color”
throughout the world, bestowing “color” upon intelligent beings, quelling
disasters caused by an excess of “color,” maintaining dungeons, and more.

Having pledged themselves to the world in pursuit of the abyss of magic, they
bear the obligation to carry out these tasks.

In return, Magic Administrators are the sovereigns of their “color.” As the
pinnacle of their assigned hue, they can wield any magic within the bounds of
the world’s laws. For those driven by an insatiable thirst for knowledge, this
is the ultimate reward.

_...Omitted..._

The path to becoming a Magic Administrator requires offering one’s entire body
to the world. By dedicating oneself wholly to a chosen “color,” one can ascend
to the rank of Magic Administrator.

However, there is a limit: only one individual per “color” may hold the
position. No matter how one struggles, a seat already occupied by a Magic
Administrator cannot be claimed. This is an immutable law of the world.

“Ugh...”

After skimming through the data, I let out an awkward groan.

That final error—it’s definitely my fault, isn’t it? The “Morning Star” likely
refers to Akatsuki-sensei, too. The thought that I unknowingly disrupted the
world’s balance sends a chill down my spine.

A silver lining, or perhaps a fortunate accident: toward the end of the system
messages, there was a note about a “Countermeasure: Cease future supply of
‘white.’” The balance has likely been adjusted.

From the phrasing, it seems no more non-elemental humans will be born. Fewer
people will face persecution, and the spread of mental magic might slow down.
All things considered, it’s a decent outcome. A result born of circumstance, but
still.

Honestly, I’m just grateful this dungeon is so sophisticated. Without it, who
knows what state the world would be in now?

Did I overdo my self-enhancement? Hmm, maybe. But I wouldn’t be where I am
without pushing myself this far. It’s all results in hindsight.

...Dwelling on “_what-ifs_” is pointless. Let’s focus on the future.

Some of the terminology was vague, but I’ve got a rough grasp of what Magic
Administrators are. In broad terms, they’re like an advanced version of Magic
Eyes. It’s the pinnacle one reaches by offering not just the eyes but the entire
body as a price—a permanent sacrifice, unlike the temporary cost of my Magic
Eyes.

Offer blood for magical power, eyes for Magic Eyes, and everything for Magic
Administrator. It sounds simple in words, but the reality is brutal. It’s
literally abandoning one’s humanity.

That said, it clears up a few things. If I may say so myself, I’ve mastered
magic to a considerable degree. Yet, I always wondered why I hadn’t reached the
level of a Magic Administrator. If the prerequisites are fundamentally
different, it makes sense I couldn’t get there. Not that I have any intention of
giving up my humanity, mind you.

It also explains why Akatsuki-sensei lacks knowledge about Magic Administrators.
He’s not a being who is rooted in this world—likely ineligible to form a
contract with it. That’s probably why he’s disconnected from such information.

I came here with modest expectations, but the results far exceeded them. Not
everything is clear, but the information I’ve gained will be invaluable for
future strategies.

That said, a few doubts have cropped up as well.

First: referring to elemental magic as “colors.” It could just be a different
term, but... I might need Minerva’s help to investigate. We’ll run some
experiments when I get back.

Second: Septem, the demon, called the Western Demon King the “Great Magic
Administrator of Light.” Yet, the term “Great Magic Administrator” doesn’t
appear anywhere in the dungeon’s terminal. The reason for adding “Great” is
unclear, and it leaves an unsettling feeling.

Third: the whereabouts of the other Magic Administrators. According to
Akatsuki-sensei, they vanished long ago, but the data suggests they’re still
alive. And the Blue Magic Administrator is none other than the infamous witch
Leviathan.

I was shocked to learn she’s been alive for so long, but... we’ll need to keep
an eye on the other Magic Administrators’ movements. They didn’t appear in the
original story, but that doesn’t mean they won’t show up in reality.

Ugh. I’ve solved some mysteries, but it feels like I’ve ended up with more
things to ponder and prepare for. No rest for me yet, it seems.

Sighing softly, I manipulate the terminal.

The floor beside me begins to glow.

This light is the mechanism to return to the surface—or rather, a gimmick that
teleports to the dungeon’s entrance. Those who reach the innermost chamber can
move freely within the dungeon.

Finally, I can go home.

I gather Marina and the others, whom I’d left aside, and step onto the glowing
floor.

The tumultuous dungeon exploration, though it brought many mysteries, was safely
completed.
