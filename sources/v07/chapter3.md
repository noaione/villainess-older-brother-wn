# Chapter 3: The Water Spirit

The interior of the dungeon’s floor was nothing short of a raging torrent.
Magical energy swirled with such ferocity that it could strip away one’s sense
of direction. For an ordinary human, their body would likely have been torn to
shreds.

But I’m no ordinary human. I unleashed the vast reserves of magical power within
me, enveloping both myself and Marina in a protective barrier. With this, I
figured the risk of death was off the table.

While I was at it, I took care of the chimera that had dragged us in here. It
had been careless enough to keep Marina’s legs bound, so I reeled it in and put
it down.

“Time to get out,” I muttered to myself.

The dungeon’s floor seemed to be some kind of alternate dimension. As far as the
eye could see, there was nothing but pitch-black darkness, filled with a
swirling mass of magical energy that dwarfed even my own reserves. There was no
sign of an exit—or anything else, for that matter, beyond the magic itself.

My own magical power has its limits, so lingering here indefinitely wasn’t an
option. I had to find a way to escape.

As for Marina, she’d passed out. She must have been hit by even a fraction of
this magical torrent. Combined with the exhaustion from fighting the chimera, it
was no surprise she’d lost consciousness.

That was fine by me. I didn’t need her help to break out of this situation, and
once we escaped, things would get hectic. I wanted her to rest while she
could—even if it’d only be a brief respite, since I planned to get us out soon.

We’d been swept some distance away, so returning to our original spot would be
tricky. Wandering aimlessly in this darkness would be reckless. The priority was
getting out.

I gradually expanded the range of my magical field, searching for a path to the
outside. The surrounding magic resisted fiercely, draining a ridiculous amount
of my power in the process. Still, I couldn’t think of any other effective
approach. I pressed on in silence.

After expending about half my reserves, I finally detected the edge of this
space. With the unconscious Marina in my arms, I hurried toward it.

I’d been a little worried about escaping without the chimera’s power, but as it
turned out, we made it out without issue.

It seemed we’d emerged from one of the dungeon’s walls. It definitely wasn’t the
third layer anymore, though the cave-like scenery remained familiar.

I immediately cast a detection spell to assess our surroundings. I picked up
traces of magical beasts here and there, but none were close enough to force us
into combat right away. For now, it looked like we could catch our breath.

“Phew.”

I let out a small sigh and sat down on the spot. Gently, I laid Marina beside
me.

That chimera, though—what was it? A magical beast capable of moving freely
within the dungeon’s walls? I’d never heard of such a thing.

What’s more, a high-level creature like that hadn’t appeared in the original
story. It didn’t even disintegrate after taking a kick from me in my divine
form. Everything about it was an enigma.

Killing it was easy enough. Even in the restrictive environment of the dungeon,
I hadn’t trained so weakly as to struggle against an enemy of that caliber.

But for anyone other than Foranada, it wouldn’t just be a struggle—they’d be
dead. If something like that got loose in the outside world, it’d cause
widespread chaos without a doubt. I’d need to pinpoint its source before it
triggered a stampede.

“Well, first things first—getting out of this dungeon,” I said with a wry smile.

Our current situation wasn’t far off from being stranded. Thanks to my \[Phase
Concealment \], we weren’t completely empty-handed, but I hadn’t prepared for a
long-term expedition. For the sake of easing Karon and the others’ worries, a
swift return was ideal.

That said, the reality of our predicament made that easier said than done.

Judging by the strength of the magical beasts around us, I estimated we were
somewhere around the fiftieth layer. My sense that we’d only been swept “a
little” was apparently way off. Even for me, traversing fifty layers would take
a fair amount of time—especially with Marina in tow.

To top it off, I couldn’t find any stairs leading upward. No matter how I
scanned with my detection spell, all I could locate were stairs heading down.
It’s possible there was some kind of gimmick on the floor above us that we’d
missed. Without unlocking it, we might not be able to ascend. In short, our only
path was downward.

If we could find a way back up along the way, great. Worst case, we’d have to
reach the deepest depths. For now, it’d be smarter to suppress my impatience and
focus on recovering my magical power.

I used mental magic to calm my mind and began meditating, aiming to restore
myself to peak condition as much as possible.

---

About thirty minutes later, Marina woke up. No magical beasts had attacked in
the meantime, and I’d managed to recover a decent amount of my magic.

At first, she was disoriented, but she gradually recalled the events before
she’d passed out. After listening to my explanation of our situation, she bowed
her head apologetically.

“I-I’m so sorry, Lord Zex. It’s my fault you’ve been dragged into this mess…”

She seemed to think our current predicament was her responsibility. Her brows
furrowed, and she bit her lip slightly.

Having a strong sense of duty is admirable, but she was overthinking this.

I ruffled her hair roughly.

“Wah\!? Wh-What are you doing\!?”

Her hair now a mess, Marina hurriedly pressed it down with her hands.

I shrugged.

“You’re too tense. You were trying to protect your childhood friend, right? Do
you regret what you did?”

“…No, I don’t.”

“Then hold your head high. I couldn’t fully react when Chimera attacked Yudai in
time. Because you acted, we gained a moment’s reprieve—and that’s why I made it
in time.”

Sure, distance played a part, but it was likely Marina’s focus on Yudai that let
her notice the assault. Maybe it was her bond as a childhood friend that kept
her attuned to him.

I met her gaze.

“You saved his life, Marina. That’s a big deal. So there’s no need to feel
burdened.”

“Thank you…”

Her eyes glistened slightly as she expressed her gratitude. The tension in her
seemed to ease, and I figured she’d be fine moving forward without slipping up.

“Alright, let’s get moving. Everyone’s waiting for us up top.”

“Yes\!”

With Marina’s spirited reply as our cue, we set off to explore the dungeon.

---

The dungeon exploration went mostly smoothly. We didn’t encounter any absurdly
powerful magical beasts like that chimera—just enemies typical of each layer.

…That’s how it’s supposed to be, of course. But after experiencing something so
out of the ordinary, I couldn’t help but stay on guard. It felt like a harbinger
of something, and that unease lingered.

Despite our wariness, no major issues cropped up, and we reached the seventieth
layer without incident.

Just as I was growing tired of the unchanging scenery, a shift occurred—as if
the dungeon had read my mind.

As usual, I cast my detection spell upon entering the new layer. That’s when I
picked up something odd: an unfamiliar space. Tucked away in a corner of the
floor was a small room that felt distinct from the dungeon itself, despite being
within it.

“There’s a strange room…” I murmured.

“Strange?” Marina echoed.

“Yeah. It’s hard to put into words, but it gives off a vibe that doesn’t feel
like part of the dungeon.”

“Inside the dungeon, but not part of it…?”

Having grown more relaxed around me during our journey, Marina tilted her head,
her natural curiosity peeking through.

I got where she was coming from. Even I knew my description sounded nonsensical.
But I couldn’t find a better way to explain it.

Now, what to do?

The saying goes, “A wise man steers clear of danger.” Our goal was to escape the
dungeon as quickly as possible, and there was no apparent need to approach
something unrelated to that.

Yet I couldn’t dismiss it outright. Why? Because Marina seemed intrigued. She
rested a hand on her chin, lost in thought.

“Curious about it?” I asked.

“Huh?”

She blinked, snapping out of her reverie, then gave a small nod.

“What was that?”

“I asked if the room’s got you curious.”

“Oh…”

She’d clearly been deep in thought. Her response came absently, her gaze
drifting into space.

After a moment of staring into the void, she spoke up unexpectedly.

“Would it be bad to take a detour?”

“Not necessarily. But why?”

“I feel… called to it. That’s the sense I’m getting.”

“Hmm.”

Marina looked straight at me, her eyes unwavering. There was little logic to
back it up—but it was too firm to call a mere hunch.

I mulled it over for a minute before nodding.

“Alright. Let’s check out the room.”

“Thank you\!”

“No need to thank me.”

Personally, I’d have preferred heading straight for the deeper layers. But I
decided to trust Marina’s instincts. Gut feelings like this can matter more than
you’d think.

Plus, I was confident I could make up for a slight delay.

---

Ten minutes later, we found ourselves face-to-face with a certain presence.

---

Activating \[Body Reinforcement\], a distance of a few kilometers was something
I could cover in no time. Less than ten minutes later, Marina and I arrived at
the small room in question.

At the entrance stood an artificial door, utterly out of place in the natural
cave-like setting of the dungeon. It was a simple design—painted blue on the
right half and red on the left—but lacked any visible handle.

Clearly, someone had added this room after the fact. That must’ve been why I’d
sensed a presence distinct from the dungeon itself.

Marina and I exchanged a glance.

“What now?” I asked.

“…Let’s open it,” she replied.

Just to be sure, I deferred to her—the one who’d suggested this detour—before
carefully placing my hand on the door. I channeled magic through it, checking
for any strange traps.

It’s less a door and more a lid, I realized.

When I pushed while infusing it with magic, it gave way, revealing a mechanism
that allowed it to detach entirely. It wasn’t fixed to the entrance and seemed
portable. The structure piqued my curiosity a little; I decided I’d take it with
me once we were done.

With such trivial thoughts in mind, I probed the room’s interior—but my
detection spell faltered. It seemed this space was judged as a separate layer
from the floor we were on, preventing my magic from penetrating fully. We’d have
to open the door and see for ourselves.

I’d already channeled magic into it. With a light push, the door shifted.

A dull clatter echoed as it slid inward. Once I confirmed it had fully detached
from its frame, I swiftly stored it in my \[Phase Concealment\]. In an instant,
the door vanished, revealing the room’s interior.

It was almost… completely empty. The small, cubic space—roughly five meters on
each side—was barren. Bare stone walls, similar in texture to the dungeon’s
caves, were all that greeted us. A desolate, unremarkable place.

Except for one thing—one single anomaly.

In the center of the room sat a figure, knees drawn to its chest. A human—no,
not quite. It was far too small to be human. Smaller even than the size of a
palm, this humanoid creature crouched alone.

“A s-spirit?” Marina whispered from behind me as she peeked inside.

Exactly as she said, no matter how you looked at it, what sat there was a
spirit. Given its tiny stature and the fact that its entire body seemed composed
of magical energy, no other conclusion made sense.

Its face was hidden by its knees, but it appeared to be female. The long,
unkempt blue hair suggested it was a water spirit.

I signaled Marina to wait, then approached the entity. I’d already confirmed
there were no traps, so the next step was to ask her what was going on.

As I drew closer, I noticed something. The water spirit seemed to be crying. Her
shoulders trembled with stifled sobs, and she hadn’t yet registered our
presence.

I got the sense this might turn into a hassle, but turning back now wasn’t an
option. Steeling myself, I spoke up.

“Uh… excuse me, spirit over there?”

“Huh?”

I’d half-expected her not to notice me, but that fear proved unfounded. My
slightly raised voice reached her ears just fine. She turned toward me, a
somewhat dazed expression on her face.

The water spirit had the appearance of a cute young girl—around eight years old,
judging by her features. She looked undeniably youthful.

She must’ve been crying for quite a while. Her eyes were red and swollen, and
her cheeks bore streaks of dirt from wiping away tears. Fresh tears spilled over
regardless, marring what would’ve otherwise been a refined, delicate beauty.

At first, she stared at me blankly, but realization slowly dawned. Her mouth
opened and closed wordlessly, and her trembling shifted into a different
kind—less sorrow, more fear.

She didn’t scream, but she was clearly terrified. Her blue eyes, brimming with
unshed tears, reflected a deep despair, as if she’d come face-to-face with her
worst nightmare.

I tilted my head inwardly. All I’d done was speak to her—why did this feel like
a scene where a pitiful citizen cowered before a tyrannical demon lord? I didn’t
get it at all.

“Um, I’m not here to harm you… so could you please not be so scared?”

“Eek\!”

I tried a gentle tone and a smile, but it was useless. The moment she heard my
voice, she let out a short, sharp squeak.

I was at a loss. I’d dealt with kids plenty of times, but never had one reacted
to me with this level of fear. If it were just shyness, I could manage, but she
seemed terrified of something specific about me. Until I figured out what and
fixed it, we weren’t getting anywhere.

Using mental magic to calm her was an option, but since she was a spirit, I’d
need to adjust it first. Suppressing strong emotions like this was tricky
anyway—without addressing the root of her fear, it’d just reset the moment the
spell wore off.

No helping it, then.

With an inward sigh, I turned to Marina, who’d been waiting behind me.

“Can I leave this to you?”

“Haha, I’ll do my best\~” she replied with a wry smile, clenching her fists. She
seemed motivated enough.

We switched places. Now it was Marina’s turn to approach the water spirit.
Letting her handle an unknown entity wasn’t ideal from a safety standpoint, but
I deemed it unavoidable this time. As a fallback, I’d keep my guard up.

“Hello there, spirit-san,” Marina said, radiating a calm, friendly vibe.

“…”

The response was silence, but it felt different from how she’d reacted to me.
When she stole a glance at Marina, her eyes held something beyond fear.

Was it… familiarity?

Thanks to observing the spirits that hung around Noma and Foranada, reading
their emotions came easily to me. This assessment felt solid. Without a doubt,
the water spirit was starting to feel a faint sense of affinity toward Marina.
And with every word Marina spoke, that connection seemed to grow stronger in
real time.

It was an odd situation, but I had a hunch about why this was happening.

What if the spirit before us was Marina’s destined contract spirit?

From what I’d heard, the bond between a spirit mage and their spirit relied
heavily on instinct. Noma once said, “You contract them when you get that
spark,” implying there’s a compatibility only they can sense.

Could this be that very “spark” moment? Maybe the water spirit’s growing
affinity stemmed from an intuitive recognition of Marina as her contract
partner.

Looking back, it was Marina who’d suggested this detour. Had she, too,
subconsciously picked up on the spirit’s presence?

The relationship between spirits and spirit mages might lean even more on gut
feeling than I’d imagined. If these two were fated partners, it’d be less a
mystery and more a downright supernatural phenomenon.

…For some reason, I felt like someone, somewhere, was snarking, “You’re one to
talk,” but it was probably my imagination.

While I mulled this over in silence, Marina kept talking to the water spirit.
Unlike with me, the spirit was now responding—albeit sparingly—and they’d
managed to establish some level of conversation. A stark contrast to my
experience.

Thanks to Marina’s efforts, we gleaned three key pieces of information. First,
the spirit’s name was Maimu. Second, she’d been captured by humans and subjected
to experiments. Third, after the experiments concluded, she’d been abandoned in
this room.

Maimu wasn’t strong enough to defeat the magical beasts outside, so being stuck
here was as good as imprisonment. Fortunately, the abundance of magical energy
kept her from starving, but the isolation had left her lonely. That’s why she’d
been crying.

She didn’t know what kind of experiments the humans had conducted, though—likely
due to her lack of knowledge on the subject.

Now, her story raised three questions.

First, how had humans managed to capture Maimu? Spirits are beings of pure
magical energy, invisible to the naked eye. Without \[Magic Sight\], humans
shouldn’t even be able to perceive them, let alone catch one. It’s a problem
that precedes the act of capture itself.

If Maimu’s account was true, then the humans who nabbed her must’ve possessed
\[Magic Sight\] or some other means of detecting spirits. That was a deeply
intriguing detail.

Second, what were these experiments? Maimu said she’d been unconscious for most
of them, leaving her clueless about the specifics. But it’s a safe bet that
experiments of this sort are rarely benign. I wanted to investigate—and, if
necessary, put a stop to them—before they spiraled into something bigger.

Third, how had they brought Maimu to this little room? As I’d mentioned before,
humanity had only charted up to the tenth layer of this dungeon. Reaching the
seventieth layer should’ve been impossible under normal circumstances. Yet here
was this room, casually set up like it was nothing. Frankly, it strained belief.

Maimu didn’t seem to be lying, though. That left one logical conclusion: the
humans who’d captured her wielded power they couldn’t reveal publicly. Combined
with the earlier points, this whole affair reeked of something shady.

“There, there. Big sis is here now, so Maimu-chan won’t be lonely anymore\~”
Marina cooed, gently stroking Maimu’s head with her fingertip as the tiny spirit
perched on her palm.

The two had grown remarkably close. If you ignored the species gap, they could
pass for trusting sisters.

At this rate, it didn’t look like she’d hinder us moving forward.

I let out a relieved breath and started to ask, “Hey, I’ve got a question—”

“Eek\!”

“…”

The moment I spoke, Maimu let out a yelp. Her face contorted in the same terror
as before, and she clung desperately to Marina.

I didn’t get it. I really didn’t. If she’d warmed up to Marina, shouldn’t she at
least be a little open to me, her friend? Was I being selfish here?

As I wrestled with this oddly disheartening feeling, Marina gave a wry smile.

“Um, Maimu-chan, this big brother’s a really nice guy, so you don’t have to be
so scared, okay?”

“…”

Marina tried to reassure her, but Maimu just shook her head, stubbornly
rejecting me.

At first, I’d assumed her fear stemmed from mistaking me for the human men who’d
captured her—a guess based on her tone. But at this point, it seemed there was a
specific reason behind her terror.

I asked Marina to find out what was scaring her.

Maimu murmured a single line: “The magic’s scary.”

“Oh?” I said, intrigued.

“Eeeek\!”

Whoops. Her unexpected answer drew a heavier tone from me than intended. I’d
need to watch myself.

Still, her response was fascinating.

If she was afraid of magic, she must’ve sensed a sliver of my immense magical
power. She hadn’t passed out, so she likely couldn’t grasp its full scope, but
even so, this piqued my interest.

Here’s why: I was currently cloaking myself with \[Magic Concealment\]. I’d
honed this technique enough to fool even Akatsuki, a former divine apostle. Yet
Maimu had pierced through it, if only slightly. That was astonishing.

Judging by the modest amount of magic composing her, she wasn’t particularly
powerful. This suggested she was a spirit specialized in perception—a niche
talent, but one that outstripped even a divine Apostle’s senses. She was someone
I absolutely wanted on our side.

“Planning to contract her?” I asked Marina curtly.

She nodded.

“Yeah. I’d really like to form a contract with her, if possible\~”

Well, that tracked. Their mutual fondness was plain as day.

The fact that she’d been an experimental subject was a concern, but her
perception abilities outweighed that minor hiccup. I could investigate any
issues myself, and until then, Noma or I could keep an eye on her.

Speaking of which, leaving Noma behind to guard the surface was a misstep. If
I’d stashed her in \[Phase Concealment\], I could’ve gotten her take on Maimu
right now.

…No point dwelling on what I didn’t have. The decision was already clear.

“If you’re both on board, I’ve got no objections.”

“Thank you so much\!” Marina beamed, her face lighting up with gratitude.

I hadn’t done anything worth thanking, but whatever.

“Maimu-chan, will you contract with me?” Marina asked.

“Yes\! Maimu will contract with Marina-neechan\!” she chirped back.

In a warm, cheerful exchange, their contract was sealed in no time.

A contract’s just a matter of linking magical pathways—no grand rituals
required.

And so, a new member officially joined our party.

---

With the water spirit Maimu now part of our group, our dungeon exploration
progressed even more smoothly than before. The reason? Her perception skills
were far more impressive than I’d anticipated.

Her detection range was slightly narrower than mine, but her precision at close
distances surpassed me. It wasn’t just magical energy—she seemed attuned to
other subtle cues, alerting us to the faintest irregularities with pinpoint
accuracy. Thanks to her, the efficiency of my \[Gunshot\] sniping skyrocketed,
cutting down the time we spent bogged down by magical beasts.

“Amazing, Maimu-chan\!” Marina exclaimed.

“Hehe,” Maimu giggled shyly.

We were now on the ninetieth layer. Maimu had just pointed out a magical beast
that had cloaked itself with near-zero magical energy, and I’d taken it down. My
own detection would’ve missed it until it got much closer, no question. Marina’s
unrestrained praise was well-deserved, and their exchange brought a touch of
warmth to the otherwise grim dungeon crawl.

I’d noticed it along the way, but Marina and Maimu were an exceptional match.
Maimu, with her low self-esteem and tendency toward negativity, was perfectly
supported by Marina’s upbeat personality and strong communication skills.

It went beyond that, too—magically speaking. On her own, Marina’s magic
manipulation was average at best, barely reaching the level of a typical mage
even with effort. But when channeling spirit magic through Maimu, she
transformed. She could handle even the most powerful spells with high efficiency
and speed—a mastery worthy of a “fully certified” title. She might even rival
Orca at this rate.

Honestly, it was like she was a different person. I’d known Marina had a knack
for spirit magic, but this drastic shift caught me off guard. Having seen the
spirit mages of Shinkoku Forest before—upper-tier mages at best—the contrast
only deepened my amazement. She was like a fish in water.

“No enemy signatures on my detection. Looks like we’ve cleared this floor’s
magical beasts,” I said, turning to Marina and Maimu. I’d already located the
stairs to the next layer.

With their agreement, we started moving.

But we didn’t get more than five steps before we stopped. Why?

“Marina-neechan, the floor\!” Maimu shouted, her voice laced with urgency.

My detection picked up nothing, but she was pointing at the dungeon floor. Nine
times out of ten, it had to be that synthetic chimera beast again.

I couldn’t confirm if Maimu was right, but that didn’t mean I’d sit idle.

Without hesitation, I spun around, scooped Marina into my arms, and leaped back
a good distance.

“GRAAAAAAAHHHHHHH\!\!\!”

The next instant, a chimera burst forth, its lion head roaring as it
emerged—right at the exact spot Maimu had indicated.

“Over there, there, and there too\!” Maimu cried, clinging to Marina’s shoulder
and pointing at three more spots on the floor and walls. Her voice trembled more
with each word, tears streaming down her face. She was teetering on the edge of
panic.

As if triggered by her fear, three more chimeras appeared. There was no doubting
it now—Maimu could sense them. To detect them amidst that torrent of magical
energy spoke to a potential beyond anything I’d imagined.

Well, the ambush was avoided. Time to wipe them out. Maimu’s emotions had been
overtaken by terror, likely because she could instinctively gauge the chimeras’
strength. Shaking like a leaf, she wouldn’t be much use in this state. To
prepare for the next surprise attack, I needed to eliminate her source of fear.

No need to worry, though. The chimeras’ only real threat was their stealth. Once
they were visible like this, finishing them off was child’s play.

But before I could act, the situation took a sharp turn.

I was just about to fire a \[Gunshot\] into one’s skull when, out of nowhere,
flames erupted, rampaging through the dungeon.

The ferocious blaze engulfed all four chimeras, reducing them to ash in an
instant. Unsated, it scorched the dungeon’s walls and floor as well.

For now, the fire wasn’t coming my way, but if this kept up, the heatwave would
roast us alive. Worse still, there was a bigger problem at play.

I glanced behind me.

There stood a spirit, spewing torrents of flame from her entire body—a fire
spirit girl with wild, flapping red hair. Her crimson eyes glinted with a manic,
battle-hungry fervor.

At her feet lay Marina, collapsed. She was conscious but too exhausted to stand,
likely drained of magical energy—sucked dry by the fire spirit in front of her,
if I had to guess.

It was hard to believe, but the evidence left no other explanation. The spirit
currently unleashing this fiery havoc was none other than Maimu, Marina’s
contracted partner. Her expression was so different that it threw me off at
first, but the facial features matched. There was no mistaking it.

A water spirit turning into a fire spirit—its polar opposite? I wanted to call
it a sick joke. Even her personality seemed to have flipped. It made no sense.

—No, wait. I had a faint suspicion about what was happening.

These chimeras, roaming the dungeon freely with unnatural strength, weren’t
naturally occurring creatures. They were unmistakably artificial, crafted by
someone’s hand.

Then there was Maimu, a spirit captured and experimented on by humans.

Two rare anomalies showing up in the same dungeon at the same time—was that
really a coincidence? My mind buzzed with the suspicion that both were the work
of the same people.

If that hunch was correct, combined with the current situation, it led to an
unsettling conclusion.

The creation of a synthetic spirit. That might’ve been what someone was trying
to achieve.

In other words, I theorized that Maimu was a spirit forged by blending water and
fire elements, much like the chimeras.

The very idea of combining sentient beings made my stomach churn. But it was the
only explanation that fit the facts.

—Well, fine. With so little information, no amount of theorizing would get me to
the truth right now. I had a more pressing task at hand.

I reassessed the situation.

The chimeras were gone. No other magical beasts were in sight, and if any were,
they’d likely be consumed by the flames. I could focus entirely on the fire
spirit Maimu.

Marina’s magic depletion stemmed from Maimu’s relentless firestorm. A contracted
spirit draws on their contractor’s magic to cast spells. If I didn’t resolve
this quickly, Marina could die.

The fastest solution would be to kill Maimu. Eliminate the source, and the
crisis ends.

But that wasn’t an option. Not with Marina, lying there, fixing me with a
resolute stare. Her eyes pleaded: \*Don’t kill her.\*

If it were some random nobody, I’d ignore it. But this was Marina, someone I
held in no small regard. Brushing off her request felt wrong.

That left me with the next best option. It was riskier and more
draining—something I’d wanted to avoid—but at this point, I had no choice.

I summoned a surge of immense magical power, aiming one hand at Maimu, who was
still spewing flames. To ensure success, I chanted aloud:

“\[Phase Disconnect Logout\]”

The spell activated, and Maimu’s form vanished like mist. Not only that—the
flames she’d been radiating disappeared completely as well.

All that remained were me, Marina, and the lingering heat of the fire.

Marina and I were sitting near the staircase leading to the hundredth floor. Due
to Maimu’s earlier rampage, both of our magical reserves had been significantly
depleted, so we were focusing on recovery.

How did we manage to descend nine floors in such a short time, you ask?

The cause lies in the spell I used to stop Maimu: \[Phase Disconnect Logout\].

That spell was an application of \[Alternate World\]. You could even call it a
byproduct of \[Time Stop\]. It’s a spell that creates a magical space outside
the world, isolating the target from reality.

By transferring Maimu to another dimension, the contractual link was temporarily
severed, halting the outflow of Marina’s magical power. It also succeeded in
isolating Maimu, making it a two-birds-one-stone solution.

However, this spell falls under the category of spatial magic, which is unusable
within dungeons. Naturally, there was a price for forcing its activation.

First, the magical cost was far greater than when used on the surface. The
expense was beyond my expectations—even for someone like me, two-thirds of my
magical reserves were consumed. And that was just to create a small space to
contain a palm-sized spirit. It was a stark reminder of how taboo spatial magic
is within a dungeon.

The second consequence was the collapse of part of the dungeon. To explain in
detail, the spell’s activation caused the floor within a ten-meter radius to
crumble, and this collapse extended nine floors downward. From the ninetieth to
the ninety-ninth floor, a massive hole was carved straight through. The reason
we’re now just before the hundredth floor is because we were caught in that
collapse.

The dungeon likely collapsed because, for a brief moment, I seized control of a
portion of its domain. It’s akin to forcibly removing a piece from a Jenga
tower. For such a small-scale takeover to cause this much damage, it’s clear
that deploying a barrier or spell of greater magnitude would be unwise. At
worst, the entire dungeon could collapse.

But I digress.

Some time had passed since we began resting—roughly an hour, I’d estimate. With
the aid of mental magic, both Marina and I had nearly fully recovered. Marina
had sustained minor burns, but all I could do was provide basic first aid. I
lacked the means to heal another person’s injuries.

As for Maimu, trapped within \[Phase Disconnect Logout\], she had gone silent a
short while ago. Even after Marina’s magical supply was cut off, Maimu had
continued to rampage, but it seems that state of frenzy doesn’t last long. Or
perhaps thirty minutes or more is plenty long enough?

In any case, the situation had calmed. The sudden crisis had been temporarily
subdued, and on the surface, everything appeared neatly resolved.

In truth, I suspected the dungeon ended at the hundredth floor. Up until now, my
detection hadn’t reached other floors, but the hundredth was different. There
was a single, vast chamber housing an immensely powerful magical beast. Nine
times out of ten, it was a boss battle.

Dungeon bosses are generally formidable foes. Their level exceeds eighty,
surpassing even the final boss of the original story. It’s clear the dungeon is
designed to prevent anyone from advancing further.

That said, to me, it’s as good as child’s play. It’s not an opponent I can
afford to underestimate, but it’s also not one I need to fight with desperate
effort. If I were to charge in now, I could likely conquer the dungeon with
ease.

But is that really the right choice?

For some reason, I couldn’t shake the feeling that acting now would be a
mistake. It was a vague, almost instinctual sensation, not entirely clear.

“Zex-sama,” Marina suddenly spoke, breaking my brooding thoughts. Since Maimu’s
rampage, she had been lost in thought with a serious expression, but now her
face showed a resolute determination.

I turned to face her, urging her to continue.

Marina spoke.

“Could you let me talk to Maimu?”

“…Right now?” I asked.

“Yes,” she replied promptly.

I furrowed my brow.

To be honest, Marina’s proposal was difficult to accept. Confronting Maimu, her
contracted spirit, was undoubtedly a necessary step for her as the contractor.
But when asked if it should happen immediately, I could firmly say no. For
optimal conditions, it would be better to hold that conversation on the surface,
in a controlled environment. Having Noma, another spirit, provide advice would
also be a significant advantage.

Marina seemed to understand my concerns. She bit her lip in frustration.

But she didn’t give up. Quickly composing herself, she looked at me directly.

“Please. I know I’m asking for something unreasonable. Even so, I want to talk
to Maimu-chan right now.”

“Can I ask why?” I inquired, probing the root of her earnest plea.

Marina paused for a breath before answering.

“When Maimu-chan went berserk, I felt her heart flow into me. It was painful,
agonizing, sad… and lonely. I think Maimu-chan needs someone to be there for her
right now. And I believe that’s my role as her contractor.”

She continued, “The enemy waiting ahead—we’ll defeat it. I’m certain this is our
trial. It’s the wall we must overcome to truly become partners.”

“…You noticed, huh?” I said.

“Yes. Maybe it’s a benefit of my contract with Maimu-chan, but my sensory
abilities have improved, even if not as much as hers,” Marina said with a
sheepish laugh.

Her smile seemed tinged with uncertainty, but deep in her eyes, I could see
unwavering conviction.

Up until now, Marina and Maimu wouldn’t have stood a chance against the dungeon
boss. No matter how heightened their senses, they lacked both the offensive and
defensive capabilities needed.

But if Marina could fully control the power of the fire spirit, that assumption
might change. Those flames had a potency rivaling even Caron’s.

I should stop her. That’s what my rationality urged. Coldly considered, Marina’s
argument lacked any solid basis. It was all driven by emotion and intuition,
with no guarantees.

Yet, at the same time, I couldn’t help but think, why not take the chance? If I
believed in Marina’s feelings and hoped for her growth, wouldn’t that lead to a
better future?

As Marina fixed her pale blue-violet eyes on me, I deliberated carefully.

After a moment, I let out a sigh. Standing up, I gave a light flick of my
fingers.

In response, Maimu appeared in Marina’s hands. She lay limp, unconscious.

Marina’s eyes sparkled at the sight.

“Zex-sama\!”

“For now, I’ll leave it to you. But you get one shot. Make sure you talk it
through properly,” I said gruffly, then leaned against a nearby wall, closing my
eyes.

I wanted to believe in the indomitable heroine I once admired in the original
story. I chose to trust in the potential of the likable Marina I’d come to know
in reality.

Rational arguments about leaving the dungeon quickly or prioritizing safety
flooded my mind. But I had no regrets about this choice. I was certain that this
heroine, Marina, would deliver results beyond my expectations.

Now, it was Marina’s task. My role was done. I focused on staying vigilant of
our surroundings, waiting for the two of them to prepare.
