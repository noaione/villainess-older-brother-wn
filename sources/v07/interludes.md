# Interludes

## Light Blinds All

_Timeline: Immediately following the conclusion of this volume_

---

I found myself stepping into the secret chamber connected to my private
quarters, a place I hadn’t visited in quite some time.

This sanctuary was crafted by _that person_… and fortified with their spells,
rendering it impervious even to Foranada’s finest intelligence operatives. If I
recall correctly, they described it as “a blinding light of intense magical
power that obscures all perception.”

Thanks to nearly seven years of keeping a low profile, the scrutiny directed at
me has noticeably diminished. Combined with the concealment granted by _that
person_… there’s no way I’d be discovered so easily.

As I waited alone in silence, a sudden burst of light materialized. A radiant
source, too intense to keep my eyes open, enveloped the entire room.

But the ordeal lasted mere seconds. After a brief moment, the light subsided,
and in its place stood a young girl—no, she _descended_.

She appeared to be around three or four years old, clad in tattered clothing.
Yet her golden hair and eyes radiated an almost divine brilliance. Unable to
withstand the dazzling intensity for more than a few seconds, I averted my gaze
downward.

As I glanced at her neck and prepared to ask a certain question, something else
caught my attention.

“That boy…?”

Beside the girl stood a slightly older child. Unlike her, everything about him
was shabby—likely an orphan. His unfocused eyes were concerning, but I had no
doubt she was responsible.

The young girl clapped her hands with a soft “Oh\!”

“I thought he’d be useful for scouting, so I nabbed him,” she said.

Her tone was cheerful, almost musical, as if she were discussing something
trivial. The content of her words, however, was anything but.

I furrowed my brow.

“You made sure there’s no trace, right?”

“No worries, no worries\! My light blinds everything. This boy’s existence has
already vanished from everyone’s memories.”

“…Good.”

What a terrifying power—to erase a person’s existence so effortlessly. And not
just people—anything she chooses can be wiped from human perception. Truly
horrifying.

But I was in no position to voice such fears. This secret chamber, like so much
else, was a product of her benevolence. I’d benefited far too much from her to
indulge in something as naive as fear. That stage had long since passed.

Then, almost as an afterthought, the girl mused aloud.

“Oh, but scouting’s done, so I guess he’s no longer needed.”

With that, she flicked her right index finger lightly, as if nudging a trinket.
No hesitation, no weight to the gesture.

In an instant, the boy dissolved into particles of light, his life reduced to
fleeting foam.

Suppressing the alarm bells ringing in my instincts, I pressed forward with the
question I’d meant to ask.

“Did you deal with Earl Roeme?”

Indeed, I had tasked her with eliminating Roeme.

You see, his research facility was one of the projects I’d been supporting.
Though I’d severed all contact seven years ago to avoid Foranada’s notice, he
was still someone tied to me.

Why order his elimination? Simple—Roeme got greedy. He sought the Hero’s body as
research material and launched an attack.

I wanted to curse his stupidity. Personally, I wouldn’t have cared, but for
someone in his position, the Hero is untouchable. Losing the Hero would allow
the Eastern Demon King to revive. That’s why the world’s nations have
unanimously decreed that “the Hero and Saintess, whose roles remain unfulfilled,
must not be harmed.”

Even if we overlook his attack on the Hero, the timing was utterly deplorable.
Why act when the Earl of Foranada was nearby? It was practically a novel form of
suicide. I was genuinely at my wit’s end.

Thus, I requested Roeme’s silencing. I’d taken every precaution to ensure no
trail led back, but against Foranada, I wanted to be extra cautious.

It so happened that the girl had business in the area, so she agreed to my
request.

However…

“The Earl of Foranada got to him first,” she reported.

A failure. Stunned by the unexpected news, I faltered.

“W-What do we do?\! You were involved with Roeme too, weren’t you? They’ll find
out\!”

She, on the other hand, remained infuriatingly calm, laughing lightly.

“No worries, no worries. Our existence is obscured from their memories. Mere
humans can’t break through.”

“But we’re talking about _the_ Earl of Foranada…”

True, she’s a being of extraordinary power, but that accursed earl is a monster
in his own right. Anything could happen.

The girl merely shrugged.

“It’s fine, I’m telling you. From what I hear, that earl’s a white mage, right?
Peering into memories is tricky, sure, but that relies on reading a person’s
perception. My magic alters perception itself, so it’s unbreakable.”

“…You’re certain?”

“I’ll swear it. There’s no way we’ll be exposed.”

“Ha… fine.”

My heart was still racing.

The Earl of Foranada’s monstrous reputation echoes through the royal palace like
a curse. Until my preparations are complete, I absolutely cannot afford to be
noticed by him.

Wiping the cold sweat from my brow, I posed a sudden question.

“By the way, did you achieve your goal?”

“Oh, absolutely,” she replied.

The girl smiled—a vivid, almost sultry grin that felt utterly incongruous with
her childish appearance.

“She was a gem. Completely unblinded by my light, she saw me clearly. To think
there’s someone in this world who radiates light equal to mine—it was genuinely
shocking. I can see why my underlings recommended her.”

“That impressive, huh?”

“Yup. ‘Seeing is believing’ is a universal truth. I knew in theory that the
combination of light and fire would be dazzling, but seeing it in person made me
think, ‘I _want_ her\!’”

“Hold your horses. That’s the monster’s sister. Touch her carelessly, and you’ll
be the one taken down.”

Sensing her eagerness to charge in, I quickly interjected.

As expected, the girl pouted, clearly displeased.

“Ugh, aren’t you worrying too much?”

“Please, I’m begging you\!” I dropped to my knees in a desperate bow.

My groveling paid off. The girl relented, backing off from her impulsive plan. A
wave of relief washed over me.

“Fine, fine. I’m used to waiting. For now, I’ll just watch her grow. I’m looking
forward to that moment.”

The girl smirked.

“Just you wait, Caroline-onee-chan.”

## Caron: The Naive Sister

One lazy afternoon, I—Caroline—spotted Onii-sama in the Magic Fragrance Flower
garden and nearly rushed to his side. But I halted mid-step. Someone else was
already at his side.

It was Marina-san. Noma and Maimu-chan were there too, it seemed. They appeared
to be engaged in a lively discussion, likely involving their contracted spirits.

“Hmm.”

Charging in would be simple, but perhaps I should reconsider the timing. If I
were to barge in now, Marina-san might not be able to fully enjoy herself. Her
kindness is one of her virtues, but she sometimes overthinks others’ feelings.
In moments like these, those of us who know her well should step in to support
her.

“Shion, let’s find another spot,” I said to Shion, who stood nearby, before
turning on my heel.

She tilted her head, puzzled. “Is that alright, Caroline-sama?”

“It’s fine. I’m not so tactless as to disrupt that atmosphere.”

The experience in the dungeon seems to have had a positive effect. Ever since,
Marina-san has grown bolder in her approach toward Onii-sama. She’s not as
aggressive as Nina, mind you, but it’s a marked improvement from her earlier
flustered self. If Onii-sama is beginning to reciprocate, then there’s no need
for me to interfere.

Besides, being overly forward here wouldn’t suit Onii-sama’s tastes. Maintaining
the distance he prefers is key.

As for myself, there’s no need to dwell. Since acknowledging these feelings,
I’ve laid plenty of groundwork. Onii-sama seems to have noticed the shift in me
as well. Our relationship will resolve itself in time, so there’s no reason to
rush.

The real issue lies with this servant, who I regard as an elder sister.

“More importantly, Shion, are _you_ alright?”

“What do you mean, Caroline-sama?”

Must she be so oblivious at a time like this? Shion showed not the slightest
hint of urgency.

A faint headache began to form.

“For now, we’re heading back to my room for a lecture.”

“Ehh\!?”

Suppressing a sigh, Shion and I left the garden behind.

Back in my room, I had Shion prepare tea. I wanted to help, but she insisted I
refrain. Something about the tea catching fire—what could she possibly mean?

Once the tea was ready, I made Shion sit across from me, and the lecture began.
With a touch of pressure in my tone, I posed a question.

“Shion, let me ask you: who spends the most time with Onii-sama in a single
day?”

“Uh…”

The unexpected question seemed to catch her off guard, a flicker of confusion
crossing her face. But true to her role as Onii-sama’s secretary, she composed
herself in under two seconds.

“That would be me, I suppose. I’m at his side during most work hours. Outside of
that… especially at the academy, other servants take over, but it’s on a
rotation. Their individual time with him is minimal. Caroline-sama and Nina
spend a fair amount of time with him, but that’s mostly in private settings.”

Shion spoke confidently, laying out her reasoning.

Indeed, Shion is the one who spends the most time with Onii-sama. It’s a
regrettable truth that it isn’t me, but work hours are the bottleneck. I can’t
meddle in the affairs of the head of the house without good reason.

But to return to the point…

As I’ve said, Shion shares the most time with Onii-sama. That means she has more
opportunities than anyone to deepen their bond. Even during work, there are
surely ways to make use of that time.

Yet, despite being in such an enviable position, Shion…

“Shion, are you even trying to advance your relationship with Onii-sama?
Fainting over something as simple as a hug is, frankly, a bit much.”

This thirty-year-old elf is far too naive, barely progressing since eight years
ago. She was the first to confess her feelings to Onii-sama, yet now she’s dead
last in terms of progress. At this rate, even Marina-san, the newest contender,
might overtake her. It’s truly lamentable.

“Your innocence is part of your charm, Shion. The sweet, almost juvenile dynamic
between you and Onii-sama warms the hearts of those watching. But settling for
the status quo is unacceptable. At your current pace, it’ll be decades before
you even think of having children.”

“C-C-Children\!?”

Shion, who had been blushing slightly and looking sheepish during my lecture,
turned beet red at the mention of children. Just how naive can she be…?

Normally, she’s the epitome of a “cool-headed, capable secretary,” but when it
comes to matters of the heart—or anything involving Onii-sama—she turns utterly
hopeless. It’s endearing, in a way. But at this rate, anything more will indeed
take decades. That simply won’t do. I want both Onii-sama and Shion to find true
happiness.

As I pondered what to do, Shion spoke up, her face still flushed. “If I may,
Caroline-sama…”

She adopted a defensive stance, ready to counter.

“Our relationship has progressed recently. I appreciate your concern, but
there’s no need to worry so much\!”

“Oh? What sort of progress?”

I hadn’t heard of any developments between them. This was intriguing.

At my question, Shion fidgeted with her index fingers, her gaze darting sideways
in embarrassment. After a long pause, she finally spoke.

“…We held hands.”

“Ha.”

I was a fool for getting my hopes up.

I understand that holding hands for the first time is a joyful milestone, and I
have no intention of dismissing her feelings. But to be _this_ embarrassed over
something so minor is unacceptable. Clearly, something must be done.

To be honest, I’m reluctant. Meddling in someone else’s love life feels
improper, especially given the complexity of Onii-sama’s romantic entanglements.
While I’ve accepted the presence of other women, that doesn’t mean I’m immune to
jealousy.

Minerva and Nina seem to handle this balance effortlessly—perhaps a product of
their noble upbringing? Not that I’m any less noble, mind you.

But complaints won’t change anything. If I don’t support the love life of
someone I regard as a sister, I’d hardly be worthy of the Foranada name. Family
supports one another, after all.

“For now, we’ll work on building your tolerance for embarrassment.”

“W-What are you planning, Caroline-sama?”

“Fear not. With Onii-sama’s cooperation, we’ll take care of everything.”

I can’t very well let anyone else see Shion in a compromising state, now can I?

In the days that followed, Shion’s tolerance for embarrassment improved
slightly. Though, for a while, she was prone to fainting spells.

## Garuna: Surrender

On a gentle spring day, bathed in soft sunlight, the academy was on its spring
break. As a result, I—Garuna—wasn’t tending to my usual duties as an attendant.
Instead, I was working as a maid at the secondary residence.

I couldn’t help but think, _If the academy’s on break, why can’t I get some time
off too?_ But I kept that to myself. Compared to servants in other domains,
we’re treated several Ascot at least a few times better than that. Sure, we’re
guaranteed generous benefits, and I already get plenty of vacation time. It’s
just my lazy nature talking, that’s all. If I wanted, I could probably put in
for paid leave right now and get it approved. Not that I’d waste it like that.

While I was lazily cleaning windows as usual, Teria and Maron—my peers and
friends—called out to me.

“Garuna, may we have a moment of your time?” Teria asked.

“I’m working here,”

“That’s no issue. We’ve received permission.”

“Is that so? Then I guess it’s fine.”

Teria was her usual composed self, but Maron seemed off—fidgety, restless.

A bad feeling crept over me, and I tried to use work as an excuse, but it seemed
they’d already cleared everything. I had no choice but to surrender.

Following Teria’s lead, we walked through the mansion. Maron trailed behind me,
likely to block any escape attempts. This definitely smelled like trouble.

If I got serious, slipping away would be easy. But doing that would mean losing
this life in Foranada for good.

I’m a wanderer by nature, but I’ve come to enjoy my life here. The work’s tough,
but it’s lively and fun, and my colleagues are all good people. A place this
good doesn’t come along often. Throwing it away over something as vague as a
“bad feeling” would be a terrible waste.

Plus, there’s the matter of _him_ still not giving me the time of day. That
stings my pride as a woman. At the very least, I want to figure out why before I
leave. With my skills… I should be able to handle whatever comes.

We walked in silence for a while until we arrived at one of the guest rooms.
Since it wasn’t a private chamber, I couldn’t tell who had summoned me. Given
that Teria and Maron were involved, I had a rough guess, but I hadn’t narrowed
down the possibilities.

Probing the room’s presence yielded only faint traces. That made sense. If they
were calling me in, they’d have taken precautions against detection.

“We’ll leave you here,” Teria said. “Please enter the room alone, Garuna.”

I shrugged. “Pretty cold, huh? You could at least tell me who’s waiting. We’re
friends, aren’t we?”

“…”

Neither of them responded, standing firm and silent.

Teria’s a stickler, so I expected that from her. But for Maron to clam up too?
That all but confirmed who was inside. It had to be our master—Zex-sama. Hers,
mine, and Teria’s.

Given the circumstances, I had to assume I’d been found out. I couldn’t recall
slipping up, but with someone like him, it wouldn’t be surprising if he’d pieced
it together from the smallest clues.

_Seriously? I’m busted? What do I do now?_

The word “escape” flashed through my mind again—but with Zex-sama so close, that
option was already off the table. He’s not the type to let someone slip away
after getting this near.

Swallowing the sigh that nearly escaped, I steeled myself and knocked. A voice
from within called, “Come in.”

As instructed, I opened the door.

As expected, Zex-sama was inside, seated on a chair at the far end of the room,
gazing at me calmly.

The mood was like a commoner facing an audience with a demon king. There was no
overt hostility, but a heavy pressure weighed on me. The atmosphere was exactly
as intense as I’d imagined.

After entering, I bowed and spoke. “I came as summoned, my lord.”

“Good work. You can relax,”

At his words, I eased my posture—but only slightly. The tension in the room
hadn’t lessened one bit, leaving no room to let my guard down.

After a brief silence, he spoke again.

“Originally, I planned to leave you be unless you made a suspicious move. I
didn’t sense any hostile intent from you, so I intended to keep it to minimal
surveillance.”

His words seemed directed at me, yet also like he was mulling them over himself,
his tone almost reflective.

“But things have changed. If there’s a chance you hold information about the
source of Caron’s fated obstacle—the ‘Western Witch’—then I have no choice but
to question you.”

“\!?”

That sealed it. For him to mention the “Western Witch” in my presence meant my
true identity was exposed.

I spun on my heel, aiming for the door I’d left open.

But it was futile. The door that had been there moments ago was gone. No, it
wasn’t just the door. The entire guest room had been overwritten. Without my
noticing, it had transformed into a vast, empty space with nothing in it.

I knew this place.

“No way… the dungeon’s deepest level?” I muttered, stunned.

The scene was unmistakable, a place I couldn’t confuse with anywhere else.

Yet I couldn’t believe it. Dungeons interfere with barrier and teleportation
magic. The only one permitted to teleport within is the person who conquered it.
Dragging someone else along forcibly should be impossible.

And disguising the space as a guest room? That kind of trick typically falls
under barrier magic, which shouldn’t function in a dungeon either.

None of it made sense. My understanding of the world was being upended at every
turn.

Unfazed by my confusion, Zex-sama spoke calmly. “Exactly. This is the dungeon’s
deepest level. Escape is impossible.”

I must’ve been making a face like I’d bitten into something sour.

I’d been trying to activate the teleportation privilege of a dungeon conqueror,
but there was no response. He’d already accounted for that too. As he said,
escape was a lost cause.

“No need to look so grim. Answer my questions honestly, and I won’t harm you. In
fact, I’ll guarantee you can continue your life here as before.”

“…What do you want to know?”

I’d served him for nearly three years. I knew he wasn’t the type to make empty
promises. While I searched for a way out, I decided to hear him out.

“First, let’s confirm: you’re the witch Leviathan… or rather, Leviathan Luschwe,
the Blue Magic Administrator, correct?”

“…Yes.”

That’s right. I’m an ancient being, feared by the world as a witch. One of the
seven seats of color, a seeker of magical truths.

For ages, I’ve wandered the world. Using blue magic to disguise my appearance,
I’ve slipped into countless places and lived among them. In this era, I was
merely posing as an ordinary citizen of the Holy Kingdom.

I’ll admit, I chose Foranada out of curiosity. When I heard that the man before
me bore a baptismal name tied to mine, I wanted to see what kind of person he
was. I never expected him to be so perfectly my type in both looks and
personality.

“When did you figure it out?”

I knew that staying near someone as extraordinary as him would eventually expose
me. I got too comfortable, lingered too long, and let my guard down.

But his response was beyond anything I could’ve imagined.

“From the start.”

“Huh?”

“I knew you were Leviathan the moment you knocked on Foranada’s door.”

“Wha\!?”

I let out an undignified yelp.

It wasn’t very ladylike, but who could blame me? To think he’d known since I
applied for the job—who could’ve seen that coming?

“H-How…?”

I don’t like to brag, but I’m a notorious witch. I’ve sunk entire nations into
the sea and trapped regions in endless rainy seasons—deeds that’ll echo for
millennia. I’ve calmed down now, but my past sins don’t just vanish.

Yet he didn’t just hire me as a servant—he entrusted me with tending to
Caron-sama and the others. What was he thinking?

I couldn’t wrap my head around the mind of the man before me.

He shrugged. “You didn’t give off a shred of malice, that’s why. And if you
tried anything, I could deal with you instantly. It was easier to keep you
close.”

“To say you could ‘deal instantly’ with an original witch, one of the Magic
Administrators? That’s a bold claim.”

“It’s the truth.”

His dismissive words made me scowl, but he didn’t seem to care. He genuinely saw
me as no threat at all.

Even I couldn’t help but bristle at that. I may not have the pride of a Magic
Administrator, but I’m still the pinnacle of blue magic. Being treated like some
lesser being was intolerable.

Sure, Zex-sama is strong. His power far exceeds human limits. But he’s still
human. Facing a Magic Administrator, who’s transcended humanity, should be a
challenge. I might only manage a stalemate—still outrageously impressive—but
there’s no way he could be this nonchalant.

Fundamentally, someone bound by the inferior concept of “attribute magic”
shouldn’t be able to overwhelm an opponent wielding “color magic.”

In the beginning, this world’s magic was called color magic—red, blue, brown,
green, purple, gold, and white, with broader scope than attribute magic. But it
was too profound for ordinary people to handle. Over time, it devolved into the
narrower “attribute magic.”

So, even limited to one color, my mastery of color magic should keep me on par
with Zex-sama. Escape might be tough, but there’s no reason for him to talk down
to me like this.

“Oh? Shall we test that?”

“If it’ll loosen your tongue, I don’t mind.”

His unshakable calm made my cheek twitch.

If he’s _that_ confident, I’ll show him.

Without preamble, I unleashed my magic: \[Submerge\], a spell to flood the
entire floor with water.

—But nothing happened.

“What?”

No matter how I focused, there was no response. I tried again and again, but the
result was the same.

Only then did I grasp the source of Zex-sama’s composure. He’d already taken
full control of the space.

“W-What did you do?” I asked, my voice trembling.

He answered casually, as if it were nothing. “I’ve restricted this space so no
one can use magic without my permission. That’s how I set up this \[Alternate
World: Baule de Tesoro\].”

“H-How did you manage barrier magic in a dungeon…?”

“You think I’d leave a weakness unaddressed forever?”

He grinned and began to explain.

“I’m always researching ways to increase firepower without collateral damage.
I’ve pursued two approaches. The first is ‘force concentration.’ If excess power
doesn’t spread, it won’t harm the surroundings. The second is ‘transparency.’ If
power spreads but doesn’t interact with the environment, there’s no issue. I
applied the latter to the dungeon.”

“Transparency’s application?”

“Exactly. Barrier and similar magics don’t work in dungeons because this is a
god’s domain. Seizing control isn’t easy, and doing so would collapse the
dungeon. Think of it like… a glass filled to the brim with water, representing
the dungeon, and my magic as a separate piece of ice. Dropping the ice in would
make the water overflow, right?”

So, he used transparency to prevent the “water” from spilling when adding the
“ice”? That makes no sense. That’s beyond the scope of non-attribute magic… it’s
something else entirely. It’s almost like—

“A white mage…” I whispered.

No, that’s not right. His magic feels like it transcends even color magic. White
magic specializes in mental effects, but his “transparency” doesn’t fit that
mold. It’s not “white.” If anything, it’s “null”—an eighth form of magic.

Zex-sama turned a smile on me. “So, Garuna… or rather, Leviathan. Care to tell
me everything you know?”

“With pleasure.”

Faced with a human who surpassed even a Magic Administrator, I surrendered
completely.

## Yurika: For a Friend

The academy had entered its spring break. The sharp chill of winter had faded,
giving way to a gentle warmth that was slowly making itself known.

The spring holiday wasn’t particularly long—about two weeks at most—so the
majority of students didn’t bother returning home. Instead, they lingered in the
royal capital, taking it easy.

Yuri—Yurika—was one of them. Or rather, in Yurika’s case, she hadn’t gone back
to her hometown even once. There wasn’t much to do there anyway.

…But her hometown wasn’t the issue right now. What mattered was the conversation
she was about to have with the girl standing before her.

“N-Nina-san,”

The setting was the Adventurers’ Guild’s royal capital branch. Gathering her
courage, she called out to the girl standing in front of the request board.

Why did it take courage? Partly because Yurika was naturally shy, but mostly
because Nina was surrounded by a gaggle of female adventurers. Judging by their
squeals and excited chatter, they were clearly her fans.

Yurika could understand why. Nina was tall, with a dignified air about her.
Yurika herself thought she was incredibly cool. Come to think of it, she’d seen
Nina swarmed by adoring female students at the academy too.

At the sound of Yurika’s voice, the attention of Nina and the surrounding
adventurers snapped toward her. Yurika let out a small “Urk” and instinctively
stepped back, but she couldn’t turn tail now—not after coming this far.

Despite the suspicious glares from the other adventurers, Nina tilted her head
curiously. “Yurika? What’re you doing in a place like this?”

“U-Um, I needed to talk to you, Nina. I went to the mansion, but they said you’d
be here…”

“I see.”

Yurika’s nervous reply earned a small nod from Nina. She glanced briefly at the
request board before turning back to Yurika.

“Alright, I’ll hear you out. There’s nothing worthwhile on the board today
anyway.”

Disappointed murmurs rose from the surrounding adventurers, but Nina paid them
no mind and approached Yurika.

“Let’s find somewhere else to talk. That’d be better, right?”

“Th-Thank you so much\!”

Feeling a twinge of guilt toward Nina’s fans, Yurika expressed her gratitude
sincerely. This time, she couldn’t afford to back down.

Nina led her to a cozy café tucked away in a quieter part of the city. Its
out-of-the-way location meant fewer customers, and it even had private
rooms—perfect for a serious conversation.

“Sorry for the trouble,”

“It’s fine,” Nina replied curtly.

Her expressionless face and clipped tone might’ve made her seem annoyed, but
Yurika wasn’t worried. After nearly a year of friendship, she knew Nina wasn’t
that petty. Besides, she had a good sense of how Nina acted when she was
actually upset.

“So, what’s this about?”

They’d settled into a private room, with their ordered café au lait and
cappuccino arriving just in time. Nina’s question came as Yurika took a deep
breath to steady herself.

“I want you to train me,”

The request seemed to catch Nina off guard, her eyes widening slightly. “Train
you?”

“Yes.”

“You mean, like, training to improve a skill or something?”

“Yes… I mean, is there any other kind of training?”

“Not really,” Nina said, tilting her head in confusion.

She must’ve been wondering what Yurika had come to discuss. The curiosity tugged
at Yurika, but she pushed it aside to avoid derailing the conversation.

Reiterating her request, she said, “I want you to train me in close-quarters
combat. Is that… okay?”

“It’s not a problem, but why?”

As expected, Nina’s expression turned skeptical.

Her doubt was understandable. It might sound like self-praise, but Yurika was
already plenty strong. She’d made it to the top sixteen in the individual
tournament and ranked seventh in her year’s overall performance at the end of
her first year—a remarkable feat for a commoner. To an outsider, it might seem
unnecessary for someone like her to beg for training from Nina, a titleholder in
her own right.

But Yurika had a reason she needed to get stronger.

“…I can’t forgive myself. I just can’t,”

She was referring to an incident from about two weeks ago, when a magical beast
far stronger than their aptitude level had attacked her group in the dungeon.
Never had she felt so powerless. A single swipe from the beast had left her
battered and unable to stand, incapable of shielding Marina-san in the backline
or her trembling classmates. They’d only survived because the earl arrived in
time, but the weight of her failure as a frontline fighter was crushing. In the
end, she and her team had been utterly useless.

## Caron: Rematch

Ever since Onii-sama took control, numerous new facilities have been constructed
within Foranada’s castle walls. Among them, the one most familiar to
me—Caroline—is undoubtedly the arena for Magic Piece matches.

This venue is unlike the outdated facilities at the academy. Utilizing

interiors that can be freely reconfigured. From barren wastelands to urban
battlegrounds, anything is possible.

It was on this cutting-edge stage that I stood. Naturally, I wasn’t alone.
Twenty meters across from me stood my dear friend Nina, clad in full battle
gear. For my part, I was in peak combat readiness, wielding Onii-sama’s
custom-made giant hammer, _Charul_.

“Are we really doing this?”

“We are. Rematch,”

Her eyes burned with an unmistakable fighting spirit.

As you may have surmised, this was a rematch orchestrated by Nina’s desire for
redemption—a reenactment of our clash from the academy’s year-level individual
tournament. She’d even gone so far as to recreate the academy’s training field
as the stage.

It seems Nina regretted her defeat far more than I’d anticipated.

Well, it’s not surprising. That match was overwhelmingly in my favor. The Damage
Conversion Ring, which relies on magical reserves, gave mages like me a stamina
advantage. The confined stage made it a feast for area-of-effect spells, and the
starting distance of twenty meters afforded me ample time to cast. With such
conditions aligned, it was like a checkmate puzzle. I ravaged the stage with
fire magic before Nina’s blade could reach me. She might’ve sliced through some
spells to close the gap, but I overwhelmed her with sheer volume. In the end, my
signature move, \[Perennial Radiance: Amaterasu\], sealed the game.

Immediately after the match, she’d said, “It’s my fault for not breaking
through.” But her emotions clearly ran deeper. Nina is more driven than anyone
to grow stronger.

I’d confirmed her intentions earlier just to be sure, but if my friend desired a
mock battle, I had no objections. She’d likely devised some counterstrategy, but
I’ve grown too. I’d turn the tables on her. As Onii-sama’s sister, I couldn’t
afford to lose so easily.

“Are you both ready?” called the servant tasked with refereeing.

She stood in the spectator seats, safely distanced from the stage. At our level
of combat, the shockwaves could injure bystanders, so her role was primarily to
signal the match’s start.

Recognizing our nods, the servant raised her voice. “Begin\!”

Without hesitation, I unleashed the advanced fire spell \[Avalanche Blaze\]. A
cascade of flames swept across the stage, engulfing it like an avalanche.

I didn’t stop there. Activating the supreme fire spell \[Flame Ring\], I set
\[Avalanche Blaze\] to trigger continuously. The stage transformed into a
hellscape of unrelenting fire.

This was a repeat of the last match so far. Back then, Nina had deflected the
flames with rapid sword strikes. It’s astonishing enough to slice through fire
with a blade, but how she managed to suppress that volume with a single sword is
beyond me. Her swordsmanship is nothing short of miraculous.

For now, I’d follow the same playbook. I was curious to see what countermeasures
Nina had prepared.

While \[Avalanche Blaze\] raged on, I raised both hands to my chest.

This was the stance for \[Perennial Radiance: Amaterasu\]. Casting another spell

the perfect moment.

\[Perennial Radiance: Amaterasu\], activate.

began to emit a dazzling glow—

“Huh?”

—or it should have.

dumbfounded, as \[Perennial Radiance: Amaterasu\] dissipated.

Let me be clear: even Onii-sama’s feat shocked me, but the core of \[Perennial
Radiance: Amaterasu\] is designed to withstand supreme-tier magic. How do you
people keep destroying it so casually?\!

I snapped out of my stupor instantly, gathering mana to cast the intermediate
light spell \[Light Cocoon\].

The moment the luminous membrane enveloped me, two earthen spikes shot up from
the ground—dual casts of the basic earth spell \[Earth Spike\].

\[Light Cocoon\] held firm against them, but the real threat was—

“Shi\!”

Bursting through the lingering flames, Nina unleashed a blinding sword slash.
Her razor-sharp strike tore through \[Light Cocoon\] like it was tissue paper.

I couldn’t just take the hit. Wielding _Charul_, I parried her blade with the
hammer’s head.

greater mass, my poor stance and lack of preparation left me vulnerable.

Still, gaining distance was a stroke of luck. I could seize the chance to
counter—except I couldn’t. With speed unthinkable for someone who’d just
delivered such a powerful blow, Nina closed in for a follow-up.

I can’t say for certain, but she likely used earth magic to manipulate her
center of gravity. Her profile claims she’s weak at magic, but that needs
revising. With control that precise, she’s more than a professional mage.

I deflected her gleaming blade with Charul, but in my unbalanced state, I
couldn’t fully fend off a master’s technique. Several hits landed. Thanks to the
ring, I avoided injury, but my mana was steadily draining.

…If I’ve been cornered this far, I have no choice. I wanted to refine it further
before revealing it, but I’d rather use it than lose by holding back.

“Charul, \[Autonomous Action: Marionette\],” I commanded.

“_Yes, Master_,” it replied.

“\!?”

Charul responded with a mechanical voice.

Even Nina, mid-swordplay, seemed startled, but this was no time to be surprised.
The real show was just beginning.

I released Charul. Unbelievably, I relinquished my weapon in the face of a sword
master.

This wasn’t surrender—far from it. Because Charul kept fighting.

Yes, with no one wielding it, Charul floated in midair, parrying Nina’s sword.

This was my trump card. Charul had been remade into a Living Weapon, imbued with
sentience. For now, it only had the \[Autonomous Action: Marionette\] function,
but according to Onii-sama, it’s a “weapon that grows by reading its owner’s
intent,” gradually strengthening over time.

It couldn’t perfectly block Nina due to the skill gap, but it succeeded in
stalling her. That brief window gave me the chance to strike back.

I gathered mana and chanted a spell. Since \[Perennial Radiance: Amaterasu\]
risked being disrupted, I opted for the next most powerful option.

“\[Volcano Ray\],” I intoned, using incantation to bolster the casting.

Instantly, the sky turned red. Something resembling dark crimson clouds
blanketed the heavens.

“Trouble—\[Volcano Rain\]?” Nina muttered.

Spotting the phenomenon, she immediately crouched, raising a small shield
skyward. It appeared imbued with an earth magic defensive buff.

Well, it’s an obvious reaction. The spell’s inspiration is a known quantity,
making it easy to anticipate.

But would such a meager defense suffice?

The magical onslaught began.

Hiss.

In an instant, a searing sound and the smell of something burning spread through
the air.

Nina’s shield had a small chunk gouged from its edge. A tiny hole marred the
ground in a straight line from it.

That was just the start. _Hiss, hiss_—the same sound rang out repeatedly, as
small holes rapidly multiplied across the stage, their pace accelerating.

“Guh\!”

Finally, a hit landed on Nina. The ring spared her injury, but I could tell a
significant chunk of her mana had been depleted.

“It’s invisible. This isn’t \[Volcano Rain\]?” she asked, panicked.

“Indeed. This is \[Volcano Ray\],” I clarified.

I wouldn’t reveal that in a real fight, but since this was a mock battle, I
obliged. The \[Volcano Rain\] Nina mentioned is one of fire’s supreme spells—a
high-powered technique that blankets an ultra-wide area with a rain of fire. In
the hands of a skilled caster, it could level a city in one shot. For me, it
could engulf all of Foranada.

The active \[Volcano Ray\], however, is a composite fire-and-light spell derived
from \[Volcano Rain\]. Its principle is simple: I imbued the fire rain with
light attributes. This not only boosted its power but transformed it into an
effect that strikes at the speed of light, making evasion or defense virtually
impossible.

Initially, I’d planned to create a spell that rained fire at light speed, but
Onii-sama stopped me. Apparently, simply increasing speed would cause secondary
effects that could reduce the surrounding area to ash. That was a close call—my
gratitude for Onii-sama’s vast knowledge knows no bounds.

Incidentally, I’m immune to \[Volcano Ray\]’s damage. Though I’m within its area
of effect, I’m excluded as a target.

From what I could see, Nina had no means to counter \[Volcano Ray\]. If I could
overwhelm her like this, victory was mine—but would it be that easy?

The fire rain, now a torrential downpour, pummeled the stage relentlessly,
accumulating damage without even needing to target the caster directly.

Yet Nina showed no signs of giving up. Her eyes remained alive with
determination.

“Phew,” she exhaled softly.

At the same time, she ceased her struggling attempts to defend, standing still
despite the fire rain battering her.

For a few seconds, Nina focused solely on regulating her breathing.

Then, she spoke a single incantation.

“\[Body Enhancement: False Deification—Limit Accel\].”

A simple chant, yet it carried an undeniable chill.

This was bad. Instinctively, I canceled \[Volcano Ray\] and invoked the supreme
light spell \[Saint Wall\].

But it was far too late.

“Ah…”

Before I realized it, I was down on one knee. My vision warped, and I lacked the
strength to stand.

My mana was nearly depleted. In a mere instant, I’d been attacked at a speed
beyond my perception.

A dull crack echoed nearby.

Glancing over reluctantly, I saw Nina standing beside me, her shattered sword
scattered at her feet.

So, her blade couldn’t withstand that last strike.

With a wry smile, I conceded, “I lose.”

“Thanks for the fight,” Nina said.

“No, thank you. This will be valuable for the future.”

As expected of Onii-sama’s disciple. My dear friend was unreasonably strong.
